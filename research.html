CS-01 · Escape-Aware Control Barrier Functions for Quadrotor Safety under Body-Rate Limits

Lei Shi, Haosong Wen, Qichao Liu · 2026-09-15 · Inspected source

Source claim (III-C Proposition 1; IV-D Theorem 1 and Remark 2): A directional stopping certificate includes clearance spent reorienting thrust. Theorem 1 concerns existence of an escape maneuver; Remark 2 identifies a thrust-ramp correction and explicitly excludes a recursive-feasibility or forward-invariance conclusion.

Read scope: Full-text targeted inspection: III-C Proposition 1, IV-C Lemma 1, IV-D Theorem 1 and Remark 2, IV-E.

Assumptions

  • Fixed obstacle normal during the maneuver; identified reorientation bound; positive aligned braking authority.
  • Delay pays the angular-budget shortfall under Lemma 1, or use the longer unconditional rotation allowance.
  • Ramp transient must be bounded explicitly or drift must use current thrust instead of its future endpoint.

Failure modes

  • Using the future low-thrust endpoint during ramp underestimates inward motion.
  • Changing obstacle normals or unsupported actuator bounds invalidate the reduced certificate.

Proposed ctrl experiment

Independent one-dimensional delayed-braking model with bounded forward acceleration during delay, constant minimum braking afterwards, and exact arithmetic. It does not reproduce the paper's quadrotor theorem.

Baseline: Instantaneous stopping distance under identical initial states and braking authority.

Measure: false-safe decisions; extra required clearance; sensitivity to delay and speed uncertainty

Falsifier: One trajectory satisfying every declared bound stops beyond the certified clearance.

Published method; proposed bounded synthesis and formalization only. Scoping confidence: 91% (subjective, uncalibrated). Direct link to existing stopping math and a small falsifiable prototype; physical-model transfer remains unverified.

CS-02 · Compute-Constrained Safety Filters with Neuromorphic Event Triggering

Tochukwu E. Ogri, Opeyemi Owolabi, Luke Fina, Christopher Petersen, Rushikesh Kamalapurkar · 2026-10-07 · Inspected source

Source claim (V-B Theorem 2; III Eq. (8); IV): A dual event trigger preserves an admissible hybrid-state set and excludes Zeno behavior while an old input remains active until the scheduled application time. Objective suboptimality is allowed; constraint infeasibility is not.

Read scope: Full-text targeted inspection: Assumptions 1-3, III constraint tightening, IV timing architecture, V-B Theorem 2.

Assumptions

  • Linear plant; bounded disturbance; compact safe set with regular boundary and locally Lipschitz barrier gradient.
  • Strictly positive uniform feasibility margin; certified residual-growth bounds and threshold inequalities.
  • Constraint-feasible solver result available within known time T; prescribed application schedule.

Failure modes

  • Deadline overrun, infeasible iterate, or underestimated growth margin removes the theorem's guarantee.
  • Computation counts alone do not establish reduced energy or physical safety.

Proposed ctrl experiment

Two-state linear plant with delayed application and logged solver deadlines; compare certificate-predicted deadline limits with adversarial delay sweeps.

Baseline: Periodic CBF and delay-tightened periodic CBF with the same plant, constraints and deadline distribution.

Measure: minimum barrier; missed deadlines; QP calls; compute time including monitoring

Falsifier: A within-contract trajectory violates the barrier or the event trigger misses the latest admissible initiation.

Published method; runtime-contract extraction is a proposed engineering extension. Scoping confidence: 88% (subjective, uncalibrated). Strong fit to explicit timing assumptions; uniform feasibility and worst-case timing need actual certification.

CS-03 · VertexCBF: Improving Neural Control Barrier Functions via Vertex-Restricted Control Search

Bojan Derajić, Sebastian Bernhard, Wolfgang Hönig · 2026-09-11 · Inspected source

Source claim (Section 4 Eq. (6)-(7); Appendix A Eq. (26)): The control-affine Hamiltonian maximum over a compact polytope occurs at a vertex. The discrete vertex Bellman expansion has a quantified local first-order consistency bound. Neural training and this consistency calculation do not certify global safety of the learned network.

Read scope: Full-text targeted inspection: sections 3-4, Eqs. (6)-(7), Appendix A Eqs. (21)-(27).

Assumptions

  • Control-affine plant and compact convex polytope input set.
  • Appendix A uses a local compact region and a C1,1 smooth test function with a known gradient Lipschitz constant.

Failure modes

  • Hamiltonian vertex exactness does not make finite-step nonlinear maximization vertex-exact.
  • A network lying below a geometric constraint may still violate the derivative condition.
  • Small PDE loss need not select the desired value function.

Proposed ctrl experiment

Low-dimensional control-affine system; enumerate vertices, export polynomial residual bounds, independently check every cell.

Baseline: Dense input sampling and an analytic barrier at the same state-grid resolution.

Measure: certified cell coverage; false-safe cells; certificate size; verification time

Falsifier: A derivative violation exists within a cell accepted by the exact checker.

Known convex optimization fact and published method; checker integration remains a proposal. Scoping confidence: 87% (subjective, uncalibrated). Small exact algebraic core fits ctrl; neural training is not the proof and state-domain verification remains substantial.

CS-04 · Contraction-Based Model Predictive Control using Bilinear Koopman Realizations with Proportional Error Bounds

Yuki Higuchi, Kazuhiro Sato · 2026-09-28 · Inspected source

Source claim (III-C Proposition 1; III-G Theorem 2; Corollary 2): Reprojection and relifting keep predictions in physical coordinates. A contraction-based tube with proportional model error supports constraint tightening and recursive feasibility under terminal conditions. The probabilistic claim is over the training dataset.

Read scope: Full-text targeted inspection: III-C Assumption 1 and Proposition 1, Theorems 1-2, Algorithm 1, Corollary 2.

Assumptions

  • Uniform proportional approximation-error bound, robust contraction metric and feedback satisfying Assumption 1.
  • Admissible connecting geodesics, terminal ingredients, initial feasibility, and the stated sampling scheme for the high-probability result.

Failure modes

  • Leaving the certified data region or assuming an unverified contraction metric breaks the guarantee.
  • A finite-dimensional lifted predictor need not stay on the lifting manifold.
  • Training-data confidence is not an independently measured per-step failure rate.

Proposed ctrl experiment

One nonlinear benchmark with a frozen dictionary, rational local error enclosure, and lifted-vs-relifted rollout ablation.

Baseline: Nominal Koopman MPC and robust MPC with a constant error bound.

Measure: constraint violations; certified feasible region; prediction error; closed-loop cost; runtime

Falsifier: A state inside the certified domain exceeds the proposed one-step tube bound.

Published method; exact restricted certificate is a proposed extension. Scoping confidence: 76% (subjective, uncalibrated). Strong library connections but contraction-metric and approximation certificates are major open implementation obligations.

CS-05 · Adversarially Robust Geometric Safety Certificates for Nonholonomic Robots Against Maneuvering Obstacles

Chandan Kumar Sah, Bazeela Banday, Jishnu Keshavan · 2026-09-29 · Inspected source

Source claim (IV-G Theorem 10; III-B Remark 4; IV-H): Contracting barrier geometry gives a conditional invariance statement when robot authority dominates obstacle capability and residual bounds. Single-obstacle boundary feasibility does not establish joint multi-obstacle feasibility.

Read scope: Full-text targeted inspection: III-B Remark 4, Theorems 3 and 10, IV-H estimator assumptions, V soft variants.

Assumptions

  • Trajectory stays in the engagement envelope; runtime constraints remain feasible; closed loop is well defined.
  • Capability and slip bounds plus a strictly positive relative-speed floor.
  • Estimator coverage additionally assumes capabilities are realized within each observation window at sampling resolution.

Failure modes

  • Leaving the envelope or approaching zero relative speed removes the certificate.
  • An adversary can withhold its maximum capability during observation.
  • Soft barrier variants trade away the strict guarantee.

Proposed ctrl experiment

Single-obstacle planar model with adversarial capability switches and a logged validity monitor.

Baseline: Nominal barrier and derivative-only robustification under the same bounds.

Measure: envelope exits; infeasible steps; minimum clearance; capability-bound misses

Falsifier: Within all declared bounds and envelope, a hard-constraint trajectory violates clearance.

Published method; validity-monitor composition is a proposed question. Scoping confidence: 78% (subjective, uncalibrated). Good source of falsification cases; assumptions make unrestricted navigation claims inappropriate.

CS-06 · Imitation Learning of Avoidance Modes for Efficient Multimodal Model Predictive Control

Changyu Lee, Byung-Kwan Lee, Kanghoon Yoon · 2026-10-12 · Inspected source

Read scope: Full-text targeted inspection: sections 3-4, Algorithm 1, section 5.1 evaluation setup.

Assumptions

Failure modes

CS-09 · Reach-Avoid Certificates for Contact-based Stochastic Hybrid Systems

Tianyi Xia, Ryan K. Cosner · 2026-10-12 · Inspected source

Source claim (Section III Theorem 1 and Corollary 1): A smooth contact certificate and compatible measurable reset policy yield an infinite-horizon reach-before-unsafe probability bound. Flow and jump conditions are checked on a sublevel corridor; a fallback policy preserves compatibility.

Read scope: Full-text targeted inspection: II-C Assumptions 1-2, III Definitions 1-2, Theorem 1, Corollary 1 and Lemma 1.

Assumptions

  • Uniform separation or positive-rate relaunch after each guard event; bounded drift/diffusion data.
  • Twice-differentiable certificate, finite reset expectation, and a declared measurable compatible policy.
  • All certificate inequalities hold throughout their continuum domains, not just sampled training points.

Failure modes

  • Near-tangential contact or an incorrect impact distribution invalidates reset assumptions.
  • An LP fitted at samples is not an independently verified continuum certificate.
  • Finite successful rollouts cannot establish the infinite-horizon probability claim.

Proposed ctrl experiment

One-dimensional bounce with a finite rational reset distribution; first prove the discrete abstraction and explicitly retain the physical-abstraction gap.

Baseline: Unfiltered reset policy and a policy constrained only by expected one-step progress.

Measure: checked probability lower bound; empirical reach-before-unsafe rate; reset count; certificate coverage

Falsifier: Exact enumeration of the finite abstraction violates its claimed probability bound, or physical reset samples violate the assumed support.

Published theorem; restricted certificate implementation is proposed, not a new theorem claim. Scoping confidence: 79% (subjective, uncalibrated). Strong new physics/mathematics pairing; full stochastic Lean formalization exceeds a first bounded prototype.

PD-01 · Following a Unique Path: A Fast Certifier Applied to Outlier-Robust Pose Registration

Connor Holmes, Abhishek Goudar, Timothy D. Barfoot · 2026-09-02 · Inspected source

Source claim (II-C2 equations (3)-(5); III Proposition 1): Primal feasibility, dual PSD feasibility and zero complementary slackness certify a feasible QCQP candidate globally. CP-Cert searches for certificates in degenerate relaxations. Its implementation admits numerical tolerances; rank tightness is established empirically for tested problems.

Read scope: II-C2, equations (3)-(5); III, Assumption 1 and Proposition 1; III-C stopping conditions. Relevant full-text passages; independent proof and code reproduction not performed.

Assumptions

  • Exact certificate criterion requires feasible candidate, symmetric data, PSD dual matrix and exact complementarity.
  • Central-path mechanism assumes the candidate's lift is the central-path limit; rank tightness plus a nonzero constraint RHS is a sufficient condition.

Failure modes

  • Floating-point PSD tolerance is not an exact proof.
  • Non-tight relaxations, nonoptimal candidates or ill-conditioned certificates can fail certification.
  • Optimality for a chosen cost does not establish correct correspondences or physical safety.

Proposed ctrl experiment

First implement a tiny rational QCQP certificate replay, including PSD LDLᵀ and equality residual checks; only then attempt pose instances. Keep external search untrusted.

Baseline: Direct exact enumeration on tiny instances; floating-point solver certificate with identical objective and constraints.

Measure: Accepted exact certificates; False acceptance on corrupted witnesses; Certified objective gap; Checker latency and witness size

Falsifier: Any accepted witness with a better feasible enumerated candidate falsifies the checker. If rational reconstruction rarely succeeds or gap bounds are useless, defer geometric scale-up.

Known certificate principle; ctrl integration is proposed, broader novelty unreviewed. Scoping confidence: 90% (subjective, uncalibrated). Primary certificate equations and numerical boundary are explicit; useful prototype is small. Scaling to realistic pose data remains uncertain.

PD-02 · Fast and Scalable Multi-Agent Distribution Matching via Partitioned Optimal Transport

Kooktae Lee, Ruchika Singh · 2026-09-30 · Inspected source

Source claim (V-B Theorem 1 and V-C Theorem 2): Mass-balanced block couplings are globally feasible; a barycentric contraction and persistent block membership give cycle-wise descent of a surrogate that upper-bounds squared Wasserstein distance. The paper explicitly does not claim monotonicity of the true Wasserstein optimum.

Read scope: III-B block feasibility; V Assumption 4; V-B Theorem 1; V-C Theorem 2 and Remark 3. Relevant full-text passages; not independently reproduced.

Assumptions

  • Matching source/target block masses.
  • Previous coupling remains feasible after motion; block membership persists.
  • Terminal barycentric contraction with 0 ≤ rho < 1.
  • Restricted transport is reoptimized at cycle boundaries; full control horizon is executed.

Failure modes

  • Changing blocks breaks the comparison coupling.
  • Collision avoidance or actuator limits may invalidate contraction.
  • A descending upper bound can conceal a rising true transport cost.

Proposed ctrl experiment

Rational 4–16 agent instance with explicit coupling; check marginals, barycenter identity and per-cycle descent. Add deliberate block migration as a negative case.

Baseline: Global assignment under the same dynamics and partitions held fixed; compare a greedy block allocator.

Measure: Surrogate and true transport cost separately; Marginal residual; Certificate rejection after migration; Solve/check latency; Collision count as separate empirical metric

Falsifier: Reject the claimed application if collision filtering routinely destroys contraction or if partition costs overwhelm any latency advantage.

Source theorem reproduction first; reassignment certificate is a hypothesis, not a novelty claim. Scoping confidence: 91% (subjective, uncalibrated). Finite sums and quadratic identities fit existing Lean algebra; system-level benefit depends on constraint interaction.

A small, gated research program

  1. Read the actual result. Freeze source version, claim location, assumptions and inspected scope. An abstract can suggest a lead.
  2. Identify the missing bridge. Connect mathematical object, physical model, available measurements and executable check.
  3. Build a falsifiable slice. Specify a fair baseline, units, admissible cases and failure conditions before measuring.
  4. Have another reviewer try to break it. Include boundary conditions, malformed witnesses, stale state and mismatched assumptions.
  5. Publish the bounded conclusion. Save replay commands and results. Write-ups and animations explain only what the evidence supports.
