## Problem and known solution
Let A ⊂ ℕ be an additive basis of order k, and let B be the set of sums of at most k *distinct* elements of A. Is the gap b_{n+1} − b_n bounded (by a constant depending on A and k)? This is a Burr–Erdős question [Er98]. Hegyvári–Hennecart–Plagne (Combin. Probab. Comput. 2007, 747–756) answer yes for k = 2, with gaps ≤ 2 eventually. That case is trivial: every odd number in A+A is a sum of two distinct elements, and A+A covers all large n. For k ≥ 3 the answer is no, via an explicit construction that takes a few pages. The JSP catalog lists the problem as Solved, Lean proof No, Eligible to claim No.

## What exactly must be proved in Lean
The full dichotomy:
(∀ A, basis of order 2 → B bounded gaps) ∧ (∀ h ≥ 3, ∃ A basis of order h with B having unbounded gaps).
This is exactly plby's `not_erdos_880` (793 lines, `import Mathlib`, ~41 lemmas, `#print axioms` included).

## Mathlib readiness (exists / missing)
- Exists: `Nat.nth`, `Nat.nth_strictMono`, `Nat.nth_mem_of_infinite` (Mathlib/Data/Nat/Nth.lean) for enumerating B; Finset sums and cardinality.
- Missing (but easy to build, as plby shows): definitions for additive basis and restricted sumset, and the k ≥ 3 counterexample construction with its gap analysis.

## Proof plan
(Moot because of priority.) The k = 2 case is a parity argument: for large odd n, n = a + a' forces a ≠ a', and n, n+2 cover the gap. For k ≥ 3, formalize the HHP07 sparse-plus-structured counterexample set: show it is a basis of order h, then show that sums of at most h distinct elements miss arbitrarily long intervals. No finite computation is needed, and no `native_decide`. Estimated effort is 1–3 days, consistent with the ~800-line existing proof.
