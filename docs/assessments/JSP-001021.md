## Problem and known solution
erdosproblems.com/1216: let f(n) be the largest k such that every n-vertex tournament contains a transitive k-subtournament. Is f(n) = ⌊log₂ n⌋ + 1 (Erdős–Moser 1964)? Stearns (1959) proved the lower bound ≥ ⌊log₂ n⌋+1 with a greedy argument. Reid–Parker (1970) disproved the formula: every 14-vertex tournament contains a transitive 5-set, so f(14) = 5 ≠ 4. Later improvements came from Sanchez-Flores (1994, 1998). The disproof is a finite case analysis built around the 7-vertex quadratic-residue tournament QR₇ (f(7) = 3, unique extremal tournament). Reid–Parker's argument is a few pages of case analysis.

JSP catalog record: Status Solved, Lean proof No, **Eligible to claim: No**, with publications St59/ErMo64/RePa70/Sa94/Sa98b.

## What exactly must be proved in Lean
The minimal disproof: every tournament on Fin 14 has an injective v : Fin 5 → Fin 14 with arcs v i → v j for i < j. Combined with log₂ 14 + 1 = 4, this refutes the formula. Stating that f(14) = 5 exactly also needs the upper bound f(14) ≤ 5. A witness tournament with no transitive 6-set would do. plby uses a 13-vertex construction (`st13`) extended to 14 vertices.

## Proof plan
(Moot given priority, but for reference.) plby's approach is a hand-written reduction to a finite certificate: a 3.3 MB `Certificates.lean` holding SAT-style clause and certificate data, checked by `decide`/definitional reduction (`of_decide_eq_true`), plus about 1.2k lines of glue. The alternative is to follow Reid–Parker by hand:
1. Pick a vertex of out-degree ≥ 7 (or in-degree ≥ 7).
2. Its 7-vertex out-neighbourhood either contains a transitive 4-set, which finishes the proof, or is isomorphic to QR₇.
3. Exhaust how the remaining vertices attach to the QR₇ copy.

Step 2 needs the uniqueness of QR₇ among 7-tournaments with no transitive 4-set, which is itself a kernel `decide` over 2^21 tournaments and too heavy without a smart reduction. Expected effort for an independent proof is about 1–2 weeks. `native_decide` is not allowed.
