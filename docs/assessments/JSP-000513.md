## Problem and known solution
Erdős–Rubin–Taylor [ERT80] asked: if G is (a,b)-choosable (from any assignment of a-colour lists you can pick b-subsets that are disjoint on adjacent vertices), is G also (am,bm)-choosable for every m ≥ 1? Dvořák, Hu and Sereni [DHS19, Adv. Comb. 2019:5, 9 pages] answer no. They build an explicit finite graph that is 4-choosable but not (8:2)-choosable, using gadget lemmas (Lemmas 3–8) and a 37-vertex intermediate gadget. The JSP catalog lists the status as Solved, Lean proof as No, and **Eligible to claim as No**.

## What exactly must be proved in Lean
Define (a:b)-choosability for `SimpleGraph V` on a finite type. Then prove that there exist n and G : SimpleGraph (Fin n) with `ABChoosable G 4 1` and `¬ ABChoosable G 8 2`; equivalently, the universal doubling statement is false. Both halves are needed. The 4-choosability half quantifies over every list assignment, so it needs the paper's structural argument; brute force is not practical.

## Mathlib readiness (exists / missing)
- Exists: `SimpleGraph`, `SimpleGraph.Coloring` (`Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Coloring/Vertex.lean`), `Finset`, `Fintype`, and graph constructions.
- Missing: list colouring, choosability, (a:b)-choosability, and anything specific to list chromatic numbers. A grep for `choosab`/list colouring in Mathlib finds nothing. All gadget machinery would have to be built from scratch.

## Proof plan
(Moot given priority.) Define `ABChoosable`. Formalize the DHS gadget lemmas: forcing lemmas that turn non-(8:2)-choosability into a list-assignment argument, plus a positive 4-choosability argument by orientation, degeneracy or case analysis on the gadget. Then assemble the final graph. The finite pieces could use kernel `decide` only on small gadgets; `native_decide` is not allowed. The existing repositories show this takes six modules (Basic/Graph/Negative/Positive/Uniformization), with about 100 s of compile time for the largest one.
