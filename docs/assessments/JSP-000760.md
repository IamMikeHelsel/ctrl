## Problem and known solution
Erdős [Er67b]: does every graph with n vertices and 2n−2 edges contain a cycle C and a vertex v ∉ C adjacent to at least three vertices of C? This would strengthen Dirac's 1960 result that such graphs contain a subdivision of K4. Thomassen (Arch. Math. 1974, 210–215) answered yes. His proof is a minimal-counterexample argument with substantial structural case analysis (connectivity reductions, 2- and 3-separations).

## What exactly must be proved in Lean
For a finite simple graph G with |V| ≥ 4 and |E| = 2|V| − 2 (or ≥ 2|V| − 2), there is a cycle (a `Walk.IsCycle`, or an injective cyclic vertex list) and a vertex v off the cycle with at least three neighbours on it. The n ≥ 4 hypothesis is needed: K3 has 2n−2 = 4 > 3 edges, so it never satisfies the count, but small cases need care.

## Mathlib readiness (exists / missing)
- Exists: `SimpleGraph`, `edgeFinset`, `Walk.IsCycle`/`IsPath`, `Subgraph`, induced and deleted subgraphs, connectivity (`Connected`, `Reachable`), basic degree-sum lemmas.
- Missing: vertex connectivity (k-connected graphs), separations and blocks, ear and path decompositions, Menger-type routing, minimal 3-connected graph theory, topological minors and K4 subdivisions. plby had to build all of this; its file list includes `AHTMader`, `AHTWatkinsMesner`, `AHTMinimalThreeConnected`, `AHTSection6/7`, `AHTSourceTheorem66Case1..5`, and `BlockPathDecomposition`, about 40 files in total.

## Proof plan
(Moot given priority.) Take a minimal counterexample. Reduce to 2-connected, then to 3-connected graphs via separation arguments while tracking the edge count. In the 3-connected case, use Mader / Watkins–Mesner-type results to route three disjoint paths from an external vertex to a cycle. Everything is a combinatorial existence proof with no finite computation; `decide` is irrelevant, and `native_decide` is neither needed nor allowed.
