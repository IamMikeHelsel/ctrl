## Problem and known solution
Erdős 735 is Murty's problem: given n points in ℝ², when can the points receive positive weights so that every line containing at least two of them has the same total weight? Murty conjectured that this happens only in four cases:
- all points on one line;
- no three points on a line (general position);
- n−1 points on a line (a near-pencil);
- a triangle together with its three angle bisectors and the incentre, up to projective equivalence. This is the 7-point "failed Fano" configuration.

Ackerman, Buchin, Knauer, Pinchasi and Rote proved the conjecture in "There are not too many magic configurations" (Discrete Comput. Geom. 2008, pp. 3–16; ABKPR08). Their proof is a long combinatorial-geometric argument that uses projective duality, line arrangements, Sylvester–Gallai and ordinary-line reductions, and a multi-stage discharging/packing argument. The JSP catalog marks the problem Solved, with "Lean proof | No" and "Eligible to claim | No".

## What exactly must be proved in Lean
This is the full classification iff, `IsMagic P ↔ collinear ∨ general position ∨ near-pencil ∨ failed Fano`, for every `P : Finset (EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 2))`. plby's challenge file gives exactly this statement: magic means positive weights `w`, a constant `c > 0`, and every spanned line fiber sums to `c`. The reverse direction (each of the four cases is magic) is easy. The forward direction is the whole ABKPR paper.

## Mathlib readiness (exists / missing)
- Exists: `EuclideanSpace`, `Collinear`, `affineSpan`, `Finset` sums, and basic linear algebra.
- Missing from Mathlib: Sylvester–Gallai (grep finds only unrelated "Sylvester" hits), projective duality and line-arrangement combinatorics, cyclic orders of lines, and discharging machinery. plby built all of this from scratch: about 150 files, with roughly 4.9k lines in just 8 of them that I sampled.

## Proof plan
No plan is worth pursuing. Reproducing the proof means formalizing all of ABKPR, as plby did: Sylvester–Gallai, an ordinary-line reduction to a "reduced core", projective/Levi-graph extraction, and Stage-3/Stage-4 discharging that ends in failed-Fano recognition. That is XL effort, and it would not have priority anyway. No finite `decide` shortcut exists, because the statement covers arbitrary n and arbitrary real coordinates.
