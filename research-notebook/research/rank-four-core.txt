Rank-four research core: bounded formalization, 2026-10-17

Result

LeanControls/RankFourCore.lean contains eight checked theorem declarations. They
formalize the local two-block algebra behind the provisional rank-four witness
argument, directly in six dimensions and with arbitrary outside-support coupling.
They do not establish the full rank-four exclusion theorem or the order-six
symmetric-maximizer conjecture.

Exact formal statement

Use J=[[0,1],[-1,0]] and X=[[0,1],[1,0]]. For arbitrary real a,b set

  K=diag(aJ,bJ,0_2),  S=diag(aX,bX,0_2).

For every real 6x6 coefficient matrix A satisfying exactly

  A[0,3]=0 and A[1,2]=0,

Lean proves

  ||L_A(S)||_F^2-||L_A(K)||_F^2
    = 4a^2(A[0,1]^2+A[1,0]^2)
      +4b^2(A[2,3]^2+A[3,2]^2) >= 0.

Indices here are zero-based. The two assumptions mean the upper active cross
block A[{0,1},{2,3}] is diagonal. All other 34 entries of A are unrestricted,
including both directions of coupling to coordinates 4 and 5. The comparison
does not assume A symmetric, normal, diagonal, or of rank four.

Lean also checks K^T=-K, S^T=S, S^2=-K^2, and

  ||S||_F^2=||K||_F^2=2(a^2+b^2).

The zero choices of a or b are allowed. When both are zero the result is a true
zero-witness comparison, not a statement about an amplification quotient. The
theorem does not divide by a witness norm or assert it equals one.

The conjugated_comparison theorem transports the result through any supplied
real Q satisfying Q^T Q=I. It constructs Q S Q^T, proves its symmetry and matching
Frobenius norm, and compares its amplification with Q K Q^T under coefficient
matrix Q A Q^T. This uses the previously checked orthogonal covariance lemmas.

What this closes and what remains

The finite-matrix algebra, all factors/signs, and the cancellation of arbitrary
outside-support couplings are now checked. The earlier random construction tests
are no longer needed as evidence for this exact conditional statement.

The theorem ASSUMES prepared coordinates. It does not prove that a general
skew witness of rank <=4 can be put in those coordinates jointly with A.
Remaining bridge:
1. Produce real orthogonal skew canonical form for a supplied witness, relating
   its rank to at most two active 2x2 blocks.
2. Prove a signed 2x2 SVD of the active cross block using two SO(2) rotations.
   The determinant-one condition preserves J in each active block; an arbitrary
   O(2) factor can reverse its sign and must be tracked if used instead.
3. Compose these transformations and invoke conjugated_comparison. Degenerate
   rank-zero/rank-two witnesses and singular cross blocks require coverage.
4. Only afterward derive invertibility requirements for a strict 6D singular
   pair, using even-rank parity, singular-pair attainment, and restricted-adjoint
   norm equality. Those analytic statements are not part of this file.

Literature check and novelty boundary

The primary Chen–Tian 2015 publisher page confirms the known n<=5 theorem:
https://doi.org/10.1137/140974031
Its full proof remained behind access control; the /epdf endpoint redirected to
the access/abstract page. Thus I cannot establish whether this exact two-block
identity is explicit there. No access restriction was bypassed.

The freely accessible Chen–Tian 2016 generalized-operator paper explicitly uses
skew canonical block form, singular-value decomposition, orthogonal invariance,
and symmetric witness comparison in the proof of Theorem 2.5 (pp. 615 onward).
Lemma 2.2 also gives the trace-based comparison criterion. This is clear prior
art for the reduction strategy, although it is not the same two-block theorem.
https://files.ele-math.com/articles/oam-10-35.pdf

Accordingly, this is a formalized, likely known proof component. Do not advertise
it as a new matrix theorem. The current open n=6 question concerns arbitrary
skew witnesses, including full-rank witnesses; it remains untouched globally.
