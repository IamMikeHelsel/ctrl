CONTROLS CRITIC 05 — source and assumption review
Final acceptance is recorded separately in report.txt after all checks finish.

Main result
RobotMotion.FIRHold.delayed_return_physical_terminal is a conditional theorem
about one scalar physical trajectory f on [0,H], with H >= 171/50 seconds.
It proves a tracking-position band of 1/5000 m on the entire interval, forward
progress of at least 1/1000 m by 1/2 second, a zero-goal position band from
71/50 seconds, and speed/acceleration bands from 171/50 seconds.
Those bands are |v| <= 1/1000 m/s and |a| <= 1/50 m/s^2. They are residual
bounds, not a claim of exact rest.

Actual equations
IsTrajectory requires continuity of f on the closed interval and the classical
derivative equations at every interior time. Its components are position,
velocity and acceleration. The physical controller is exactly
u=q''-800(x-q)-60(v-q')-(a-q'')/2+q'''/96.
The actual acceleration equation is a'=r(t)*(u+w-a). In particular, the
controller's -a/2 term does not replace the plant's additional -a term.
The review includes a literal-coefficient client for this equation.

The fixed four-box FIR history uses width 1/5 second, an increment 1/50 m at
11/50 second and an increment -1/50 m at 31/50 second. The reference and its
first three derivatives are zero after 71/50 seconds. The proof imports the
accepted exact reference and checker facts rather than assuming this tail.

One initial cube
The three coordinates are ex, ex+ev/40, and ex+ev/20+ea/1600. Their absolute
values are bounded by 1/5000 at global time zero only. This is a coupled error
condition; it is not three independent physical position/velocity/acceleration
limits with the same numerical value. The source derives the entire tracking
cube using checked_tracking, then evaluates that result at the handoff time.
No new initial-state assumption, state reset, exact endpoint, or separately
supplied hold trajectory occurs in the main statement.

General composition
trajectory_shift restricts f to f(t0+s) and shifts both input functions by t0.
Its general statement can have an empty horizon if t0>H; the main use has
t0+2<=H, so that case cannot make the claimed terminal interval vacuous.
constant_tail_hold changes the controller formula only after q=[c,0,0] and
j=0 are known. It retains the same f(t0+s), r(t0+s), and w(t0+s).
tail_cube_identity is a direct coordinate identity at t0.
tracked_tail_terminal assumes the full inherited tracking cube and derives its
hold initial cube. The main theorem discharges this full-cube premise from the
single global-time-zero condition. The local hold time t-t0 is at least two
seconds exactly when global t>=171/50 in this example.

Changing inputs and existence
The pointwise bounds are 80<=r(t)<=120 per second and |w(t)|<=1/100 m/s^2.
There is no separate continuity, derivative bound, dwell time, constancy or
input-estimation assumption. The imported energy comparison uses a constant
coefficient 20, so its continuity hypothesis does not constrain physical r.
However, a classical trajectory is supplied as a premise. The result is not
an existence theorem for every bounded, possibly discontinuous pair r,w.

The imported certificate is dimensionless: z=(80000*(x-c),2000*v,50*a),
V=z^T P z. It is not mechanical energy. It derives V'<=20*(1-V), then
V(t)<=1+999999*exp(-20*t), and the stated speed and acceleration bounds for
local hold time >=2. The original initial cube supplies V(0)<=1000000;
the proof does not substitute a tighter unit ellipsoid as an extra premise.
The endpoint inequalities and coordinate bounds were independently accepted
in critic-04. This review rebuilds their actual source and kernel dependencies.

Non-vacuity and negative evidence
The builder's constant-bias witness has a permanent 12.5 micrometre position
offset. It refutes an endpoint reset and exact goal arrival. Its moving speed
and acceleration equal the reference. The changing-input witness changes
r from 92 to 100 at 1/2 second and adjusts a prescribed bounded w so the same
smooth state satisfies the classical ODE. Both inputs have become constant
by the hold tail. This witness does not itself demonstrate variation during
the hold, although the universal theorem allows such variation and the
previous hold gate separately checked a changing-hold witness.

The reviewer adds a full FIR trajectory with an exponential error, rate 640/7,
and disturbance q'''/1920. It satisfies the sole initial cube and the actual
ODE, but has nonzero speed at the guaranteed terminal time. This tests the
difference between residual bounds and exact finite-time rest. A separate
large-initial-state hold trajectory tests why the initial cube cannot simply
be deleted. It is a logical counterexample, not a hardware test scenario.

The reviewer also tests the wrong-clock substitution as a whole trajectory
claim. The correctly shifted state has derivative zero at local time 1/2;
the unshifted rate and disturbance would require -1/48 m/s^3 there. Derivative
uniqueness rules out that substituted trajectory. No plotting tolerance or
sampling argument is used in this test.

Publication limits
The new result closes the previously separate FIR tracking and terminal-hold
contracts. It supports a formalization and worked-case contribution. It does
not establish a new controller, a new quadratic method, or research novelty.
3.42 seconds is a sufficient guarantee; it is not a sharp stopping time.
It proves neither the 1.7-second numerical task's velocity/acceleration targets
nor feasibility of the 1.3-second task. The position band at 1.7 follows from
the earlier position tail, but the new terminal bands start later.

No theorem here covers sampled feedback, sensing or dispatch delay, command
clipping, friction loss outside the rate band, multi-axis coupling, contact,
or Franka hardware. It is not a PL d/e safety claim. A reference velocity or
jerk admission result is not automatically an actual velocity or jerk limit.
No additional scalar proof branch is needed to make this claim coherent.
The next work is manuscript integration, artifact replay, and human evaluation
of the result's value; any shorter deadline or physical implementation needs
a separate declared model and test.
