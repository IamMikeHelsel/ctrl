INDEPENDENT HISTORY-PRUNING REVIEW

Decision: ACCEPT for future reference equivalence and guard equivalence.
Evidence gate: A. This grade concerns proof evidence, not novelty or speed.
No required repair was found.

The partition is correct. Events with tau+4*h <= c contribute their signed
increment to the new position offset. Events with c < tau+4*h remain in their
original list order. Thus active events, queued events and repeated increments
are handled without assuming they are distinct or positive.

For positive h, future_jet_eq proves equality of position, velocity, acceleration
and jerk for every t >= c, including t = c. Its rational version connects the
executable filter and offset sum to the real reference. The existing exact
jerk and acceleration guards accept equivalent histories after pruning.

Invalid widths do not create a hidden gap in guard equivalence. Both guards
reject h <= 0 before their real interpretation applies. Future jet equality
does require h > 0. Our zero-width witness shows why that premise is necessary.

The useful boundary

A constant offset cannot reconstruct past motion. The proof does not reset or
otherwise alter physical state. It does not prove an actual-state trajectory
equivalence, an admission-state update algorithm, infinite-stream behavior,
bounded storage, or a hardware effect. Integrating it into an online admission
state needs an explicit update of both the offset and retained list.

Removing expired events can reduce future checking work. It does not bound the
number of active or queued events and does not establish a speedup when those
events dominate the list. Keep that limit beside any cost discussion.

Next productive gate

Use this as an implementation-preservation lemma. Prove the online state update
maintains the same future reference when pruning is followed by admission.
Measure a declared workload with expired events if a speed claim is needed.
For many active events, the adjacent-cell checker remains the relevant separate
optimization. Do not treat pruning as evidence of better physical motion.
