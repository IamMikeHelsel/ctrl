Domain
GUComplex.CCl is exactly C tensor_R GUClifford.Cl. The real domain is the fixed
14-generator Clifford algebra with seven positive and seven negative squares.
No identification with a separately defined Clifford algebra over a complex
quadratic form is needed or claimed in this gate. The codomain is Matrix
(Finset (Fin 7)) (Finset (Fin 7)) C, with 128 rows and 128 columns.

Construction of the map and inverse
The accepted real algebra equivalence is transported through the tensor product.
TensorProduct.piRight and piScalarRight then distribute the tensor over the two
finite matrix indices. The pinned library defines their inverses by finite sums
of single-coordinate maps and proves both inverse identities. These are linear
maps. They do not install pointwise multiplication on the matrix codomain.

The local source separately proves preservation of multiplication on every pair
of pure tensors. realToComplexMatrix is the coefficient algebra map applied to
matrices, and therefore preserves genuine matrix multiplication. The library
algEquivOfLinearEquivTensorProduct extends the pure-tensor multiplication result
by tensor induction. The actual linear equivalence supplies bijectivity. Thus
neither faithfulness nor multiplication is an extra premise of the final claim.
The forward scalar map uses z, not its complex conjugate.

Adjoint
Kc is the coefficient extension of K=N0*N1*N2*N3*N4*N5*N6. Each Ni is the
accepted actual negative generator in the ordered exterior basis. The source
uses K^T=K and K*K=I to prove the corresponding complex Hermitian and square
identities. It does not replace K with a matrix chosen to fit the conclusion.

The inherited bar is unchanged. It conjugates scalars, negates real vectors,
and reverses multiplication. The pure-tensor bridge uses the accepted real
adjoint identity. Tensor induction extends the bridge to every element. The
all-matrix inverse version is obtained from the actual surjective equivalence.
The skew-condition theorem transports an equality; it does not by itself define
or prove a global Lie-group representation.

Primary-source boundary
The fixed April 2021 author draft states the real matrix identification in (3.18)
and a complex grade decomposition in (3.27), printed pages 22–23. Its adjacent
prose does not explicitly state scalar conjugation. The present bar convention
must remain explicit. The critic-01 author question about that convention remains
open. The current proof establishes the algebra under this declared convention;
it does not establish what an omitted source convention was intended to mean.

The accepted critic-01 errata remain authoritative: action (9.4) exists; its
variation domain and choices are a separate issue. Invariance under all real
bivectors is weaker than requiring every vector generator to be skew-adjoint.
The iKD counterexample prevents claiming that Spin invariance alone selects K.

Not established
No Hermitian signature theorem, global Spin-group embedding, Phi selection,
action variation, field equation, experimental prediction, or publication novelty
is established by this gate. It is standard algebra formalization. General
complex tensor and matrix statements are formal; the independent Gaussian-integer
examples are finite regression checks only.
