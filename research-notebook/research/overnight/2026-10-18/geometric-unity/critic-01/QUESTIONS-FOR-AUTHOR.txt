Questions to make the GU formalization precise
Prepared 18 October 2026 UTC. These questions have not been sent.

We are formalizing selected algebraic statements from the April 2021 draft.
The current work concerns a split Clifford action and its adjoint. It does not
claim a completion or a validation of Geometric Unity. These questions concern
the definitions needed for further source-specific proofs.

1. In Definition 8.1 and equation (9.3), which normalized invariant tensors are
Phi_1 and Phi_2? Could you give basis coefficients, or a construction that fixes
them uniquely? If an invariant space has dimension greater than one, which
element or basis index is intended?

2. What exact bilinear operation does each bracket in (9.3) denote? Please state
the Clifford or matrix product, any projection, all graded signs, and numerical
factors. Which orientation, Hodge-star convention and curvature normalization
apply? We do not want to choose between commutator, anticommutator-derived or
projected operations without the intended definition.

3. What is the domain of the Weyl-annihilation claim following (9.3)? Does it mean
ordinary algebraic Riemann curvature tensors, a specified subspace of ad-valued
two-forms, or a space transported by epsilon? What algebraic identities define
that domain?

4. Which Bianchi identity selects the operator discussed in Section 8.2? Which
connection and torsion assumptions are used? Does the argument determine a unique
operator, a scale, or a family? What establishes the relative normalization of
the two terms in (9.3)?

5. Which conventions for the tilted gauge maps and displaced torsion should be
used together? Could you state their signs and transformation rules in one
notation, including the relation between the 2021 draft and the 2025 discussion?

6. For the action already displayed in equation (9.4), page 44, which fields are
independent in the variation: epsilon, varpi and the metric on X? When the metric
varies, which induced metric, Hodge star, connection and pullback data vary with
it? What integration domain and boundary or compact-support assumptions define
the variational problem? At what stage is pullback to X performed?

7. For the complex adjoint behind (3.27), is the intended map conjugate-linear on
scalars, with i mapped to -i, real Clifford generators mapped to their negatives,
and product order reversed? Our compatible-form result uses all 14 vector
generators being skew-adjoint. Spin invariance alone is a weaker condition.
