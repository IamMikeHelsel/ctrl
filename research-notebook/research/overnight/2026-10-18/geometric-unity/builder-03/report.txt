The actual real Clifford conjugation now has a matrix adjoint formula
18 October 2026 UTC — builder gate, independent acceptance required

Result
For the existing concrete real algebra equivalence splitMatrixEquiv from Cl(7,7)
to 128 by 128 real matrices, the new theorem states:
  splitMatrixEquiv(star x) = K * transpose(splitMatrixEquiv(x)) * K.
It holds for every real Clifford element x. K is the ordered product of the seven
actual negative generator matrices. Lean also proves K^T=K and K*K=I.

In plain terms, the known real representation preserves Clifford conjugation.
Writing the algebra as matrices does not change that operation: negate each
vector and reverse products on the Clifford side, or apply transpose with K on
both sides on the matrix side. This closes a concrete representation gap. It is
standard algebra, not a new physics result or a publication-novelty finding.

Why this is the actual representation
creation_transpose uses the ordered exterior basis from the accepted gate. The
same A ++ i :: B decomposition sets the creation and contraction signs. This
proves each positive matrix is symmetric and each negative matrix is skew.
Their Clifford relations then prove the K identities. A vector-basis expansion
and Clifford induction extend the result to every real element. No new adjoint,
faithfulness, matrix-unit or basis-action premise is assumed.

Scope and assumptions
The coefficients are real. Clifford star fixes real scalars, negates real vector
generators and reverses multiplication. The complex tensor algebra has a separate
bar that also conjugates complex scalars. This folder does not prove that complex
representation, its Hermitian signature, a unitary Lie-algebra identification,
operator Phi, variational equations or a physical prediction.

Controls
The positive generator is not skew for ordinary Euclidean transpose. Omitting
the right K fails its vector identity. Substituting creation plus contraction
for the negative generator gives square +I instead of -I. Plain transpose on iI
fails the conjugate-linear convention. These inequalities are proved in Lean.
All six intentionally false client claims failed compilation in the verifier.

Reversing the seven K factors gives -K. Lean proves this and proves that the two
K signs give the same adjoint. It is not used as a false counterexample.

An exact ordered-wedge integer check covers all 128 basis columns, all fourteen
generators and all 91 distinct bivectors. It also retains H=iKD, where D is
exterior parity. H is Hermitian and bivector invariant but not vector-skew
compatible, and H is not a scalar multiple of K. That last witness is a finite
exact calculation, not a new Lean uniqueness or complex representation theorem.
