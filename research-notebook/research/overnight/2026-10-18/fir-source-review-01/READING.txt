2012 — relevant method read: Sections 3 and 4.1, pp25-34
The paper derives discrete moving-average implementations and states that they
approximate the continuous trajectories. Its online multi-point example covers
early direction reversals. Overlapping contributions can double acceleration;
waiting avoids that case. It also discusses changing the first filter's tap
count while preserving its state. Pages33-34 were checked visually.
Source: https://web.ing.unimo.it/~lbiagiotti/Papers/FIR%20filters%20for%20online%20trajectory%20planning%20with%20time-%20and%20frequency-domain%20specifications.pdf

2019 — relevant method read: Sections2,3,5 and AppendicesA-C
The method adjusts individual filter durations to meet kinematic limits and
combines them with frequency constraints. Its main optimization scope is
rest-to-rest motion. The paper includes recursive parameter adjustment and
an extension intended to avoid unwanted derivative oscillation. It compares
integrated filter design with additional vibration filters. This read does
not independently verify its optimality claims or reproduce its experiments.
Source: https://web.ing.unimo.it/~lbiagiotti/Papers/Trajectory%20Generation%20via%20FIR%20Filters%20%20a%20Procedure%20for%20Time-Optimization.pdf

Consequence for our paper — coordinator assessment
The reversal-limit failure is prior art. Treat our example as a checked
regression case. Fixed equal-width filtering is a design choice, not a new
trajectory family or a time-optimal method. The narrower candidate contribution
is an exact list-parametric rational admission test, its real-time-domain
meaning, retained execution commitments, and a separate uncertain-plant bound.
These source reads do not establish novelty of that combination. The earlier
governor and ModelPlex audit still applies. Human specialist review remains open.

Implementation boundary
A fast reference evaluator and an all-future admission check are different
operations. Do not compare their runtimes as though they solve the same task.
Our current theorem covers an analytic continuous reference. It does not
certify a sampled moving-average implementation by naming both of them FIR.
