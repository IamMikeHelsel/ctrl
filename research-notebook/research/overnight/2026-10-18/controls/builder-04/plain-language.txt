The useful claim is that the axis settles while its response keeps changing.
It does not need the same response on every attempt. It does need a stated
lower and upper response bound, and a limit on the added disturbance.

The proof carries the actual position, speed, and acceleration into the hold.
It measures them together with one quadratic formula. Outside a small allowed
region, that measure must decrease. After two seconds, it is small enough to
force both speed and acceleration inside the target bands.

A numerical solver proposed the formula. Exact rational arithmetic and Lean
check the formula and the implication from the physical equations. The solver
is not asked to certify its own numerical answer.

Here, “settled” means speed below 1 mm/s and acceleration below 0.02 m/s^2.
It does not mean exact rest. The proof also does not cover complete loss of
grip, an unmodeled delay, or a real robot arm without a justified model.
Independent review must accept the frozen result before we present it as a
completed project result.
