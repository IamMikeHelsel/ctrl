Next gate: separate a loose bound from a real varying-disturbance limit
Exploratory problem statement; no new result is claimed here.

Keep the original scalar controller, sigma=10, r(t) in [80,120]/s, and
|w(t)|<=0.01 m/s^2. Keep the inherited transformed cube R=0.0002 m.
First falsifiable target: does every admissible trajectory satisfy
|v|<=0.001 m/s and |a|<=0.02 m/s^2 for every t in [2,H], H>=2,
without assuming that w is constant or differentiable? The answer must cover
changing r and w and include the inherited initial-state transient.
Start a bounded scout on [0,4] seconds with all eight initial-cube vertices
and adverse changing inputs. Use bounded continuous ramps for r and w so a
reported counterexample can satisfy the existing classical-trajectory premise.
A jump-input scout alone would need a separate piecewise-trajectory argument.
One verified admissible violation at t>=2 refutes this proposed two-second
universal claim. It does not rule out a later entry time or a weaker bound.
A cube-vertex counterexample rejects the general cube-based hold claim. It
does not show that the frozen eight-phase motion reaches that endpoint state.

The critic's exploratory constant-rate calculation is a useful target, not an
answer. At r=8 sigma with zero initial state, the velocity impulse response is
8 sigma t(1-2 sigma t) exp(-4 sigma t). The acceleration impulse response is
8 sigma(1-8 sigma t+8 sigma^2 t^2) exp(-4 sigma t). Their suggested L1 factors are
2 exp(-2)/sigma and about 1.2400500303. At sigma=10 and W=0.01, those would give
forced-response limits about 0.27067 mm/s and 0.0124005 m/s^2. These constant-rate
calculations do not establish a changing-rate bound.

A bounded numerical scout can search for a counterexample with both inputs
changing, followed by independent high-precision replay. Search failure is not
a proof. A promising proof route is an output-aware robust invariant set for
the linear differential inclusion, with exact rational face inequalities for
rate/disturbance vertices and a separate entry-time argument. The old common
coordinate cube loses correlations that matter for velocity and acceleration.

The four endpoint pairs r in {80,120}, w in {-0.01,0.01} describe the extrema
of the scalar acceleration derivative at a fixed state. A proposed set proof
must establish the required convexity and face inequalities, rather than infer
an all-time result from sampled simulation or constant-rate transfer functions.

Success gate: either an exact reviewed certificate with a finite entry time and
both physical output limits, or a verified admissible varying-input trajectory
that violates one proposed universal limit. If neither is within a bounded
batch, report the best explicit gain/time bound and the remaining inequality.
Only then assess a weaker, stated disturbance model such as bounded bias rate
or a decaying disturbance. Do not silently replace varying w with constant b.

Alternative only after this test
If the arbitrary-disturbance target fails or remains too loose, a distinct
model can assume a bound on the bias derivative. A moving analysis equilibrium
delta(t)=w(t)/(8 sigma^2) subtracts delta(t) from all three coordinates and
adds -delta'(t) to each coordinate equation. This creates a real new regularity
and magnitude assumption. Its proof and bounds would need a separate gate;
neither the constant-bias theorem nor the old floor proves that extension.
