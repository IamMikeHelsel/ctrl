Settling under constant bias, without changing the controller
Builder milestone 02 — verification and independent review pending

Main claim
The original holding controller can reach small speed and acceleration bounds
under a constant nonzero acceleration-target bias. The position bound includes
the resulting offset from the true commanded goal. This statement is not about
an arbitrary changing disturbance.

Physical model and unchanged command
  x'=v, v'=a, a'=r(t)[u+b-a]
  u=-8 sigma^2(x-c)-6 sigma v-a/2
  sigma>0, 8 sigma<=r(t)<=12 sigma, b constant.
The goal c and the controller remain unchanged. The bias b can be unknown to
the controller. The analysis uses delta=b/(8 sigma^2) and c*=c+delta.
The proved identity is
  holdInput(sigma,c)+b = holdInput(sigma,c*).
It rewrites an equal vector field on the same actual trajectory. It does not
command c*. Changing the commanded goal to c* while keeping the physical bias
would count the bias twice. An exact witness checks this error.

The supplied actual trajectory must be continuous on the closed hold interval
and satisfy the ODE classically on its open interior. The pointwise rate can
change without a stated derivative bound. Existence for arbitrary discontinuous
rate signals is not proved. Full actual-state feedback is assumed.

Inherited state and bound
The hold starts from the actual endpoint state of the preceding motion. If the
original transformed state lies in a cube of radius R about c, the same state
lies in a cube of radius R*=R+|delta| about c*. Only the analysis box changes.
No position, velocity or acceleration state is reset.

Let E*=(5/4)(R+|delta|) exp(-2 sigma t/5), where t is time since the hold starts.
The new theorem gives
  |x-c| <= |delta|+E*;
  |v| <= 8 sigma E*;
  |a| <= 64 sigma^2 E*;
  |u+b| <= 24 sigma^2 E*;
  |u| <= 24 sigma^2 E*+|b|.
The final line retains the offset for the original command. It is not the same
quantity as the effective acceleration target u+b.

Concrete two-second result
For sigma=10/s, R=0.0002 m and |b|<=0.01 m/s^2:
  |delta| <= 1/80000 m;
  R* <= 17/80000 m;
  E* <= 17/16384000 m after two hold seconds.
The true-goal and physical bounds after two seconds are
  |x-c| <= 1109/81920000 m (about 13.538 micrometres);
  |v| <= 17/204800 m/s (about 0.083008 mm/s);
  |a| <= 17/2560 m/s^2 (0.006640625 m/s^2).
The speed and acceleration bounds meet the illustrative 1 mm/s and 0.02 m/s^2
limits. The theorem requires H>=2, and has a separate conclusion at t=2. Its
certified interval is therefore nonempty.

DirectBias connects the result to the accepted eight-phase DirectPlant. That
motion still has its original varying-disturbance assumptions. Only its new
final hold has constant bias. The actual endpoint is retained. Two hold seconds
correspond to global time 3.5 seconds for that fixed reference. This does not
certify the shorter deadlines in the separate moving-goal experiment.

Witnesses and false claims
A resting biased trajectory is checked against the original physical ODE and
the original goal. Its nonzero position offset makes omission of the offset
term false. Another exact trajectory has a constant nonzero bias and initial
velocity -4 mm/s. It retains that state and satisfies the two-second theorem.
This witness realizes the holding assumptions. It is not a construction of
that same nonzero-error state through all eight preceding phases.
The wrong-sign, double-counted-bias and omitted-position-offset controls each
have an exact witness and a separate rejected false Lean statement.

Limits
This result does not reduce the previous bound for arbitrarily changing
bounded disturbance. It does not prove that bound was a physical limit. It
adds a distinct constant-bias theorem with a stronger disturbance assumption.
No frozen experiment, accepted proof, limit, initial state or controller was
changed. No bias estimator or runtime compensation method is proposed here.
The result does not cover sampled control, saturation, measurement error,
joint coupling, contact, Franka firmware or physical acceptance. It is not a
claim of new mathematics or submission readiness.

Read next-gate.txt for a bounded question about tighter output bounds under
arbitrarily changing disturbance. That note is a proposal, not a new theorem.
