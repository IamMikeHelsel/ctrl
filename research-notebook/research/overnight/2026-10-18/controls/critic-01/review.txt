Mathematical audit
The main theorem derives a shrinking bound from a supplied physical trajectory.
It does not assume that the physical error decays. The plant has x'=v, v'=a,
and a'=r(u+w-a). During the final hold,
u=-8 sigma^2(x-c)-6 sigma v-a/2. The assumptions are sigma>0,
8 sigma<=r(t)<=12 sigma, and |w(t)|<=W. Full-state feedback is assumed.

The normalized coordinates are z0=x-c, z1=x-c+v/(4 sigma), and
z2=x-c+v/(2 sigma)+a/(16 sigma^2). Each coordinate has units of position.
Physical time is mapped to tau=sigma*t. The disturbance becomes W/sigma^2.
The final exponent is therefore exp(-2 sigma*t/5), not exp(-2t/5) in seconds.
The common physical radius is 3W/(8 sigma^2)+(5R/4)exp(-2 sigma*t/5).

The face proof is consistent with direct algebra. Write a moving radius as
F+d_i K, d=(1,9/10,4/5), F>=3W/8 and K>=0 in normalized units. At the upper
first face, the worst derivative is -2K/5. At the second it is -2K/5,
which is at most -(2/5)(9/10)K. At the third it is bounded above by
(r/2-8)F+(13r-168)K/20+rW/16. The rate interval and F>=3W/8 give the stated
inward bound. The lower faces follow by the signs of the same linear system.
The first face makes an exponent 1/2 fail for these particular weights.
This does not rule out a faster estimate with another norm or another bound.

The proof inflates every radius by epsilon*(1+t). This makes contact strict.
The inherited finite-barrier lemma uses continuity on the closed interval and
the ODE on its open interior. It excludes first contact before a positive
violation. Letting epsilon approach zero gives the non-strict bound. No
derivative or continuity assumption on r or w is introduced. The theorem is
conditional on a classical trajectory satisfying the ODE; it does not prove
existence for arbitrary discontinuous signals. That boundary is stated in the
builder report and must remain in any paper claim.

Composition and time
The final hold starts with f(0)=previous phase 7 at its endpoint. The accepted
DirectPlant theorem supplies the endpoint cube. The reference endpoint is
(3/5,0,0), and matched_hold_cube transforms the actual endpoint into the hold
coordinates. Neither velocity nor acceleration is reset or assumed zero.

The concrete theorem uses hold time. One hold second is global time 2.5 seconds;
two hold seconds is global time 3.5 seconds. Its quantified sets are [1,H] and
[2,H]. These sets are empty if the horizon is shorter. The report explicitly
requires H>=1 or H>=2 for the corresponding useful claim. Reviewer fixtures
instantiate H=1 and H=2 at their included endpoints. They also construct an
exact eight-phase DirectPlant with the reference itself, r=96/s, and w=0.
This is a full nonempty composition witness. A separate nonzero initial-velocity
hold witness checks the retained-state settling theorem. It is not presented
as a nonzero-error realization of all eight preceding phases.

Disturbance and physical meaning
The W=0.01 certificate has a common-radius floor of 37.5 micrometres. Its
unpacked speed and acceleration floors are 3 mm/s and 0.24 m/s^2. These are
upper-bound limitations. They are not lower bounds on actual motion. A physical
constant-bias witness at sigma=10 and w=0.01 has x-c=1/80000 m, v=0, a=0,
and satisfies the ODE for every rate function. Thus a claim that nonzero
disturbance requires nonzero speed would be false.

With zero disturbance only during the hold, the two-second claim meets the
stated illustrative speed and acceleration bands. Earlier phases can retain
their original disturbance allowance. A nonzero exponential physical trajectory
meets the finite tolerance but has nonzero velocity at every finite time.
Finite tolerances and exact finite rest must remain distinct.

The command bound is for the feedback command u. The plant input is u+w.
This theorem does not include actuator saturation, sampled control, sensor
errors, coupling, contact, robot firmware, or a physical stop test. The new
two-second hold guarantee does not establish the shorter moving-goal deadlines
from the other experiment. Those are different tasks and horizons.

Productive next gate
CONTINUE with a bounded constant-bias proof. Keep the original commanded goal c
and the original controller. Let delta=w0/(8 sigma^2) and c*=c+delta only for
analysis. First prove the exact identity
  holdInput(sigma,c,f,t)+w0 = holdInput(sigma,c*,f,t).
Changing the actual command goal to c* would count the bias twice and is not the
proposed result. No runtime bias measurement or goal change is needed by this
analysis statement; the constant value is a theorem parameter.

Then prove that the original matched endpoint cube of radius R gives a cube of
radius R+|delta| about c*. Apply the zero-disturbance settling theorem to this
same physical trajectory. State the true-goal result explicitly:
  |x-c| <= |delta| + E*, |v| <= 8 sigma E*, |a| <= 64 sigma^2 E*,
  E* = (5/4)(R+|delta|)exp(-2 sigma*t/5).
The original command also needs its offset if a command bound is reported.
This is a suggested next theorem, not a result accepted by this review.

For |w0|<=0.01, sigma=10 and R=0.0002, the projected two-second arithmetic is
R*=17/80000 m, E*<=17/16384000 m, true-goal error<=1109/81920000 m,
speed<=17/204800 m/s, and acceleration<=17/2560 m/s^2. The proposed speed and
acceleration values meet 1 mm/s and 0.02 m/s^2. These values must be connected
by a new proof before they are reported as a theorem. Arbitrarily changing
bounded disturbance is outside this constant-bias extension.

This next gate closes a practical assumption gap in the present chain. It is
not, by itself, a claim of new mathematics. After it passes independent review,
assess a precise slowly changing or decaying disturbance model if the use case
needs changing bias. Do not repeat common-radius algebra and call the unchanged
floor a physical impossibility.
