Possible later gate — exploratory algebra, not a Lean result

The current common-radius floor may be much too loose even for changing bounded
disturbance. Before imposing disturbance regularity, test stronger output bounds.
A simple constant-rate case gives a reason to investigate this route.

At r=8 sigma the position transfer from w is
  8 sigma / (s+4 sigma)^3.
Its velocity impulse response is
  8 sigma t (1-2 sigma t) exp(-4 sigma t).
Its acceleration impulse response is
  8 sigma (1-8 sigma t+8 sigma^2 t^2) exp(-4 sigma t).
These expressions concern the linear, constant-rate, zero-initial-state forced
response. They do not establish the pointwise changing-rate result.

Using tau=sigma*t, direct integration of their signed lobes suggests induced
bounded-input bounds with velocity factor 2 exp(-2)/sigma and acceleration
factor 2[F((2-sqrt(2))/4)-F((2+sqrt(2))/4)],
where F(tau)=8 tau(1-2 tau)exp(-4 tau).

Numerically, these factors are 0.2706705665/sigma and 1.2400500303. At sigma=10
and W=0.01, the candidate forced-response bounds are 0.27067 mm/s and
0.0124005 m/s^2, both below the illustrative limits. Initial-state transients
must still be added. This calculation is not independently formalized here.

Question: can a uniform bound for r(t) in [8 sigma,12 sigma] achieve comparable
output gains? Constant-rate transfer functions cannot answer that question.
This is a later hypothesis, not a reason to skip the small constant-bias gate.
