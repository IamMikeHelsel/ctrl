Decision
Accept RobotMotion.FIRHold.delayed_return_physical_terminal for its stated
classical scalar model. It carries one actual trajectory from global time zero,
through the delayed FIR return, into the terminal hold. The proof uses one
initial tracking-error cube. It does not reset the state or add a new endpoint
cube. The response, disturbance and state use the same shifted clock.

The result gives a 0.2 mm position band from 1.42 seconds, and speed <=1 mm/s
and acceleration <=0.02 m/s^2 from 3.42 seconds to the supplied horizon.
The tracking-position band holds from time zero. The theorem also proves
at least 1 mm of forward progress by 0.5 seconds. H>=3.42 ensures that the
terminal interval is nonempty, including its boundary case.

Independent challenges
The literal-controller and literal-cube clients confirm coefficients and units.
The minimal-horizon client checks a nonempty terminal boundary. A negative-bias
client checks that the actual endpoint is carried through rather than reset.

The wrong-clock client proves that the substituted input clock does not define
the same actual trajectory. At its test time, derivative uniqueness would
require 0=-1/48. This is a formal differential-equation contradiction.

A new full FIR witness adds a decaying exponential tracking error, uses rate
640/7 per second, and disturbance q'''/1920. It satisfies the original initial
cube and every main premise. Its actual speed at 3.42 seconds is nonzero.
Thus the proved residual bands cannot be restated as exact finite-time rest.
A separate actual hold trajectory with a large initial error violates the
speed and acceleration bands at two seconds. This tests omission of the
initial cube; it is not a proposed physical experiment.

Scope and publication verdict
No mathematical repair is required for the declared result. The evidence gate
is A for this bounded formal claim; this is not a publication or novelty grade.
The work closes the previously separate FIR tracking and terminal-hold proof
contracts. It can support a formalization and worked-case contribution.

The result does not prove a 1.7-second physical terminal deadline, make the
1.3-second task feasible, or establish an optimal stopping time. The two-second
hold is sufficient. The rate and disturbance may change within their bounds,
but the existence of a classical trajectory is assumed. There is no existence
theorem for every arbitrary bounded signal pair.

There is no new controller-method, real-time implementation, hardware, coupled
Franka, contact, loss-of-grip, PL d/e, or publication-novelty result here.
The numerical comparison remains separate evidence with its declared timing
limits. Read scope-review.txt for the complete source-to-claim boundary.

Stop recommendation
Stop new controls proof branches for this gate. Integrate the accepted statement
and its assumptions into the paper and replay bundle. A shorter physical
deadline or a hardware claim requires a separately declared model and test.
Human assessment of novelty and practical value remains open.
