Independent review of constant-bias settling
Decision: ACCEPT the bounded constant-bias theorem. CONTINUE to next-test.txt.

Scope
The bounded claim is about one unchanged scalar continuous-state controller.
It permits a pointwise changing actuator rate and a constant acceleration-target
bias. It is not a theorem about arbitrary changing disturbance, sampled control,
hardware, or publication novelty.

The controller and coordinate shift
The original commanded goal remains c. The original physical input is
u+b, where u=-8 sigma^2(x-c)-6 sigma v-a/2. The constant analysis offset is
delta=b/(8 sigma^2). The exact identity is u(c)+b=u(c+delta). The proof rewrites
equal inputs for the same f and r; it does not construct a new physical state
or alter the controller. The positive sign is required. The bias can be unknown
to the controller because this is an analysis translation, not compensation.

Each transformed coordinate shifts by -delta. An original cube of radius R
therefore gives a cube of radius R+|delta| about the analysis equilibrium.
The adverse initial cube sign makes this enlargement exact in a reviewer
witness; keeping radius R can be false. The original endpoint is retained.

The zero-disturbance settling theorem then applies to the same trajectory,
about c+delta. The true-goal position bound adds |delta| back. Speed and
acceleration need no equilibrium offset. The effective target u+b decays with
the transient bound, while the original command u retains an added |b| term.
At biased rest, u=-b and u+b=0. The theorem and report distinguish them correctly.

Units and time
The bias b has units of acceleration, sigma has units 1/s, and delta has units
of position. The transient exponent is -2 sigma t/5 in physical seconds.
At sigma=10, R=1/5000 m, |b|<=1/100 m/s^2 and t>=2 hold seconds, the checked
arithmetic is |delta|<=1/80000 m, R*<=17/80000 m, E*<=17/16384000 m. Thus
true-goal error<=1109/81920000 m, speed<=17/204800 m/s, and acceleration
<=17/2560 m/s^2. These match the stated decimal approximations and tight bands.

The concrete theorem requires H>=2. Its endpoint theorem explicitly evaluates
t=2. The original DirectPlant supplies the endpoint cube; no new initial cube
is assumed. Two hold seconds correspond to global time 3.5 seconds. The earlier
motion retains its varying-disturbance assumptions; only this new hold has
constant bias. This does not establish the shorter moving-goal deadlines.

Non-vacuity and false controls
The independent review constructs the exact eight-phase reference at r=96/s,
w=0. Its exact endpoint starts a new hold at r=80/s with b=0.01 m/s^2. A closed
form step response satisfies the original physical ODE, preserves position,
velocity and acceleration at the join, and exercises the full composed theorem
at H=2. The nonzero-bias composition therefore has an actual nonempty witness.
Its velocity is still nonzero at t=2; the result concerns a finite tolerance.

The builder's different witness begins with velocity -4 mm/s. The review checks
that it retains that state and reaches the certified tolerance. It does not
claim that this particular state was produced by the preceding eight phases.

The five rejected false controls test the wrong offset sign, changed commanded
goal with double bias, omitted true-goal position offset, omitted original
command offset, and omitted initial cube enlargement. Their accepted exact
counterexamples appear in the builder or reviewer theorem sources.

Proof boundary
Continuity on the closed hold interval and the ODE on the open interior are
assumptions on a supplied trajectory. Existence for arbitrary discontinuous
rate functions is not claimed. The inherited shrinking-box proof supplies
decay; the new theorem does not assume it. Full physical state feedback remains
an assumption. No sensor error, actuator saturation, joint coupling, contact,
Franka interface, or physical experiment is covered.
