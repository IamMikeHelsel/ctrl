One falsifiable next test: adverse changing response and disturbance
Recommendation only. No scout or numerical experiment was run in this review.

Decision
Test the stronger arbitrary-bounded-disturbance target before adding a bias-rate
or disturbance-decay assumption. Builder-02/next-gate.txt and critic-01's earlier
constant-rate calculation agree on this direction. The constant-rate calculation
does not bound a changing-rate system; it only makes the target worth testing.

Frozen question
For the original hold controller, sigma=10/s, r(t) in [80,120]/s,
|w(t)|<=0.01 m/s^2, and the inherited coordinate cube R=0.0002 m, can a supplied
admissible trajectory violate either |v|<=0.001 m/s or |a|<=0.02 m/s^2 at a
hold time t in [2,4] seconds? Keep the commanded goal fixed. Do not retarget it.

Search class
Use bounded continuous piecewise-linear r and w on [0,4]. Predeclare control
knots every 0.01 second, then permit one predeclared refinement to 0.002 second
around the strongest candidate. Use exact rational knot times and values in the
saved candidate. Interpolation stays within the input bounds and gives a
classical solution. These knot restrictions define only the search class; they
must not appear as an unstated regularity assumption in a claimed theorem.

Include all eight initial cube vertices. Convert them to physical states with
e=z0, v=4 sigma(z1-z0), a=16 sigma^2(z2-2z1+z0), where z_i is plus or minus R.
Also include the zero state as a baseline. A cube-vertex failure refutes the
general cube-based hold target. It does not establish reachability from the
specific eight-phase reference. Store that distinction with every result.

Use one objective: maximize max(|v|/0.001,|a|/0.02) over hold times [2,4].
Search both r and w. Merely sweeping sinusoidal w with constant r is too weak.
Start with output probes every 0.001 second. Refine a candidate peak locally,
then freeze a rational candidate time for replay. Probes do not cover every time.
For a fixed r schedule and one output/time, the output is affine in the initial
state and in the knot values of w. Thus an adjoint or linear sensitivity pass
can choose adverse w knot signs and initial cube signs. This is a way to find
strong candidates, not an all-time or changing-r proof. The outer r search is
nonconvex. Retain failed searches and repeated seeds as evidence, not coverage.

Bound the first scout by 60 minutes and a declared evaluation budget. Freeze the
best candidate before independent replay. Save exact input knots, interpolation
rule, initial state, output, time, solver settings, and the margin above or below
each limit. Keep the limits and the two-second entry time fixed.

Acceptance rule
If the scout finds a violation, replay the same continuous inputs independently
with higher precision and time refinement. Numerical agreement alone remains
numerical evidence. To call it a mathematically verified counterexample, bound
the trajectory error at the claimed violating time by interval integration or
a checked residual bound, with the entire error interval beyond the limit.
One such witness refutes this universal target. It does not refute a later
entry time or a different limit.

If no violation is found, report the largest observed normalized output and
the tested class. Do not report a universal pass. The next proof candidate is
an output-aware robust invariant polytope plus a transient entry argument.
Its face checks must cover rate/disturbance endpoint pairs and all relevant
state-face vertices. A rectangular input extremum follows from the multi-affine
vector field; that fact still needs proof in the certificate. A steady invariant
set by itself does not prove entry from the inherited initial cube by t=2.

Stop/redirect rule
If an admissible violation is verified, extract the input feature that causes
it before weakening the disturbance model. If the search has no failure and no
credible exact certificate after the bounded gate, report the remaining gain
or entry-time inequality. Only then assess a separate bounded bias-rate model.
Do not let the already accepted constant-bias result substitute for this test.
