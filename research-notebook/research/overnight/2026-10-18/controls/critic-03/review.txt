Adjoint logic
For a fixed rate schedule, the terminal output is a linear function of the
initial state and the piecewise-linear disturbance knot values. The physical
adjoint obeys lambda'=-A(r)^T lambda backward in time. Integrating its input
sensitivity against each linear hat basis gives the disturbance coefficients.
The maximum over the initial cube and the independent disturbance knot box is
R times the sum of absolute initial coefficients plus W times the sum of
absolute knot coefficients. Central symmetry also covers the absolute output.

The C++ implementation uses backward-time length, so its positive A^T update
and positive sensitivity accumulation have the correct signs. Its initial
coefficient transformation is the transpose of the inverse coordinate map.
It resets only the two interval sensitivity accumulators, not the adjoint state.
No derivative of r is needed for this sensitivity identity.

An independent physical-coordinate DOP853 adjoint checked the fixed refined
target at 3.997924 seconds. Its support was 0.6575610390301684, compared with
0.6575602126503732 from the exploratory RK4 kernel, a difference of about
8.264e-7. This is numerical agreement, not an exact optimality certificate.
The final refined peak time 3.998177 seconds is different from that optimizer
target; the saved input remains admissible at both. The report does not claim
the outer changing-rate search found a global optimum.

Search budget and disclosed deviation
There are 20 fixed constant-rate controls, 12 original optimized candidates,
and one refined candidate. The 13 optimizer histories contain 11,698 objective
calls. Every history matches its call count and respects its individual budget.
One original run and the refinement stopped at their budgets. Their unsuccessful
termination statuses are preserved. No replacement search was run in review.

The intended constant rate prefix was not executed. The decoder instead uses a
linear ramp from the time-zero rate to the first knot of the last second. The
review confirmed this in a saved nonconstant case. The actual input still
belongs to the bounded continuous piecewise-linear class, so the candidate
and its replay remain useful. This is partial protocol adherence, not full
compliance. Do not erase the deviation or label a future correction as this run.

Independent high-precision replay
The reviewer derived a scalar physical equation directly:
  e''' + 1.5 r e'' + 60 r e' + 800 r e = r w.
A separate coefficient recurrence integrated this equation with exact rational
input knots, 80-digit arithmetic, and orders 80 and 110. It did not import a
builder solver. Across 526 intervals to the frozen time, both orders agree at
the working precision. The result is
  e = -0.00000331455621355709486430421165186115 m,
  v = -0.000117982402767376605305601025730679 m/s,
  a =  0.0131539094406987410512788646051706 m/s^2.
Its acceleration ratio is 0.65769547203493705256. The difference from the
builder's 60-digit acceleration is about 2.98e-62 m/s^2, consistent with its
printed precision. Neither computation encloses truncation or roundoff error
rigorously. This remains numerical evidence, not a mathematical certificate.

Scoring and interpretation
The reconstructed search summaries match the stated maximum observed velocity
and acceleration. The original 0.02 acceleration limit is not violated at the
frozen target. A separate 0.013 diagnostic threshold is detected as violated.
This tests scoring; it does not change the original limit. The numerical state
has nonzero velocity at the target. The builder sentence that the candidate
"refutes exact rest" must be read as a numerical diagnostic. Do not repeat it
as a mathematically certified counterexample without a trajectory-error bound.

No violation found means no violation among the tested inputs, sampled times,
and local peak searches. It does not establish the universal claim for every
continuous r,w, every initial state, or every time. This limitation is material.

Next-template assessment
The proposed scaling, endpoint LMI, coordinate output bounds, and homogeneous
transient arithmetic are correct. The LMI is sufficient, not necessary. Its
feasibility is still unknown. The initial-state split is valid only for the
same r and for the error state e=x-c. Summing two absolute-position trajectories
about c would double-count the goal. Existence and decomposition must be proved
if that route is used.

There is a simpler possible route: apply the same LMI directly to the actual
error state's quadratic V. Its scalar decay bound can include the entire
initial cube, avoiding a new forced-response existence lemma. next-gate.txt
states exact acceptance conditions for that route. No matrix was constructed,
no certificate feasibility search was run, and no new control bound was proved
by this review.
