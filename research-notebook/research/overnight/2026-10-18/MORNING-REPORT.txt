Robot motion — proved within the reference model
A new reference can take over only when its certificate matches the exact live
history, task, goal, limits, deadline and declared execution time. The proof
covers its reference speed, acceleration and jerk bounds, unchanged past motion,
and final target. Late, stale or duplicate commits are rejected. Rejection keeps
the old plan; it does not guarantee that the new task will finish.

The prototype accepts clock values and atomic-state assumptions as inputs. It
does not measure a real controller or implement its atomic handoff. A speed
check is sufficient and can reject a safe reference. A failed finite search is
not proof that no feasible motion exists.

Other controls — proved within the scalar physical model
One trajectory carries through the return and hold, without resetting physical
state. The supplied classical trajectory is defined on [0,H], with H at least
3.42 s. Under the declared initial-error, response and disturbance bounds, actual
position is within 0.2 mm of the zero goal from 1.42 s through H. From 3.42 s
through H, speed is at most 1 mm/s and acceleration at most 0.02 m/s². The response
can change within its bounds. This is a conditional settling-band result, not an
emergency stopping-distance certificate. A checked witness shows that exact rest
at 3.42 s cannot be guaranteed.
The proof does not establish the faster 1.70 s sampled task.

Comparison — numerical evidence
All methods share the same moving state when the new request arrives. Retained
FIR lowers the squared-jerk integral against the native baselines but adds
forward travel. A stronger offline smooth plan lowers both measures relative to FIR,
while raising peak jerk. It still travels farther than the native methods. Native fast alone passes the tight task. No method wins
all measures. Squared jerk is a motion metric, not physical energy.

Independent 80-digit checks support the saved comparisons. The stronger study
contains 90 traces and 363,564 saved state rows. It omits planning latency. A
10 mm reference cap allowed actual travel slightly above 10 mm in two cases.
The three inaccurate optimizer attempts are retained as status records; their
raw vectors were not saved. These limits remain explicit in the paper.
