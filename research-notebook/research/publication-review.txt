PUBLICATION REVIEW — 17 October 2026
Independent literature and contribution assessment; read-only review of the
62-theorem baseline in lean-research-report.html. This review did not rebuild
external repositories or independently replay their axiom audits.

DECISION

The present work is a useful checked case study and learning artifact. It is
not yet a demonstrated novel research contribution. Increasing the sufficient
sampling interval for one normalized double integrator, adding ideal rounding,
or proving the diagonal Lyapunov-operator case are not strong publication
claims by themselves. The best near-term route is a reusable, evaluated Lean
certificate-to-closed-loop theorem pipeline. Keep the order-six problem as a
bounded exploration track, rather than making publication depend on solving it.

1. WHAT THE LITERATURE ALREADY COVERS

A. Lean control foundations already exist.
Moritz Doll and Iman Shames, Foundations of Machine-Checked Control Theory in
Lean, arXiv:2607.19727v1, presents Lyapunov and small-gain formalizations. Its
repository is active and broader than its July paper. LeanForControl separately
contains continuous/discrete stability and linear-system theorems. Our local
work reports a completed LeanForControl build and five central axiom checks;
that is stronger evidence than a README, but not an audit of every result.
Do not claim that Lean lacked Lyapunov theory, global linear ODE existence,
or general discrete stability before this project.
https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19727v1
https://github.com/mcdoll/DynamicalSystems
https://github.com/AnandGokhale/LeanForControl

B. Exact polynomial certificates in Lean are an established approach.
leanprover/sos searches using CSDP, reconstructs rational certificates, and
checks certificate soundness in Lean. It supports constrained polynomial
nonnegativity, with explicit search limitations. Search failure proves nothing.
Reimplementing numerical discovery followed by rational SOS checking is not,
on its own, a fresh contribution.
https://github.com/leanprover/sos

C. General univariate matrix-polynomial interval certificates are available.
Mocho Go's lean-matrix-sos exposes bounded Markov–Lukacs SOS and Gram forms on
finite intervals, including both even and odd degree. Source inspection found
public theorem statements and #guard_msgs axiom audits. This is stronger and
more general positivity machinery than our specific two-by-two Bernstein
calculation. It is an existence formalization, not evidence that every numerical
control-certificate search problem is already solved. Integration or a small
computable rational checker may still be useful, but the distinction must be
explained. This source was read, not built locally in this review.

D. Sampled-data theorem proving and safe-envelope certificates predate this work.
Using Theorem Provers to Guarantee Closed-Loop System Properties (2012) treats
nondeterministic sampling and zero-order-held control in KeYmaera. Therefore,
"formal proofs despite timing variation" is not novel as a general concept.
https://logic.kastel.kit.edu/ap/pub/TPgenloop.pdf

Hellwig, Schafer, Qian, Platzer, and Althoff, From Zonotopes to Proof
Certificates: A Formal Pipeline for Safe Control Envelopes (iFM 2025,
proceedings 2026), links numerical invariant-set witnesses to KeYmaera X
proofs. It explicitly separates one-step invariance, intersample safety, and
control admissibility. This is a close methodological comparison for our
proposed pipeline. A Lean implementation must state its distinct representation,
assurance boundary, automation, or scope advantage rather than implying the
general bridge is new.
https://logic.kastel.kit.edu/ap/pub/zonotope-proofs.pdf
https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-10794-7_13

E. Lean controller quantization examples are already public.
Sparkle contains an LQR controller example with quantized gains, a quadratic
contraction, an ISS-style disturbance inequality, and an ultimate bound, plus
fixed-point/RTL correspondence work. The inspected source makes its remaining
saturation limitations explicit. Thus "Lean + fixed-point controls" is not a
novelty claim. Our exact zero-order-hold and variable-period setting differs
from that particular discrete example; whether the complete combination makes
a substantive contribution remains to be demonstrated. Source inspected only.

2. THREE CONTRIBUTION SHAPES WORTH PURSUING

PREFERRED: reusable sampled-data assurance pipeline.
Provisional question: Can an untrusted numerical design tool export a compact
rational certificate which Lean checks and composes into an all-time statement
covering exact held dynamics, arbitrary sampling within an interval, bounded
disturbances, specified sensor arithmetic, and actuator constraints?

Minimum useful package:
  - A generic theorem/certificate interface, not one hard-coded controller.
  - Explicit state, input, sampling, rounding, saturation, and disturbance
    semantics. Separate sample-time and intersample conclusions.
  - Existence of actual executions and a nonempty initial region.
  - At least three meaningfully different instances, including a coupled
    higher-dimensional plant. A diagonal product of the current example is
    not a convincing scalability demonstration.
  - Replay without the design solver; source pins, exact certificate data,
    audited theorem dependency closure, and an informal-to-formal claim map.
  - Measured certificate size, checking time, conservatism, and failures;
    compare a direct proof and relevant existing certificate infrastructure.
  - A clear reason for the chosen Lean architecture and what downstream users
    gain. A fully proved bridge to actual bounded integer/BitVec controller
    arithmetic would materially strengthen the result, but must not be
    promised until implemented.

This is a candidate for a formal-methods methods/artifact paper, not a claim of
new control theory. A concise technical report can document intermediate work.

SECOND: checked operator-norm certificates for matrix analysis.
Build a reusable rational LDL/SOS witness checker and a theorem connecting its
coordinates to the Frobenius norm on symmetric/skew matrix subspaces. Replay
the published seven-dimensional counterexample in Lean, then use the same
interface for new experiments. The known example is attributed validation;
any contribution would be the reusable checker/representation and lessons from
formalization. Check existing mathlib and SOS interfaces before duplicating.
This offers an artifact even if dimension six remains unresolved. It may be
better as a component or short paper than as a stand-alone major result.

HIGH-RISK: resolve the six-dimensional symmetric-maximizer question.
A complete proof or exact counterexample would be a new mathematical result if
novelty survives a renewed literature check and independent expert scrutiny.
Structured-family results can also matter, but first compare them with known
nonnegative, nonpositive, and tridiagonal cases. Diagonal matrices and ordinary
orthogonal invariance are preliminary lemmas. Failed floating-point searches
cannot establish the universal conjecture or a percentage of completion.

3. CURRENT STATUS OF THE SIX-DIMENSIONAL QUESTION

The canonical arXiv record and v1 paper by Daniel Kressner and Bart
Vandereycken (21 August 2026) still explicitly leave order six unresolved.
The work gives exact integer/rational certificates at order seven and extends
failure to larger dimensions. The paper attributes the n <= 5 result to Chen
and Tian (2015), and structured cases to Feng et al. (2015). Searches on
17 October did not locate a subsequent resolution. That is a bounded search,
not a guarantee that no unpublished solution exists. Use "open in the checked
literature" with a date.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20875
https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20875v1

Important unreviewed lead: a 2 October Hugging Face release called ORISON VI
claims a generic reduction via coupled skew singular vectors to families of
ten-variable SDPs. Its own card explicitly labels the work AI-generated,
unreviewed, not proof-assistant formalized, and the universal conjecture
unresolved. No repository-wide license is established. Treat the formulas as
ideas to independently derive and audit; do not cite them as accepted results
or copy their implementation wholesale. Its arXiv metadata points to the
Kressner–Vandereycken BACKGROUND paper, not to a peer-reviewed ORISON paper.
https://huggingface.co/datasets/PureOne/orison-vi-order-six-lyapunov-norm-certificates

4. PUBLICATION PATH AND CLAIM DISCIPLINE

Journal of Formalized Reasoning is a plausible eventual fit for a significant,
reusable formalization with methodological lessons and accessible proof scripts.
CPP/ITP are plausible for novel certification infrastructure and evaluated
applications. This is a scope assessment, not an acceptance prediction.
https://jfr.unibo.it/about
https://www.sigplan.org/Conferences/CPP/
https://itp-conference-2026.github.io/

Annals of Formalized Mathematics explicitly requires mathematical novelty or
insight into proof-assistant theory/use. Routine known-result formalization
without that insight is outside its scope. Its emphasis on human statement
auditing is directly relevant to this project.
https://afm.episciences.org/page/aims-and-scope

CPP 2027's paper deadline was 10 September 2026, so it is not an available
ordinary submission slot today. Its call explicitly discusses substantive
human intellectual contribution, responsibility for AI-generated material,
and accurate AI disclosure. We should develop the user's problem choice,
modeling/design judgments, mathematical understanding, and review contribution
as real parts of the research, not treat ownership of an account as a substitute.
Select the eventual venue and reread its current policy before submission.
https://popl27.sigplan.org/home/CPP-2027

Practical release gate: an independent reader should be able to match every
headline claim to a theorem, reconstruct its assumptions without reading the
chat, run a clean pinned build, and distinguish proven results from numerical
observations and research hopes. Today there is no new open-problem solution,
no physical-robot certificate, and no demonstrated publication novelty yet.

Assessment confidence: high that the current narrow examples alone are below
a strong research-paper contribution threshold; moderate that the reusable
pipeline can become publishable after implementation, comparison, and external
review. These judgments are not calibrated success probabilities.
