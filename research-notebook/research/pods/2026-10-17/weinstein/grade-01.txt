Exact accepted result and assumptions
The model is V=R^14 with its ordered coordinate basis. The metric has seven +1
entries at indices 0..6 and seven -1 entries at 7..13. Its bilinear map to the
dual is proved bijective. The ordered top exterior wedge has volume +1.
The exterior powers, induced metric and exterior product are Mathlib's actual
objects. This is a declared pointwise model, with no claimed global bundle or
spinor identification.

Let mu(I) be the product of metric entries over an increasing subset I. Let
eps(I) be the sign for the block order (I, complement I). The source proves
  H_k(e_I) = eps(I) mu(complement I) e_(complement I),
where H is exactly Mathlib exteriorPower.hodgeStar. Its defining equation is
  B_(14-k)(H_k(alpha), beta) = vol(alpha wedge beta).
The formula holds in every pair of complementary degrees. H_expansion extends
it to arbitrary forms. In particular, the source proves
  H_12(H_2(x))=-x for every x in Lambda^2 V,
  H_2(H_12(y))=-y for every y in Lambda^12 V.

The alternate convention S=-H is separately characterized by
  vol(alpha wedge S_k(beta)) = B_k(alpha,beta).
Its coefficient is eps(I) mu(I), and S_12(S_2(x))=-x. The statement H=-S alone
is definitional; the independently proved S_pairing gives the mathematical
content needed to identify S as the other Hodge convention. The negative total
metric product, and the actual exterior swap sign in degrees 2/12, supply the
square sign. No square-law premise is hidden in a definition or theorem input.

The star2 array is defined from this actual Hodge map's basis images. A named
subset/Fin equivalence reindexes the actual exterior basis. The representation
theorem identifies the resulting sum with the frozen linearCoeff interface over
R. Both H and S arrays are supplied, with opposite signs. This is not arbitrary
array relabeling. Numeric array labels are not claimed to be lexicographic.
