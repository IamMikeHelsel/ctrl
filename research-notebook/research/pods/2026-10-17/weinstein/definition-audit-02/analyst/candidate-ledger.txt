Declared model and normalization
V has ordered basis e_0,...,e_13 and g(e_a,e_b)=epsilon_a delta_ab, where
epsilon_0,...,epsilon_6=+1 and epsilon_7,...,epsilon_13=-1.
theta^a is the dual basis, so theta^a(e_b)=delta_ab. Forms are in Lambda(V*).
The orientation is theta^0 wedge ... wedge theta^13 with coefficient +1.
The Hodge convention S satisfies alpha wedge S(beta)=<alpha,beta> volume.
For an increasing index set I, S(theta^I)=sgn(I,I^c) product_I(epsilon) theta^I^c.
In particular S(volume)=-1. This sign is used, not suppressed.

Let gamma_a be the generator of Cl_R(7,7), with gamma_a^2=epsilon_a and
gamma_a gamma_b=-gamma_b gamma_a for a!=b. Write gamma_ab=gamma_a gamma_b
when a<b. Complexify this algebra. The adjoint bar conjugates complex scalars,
reverses products, and sends gamma_a to -gamma_a. Set u={A:bar(A)=-A}.
This is the declared u(64,64) real form in the explicit model below.

Phi_1 = sum_a theta^a tensor gamma_a.
Phi_2 = sum_(a<b) theta^a wedge theta^b tensor gamma_ab.
These are canonical coevaluation tensors followed by the Clifford inclusion.
Their stated coefficients are the normalization. No unit-norm claim is made.
The coevaluation is basis independent: the exterior dual action and the
Clifford vector action cancel. The Clifford inclusion is Spin-equivariant.
Thus both tensors are Spin-invariant and are valued in u.

The source does not uniquely force these choices. Let Omega=gamma_0...gamma_13.
It is fixed by Spin, Omega^2=1, and bar(Omega)=-Omega. The tensors
  Psi_1 = Phi_1 Omega,       Psi_2 = i Phi_2 Omega
are also invariant and u-valued. Their Clifford grades are 13 and 12, while
Phi_1/Phi_2 have grades 1 and 2. They are linearly independent of Phi_1/Phi_2.
Hence each of the two invariant spaces has at least two independent elements.
This lower bound is enough to establish a real selection issue. No complete
classification or claim that its dimension is exactly two is needed here.
There is also a separate scale choice. Replacing Phi_1 by alpha Phi_1 and
Phi_2 by beta Phi_2 gives
  D_(alpha,beta)(R)=-2 alpha S(Ric^sharp)+alpha beta scal S(Phi_1).
Every such choice kills ordinary Weyl tensors. Thus Weyl annihilation alone
does not select beta=1 or the Einstein trace ratio. The normalization must be
supplied separately; it is not inferred from the zero result on W.

Two fibre products, extended by exterior wedge
  C(A,B)=AB-BA,              J(A,B)=i(AB+BA).
If A and B are bar-skew, then bar(C(A,B))=-C(A,B) and
bar(J(A,B))=-J(A,B). These follow from the conjugate-linear anti-involution.
Every real coefficient of a resulting form remains in the declared real form.
The products here act on fibre coefficients; no form-degree sign is inserted
into C or J. The ordinary exterior wedge already carries its own signs.

Explicit spinor and Hermitian model
Let S_R=Lambda*(R^7), with its 128 ordered subset basis vectors u_J.
Let a_j be exterior creation and b_j exterior contraction. Define
  gamma_j=a_j+b_j,   gamma_(7+j)=a_j-b_j,  0<=j<7.
On u_J, both change membership of j and multiply by
(-1)^(number of elements of J less than j). The second formula has an extra
minus sign when j is already in J. These are explicit real 128 by 128 matrices.
Define K=gamma_7 gamma_8 ... gamma_13. Exact checks establish K^T=K,
K^2=1, tr(K)=0, and gamma_a^T K+K gamma_a=0 for every a. Thus K has 64 positive
and 64 negative eigenvalues. On complex matrices bar(A)=K^-1 A^dagger K.
The real matrix representation is faithful: creation, contraction and the
vacuum projector generate all matrix units, while both algebras have dimension
2^14=128^2. The finite checks do not themselves formalize that argument.
See fock_realization.py and fock_realization_results.json.

Exact algebraic curvature domain
R_abcd is a real covariant 4-tensor with
  R_abcd=-R_bacd=-R_abdc,
  R_abcd=R_cdab,
  R_abcd-R_acbd+R_adbc=0.
Define
  F_R = sum_(a<b,c<d) epsilon_c epsilon_d R_abcd theta^ab tensor gamma_cd,
  Ric_bd = sum_a epsilon_a R_ab a d,
  scal = sum_b epsilon_b Ric_bb,
  T^sharp = sum_(b,d) epsilon_d T_bd theta^b tensor gamma_d.
This is a fixed curvature normalization. Some spin-connection conventions use
F_R/2. That rescales all the conclusions below by 1/2.
The Weyl space W in this audit means exactly the tensors above with Ric=0.
This is the ordinary algebraic Weyl sector in signature (7,7).

Declared candidate for the displayed two-term formula
  Q(R) = S J(Phi_2, S F_R),
  D(R) = C(Phi_1, S F_R) - (1/2) S J(Phi_1, Q(R)).
The source permits both products, but this C/J/J assignment is our choice.
All intermediate terms are valued in u. The respective exterior degrees are
13, 0 and 13. The candidate is therefore a defined pointwise map.

Mathematical derivation, not yet Lean-certified
The Clifford relation gives
  [gamma_a,gamma_c gamma_d]
    = 2 epsilon_a(delta_ac gamma_d-delta_ad gamma_c).
Also theta^a wedge S(theta^ab)=-epsilon_a S(theta^b).
Expanding and collecting terms therefore gives
  C(Phi_1,S F_R)=-2 S(Ric^sharp).
This first identity uses pair antisymmetry and the definition of Ric.

For the scalar ingredient, define the real Clifford contraction
  K_R = sum_(a<b,c<d) epsilon_a epsilon_b epsilon_c epsilon_d R_abcd
          (gamma_ab gamma_cd+gamma_cd gamma_ab).
Equal pairs contribute -scal times 1. Pairs sharing exactly one index contribute
zero. For four distinct indices a<b<c<d, the contribution is
  4 epsilon_a epsilon_b epsilon_c epsilon_d
    (R_abcd-R_acbd+R_adbc) gamma_a gamma_b gamma_c gamma_d.
Pair symmetry supplies the factor 4; Bianchi kills this last coefficient.
Consequently K_R=-scal times 1. Since S(volume)=-1, Q(R)=i scal times 1.
Then J(Phi_1,i scal)=-2 scal Phi_1, so
  D(R)=-2 S((Ric-(scal/2)g)^sharp).
In particular D kills W. On this ordinary curvature domain its kernel is
exactly W: tracing Ric-(scal/2)g gives -6 scal in dimension 14.
Using the accepted Mathlib H=-S convention in all star positions changes the
whole displayed candidate by a minus sign. Thus D_H=+2 S(Einstein^sharp), or
equivalently D_H=-2 H(Einstein^sharp). The metric and tensor ledger stay fixed.

Complete-basis exact evidence
There are 91 coordinate 2-forms. A pair-symmetric curvature coefficient array
has 91*92/2=4186 independent entries. Its 1001 disjoint four-index blocks each
have three entries x,y,z and one Bianchi equation x-y+z=0.
A basis therefore consists of 1183 entries whose two index pairs intersect,
plus two vectors (x,y,z)=(1,1,0),(0,1,1) in each disjoint block.
This yields 1183+2*1001=3185, equal to 14^2*(14^2-1)/12.
basis_gate.py checks the two contraction identities and the assembled candidate
on every one of these basis elements using Gaussian rationals and explicit
Clifford/exterior multiplication. All pass. By linearity and the basis argument,
this is an exhaustive finite coefficient check of the stated identities.
It is not a trusted-kernel proof. The implementation and the basis argument
still need independent review. No theorem count is used as a value claim.

Negative controls that delimit the conclusion
1. The fully alternating four-form with R_0123=1 has both pair antisymmetries
   and pair symmetry, but fails Bianchi. Its Ricci tensor is zero, while
   K_R=12 gamma_0123 and Q(R)=-12i gamma_0123. The candidate is nonzero.
   Thus Ricci zero alone is insufficient outside the declared curvature domain.
2. On constant sectional curvature +1, C/J/J yields 156 S(Phi_1), as the
   Einstein formula requires. The all-commutator choice gives -26 S(Phi_1).
   Among the eight C/J choices, only C/J/J gives this normalization. This test
   does not classify all invariant tensors or every possible product choice.
3. At general epsilon, Weyl annihilation concerns a transported domain.
   Let h=gamma_0 gamma_1 in Spin(7,7), with h^-1=-h. Take a Weyl tensor with
   independent entries R_0101=R_2323=1 and R_0202=R_1313=-1.
   Conjugating Phi_1/Phi_2 by h while leaving F_R fixed gives a nonzero output:
   its S(theta^4) tensor gamma_4 coefficient is 8. Conjugating F_R too gives
   zero. Thus the valid claim is at epsilon=1, or on Ad_(epsilon^-1)(W).
   It is not annihilation of a fixed geometric Weyl subspace for every epsilon.
   This fixed-space counterexample is not a GU refutation. The author's domain
   and its relation to epsilon must be settled before such a comparison.
