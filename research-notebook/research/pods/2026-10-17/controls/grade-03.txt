The proposed 50 ms ramp is a different task
A centered +10 to -10 linear jerk ramp over [0.225,0.275] s starts 25 ms before
the nominal replan at 0.25 s. It requires preview or an explicitly earlier
admission. A causal reaction to new information arriving at 0.25 s cannot use it.

Even with preview, replacing only that jerk step changes the integrated reference.
Let J=10 and h=0.025 s. Against the original reference, exact integration gives
at ramp end:
  Delta(q2)=0,
  Delta(q1)=-J*h^2/3=-1/480 m/s,
  Delta(q0)=-J*h^3/3=-1/19200 m.
If all later jerk remains unchanged, Delta(q0)=-1/1920 m at t=0.5 s and
-1/640 m, or -1.5625 mm, at t=1 s. Thus measuring error only relative to the new
reference changes the task behind the 0.2 mm test. If that altered-reference
error were within 0.2 mm at t=1, its error relative to the original reference
would be at least 1.3625 mm. A new-reference sample pass would not repair the
assigned task. These are exact reference-kinematics calculations, not a new
controller simulation; see ramp-proposal-review.json.

There is also a causal endpoint constraint. At the original switch time 0.25 s,
q2=2.5 m/s^2. Reaching q2=0 by 0.5 s under |j|<=10 requires integral(j)=-2.5,
the lower bound across that interval. Hence j must be -10 almost everywhere.
A nonzero continuous ramp from +10 cannot keep that acceleration endpoint and
deadline under the same jerk limit. Some timing, endpoint, jerk-limit, or broader
future-reference condition must change. This is a limitation of this proposed
replacement, not a ban on motion planning with explicit slack.
