Exact accepted result
Let a0,J,S>0 and T>=0. Require a and j continuous on [0,T], a(0)=a0,
j(0)=J, a'=j on (0,T), and |j(t)|<=J on [0,T]. At each t in [0,T),
require the actual right derivative s(t) of j and |s(t)|<=S. No continuity
of s is required. Then a(T)<=0 implies T>=a0/J+2J/S.

The proof derives j(t)>=max(J-St,-J) from these right-derivative hypotheses.
It does not assume an integrated jerk envelope or an all-time acceleration
bound. The fundamental theorem of calculus then gives the acceleration lower
bound. Both time cases, before and after L=2J/S, are present.

The attaining jerk max(J-St,-J) meets every stated assumption. Its right snap
is -S before L and zero at and after L. The acceleration defined by its integral
reaches zero at a0/J+2J/S. The missing two-sided jerk derivative at L is allowed.
T=0 cannot satisfy the terminal premise when a0>0. No a.e. theorem is claimed.

For a0=5/2, J=10, S=400, the exact deadline is 3/10 s. At 1/4 s, every
admissible acceleration is at least 1/2 m/s^2. With continuous v, v(0)=5/16,
and v'=a on the open interval, every admissible velocity at 3/10 s is at least
181/240 m/s. This exceeds the old endpoint 5/8 by 31/240 m/s. This is a bound
for all supplied admissible trajectories, not only the attaining example.
