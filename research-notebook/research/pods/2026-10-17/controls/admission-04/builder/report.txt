CAUSAL REFERENCE ADMISSION — proof batch 04

The result is a sharp necessary deadline for removing positive acceleration.
It is not a stopping-time result, a control law, or a robot safety certificate.
The old 0.2 mm controller failures remain unchanged.

Statement and regularity
Let a0,J,S>0 and T>=0. Time zero is when the new request arrives.
The acceleration a is continuous on [0,T], has a(0)=a0, and has classical
derivative a'=j at every point in (0,T). The jerk j is continuous on [0,T],
has j(0)=J, and satisfies |j(t)|<=J throughout that closed interval.
At each t in [0,T), j has right derivative s(t), with |s(t)|<=S.
No continuity of s is assumed. No almost-everywhere or Sobolev result is claimed.

Then a(T)<=0 implies T>=a0/J+2J/S. This includes the endpoint a(T)=0.
The result applies to reference acceleration and reference jerk in the current
task. Applying it to physical acceleration requires these same physical jerk
and snap hypotheses. The existing filtered controller does not supply them.

Proof content
The one-sided mean-value theorem gives |j(t)-J|<=S*t. Combining this with
j(t)>=-J proves j(t)>=max(J-S*t,-J). Thus the lower envelope is derived from
actual derivative hypotheses, not supplied as a trajectory assumption.

Put L=2J/S. Integration and the fundamental theorem of calculus give
  a(t)>=a0+J*t-S*t^2/2         for 0<=t<=L,
  a(t)>=a0-J*(t-L)            for L<=t<=T.
The first lower bound is at least a0. The second cannot be nonpositive before
a0/J+L. Both time cases are present in the Lean proof.

Sharpness is proved for every positive a0,J,S. The attaining jerk is
  j_min(t)=max(J-S*t,-J),
and a_min(t)=a0+integral(0,t,j_min). Its right snap is -S before L and zero
at and after L. The file proves continuity, the derivative relations, both
motion bounds, the initial state, and a_min(a0/J+L)=0. At L the jerk lacks a
two-sided derivative; its right derivative exists. This is why the stated
regularity matters.

Velocity consequence
For a continuous velocity v with v(0)=v0 and v'=a on (0,T), integrating the
acceleration comparison gives, for L<=t<=T,
  v(t)>=v0+a0*t-J*t^2/2+(2*J^2/S)*t-4*J^3/(3*S^2).
The Lean file proves this for every admissible trajectory. It is not only an
evaluation of the attaining ramp.

Exact old-task instance (SI units)
At arrival, a0=5/2 m/s^2, incoming jerk J=10 m/s^3, and snap limit S=400 m/s^4.
The earliest acceleration-zero time is 3/10 s after arrival. At the old 1/4 s
deadline, every admissible acceleration is at least 1/2 m/s^2.
