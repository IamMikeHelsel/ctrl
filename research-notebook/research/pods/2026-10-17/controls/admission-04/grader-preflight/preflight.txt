Deadline theorem review
For a0,J,S>0 and T>=0, require a and j continuous on [0,T], a(0)=a0,
j(0)=J, a'=j on (0,T), |j|<=J on [0,T], and a right derivative s of j
at every t in [0,T), with |s|<=S. Then the one-sided mean-value bound gives
j(t)>=max(J-St,-J). Integration gives
a(t)>=a0+Jt-St^2/2 for t<=L=2J/S, and
a(t)>=a0-J(t-L) for t>=L.
Thus a(T)<=0 implies T>=a0/J+2J/S. The jerk max(J-St,-J) attains equality.
Its right derivative at L is zero. This is an acceleration-zero deadline.
It is not a stopping deadline. The theorem does not derive physical jerk/snap
bounds from the feedback controller. The latency formula a0/J+2ell+2J/S
is valid only for the stated +J hold policy during latency ell.
