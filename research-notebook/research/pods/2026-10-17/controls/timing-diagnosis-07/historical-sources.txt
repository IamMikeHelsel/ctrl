TIMING, HUNTING, AND THE MEANING OF A CORRECTION
18 October2026UTC. Primary sources inspected after Mike's cruise-control analogy.

A concrete early cruise-control example
Robert H. Thorner, US3476206A, published4 November1969, discusses the design
tradeoff in automotive speed control. A larger servo can overcome linkage
friction and inertia, but its slower response can produce hunting or sluggish
motion. A smaller servo can respond faster yet suffer lag or hysteresis from
insufficient force. This is the inventor's technical account, not a controlled
fleet study. It supports a historical architecture/actuator example. It does
not prove that every early cruise controller failed for one cause.
https://patents.google.com/patent/US3476206A/en
See the paragraphs on connection of the governor to vehicle linkage.

An experimental timing example, distinct from road cruise control
Yoshihiko Kawazoe,1985, Low Speed Hunting of the Pneumatically Governed
Compression-Ignition Engine, first report, Bulletin of JSME28(243),2022-2027,
DOI10.1299/jsme1958.28.2022. The primary abstract reports experiments in which
reducing pressure-signal phase lag removed hunting. This is an engine governor,
not an automotive cruise-control road trial. The abstract says the conventional
linear model did not explain the observed phenomenon.
https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsme1958/28/243/28_243_2022/_article/-char/ja

A mode-transition example
Nakajima and Aoi, Honda, US4598370A, published1 July1986, describes overshoot
and hunting after acceleration/deceleration transitions in auto-cruise. Its
proposed compensation depends on operating state and transition timing. This
supports the importance of the transition policy. Patent descriptions do not
provide independent evidence of the proposed remedy's performance.
https://patents.google.com/patent/US4598370A/en

An established, different mechanism
Astrom and Murray, Feedback Systems, example10.6 and figure10.10 in the2012
author PDF, use cruise control on a steep hill to show integral windup. The
throttle reaches its limit while the controller's integral state keeps growing.
Feedback of the actuator discrepancy limits the overshoot. This is saturation
and integral memory, not the mechanism in our current linear simulation.
https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/books/AM08/pdf/am08-complete_28Sep12.pdf

What our model supports
No measurement is stale and no timestamp is shifted. The extra command filter
adds a50ms time constant inside a loop with a12.5ms actuator time constant.
The unchanged feedback gains then produce a lightly damped mode near4.92Hz.
Its amplitude decays with a1.88s time constant. The direct model instead has
three poles at-40/s. The two filtered laws have identical homogeneous dynamics;
the extra feedforward term changes how the reference excites those dynamics.
These statements come from exact characteristic polynomials and numerical
roots, saved in result.json. They are not new Lean theorems.

The broader design interpretation
A useful correction must be defined for a stated state, execution time, and
actuator response. Filtering, queueing, saturation, and late inference can each
break a different part of that contract. Similar-looking oscillation does not
establish the same cause. Timing is already central to classical control;
our possible contribution is a checked, explicit execution contract for learned
updates and a clear comparison. A new explanation alone is not a new theorem.
