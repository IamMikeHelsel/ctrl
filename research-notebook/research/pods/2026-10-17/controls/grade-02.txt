Accepted result
QuadraticSlowFilter.lean supplies one rational symmetric positive definite P for
normalized sigma=1, lambda=2, and every r in [8,12]. It proves
  (1/2)||xi||^2 <= V(xi) <= 500||xi||^2,
  V'=xi^T*(A(r)^T P+P A(r))*xi <= -(1/2)||xi||^2,
  V(t) <= exp(-t/1000)*V(0),
where xi=(x-q0,v-q1,a-q2,u-q2) and V(xi)=xi^T P xi.
This closes the fixed common-quadratic search requested by grade 01. The filter
rate 2 is below the entire actuator-rate band [8,12], while the old box budgets
required at least 54. The earlier restriction is therefore conservative for
homogeneous stability. It is not a necessary physical stability condition.

Exact hypotheses and semantics
The last theorem uses the actual equations x'=v, v'=a, a'=r*(u-a),
q0'=q1, q1'=q2, q2'=0, and
  u'=2*(q2-8*(x-q0)-6*(v-q1)-(a-q2)/2-u).
Thus disturbance and reference jerk are zero. H>=0. Physical, reference, and
filter trajectories are supplied and continuous on [0,H]. Their differential
equations hold on (0,H). The rate obeys 8<=r(t)<=12 at every time in [0,H].
No explicit derivative or continuity hypothesis on r is used. Supplied classical
trajectories still restrict admissible executions. Existence is not proved;
uniqueness is neither assumed nor proved. There is no all-time tracking,
filter-error, or command-rate bound among the premises. No finite forced-switch
or globally nonsmooth execution theorem is silently included.

Practical limits
The reported norm bound is
  ||xi(t)|| <= sqrt(1000)*exp(-t/2000)*||xi(0)||.
Its initial multiplier is about 31.62. Its guaranteed energy time constant is
1000 normalized time units; the norm exponential time constant is 2000. These
weak certified rates are not observed settling times or evidence of useful
smoothing. The current energy ellipsoid alone permits |ex| up to about 4.320R
from initial xi=(R,0,0,0). This is a geometric permission, not a proved transient
overshoot. The certificate does not restore the old position tube unchanged.

Why screen forcing next
For the same normalized model with target jerk coefficient kappa and filter rate
lambda, direct algebra gives the forcing
  g=(0,0,r*w-j,(lambda*kappa-1)*j).
At lambda=2, the accepted controller coefficient kappa=5/48 gives fourth forcing
-19*j/24. Constant-input equilibrium has
  ev=ea=0, b=j/r-w,
  ex=[w+(kappa-1/r-1/lambda)*j]/8.
With sigma=10, physical lambda=20/s, r=100/s, j=10 m/s^3 and w=0, this is
-119/192000 m, about -0.619792 mm. It exceeds the old 0.2 mm radius in magnitude.
This exact equilibrium calculation is not a finite-horizon violation proof.

The standard lag compensation kappa=1/lambda+5/48 gives kappa=29/48 here. It leaves
the homogeneous A and P unchanged, but changes the controller and its forcing to
(0,0,r*w-j,5*j/24). The same equilibrium example becomes +1/192000 m, about
+0.005208 mm. The target's jerk coefficient increases. Transients and physical
command demands still need to be checked. This is a named controller change,
not a property of the accepted homogeneous theorem or a novelty claim.
These reviewer calculations are recorded in forced-feasibility-review.json.
