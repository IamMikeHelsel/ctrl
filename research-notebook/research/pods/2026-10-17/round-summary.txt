CONTROLS

Closed proof 1 — grade A, independently accepted.
The earlier tracking proof assumed that a command filter kept its error small.
The new joint-filter proof derives that bound from the actual filter and plant
equations, initial conditions, and explicit budgets. It also checks finite
replans that retain the filter state. Sixteen target statements passed a fresh
independent build, axiom audit, kernel replay, and a rejected false-bound test.

That certificate has a practical limit. Its budgets require lambda >= 54 sigma.
The illustrative feasible filter is much faster than the actuator. This does
not show a useful smoothing effect. The limit applies to these budgets, not to
all stable filters. See controls/grade-01.txt and report-correction-01/ for the
traceable correction to an existence/uniqueness sentence in the builder report.

Closed proof 2 — grade A, independently accepted.
A different certificate proves stability for the much slower normalized filter
lambda=2, for every actuator rate in [8,12]. The proof uses one rational quadratic
energy function across that whole interval. It derives the error dynamics from
the actual plant and filter. Twenty-five target statements passed independent
fresh compilation, axiom checks, kernel replay, certificate reproduction, and a
rejected false-margin test. See controls/grade-02.txt.

This second proof has zero reference jerk and zero disturbance. Its decay bound
is conservative. It does not prove useful tracking, settling time, stopping,
smoothing, sampled execution, or hardware performance.
It assumes supplied classical trajectories on the interval interior. The finite
rate jumps used in the screen are separate numerical cases; no assembled forced
trajectory theorem for those cases has been proved here.

Practical screen — grade B; current 0.2 mm proof path paused.
The next fixed screen compares two filtered laws in 48 cases. The second law
adds explicit feedforward compensation for filter lag. This is a changed
controller, not a new algorithm claim. The cases include constant and changing
actuator response, disturbances, and reference-jerk changes. The requested
position tolerance is 0.2 mm. Full traces and model equations are retained in
controls/forced-screen-03/. Independent replay checked all 48 traces, 72,120
rows, and 72 retained-state handoffs. A separate physical-coordinate calculation
reproduced all position peaks. The original law has a sampled target violation
in 24 of 24 cases; the changed law has one in 4 of 24. Worst sampled errors are
1.152601 mm and 0.280948 mm. This screen has no unfiltered direct-controller
baseline. Sampled peaks alone cannot establish between-sample safety.

The grader rejected the proposed single-case jerk-ramp rescue as a repair of

reference position by 1.5625 mm at one second. This conclusion follows from
exact reviewer algebra; it is not a new Lean theorem. Restart requires explicit
timing, endpoint, and reference-admission rules. See controls/grade-03.txt.
The earlier accepted proofs remain valid under their stated assumptions.

WEINSTEIN / GEOMETRIC UNITY

Grade B for the verified mathematical bridge; further GU expansion paused.
The new Hodge result uses Mathlib's actual exterior algebra, an explicit real
14-dimensional metric with seven positive and seven negative directions, and
a fixed volume convention. It proves the signed-complement formula, both
conventions, the negative-identity square, exact sign witnesses, and the link
to the earlier coefficient-array interface. Independent fresh compilation,
kernel replay, and all 53 target axiom audits passed. Two false positive-square
tests were rejected for their intended mathematical error. Builder and frozen
dependency files remained unchanged. See weinstein/grade-01.txt for the review.

This supplies one pointwise mathematical ingredient. It does not supply the
missing Phi tensors, spinors, other arrays, or a complete GU operator. It gives
no claim about GU physics, experimental validity, or publication novelty.
Restart needs explicit normalized Phi tensors, a declared spinor realization,
the real algebra and product or bracket conventions, and a curvature domain.
The proposed next test would then check output closure and the operator's value
on a basis of the declared Weyl subspace. These inputs are still missing.
