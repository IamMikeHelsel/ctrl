INDEPENDENT REVIEW — TASK CONTRACT08 AND MOVING GOAL08

Decision: accept both bounded gates. Grade A for the proof milestone. Grade A
for the independently replayed numerical batch. These grades do not estimate
publication novelty, hardware acceptance, or a general advantage of one method.

What is proved
The isolated-task theorem derives its position tube from the actual plant ODE,
rate band, disturbance bound, budget, and initial error cube at time zero. All
old events remain in the list. Every old tail must finish by issue time, and the
completed old target must equal the old authoritative goal. The new rational
event is appended with target increment g-a. The theorem derives exact guard
acceptance and position hold, transition, directed progress, and arrival bounds.
It does not take a desired tracking or task conclusion as a final assumption.

Review found that an initial draft allowed a deadline after the proof horizon,
which could make the deadline interval empty. The frozen theorem requires
D <= horizon and gives a bound at D itself. This issue is closed.

For the fixed 20 mm request, the theorem gives at least 9.8 mm of directed
progress at .62 s and position arrival within .2 mm from 1.02 s through 2 s.
The original 1.5 s deadline is included. A separate conditional lower bound
gives at least 9.8 mm of goal error at the premature .62 s deadline.

This is a position result, not a proof of physical rest or the benchmark's
speed/acceleration endpoint limits. Actual position need not be monotone.
Bounded disturbance can sustain physical motion arbitrarily late even from
exact initial rest; the independent Fourier-coefficient example documents this
analytically, without claiming a Lean trajectory proof. The global cube also
contains states outside the tighter endpoint limits; that witness is Lean checked.

Proof validation
The reviewer copied and compiled 49 local modules with Lean 4.35.0 and the
unchanged Mathlib pin. All 689 public proof statements in this closure have only
standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound, or no axioms). Thirteen
separate kernel replays passed. The new builder contributes 25 core statements
and 12 fixture statements. The closure also contains 637 inherited statements
and 15 independent reviewer witnesses. These are audit counts, not discoveries.

Five false acceptance controls were rejected: too-short deadline, future event
misclassified as complete, jerk guard misused as an acceleration guarantee,
deadline beyond the horizon, and premature reference arrival. Positive witness
proofs show why these implications fail; compiler rejection alone would not.
A reviewer-only retained-history fixture needed explicit rational-to-real cast
normalization. Its failed source and log are preserved under attempts/. No
frozen builder source changed. Already fresh, unchanged dependencies were
hash-checked and reused; all new theorem axioms and kernel replays ran afterward.

What the reversal batch establishes
An independent 80-digit implementation used exact rational particular-solution
coefficients plus a 3-by-3 homogeneous matrix exponential. It started each plant
from zero, carried its state through every switch, and imported no builder
physical state. It checked all 144 traces: 544,728 state rows and 3,264 one-sided
event/jump rows. Every task, endpoint, timing, admission and reference-limit
classification matched. Maximum x/v/a differences were 4.20e-16 m,
5.61e-15 m/s and 7.55e-14 m/s^2. Actual-state jumps were zero.

Independent critical-point calculations reproduced all reference V/A/J peaks.
Exact arithmetic also bound all 36 FIR polynomial pieces to the retained events,
checked every FIR admission knot, and bound all 16 native exported plans to their
input targets, execution clocks and saved carried states. The saved finite
native gate contains 40,439 queries over eight histories. This review audits
that saved gate and its bindings; it does not claim a new native executable
run, a firmware proof, or bit-identical arbitrary-query native execution.

The ordinary FIR return passes its jerk guard, with exact peak 25/4 m/s^3,
but acceleration reaches 21/32 m/s^2, above .5. An independent Lean witness also
proves accepted guard plus acceleration 21/32 at .77 s. The buffered FIR return
passes the declared numerical task. The far reversal has candidate jerk
45/4 m/s^3 and is rejected; prior motion remains active and the task fails.

Fine-grid valid tasks: fast Ruckig 18/24, minimum-duration Ruckig 18/24, FIR 6/24.
All 72 initial-progress obligations pass. There are 48/72 deadline-and-remain
passes and 42/72 overall valid tasks. All decisions agree at both resolutions.
A/B share byte-identical physics in 36 trace pairs and differ only in scoring.
Thus 72 scoring cases include 54 distinct physical parameter cases. A deadline
before delivery fails the service promise; it is not a universal impossibility
claim about coincidental endpoint arrival.

The common numerical reference-limit allowance is 1e-10 in each respective SI
unit. It is separate from native reconstruction tolerance and is not an exact
limit theorem. Remain, settling, and physical extrema are finite sampled results.
Different methods have different remaining distances at revision. Squared jerk
and command variation are not energy use or an equal-distance efficiency test.
