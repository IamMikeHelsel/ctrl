# Motor control: task goals, paths and correction

Mike's concern has a defensible control interpretation: an interface can turn uncertain estimates into unnecessary new motion commands. A useful design can preserve a stable task and change the action only when the estimated state threatens that task. This is a robotic hypothesis to test, not evidence that explicit goals themselves cause human or robot tremor.

There is no single established account of how the brain computes a complete path. Different experiments support task-dependent feedback, learned movement representations and predictive state estimates. The following bounded reading does not identify a literal neural implementation of an optimizer or our four-box filter.

| Question | Primary result or model | Careful implication |
|---|---|---|
| Must every detail of a path be specified? | [Todorov and Jordan, 2002](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~jordan/papers/coordination.pdf), pp.1226–1229: their stochastic optimal-feedback model corrects deviations that affect the task while permitting variation in redundant directions. They call this the minimal intervention principle. | A task can specify what matters, while a feedback policy selects actions from the current state. This is stronger than simply lowering update rate. It does not mean ignoring errors, omitting obstacle constraints, or that no human task uses a planned path. |
| Are movement representations layered? | [Kornysheva and Diedrichsen, 2014](https://diedrichsenlab.org/pubs/Kornysheva_eLife_2014.pdf), selected Results and Discussion, PDF pp.4,12–13: the trained finger-sequence experiment found separable spatial/temporal representations in premotor areas and integrated patterns in contralateral primary motor cortex. | This supports flexible component representations in this task. It does not establish a universal symbolic goal-to-waypoint hierarchy, a full arm planner, or a sequence of convolution filters. |
| How can delayed sensing control a moving limb? | [Miall et al., 2007](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0050316), Results and opening Discussion: cerebellar stimulation disrupted reaching in a way consistent with an outdated hand-state estimate. | The study supports predictive state estimation. A robot analogue is to preserve an estimate of current executed state and account for command/sensor age. Prediction is an estimate with error, not knowledge of future intent. |
| Does smoothness mean the brain minimizes jerk? | [Flash and Hogan, 1985](https://newmanlab.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/1985-the-coordination-of-arm-movements-an-experimentally-confirmed-mathematical-model.pdf), Model, straight-movement Results, and Discussion, printed pp.1689,1692,1697–1698: minimizing integrated squared hand jerk predicted several planar reaching features. The discussion gives alternative physiological interpretations. | Minimum jerk is a useful model for those movements. A good fit does not establish literal jerk computation, universal optimality, or minimization of electrical/metabolic energy. It does not justify escalating automatically to snap, crackle or pop. |
| Is tremor evidence of excessive correction? | [Lakie et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3424765/), Results and Discussion: EMG/hand-acceleration measurements and a mechanical model support a substantial resonant contribution to the physiological hand tremor studied; movement changed the resonance. | Delayed feedback can affect a control loop, but tremor is not evidence by itself of over-articulated goals. This experiment does not settle all tremor mechanisms. It does not diagnose our robot traces. |

The design hypothesis is therefore modest: pass a stable goal or permitted region, a deadline and meaningful constraints to a persistent controller; pass state estimates and their uncertainty through a separate channel. Preserve the controller's executed state and committed future motion across revisions. Issue a new goal when task authority changes it. Within a redundant arm, a policy can also avoid correcting motion that leaves the task satisfied. Our scalar model has no such joint-space redundancy, and its fixed 0.2 mm tube currently consumes the whole 0.2 mm hold tolerance.

This does not remove the need for a geometric path where collisions, contact, process quality or timing constrain the route. Fewer task updates also cannot substitute for adequate feedback bandwidth. The proposed intent-contract comparison isolates the semantic channel only; it does not test a brain model, a new predictive estimator, or a new industrial controller. The earlier command-filter oscillation remains evidence about that added filter and plant, not a demonstrated stale-frame bug or a biological mechanism.

Reading scope: the linked full primary texts were accessible. Only the named sections/passages were inspected; this is not a systematic motor-control review. No medical inference, new model fitting, or experiment was performed.
