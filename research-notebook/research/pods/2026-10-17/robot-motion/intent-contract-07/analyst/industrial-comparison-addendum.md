For a later declared industrial gate, compare a correctly configured native blended program, its supported buffered replacement interface, and the proposed external contract. Hold robot, payload/tool data, route, deadlines, path tolerance and allowed limits fixed. Record which layer owns interpolation. Score command age, queue contents and response to replacement, in addition to per-plan smoothness. This is a design requirement, not authorization for hardware runs or a claim that the present evidence covers those cases.

The added comparison axes are:

1. **Across replacement:** retain both sides of position, velocity, acceleration and jerk at every actual execution boundary. Record discarded/retained committed motion, reference-state mismatch, physical state continuity, task error and deadline. A small cost inside each isolated plan does not bound its joins to the preceding plan.
2. **Derivative order:** jerk, snap, crackle and pop are the third through sixth time derivatives of position, where those derivatives exist. The four-box reference is C3; its snap is piecewise constant and generally jumps at knots. It is not generally C4, and it has no global classical fifth/sixth derivative guarantee. A higher derivative penalty needs an explicit purpose, continuity constraints and a speed/path-error tradeoff. We have not established either vendor's derivative order or cost.
3. **Physical energy versus proxies:** for joint angles theta and torques tau, mechanical work is `integral tau dot theta_dot dt`, in joules. State whether net work or positive work is reported. Electrical energy requires a defined supply/drive measurement boundary, such as `integral V I dt`, with losses, idle draw and regeneration accounted for. `integral |tau|^2 dt` is a torque-effort proxy. `integral |x'''|^2 dt` has units m^2/s^5, and command total variation here has units m/s^2. Neither is joules. The present scalar acceleration-command model cannot measure industrial joint mechanical or electrical energy.

Retain the existing squared-jerk and command-variation results under their exact definitions. Do not call lower values measured power savings. Keep the continuous and jump parts of variation separate. Compare them only alongside successful hold, real-step progress, deadline and settling criteria; slowing or ignoring a requested move cannot count as an improvement.

A simple analytic counterexample shows why signed work alone is inadequate. For an ideal frictionless point mass, force `F=m v'` gives `integral F v dt = (m/2)(v(T)^2-v(0)^2)`. Every rest-to-rest trajectory therefore has zero net work, including a jerky one. Positive work, drive losses and squared-torque effort are different objectives with different model assumptions. This is an elementary calculation, not a new Lean result or a model of either industrial controller.
