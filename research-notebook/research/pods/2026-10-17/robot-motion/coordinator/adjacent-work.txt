TIMING, PRIOR WORK, AND THE COMPARISON IMPLEMENTATION
Coordinator note, 18 October 2026 UTC. This supplements the frozen lead proposal.

Timing contracts are established work. We should test a specific certificate
and execution layer, rather than claim that robotics has ignored time.

Törngren, Tripakis, Derler and Lee (2012), Design Contracts for Cyber-Physical
Systems: Making Timing Assumptions Explicit, UCB/EECS-2012-191.
https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2012/EECS-2012-191.pdf
Inspected title, abstract, introduction and sections 2–4. The paper makes timing
assumptions explicit between control and software teams. It covers sampling,
actuation delay, jitter and logical execution time. Our broader design framing
has clear precedent here. A new result needs a precise additional claim.

Wang et al. (2026), Real-Time Robot Execution with Masked Action Chunking.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20130v1
Inspected abstract and version metadata. REMAC addresses mismatch between
intended actions and execution during asynchronous inference, with learned
corrections and prefix preservation. Its reported performance is the authors'
claim; we have not reproduced it. Full technical comparison remains necessary.

Jiang et al. (2026), FutureRTC: Real-Time Robot Execution with
Anticipatory-Conditioned Action Chunking.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24008v1
Inspected abstract and version metadata. It predicts execution-time observations
and corrects the difference between rolled-forward and actual proprioception.
This is close prior work for the stale-context hypothesis. It does not establish
that our aligned-timestamp filtered-controller example has a stale-context bug.
Full technical comparison remains necessary; no reproduction is claimed.

Ruckig Community API
https://docs.ruckig.com/classruckig_1_1Trajectory.html
https://github.com/pantor/ruckig/blob/main/include/ruckig/trajectory.hpp
The inspected official documentation identifies version 0.19.7 and exposes
get_profiles() and get_brakes() for Community trajectories. at_time also exposes
jerk. This suggests that the required segment export is feasible. The actual
pinned local source, profile fields, brake segments, endpoint extrapolation and
export consistency still need to be checked before accepting the experiment.
These are floating-point polynomial segments, not exact rational solutions.
Do not invent switch times from samples or silently omit zero-duration phases.

Required reporting boundary
The four-box filter, retained motion state, timing contracts and online motion
generation are established ideas. The proposed contribution is a machine-checked
admission test with explicit causal history, plus a tested connection to a
specific uncertain-lag physical model. Novelty and practical value remain open.

Further established motion-generation work
https://iris.unimore.it/handle/11380/1175126?mode=complete
Biagiotti and Melchiorri (2019), Trajectory generation via FIR filters: A
procedure for time-optimization under kinematic and frequency constraints.
The inspected institutional abstract and publisher introduction discuss FIR
parameter selection, derivative constraints and rest-to-rest time optimality.
The author-hosted full PDF fetch failed. We have not completed a full comparison.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S096706611730151X
Besset and Béarée (2017), FIR filter-based online jerk-constrained trajectory
generation. The inspected abstract and introduction describe online updates from
nonzero velocity and acceleration, fixed-time and time-optimal variants, and
experiments on a seven-axis Kuka. This is stronger prior work than a raw-target
baseline. Its experiments are not reproduced by this scalar gate.

https://www.mdpi.com/2218-6581/11/4/73
On Fast Jerk-Continuous Motion Functions with Higher-Order Kinematic Restrictions
for Online Trajectory Generation (2022). Primary search excerpts describe
four-filter parameter checks and jerk-continuous motion. Full-page access was
rate-limited. Treat this as a follow-up source, not a completed paper audit.

Pinned-source correction from the independent assessor
Ruckig main at 99751e6ebc7c963bb80e99fbbe66f2c279d1db57 declares version0.19.6.
It exposes get_profiles(), but does not expose the documented get_brakes()
method. The public Profile.brake and Profile.accel fields expose brake data.
The assessor retains inspected headers and hashes. The comparison builder must
use the pinned source API rather than assume that latest documentation matches.
