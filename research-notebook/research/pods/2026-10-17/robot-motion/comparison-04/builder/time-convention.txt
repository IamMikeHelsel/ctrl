TIMING AND FLOATING-PROFILE REPLAY

Packet commit times, FIR roots and sample ticks use exact rational times from the
frozen input. Admission equals execution at the commit tick; no future queue is
added. Raw and FIR reference coefficients are rational before conversion to the
numerical plant solver.

Ruckig computes durations, cumulative t_sum and state arrays as binary doubles.
The exporter retains those native values. JSON uses 17 significant digits; its
hexadecimal and rational metadata also record each exact binary time. A main
phase boundary is brake.duration + native t_sum, computed in the exporter.
It is not an exact sum of the binary durations. Those two constructions differ
by at most 1/18014398509481984 s over the saved smoke and primary plans.

For the canonical plant replay, the absolute boundary is rational commit time
plus that exact exported binary offset. This avoids losing very short positive
phases when adding a small offset to a larger double absolute time. All positive
phases and all zero-duration metadata remain in the native exports. The schedule
uses every positive phase and retains both sides of each boundary, even where
the decimal float time_s column is equal. The time_exact column orders them.

The initial state of a new plan comes from the previous native trajectory's
at_time query at the elapsed rational commit interval rounded once to a double.
The native diagnostic instead subtracts double absolute commit clocks. These are
explicit numerical conventions, not bit-identical executions. The diagnostic
quantifies state agreement and pointwise jerk-branch disagreements at rounding
boundaries. It does not erase them or turn a finite sample test into equivalence.

The numerical plant sees a piecewise polynomial reference. It retains actual
x,v,a at every boundary. Tiny reference q,v,a discrepancies from exported double
states are recorded in event CSV files and included in the full command-jump
identity. They are not described as mathematical C2 equality. Ruckig's physical
jerk jumps are not assigned a finite snap bound.

Implementation corrections before freeze did not change any input, gain, limit,
deadline or horizon. The first reference assembly correctly rejected a collapsed
absolute-double interval; rational absolute boundaries replaced that representation
before the 72 plant cases. A native diagnostic parser then encountered a subnormal
nextafter(0) input; strict parse checks and removal of that redundant zero-adjacent
query fixed the diagnostic. No primary plant result depended on that parser.
