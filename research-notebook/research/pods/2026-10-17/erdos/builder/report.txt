NORMALIZATION AND FINITE ROOT COST

Use n=|V(G)|, m=|E(G)|, r=2m/n^2 and c=2/47. The Lean target is an
unordered internal-edge count at most c*n^2. The paper also uses
rho=m/binom(n,2). For n>1, rho=r*n/(n-1); the two are not equal at finite n.
In the flag limit, a cut cost normalized by an unordered pair has threshold
2c=4/47, not c. The paper's displayed 2/25 belongs to its stronger 1/25
contradiction setup; it is not a ready part-(a) coefficient table.

For an injective k-root and admissible probabilities, changing root vertex
probabilities can affect only edges incident to a root. Their number is at
most k*(n-k)+binom(k,2)=k*n-k*(k+1)/2. Each edge's same-side probability lies
in [0,1]. Therefore
  |rootedCutCost - bulkRootedCutCost| <= k*n-k*(k+1)/2 <= k*n.
This is a hand-audited bound, not an existing theorem in Semantics.lean.
After division by n^2, the bound is at most k/n. For n-k>=2, an exact
non-root pair average F contributes binom(n-k,2)*F, plus at most the same
root-incident cost. Both the pair denominator and this error must be tracked.

There is an alternative when the certificate already bounds the full bulk
cost: bulkVertexProbability_mem_Icc and exists_cut_le_expectedInternalCost
can round bulk probabilities directly. Then the root override is unnecessary.
This does not remove sampling/collision errors from a flag-limit calculation.
The error must be uniform for the finite list of cuts and certificate terms.

ASYMPTOTIC TO EXACT: A PRECISE ROUTE

The current wrapper asks for an exact eventual c bound. A statement with
fixed positive error or an unquantified numerical residual does not imply it.
A sufficient replacement premise is the following uniform asymptotic claim:
  For every rational eps>0, there is N such that every triangle-free H with
  |H|>=N has a cut of normalized internal cost at most c+eps.
This includes all required density regimes. A mere convergence claim for one
sequence of graphs is insufficient.

For a fixed nonempty graph G of order n, choose eps=1/(94*n^2). Apply the
uniform claim to a positive balanced blow-up G(k) with k*n>=N. The existing
hasNormalizedInternalCutBoundQ_balancedBlowup_iff descends the bound c+eps
exactly to G. For its integer cut cost d this gives
  d <= 2*n^2/47 + 1/94, hence 47*d <= 2*n^2 + 1/2.
Since 47*d and 2*n^2 are integers, 47*d<=2*n^2. Thus G has an exact c cut.
The empty graph is immediate. This gives all orders, hence the eventual
premise. No strict slack at c is necessary for this route.

The small missing Lean proposition is this uniform-epsilon descent theorem.
It consumes the existing blow-up equivalence; it cannot manufacture the
uniform asymptotic premise. With a verified uniform bound c-delta and a
quantified O(1/n) error, positive delta would be an alternative route. No
such part-(a) slack or error constants were found in the inspected data.

DENSITY COVERAGE AND THE SEPARATE HIGH-DENSITY SKETCH

Part (a) is stated for all triangle-free densities. The source does not give
the domain of each numerical program. Parts (b) and (c) claim the stronger
1/25 bound for rho>=0.3197 and rho<=0.2486, respectively. They cannot be
assumed as certified branches without their proofs. Section 2.2 treats
m/n^2>=1/5-10^-8 at target 1/25 and omits computations. No complete check of
these branches was found. Thus original part-(a) numerical coverage is unknown.

For the weaker target c, the separate near-2/5 argument is dispensable if
one supplies the following elementary finite branch. This is an audited
dependency reduction, not a new BCL theorem or an implemented Lean proof.

1. A fair random cut has expected internal cost m/2. Hence r<=8/47 gives
   D2/n^2<=r/4<=2/47. The retained rounding theorem supplies the basic API.
2. For a triangle-free graph, N(v) is independent. The cut (N(v),V\N(v))
   has crossing cost sum_{u in N(v)} degree(u). Summing over v gives
   sum_u degree(u)^2 >= 4*m^2/n. Some cut therefore has
   D2<=m-4*m^2/n^2. This is the elementary bound also stated as Theorem 1
   in the primary paper; no standalone proof of this branch was found in
   the inspected retained BCL API.
3. For r>=49/125, g(r)=r/2-r^2 is decreasing and
   g(49/125)=1323/31250=2/47-319/1468750.
   Indeed g(a)-g(r)=(r-a)*(r+a-1/2), with 2a-1/2=71/250>0.
   This covers the entire near-2/5 region with strict slack for part (a).

Under this replacement, a recovered certificate need only cover the remaining
middle interval 8/47<r<49/125, with the endpoint cases assigned to the finite
branches. A closed interval certificate would also suffice. No located data
establish this coverage. If a certificate uses rho instead, its finite domains
and endpoint overlap must be translated; balanced blow-up preserves r exactly.
The root error does not fix an unknown or uncovered density domain.
