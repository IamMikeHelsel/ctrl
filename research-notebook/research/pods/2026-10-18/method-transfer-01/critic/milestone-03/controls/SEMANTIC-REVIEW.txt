Read in full: RecoveryPeak.lean and RecoveryPeakWitness.lean. Rechecked the
actual IsTrajectory, linear comparison, recovered_upper_flow and FirstStop
interfaces from copied dependencies. This note does not replace replay results.

The supplied physical model is x'=v, v'=a, a'=r(q-a). q is the effective
physical acceleration target. A command label alone does not establish it.
The generic theorem has v0,A,T,S>=0 and b,k>0. It permits any real X,B.
Initial conditions are x(0)<=X, 0<=v(0)<=v0, a(0)<=A. The loss rate is
continuous and nonnegative, and loss target <=A, on [0,T]. Recovered rate
is continuous and >=k, and recovered target <=-b, on [0,S] in local time.
One f is continuous on [0,T+S]. Its shifted recovery state is f(T+s), not
an independent trajectory initialized from an assumed box. All coordinates,
including acceleration, agree at the boundary. Derivatives are required on
the open phases; jerk need not exist at the target change.

The acceleration loss bound follows from the integrating-factor comparison
against A. Integrating it gives the upper state
[P,U,A]=[X+v0*T+A*T^2/2, v0+A*T, A] at T. The recovered acceleration upper
flow g=-b+(A+b)exp(-ks) stays above -b. This sign permits a rate floor without
a ceiling. The proof compares a'-g'+r(a-g), not the false inequality
 a'<=-k(a+b) for arbitrary a below -b. Velocity and position order follow
by integration. No cube, physical endpoint or physical path bound is supplied.

The generic hEnd and hPeak are analytical certificate premises for the
explicit heldSolution from [P,U,A]. hPeak includes s=0, hence P<=B. Because
v0,A,T>=0, this also bounds the entire preceding loss phase by B. The theorem
then derives a whole-horizon physical path bound and nonpositive terminal
velocity, and applies the first-zero result. It proves velocity strictly
positive before tau and position <=B through tau. S=0 and initial-zero cases
are permitted; the conclusion may return tau=0. No root uniqueness is needed.
