C6 DOES NOT IMPLY A ONE-SIDED ACCELERATION DERIVATIVE AT THE OLD JOIN
18 October 2026. Analytic counterexample, not a Lean-checked theorem.

Set T=1/20, A=-3/5 and e=1/100. On 0<=t<T let z=T-t and
 a(t)=A+e*z*sin(log z),     a(T)=A.
Then a is continuous through T and, on the open loss phase,
 a'(t)=-e*(sin(log z)+cos(log z)).
Take rLoss=8 and qLoss(t)=a(t)+a'(t)/8. At the phase endpoints
assign any legal target value, for example A. The open-phase ODE holds.

Since |a-A|<=e*T=1/2000 and |a'|<=2e=1/50,
 |qLoss-A|<=1/2000+1/400=3/1000.
Thus qLoss lies in [-603/1000,-597/1000], inside C6's allowed
[-61/50,1/10]. The initial acceleration also lies in C6's initial box.
Choose v(0)=2/5 and x(0)=1/1000. Define v by integrating the continuous
a, and x by integrating v. This gives the required continuous full state
and the classical position/velocity derivatives on the open loss phase.

After T attach the exact constant-rate lag flow with rBrake=8 and
qBrake=-6/5, starting from the full state at T. This gives C6's recovered
open-phase ODE, rate continuity and all target bounds through .530.
The acceleration is continuous at T. The supplied C6 class permits this
example because it does not require continuity of qLoss or a derivative
at the join.

The left difference quotient at T is
 [a(T-h)-a(T)]/(-h)=-e*sin(log h).
Along h_n=exp(-2*pi*n) it is zero. Along
 h'_n=exp(pi/2-2*pi*n) it is -e. For sufficiently large n both sequences
are positive and below T, and both tend to zero. The two limits differ.
There is no left acceleration derivative at T.

This refutes automatic promotion of C6 to a global two-sided-channel
Execution at the old recovery join. It does not refute C6 or C8's narrowed
proposal. C8's new sample/dispatch interval lies strictly inside the
recovered classical domain, where derivative restriction is valid.
check.py checks the exact bounds. The trigonometric and limiting argument
above is reviewed mathematical reasoning, not numerical evidence.
