Statement review
Let Y carry positive vertex masses of total y>1/2, with simple triangle-free
adjacency. The neighborhood N_Y(v) is independent. Thus s_v counts each edge
incident to that neighborhood once, and j-s_v is the remaining edge cost.
Both proposed profiles have selected mass 1/2 and values in [0,1]. Their
displayed costs follow by expanding the actual edge objective. The branches
meet at d_v=y-1/2. Zero degree uses the first branch. The y=1/2 case is separate.

With v drawn with probability w_v/y, finite sum interchange gives
E[d_v]=2j/y and E[s_v]=E[d_v^2]. If all candidate costs exceed R, each s_v is
strictly below psi(d_v). Hence E[psi(d_v)-d_v^2]>0. An affine majorant with
mean C<=0 contradicts this. No regularity assumption or Jensen step is needed.
The certificate's domain d>=0 is stronger than the actual degree domain.

At the supplied y,j, independent coefficient comparison checks both completed
squares exactly. C<0 and S<0, so the majorant holds on both entire branches.
The derivative jumps upward at the join. Global concavity is false; it cannot
replace the majorant argument. The regular-profile cost is not needed here.

The six-cluster example has positive rational masses, an exact finite even
blowup order, the stated x,q,j, and an independent half of mass 1/2. Its true
minimum half cost is zero. Its chosen cut is not a maximum cut. It refutes
the idea that bare x,q,j realizability will remove the scalar obstruction;
it does not realize the scalar beta as the graph optimum or satisfy a claim
that this particular cut is optimal.

Independent evidence
Three scripts ran from fresh copies in replay/: preflight.py, followed by
point_moment_screen.py, and neighborhood_screen.py. All three result files are
byte-identical to their frozen inputs. The builder's finite screen covers
6,228 labelled triangle-free graphs through six vertices and 3,000 seeded
weighted C5/C7/Petersen tests. No counterexample appeared. This is finite
evidence only, not proof of the regular-profile majorant.

independent_check.py uses separate code and exact fractions. It checks all
polynomial coefficients, the six-cluster integer realization, and 439 labelled
triangle-free graphs through five vertices with unequal rational weights.
It directly computes each selected profile's mass and edge cost, both moment
identities, both branches, zero degree, and a branch-boundary example. On a
triangle the claimed neighborhood formula fails, as expected; this checks
that triangle-freeness is a material premise. Results are in
independent-results.json. No external package or proof kernel is needed for
these small checks. The general statement is supported by the argument above,
not by finite enumeration.

Prior-work overlap changes the novelty description
The construction F_v already occurs as one feasible endpoint of the local
source sparse_halves_simple.tex, lemma onestep (lines about 490–545).
Normalize Y weights by y, set alpha=1/(2y), delta=d_v/y, and use direction
1_N-delta. The negative endpoint radius is
  min(alpha/(1-delta), (1-alpha)/delta).
Its two cases give exactly the two F_v formulas. Our independent code checks
this identity on every tested profile. The source's treatment also includes
an arbitrary linear term and a barycentric comparison of both endpoints.
The candidate addition here is the affine moment certificate and uniform
composition with the old envelope. The construction itself is reuse.

The external literature also uses weighted halves and neighborhood-based
profiles. Norin–Yepremyan, arXiv:1311.5818v2, Section 2 and Lemma 2.1, defines
weighted half mass and rounds the uniform case. Razborov,
arXiv:2104.09406v2, Lemma 4.1, uses a fractional half on a neighborhood
complement. These targeted readings do not establish whether the proposed
affine certificate is new. No complete literature review was done.
