18 October 2026. Preparation only; no Lean or hybrid theorem acceptance.

Verdict

the added physical bounds. The original sample-gap sentence needs a coverage
condition. Root's optional 0.5 ms periodic repair supplies one and resolves
the worst-case sample-plus-arrival horizon. C5 is still a candidate proof;
this gate neither accepts C5 nor assigns a new builder route.

Why the original C1 is insufficient
For example, v0=0,a0=.1 with constant loss target .1 has first zero at time
zero and positive acceleration throughout the next millisecond. It meets
the concrete C1 upper premises but cannot supply the negative acceleration
guard. Alternatively, v0=.4,a0=qLoss=-100 gives a zero at .004 during loss;
its acceleration is outside the guard. C1 allows that initial value and
effective loss target. These show why the new lower bounds and positive
initial speed must be visible, rather than hidden in a detector corollary.

Sampling gap and optional repair
An ordered sequence with gaps at most 1 ms need not reach the window.
The sequence sigma_n=(1/2000)*(1-1/(n+1)) starts at zero and increases
strictly, with gaps 1/(2000(n+1)(n+2))<=1/4000, yet all samples precede
.0005<tau. A late first sample is another coverage failure. A concrete
finite schedule reaching the required endpoint avoids these issues.

Root's proposed refinement uses physical sample times n/2000 seconds.
N=ceil(2000*tau) gives sigma=N/2000 in [tau,tau+.0005]. A finite sample
list through .530 suffices; no infinite schedule theorem is needed for this
one existence claim. Exact grid coincidence at tau is allowed by the guard
proof. A strictly later grid point also fits the conservative .530 budget.
With delay D<=.0005, sigma+D<=tau+.001<=.530. The earlier 1 ms sample
window alone could need pending braking through .5305 instead.

After selecting sigma, absolute samples are sigma+n/2000. Local samples
are n/2000 after subtracting sigma from ALL physical times and stamps.
A fixed latency D gives absolute dispatches sigma+D+n/2000 and local
dispatches D+n/2000. Incoming age is at most .0005+D<=.001; outgoing
age is D. This supplies strict dispatch order. Independently varying delays
in [0,.0005] can tie neighboring dispatches and need an additional strict
ordering or coalescing rule. A 1 ms period plus .5 ms latency gives 1.5 ms
incoming age and fails the C5 contract. No retained sample is renewed.

Remaining hybrid gaps
A supplied sequence of sensor-box readings has a finite first qualifying
index no later than the guaranteed post-zero index. That first index can
precede zero. Protect its physical prefix and pending interval, not just the
late holding tube. The .142 prefix bound plus subsequent negative velocity
can protect continued braking up to the guaranteed sample; the actual
pending interval and later controller still need their own proof.

The continued-braking execution is a counterfactual after an earlier switch.
A real hybrid argument must construct a shared prefix/state, causal detection
and arrival, a single selected center, and a valid post-dispatch execution.
It must justify prefix agreement (or the appropriate uniqueness argument)
and avoid making a detector hit an input premise to a liveness claim.
Old queued readings cannot acquire nonnegative local times by being renamed;
the post-handoff schedule must retain or reject them consistently with C5.
The finite braking horizon does not supply existence for the later hold.

This gate supports a continued-braking reachability test after C5 review.
It does not establish hybrid liveness, arbitrary-data existence, C1-to-C5
composition, hardware safety or novelty. No new Lean module was built.
