METHOD-SELECTION PROTOCOL — SHORT INDEPENDENT REVIEW
18 October 2026. Bounded protocol review; no new literature audit.

Verdict: suitable for an exploratory record and a later bounded paired trial.
It is not evidence that this selection policy causes discoveries or outperforms
expert same-field search. The copied corrected protocol says this clearly.
No strategy-superiority, corpus-wide success rate or publication claim is accepted.

The strongest features are the explicit source-to-target map, assumption filter,
ordinary baseline, cheap falsifier, stop rule and separate independent acceptance.
The protocol does not treat unusual subject labels as evidence. It preserves
failed routes and separates a known theorem's formalization from new mathematics.
The corpus's 372 families and ten selected summaries cannot supply a denominator
or causal discovery history. Abstract-level source reading is disclosed; I did
not independently audit the three cited studies for this short review.

Four controls matter before a future trial:
1. Freeze the baseline, primary benefit measure and cost rule before seeing
   outputs. Reject gains that strengthen premises or weaken conclusions.
   A label-blind first assessment helps, but recognizable methods can still
   reveal the strategy. Do not claim full blinding or absence of training leakage.
2. Count bridge search, source validation, all failed attempts and independent
   review as effort. Construction time alone favors an expensive preselected
   bridge. Keep unknown human/model effort unknown; shared account use cannot
   be assigned to ideas without measurement.
3. Treat E1/E2/E4 as one dependent lineage and C1/C2 as another. Later trials
   inherit earlier insights and are not independent successes. Freeze future
   cards and append outcomes with timestamps. The initial case ledger's active
   statuses are historical; do not backfill a clean pre-test history after results.
4. Match or randomize worker roles and repeat paired trials across new problem
   families before attributing a gain to the selection rule. Keep context,
   allowed references, tool access, budgets and stopping rules comparable.
   The treatment may contain a bridge; an answer or target proof smuggled in
   that context is leakage. Record contamination rather than silently dropping it.

Root incorporated these controls after the first short review message. It also
corrected the C1 baseline description: the older proof had a rate ceiling and
assumed the delay acceleration bound, while C1 derives that bound. The numerical
comparison is useful, but it is not a perfectly identical-domain experiment.

One useful scope limit remains: cheap decisive tests favor accessible short
routes. The speculative queue partly addresses this, but this policy should be
evaluated as bounded research triage, not as a general theory of deep discovery.
A failed bridge can still be a useful outcome if it removes a real uncertainty.
No additional software or full literature review is needed for the current gate.
