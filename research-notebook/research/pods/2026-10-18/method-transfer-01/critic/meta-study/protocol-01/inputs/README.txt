STUDYING WHICH MATHEMATICAL BRIDGES ARE WORTH TRYING
18 October 2026. Working protocol, not an evaluated discovery system.

Question
Can selecting methods by the structure of a missing proof step make our
research more useful per unit of effort than selecting by subject or habit?
An unusual combination is a candidate explanation, not an outcome measure.

Mike's proposed contribution is the choice of bridge: find where one field
has a tool for an obstruction in another, then make that connection precise.
We can study that choice. We have not yet shown that our selection rule is
better than ordinary expert search or that it predicts new discoveries.

The unit of study
One candidate is a source mechanism mapped to one target obstruction. It
must name the mathematical objects on both sides. "Geometry plus controls"
is not a candidate. "A finite active-branch derivative bound implies entry
into this weighted state box under the original sampling clock" is one.

Each card must state, before its next test:
1. The exact target statement and the obstruction in the present proof.
2. The source statement and its proof status, with a pinned source.
3. The map between objects, including units, normalizations and quantifiers.
4. Every source assumption that still needs a target proof.
5. The ordinary same-field baseline under the same assumptions.
6. A concrete falsifier and the smallest next experiment.
7. The promised benefit, the time cap, and the stop or repair condition.
8. Whether the idea was known before the corpus review or reconstructed later.

Selection rule
First reject invalid translations: wrong quantifiers, an unavailable clock,
unproved positivity, a changed field characteristic, or a missing operator.
Next prefer a candidate that resolves the present obstruction and has a cheap
decisive test. Compare its possible gain with the existing baseline. Use the
distance between subject labels only as a secondary clue. Extra abstraction
can cost more than it gives back.

Keep one well-specified speculative candidate in the queue when useful. Do
not allocate it a worker until it has a concrete test and a worker is free.
This prevents the selection rule from discarding every deep idea merely
because an elementary improvement is easier to verify.

What counts as progress
Record these separately:
- A stronger proved statement under unchanged assumptions.
- A useful numerical improvement with a complete exact certificate.
- A counterexample that closes a route or identifies a missing assumption.
- A formalization of a known theorem or a reduction in its trusted inputs.
- A clearer obstruction without a new theorem.
- A failed or timed-out attempt.
Theorem count, subject count, sampled success and narrative appeal are not
progress measures. Hardware evidence and publication novelty have separate
gates. A formal proof of a known fact can be useful without being new math.

How to evaluate the method
The current round is an exploratory case series. E1, E2, C1 and the rejected
analogies have already influenced us. They cannot become a clean held-out
benchmark by relabeling them. E4 and C2 have pre-test cards, but no randomized
baseline allocation; they still do not support a causal advantage claim.

For a later bounded paired trial, freeze a new subproblem and its context.
Freeze the baseline, primary benefit and cost rule before any trial output.
Give one fresh worker the strongest same-field baseline sources. Give another
the same context plus a selected structural bridge. Match model settings,
tools, time and compute caps. Randomize worker roles or repeat paired trials
before attributing an advantage to the selection strategy. Log every attempt,
including failures. Keep the
two searches separate until the comparison is frozen. Compare their actual
statements with an independent reviewer who does not see the strategy labels
at first. Reject any gain bought by a weaker conclusion or stronger premise.
Do not call this blind to model training: prior mathematical knowledge may
be in either model. If the bridge leaks through the supplied context, label
that trial contaminated and keep it out of a clean strategy comparison.

Treat E1/E2/E4 as one dependent lineage and C1/C2 as another. They are not
five independent samples. Use more than one problem family before drawing
a general conclusion. Keep
families and paper versions together when making data splits. The released
372-family corpus is a selected set with mixed verification and corrections.
It is not a denominator for success rates. Its ten public reasoning summaries
do not reveal every attempted route or establish causal discovery histories.

Costs
Save actual command elapsed time, worker limits, compute jobs and review work.
Charge bridge selection, source validation and failed attempts to effort too.
Account usage is shared, so do not invent per-idea token or credit costs.
Time to independent acceptance matters more than time to a first draft.
If exact human or model effort was not measured, mark it unknown.

Current case interpretation
E1: graph moments and an affine majorant gave a written conditional 0.02586
bound. Its construction was already in sparse-half work. Source inspiration
is documented; superiority of cross-field search is not established.
E2: a direct same-field comparison gave the stronger conditional 0.02584
bound. This is an essential control against celebrating the first new idea.
E4: a relaxed moment condition has a developing exact 0.0252 certificate.
Full written and independent review remain open at this protocol's creation.
C1: retaining a known exponential transient improves a useful clearance
certificate. The older baseline also assumed a delay-phase acceleration cap; C1 derives
that cap from the loss-phase ODE. Standard comparison theory supplied the
result; this is not
evidence that an imported recent theorem was necessary.
C2: active affine branches are being tested for finite entry under sampled
hold. The chemical theorem itself is not applied. The target drift must be
proved separately. Development compilation is not independent acceptance.
Rejected cases: offline vector balancing does not give adversarial causal
control; characteristic-two cancellation does not transfer to positive real
matrix sums; field reconstruction does not choose GU's missing operators.

Prior work that shapes this protocol
Davies et al. (2021) used learned relationships and attribution to guide
mathematicians toward connections between knot invariants. This supports
testing intermediate quantities across representations, not assuming that
subject distance predicts discovery. We have read the abstract and indexed
method description; the complete paper has not been audited here.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04086-x

Yang et al., LeanDojo (2023), treats premise selection as a practical theorem-
proving problem. It includes accessible-premise analysis, hard negatives and
evaluation with previously unused premises. Those distinctions motivate our
assumption filter and data-split rules. Its benchmark is not a test of our
cross-field research policy. Reading depth: proceedings abstract.
https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/4441469427094f8873d0fecb0c4e1cee-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html

Romera-Paredes et al., FunSearch (2023 online; 2024 issue), pairs generated
programs with a systematic evaluator. It seeks checkable constructions and
algorithms. This supports separating proposal from acceptance and searching
over interpretable generators. It does not establish which fields we should
combine. Reading depth: abstract and indexed method description.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6

Deliverable for this round
Keep the method map, explicit rejected bridges, source provenance, pre-test
cards and independent outcomes. Propose a small paired trial only after the
current useful proofs are reviewed. Do not turn this protocol into another
large software project or claim a publishable meta-result from this case set.
