# Problem
Fix an even integer $p\ge2$, a real number $\alpha>0$, a random function
$\theta:\{-1,1\}^p\to\mathbb R$, and a real random variable $h$. Assume
$$
\mathbb E\max_{s\in\{-1,1\}^p}|\theta(s)|+\mathbb E|h|<\infty.
$$
Require the interaction hypotheses of Panchenko--Talagrand (1.2)--(1.4): for all
$s=(s_1,\ldots,s_p)$,
$$
e^{\theta(s)}=a\left(1+b\prod_{\ell=1}^p f_\ell(s_\ell)\right),\qquad
\left|b\prod_{\ell=1}^p f_\ell(s_\ell)\right|<1\quad\text{almost surely},
$$
where the $f_\ell:\{-1,1\}\to\mathbb R$ are independent copies of one random function, $b$ is
independent of them, and $\mathbb E[(-b)^n]\ge0$ for every integer $n\ge1$. The positive random
variable $a$ may depend on the other factorization variables.
For each $N$, let $M_N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\alpha N)$, let $(i_{\ell,k})$ be independent
uniform indices in $\{1,\ldots,N\}$, let $(\theta_k)$ be independent copies of $\theta$, and let
$(h_i)$ be independent copies of $h$. These families are mutually independent; indices are sampled
with replacement. For $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^N$, set
$$
-H_N(\sigma)=\sum_{k=1}^{M_N}\theta_k(\sigma_{i_{1,k}},\ldots,\sigma_{i_{p,k}})+\sum_{i=1}^N
h_i\sigma_i,\qquad
F_N=\frac1N\mathbb E\log\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^N}e^{-H_N(\sigma)}.
$$
All model parameters and disorder laws are fixed as $N\to\infty$; any inverse-temperature parameter
is absorbed into $\theta$ and $h$.
Define $q_x(s)=e^{xs}/(2\cosh x)$ for $s\in\{-1,1\}$ and $x\in\mathbb R$, and the cavity quantities
$$
C_\theta(x_1,\ldots,x_p)=\sum_{s\in\{-1,1\}^p}e^{\theta(s)}\prod_{\ell=1}^p q_{x_\ell}(s_\ell),
$$
$$
U_\theta(x_1,\ldots,x_{p-1};\varepsilon)=\log\sum_{s\in\{-1,1\}^{p-1}}e^{\theta(s_1,\ldots,s_{p-1}, ⌋
\varepsilon)}\prod_{\ell=1}^{p-1}q_{x_\ell}(s_\ell).
$$
These are $\langle\mathcal E\rangle_x$ and $U$ from (1.5)--(1.7) of the cited paper.
Here is a complete definition of its finite-level trial functional. Write $\mathcal M_1=\mathcal
P(\mathbb R)$ and $\mathcal M_{j+1}=\mathcal P(\mathcal M_j)$, where $\mathcal P$ denotes Borel
probability measures and each space carries its usual weak topology. Fix an integer $r\ge1$,
$\zeta\in\mathcal M_{r+1}$, and $0<m_1<\cdots<m_r<1$. For each message label $\omega$,
independently generate a chain
$$
\eta_0^\omega\sim\zeta,\qquad

1 From structural obstacles to marked hierarchies
The reasoning begins with the gap between the interpolation upper bound of Panchenko and Tala-
grand [5] and the desired lower bound. Panchenko’s finite-RSB analysis [ 4] supplies a model for iden-
tifying the spin distribution, but pair-overlap ultrametricity alone does not determine the multioverlaps
needed in a diluted system. Initial checks look for a counterexample in the assumptions: a nonintegrable
factorization constant, asymmetric fields, interactions close to the positivity boundary , or messages tend-

Enriched Gaussian fields, as in Mourrat and Panchenko [3], and the Hamilton–Jacobi viewpoint of Mour-
rat [2], suggest auxiliary hierarchies. Poisson perturbations motivated by the multioverlap identities in
Barbier and Panchenko [1] offer more direct tests. The Bayesian setting does not transfer automatically:
posterior-sampled observations and fractionally weighted observations have different laws.
The investigation therefore works directly with iterated power means and their tilted path transitions.
A marked perturbation can specify where replicas branch; expanding the change of density gives signed
coeﬀicients for extending an old replica tree. For two replicas, the resulting identity suggests approxi-
mate invariance under replacing a child when adjacent exponents are close. Higher products are harder:
their coeﬀicients alternate in sign, dummy anchors cancel, and conditional projections cannot simply be
inserted as arbitrary test functions.
The target becomes conditional decorrelation of sitewise product vectors, followed by concentration
of ordinary multioverlaps. Fresh subtrees isolate useful conditional moments, but recovering a whole
prescribed shape requires division by products of small exponent gaps. Coarse partitions and equal-
exponent refinements repeatedly encounter the same problem: available continuity errors are too large
after that division.
2 Shifting branching depths
Further pinning attempts fail to reconcile their observation law with the cavity comparison. An exact
purity claim also proves too strong: hidden Poisson–Dirichlet clustering inside a strict exponent jump
can retain nontrivial overlap variation. Factorial-polynomial expansions and a cancellation using two
copies expose squared covariance terms, but do not yet turn the signed identities into the desired ordi-
nary covariance estimate.
The change is to shift every internal branching depth of the target tree by one level. These shifted
splits avoid the old tree’s branching depths. Each new branching vertex must then pay a small fresh-
divergence factor, matching the small factor in the prescribed shape’s normalization. The proposed

3 Checking the identities and closing the comparison
The final stage returns repeatedly to whether a target subtree remains correctly distributed when its
anchor already belongs to an old tree. Marginalizing the unused paths preserves the prescribed in-
dependent transitions. This permits conditional covariance estimates even when the observable being
tested is correlated with the extension.
The coeﬀicient calculation is checked at two levels and reorganized as a positive squared-difference
identity. Distinct new branching vertices are charged to distinct fresh divergences, including when their
depths coincide. This is the point of the shift: after normalization, the absolute coeﬀicient bounds remain
controlled. Along unary stretches, bounded vector martingales have bounded total squared increments,
so the averaged error from moving a depth by one tends to zero.
Another concern is whether varying the small insertion parameter silently changes the background
measure. The calculation instead treats each value as a hypothetical extra factor in the same fixed back-
ground. Poisson removal supplies that background, cancellation removes the anchor factor, and uni-
form analytic estimates permit coeﬀicient extraction. Polarized marks then select the required tree con-
straints.
The conditional-decorrelation induction and the marker-anchor variance argument are checked to-
gether. Proper child groups are replaced by conditional means, and the resulting three-copy estimate
controls the normalized squared conditional covariance. Ordinary multioverlap concentration follows
by comparing a fresh copy with the original shape. In the cavity comparison, fresh uniformly sampled
sites give empirical pattern masses; the trial hierarchy uses their expectations. Finite cutoffs leave only
finitely many such comparisons, while nonregular shapes and analytic tails contribute vanishing errors.
T o return from bounded disorder to the stated first-moment assumptions, the construction subtracts
the all-plus interaction value and truncates the normalized interaction and field. Uniform free-energy
and trial-functional error bounds allow the truncations to be removed. The final response claims that
the resulting lower comparison matches the interpolation upper bound, giving the existence of the limit
and the infimum over finite hierarchies.
