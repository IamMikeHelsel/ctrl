WHAT THE REPORTED SEARCHES CAN TEACH US
18 October 2026. Exploratory source study; independent method review pending.

Main finding
The useful unit is a missing lemma, not a pair of subject names. The selected
reports repeatedly describe a promising change of representation followed by
failure to preserve one needed property. Examples include independence, rank,
positivity, a common clock, or a uniform error bound. This suggests a way to
choose our next tests. It does not show that our method improves success rates.

Coverage and trust
The pinned catalogue contains 372 families and 719 manuscript links. Its ten
published, abridged process summaries are a much smaller evidence set. We saved
all ten with Git-blob and SHA256 checks. The source set has 202 PDF pages.
We read four short summaries in full and selected pages of the other six.
READING-COVERAGE.json records exact full-page coverage and introductory excerpts.
Downloading or indexing a paper does not mean we read or checked its proof.
The fetch-time reading fields in source-manifest.json are historical; the
separate coverage ledger records the subsequent reading.

These are publisher-reported retrospectives. They are selected and abridged.
They contain proposed conclusions and repeated self-checks. We have not
independently verified these ten headline mathematical claims. A stated final
success, or failure to find an objection, is not an acceptance verdict.
We do not call this a study of 372 verified new solutions, and do not estimate
how often an uncommon combination succeeds from this selected set.

The following short case notes summarize the cited selected pages. The final
sentence of each case is our proposed use, not the source authors' finding.

1. Basic semidefinite threshold / complexity and coding
Pages 2,5,6,11,12. The report contrasts stable nonlinear decoding with linear
detection, then seeks a hardness reduction. Several earlier approaches lose
global consistency, shared normalization, or a dimension-independent constant.
The final proposed reduction depends on uniform slice bounds and matched pair
moments. Its conclusion is not independently accepted here.
Our use: graph parameters that pass a scalar test still need a graph witness
or a proved realizability constraint. Local consistency alone is a poor gate.

2. Free group factors / geometry, free probability and dynamical flows
Pages 2,6,7,8. Width arguments fail when limits do not preserve derivative or
rank control. The reported later route uses small perturbations, long words
and a diagonal construction. It separates convergence of generators from
generation of the ambient algebra. Trace preservation and limiting generation
remain essential parts of the proposed conclusion.
Our use: convergence of individual robot command segments would not establish
the behavior of their concatenation. Test the junction and retained history.

3. Irrationality exponent / number theory and algebraic geometry
Pages 29,30,39,40. The report seeks interpolation rank through geometric
positivity while controlling arithmetic denominators. Changing a rectangular
index set to a weighted simplex is a proposed way to improve competing costs.
Liouville-type tests expose overbroad arguments. The geometric hypotheses,
nonvanishing and determinant estimates are not verified by this reading.
Our use: compare all costs in a proposed transfer. A smaller analytic bound
is no gain if the needed certificate or normalization costs more than it saves.

4. Direct finiteness / coding, finite fields and group constructions
Pages 10,11,20. The reported proposal uses local polynomial cancellation on
lines and a global defect after removing interpolation points. It then needs
group embeddings and a matrix-to-scalar construction. The cellular-automaton
consequence explicitly takes the earlier one-sided-inverse claim as input.
Our use: a downstream theorem can be easy while its upstream premise remains
unproved. Characteristic-two cancellation supplies no real GU operator law.

5. Diluted spin glasses / probability, hierarchies and cavity arguments
Pages 1-5. The report warns that shared randomness can adapt to sampled sites.
It proposes shifted branching levels, martingale estimates and careful limits
to recover the needed decorrelation. A Bayesian analogy does not preserve the
observation law automatically. The final lower comparison is a proposed result.
Our use: do not replace bounded, possibly dependent sensor errors by independent
noise because a probabilistic bound would be smaller. Preserve the actual law.

6. Ordinary correlations / number theory, graphs and circuit ideas
Pages 1-7. The report moves between arithmetic correlations and weighted divisor
graphs. It separates prime families to retain signed cancellation and explores
bounded-independence tools for a finite-residue comparison. Removing a factor
too early can destroy the cancellation. Uniform losses and conditional sieve
estimates remain central dependencies of the proposed all-scale result.
Our use: preserve joint constraints until the step that uses them. A marginal
moment calculation can erase the graph information needed below .025.

7. Arithmetic progressions / additive combinatorics and structured sampling
Pages 12,24,33,38. The report distinguishes the number of coordinates from the
precision needed for each coordinate. Narrow regions and repeated refinement
can consume the expected density gain. The later proposal uses layered budgets,
buffered regions and ordered parameter choices. Its new transfer estimates
are not established by our reading of the summary.
Our use: budget all sample age from the original measurement. A new interface
must not silently give the controller a fresh delay allowance.

8. Vlasov-Maxwell / geometric events and signed differential estimates
Pages 1-6. The reported approach combines terms before taking absolute values,
then counts directional events to bound occupation times. An initially chosen
time window fails to preserve the needed speed condition. The proposed repair
shortens that window. The known continuation theorem does not itself prove the
new signed estimate or repair a mismatch in regularity assumptions.
Our use: separate a stopping-bound lemma from the existence and continuation
premises needed to apply it. Event times must satisfy the same physical model.

9. Heisenberg magnetization / combinatorics, algebra and diffusion
Pages 1-6. The report uses permutation and determinant descriptions to seek
conditional moment estimates, then geometric routing and dissipation. It
explicitly separates a conditional first moment from a complete path law.
Time-dependent transport cannot simply be replaced by inverse diffusion.
The claimed magnetized state still depends on the proposed estimates.
Our use: a sampled mean, or separate scalar responses, cannot justify a full
time-dependent multi-axis model. Such a transfer needs its own statement.

10. Mahler / convex geometry, entropy and matrix inequalities
Pages 17,34,39,41, plus the introductory excerpt. Reported tests reject scalar
criteria on positive-semidefinite cones. Later arguments retain correlated
profiles, matrix order and commutator costs. Uniform scalar inequalities remain
dependencies of the proposed general conclusion. We do not infer a verified
general theorem from these self-audits.
Our use: test the reduction on a structured counterexample before optimizing
its scalar constants. E6 now applies this principle to cut selection.

What changes in our work
Use the following sequence for each candidate. Name the failed target step.
Write the source lemma and all its hypotheses. Map its objects to ours. List
each property the map must preserve. Give a counterexample test for each
uncertain property. Compare the full cost with the current baseline. Only then
schedule proof or compute work. A summary can suggest a lemma; it cannot be
used as proof that the lemma holds.

Current consequences
- Erdos: E5 has an independently checked .0251 written conditional bound. Its
  arithmetic does not close .02 or establish novelty. E6 tests cut selection
  with an actual graph, instead of another scalar scan. E6 review is pending.
- Controls: C3 passed independent copied-source review. C4 tests one missing
  original-clock composition. A smaller terminal region is useful only if its
  physical tolerances and clearance fit the task. Neither result is hardware.
- GU: no operator is supplied by analogy. Missing source definitions remain
  an obstruction. No new GU route follows from these ten summaries alone.

Experiment still needed
To test whether this selection process helps, compare it with a fair baseline
under equal task information, model, time and review budgets. Register targets
before assigning methods. Count a rejected false hypothesis as a useful result,
but report it separately from a proved useful theorem. Include literature,
failed attempts and reviewer costs. Blind the reviewer to the assignment where
possible. Related E1-E6 results are one dependent lineage, not six independent
successes. The current retrospective cannot support a causal performance claim.
