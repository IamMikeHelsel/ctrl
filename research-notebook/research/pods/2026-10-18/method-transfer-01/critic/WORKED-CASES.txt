197 — A coding-inspired cancellation, with the arithmetic exposed
Reported search: the summary identifies quantum-LDPC multiplication and affine
line codes as inspiration. The underlying pairing section says it uses no
coding theorem from those works. It proves the needed interpolation and line
identities directly. This is evidence of a specific connection; it does not
measure the rarity or causal importance of cross-field work in the corpus.

Inspected source argument: low-degree interpolants distinguish deleted points
in a finite affine space. Their pairwise products have degree at most q-2 and
sum to zero on every full line. The deleted values contribute I, leaving -I
on the retained points. Regularity and girth are separate geometric hypotheses.
Characteristic two permits even regularity D to be zero as a field scalar.
We checked this section, not the full existence/embedding construction.

Transfer rejection: real positive outer products cannot implement this same
zero-sum line mechanism. The trace of a sum of real xx^T is a sum of squared
norms. If it is zero, all terms vanish. Also, summing a D-regular system of
line identities gives D times the total; a positive integer D is invertible
over R or C. The source's characteristic-two escape is absent.

Exact toy: in GF(4), the sums of z^k for k=0,1,2 are zero; k=3 sums to 1.
The real constant sum over four points is 4. This checks the degree/field
boundary only. It is not a toy proof of the group-ring counterexample.

GU implication: first specify the normalized operators, brackets, projections,
domains and variation conventions. Existing real/complex Clifford algebra does
not acquire a characteristic-two cancellation by analogy. The source action
exists; the missing normalized operator data remain the gate. No GU proof route
is licensed by this case. A finite matrix identity is the fair first baseline
once those definitions are fixed.

221 — Uniform stability can connect two proofs; averaging has a price
Reported search: the spin-glass summary distinguishes pair overlaps from the
multioverlaps needed later. It uses conditionally fresh marked trees and a
small depth shift to obtain the required averaged comparison. This is more
specific than a claim that statistical physics and probability were combined.

Inspected source argument: reduction:stability couples interaction and field
laws with mean sup-norm errors D,d. It bounds pressure error by alpha D+d and
trial-functional error by alpha(2p-1)D+d, uniformly in size, hierarchy depth
and trial law. A scalar shift cancels a nuisance multiplier. Truncation may
lose factorization, so the lower bound uses hypotheses it still satisfies;
the original law supplies the upper bound. Uniform error bounds justify the
infimum and truncation limits. Separately, depth-continuity uses bounded
martingale energy to give an averaged O(1/L) squared-error estimate.

Transfer: before simplifying a recovery envelope, preserve the exact envelope
and quantify the cost of the simplification under the same initial/input set.
This is a workflow analogy. The spin-glass stability lemma does not apply to a
plant merely because both use an error bound. Our controls baseline is the
already proved exact exponential envelope and its conservative simple bound.

Rejection: averaged depth continuity cannot certify worst-case sensor latency
or every switching time. An exact bounded martingale is zero until its last
step, then reveals a fair sign. For L=100, average squared increment is 1/100
and maximum is 1. Our script checks these numbers. Turning that average into
a deterministic per-step bound is false.

Missing bridge: a plant-specific uniform comparison under the real clock,
sensor and effective-target assumptions. Next test: retain the exponential
term, compare on a fixed operating set, and test limits such as zero initial
speed. This direct route outranks imported hierarchy machinery for this scalar
problem. First zero velocity still does not mean permanent rest.
