Why the graph argument works
The identity E[d]=2mu follows from the definition of edge mass. Symmetry of
adjacency and finite sum interchange give E[s]=E[d^2], without triangle-free
assumptions. Strictly positive normalized weights and looplessness give d<1.
The proof derives nonemptiness from the weight sum rather than assuming that
an empty type has a normalized weight.

For a two-level profile a+b*1_N(v), the actual edge cost expands to
 a^2*mu+a*b*s_v.
Triangle-freeness makes N(v) independent and removes the b^2 term. This is the
substantive graph condition. It is not supplied as an assumed cost formula.

The low profile is zero on N(v) and alpha/(1-d_v) elsewhere. Its branch gives
unit capacities, exact mean alpha, and cost alpha^2*(mu-s_v)/(1-d_v)^2.
The high profile is 1-(1-alpha)/d_v on N(v) and one elsewhere. Its branch gives
unit capacities, exact mean alpha, and cost mu-(1-alpha)*s_v/d_v.
The low denominator is positive. The high branch implies d_v>1-alpha>0.
At d_v=1-alpha, both definitions agree and the proof uses the low branch.
At d_v=0, only the low branch is used.

If every candidate cost exceeded T, positive denominator arithmetic and the
stated scalar bounds would give, for every vertex,
 s_v-d_v^2 < L*(d_v-2mu).
Positive weighted summation is strict, since the type is nonempty. Both sums
are zero by the graph moments. This gives 0<0. The inequality direction is
correct; no circular invariant or assumed existence conclusion is used.

Nonvacuity and semantic challenges
The full endpoint is invoked on K2 with weights (1/2,1/2), alpha=4/5,
T=3/20 and L=-1/2. Both scalar certificates are proved over their full required
domains. The explicit high profile (1,3/5) has that mean and nonzero cost.
Separate controls verify the branch boundary at alpha=d=1/2 and the zero-degree
low profile on the one-vertex graph.

The K3 test is an actual counterexample to removing triangle-freeness. With
weights 1/3, alpha=1/2,T=0,L=-2/3, both scalar inequalities hold everywhere:
their differences are -(d-1/3)^2 and -(d-2/3)^2. Every unit profile of mean 1/2
has cost >=1/18, and (1,1/2,0) attains it. The lower bound follows from
nonnegativity of xyz and (1-x)(1-y)(1-z), together with x+y+z=3/2.
This is more than a failed proof tactic.

My four added theorems use K2 with unequal weights (1/3,2/3), then the isolated
vertex. They establish degrees (2/3,1/3), E[s]=E[d^2]=2/9, and (E[d])^2=16/81.
Thus the actual second moment cannot be replaced by a regular-degree shortcut.
At alpha=2/3, a low boundary candidate has mean 2/3 and cost zero, while the
other root's high candidate has cost 1/9. On the isolated vertex, the high
profile has mean one, so the nonzero-degree premise of high_profile_mean
cannot be dropped. Its false mean-4/5 assertion is an independent rejection.
