E6: LOCAL CUT OPTIMALITY DOES NOT REPAIR THE RETAINED CERTIFICATE
18 October 2026. Written exact argument and rational computation.
Builder submission for independent review. No self-acceptance or novelty claim.

1. Statement tested and result

The following statement is false. For every finite simple triangle-free graph,
every single-vertex-locally-optimal cut X,Y with |X|<=|Y| and at most
(2/47)n^2 internal edges has retained certificate at most (1/40)n^2.
The retained certificate means the minimum of the uniform-Y cost, the existing
completion envelope Q(x)+q on its valid domain, and all neighborhood-profile
costs F_v on Y. It does not mean the best output one could extract from the
whole proof of the completion theorem. Section 6 explains this distinction.

We give a rational weighted support on 28 vertices and its exact finite simple
blowup. Its bad cut is strictly locally optimal, meets the BCL cut budget with
equality, and has retained certificate
  240199/9600000 = 1/40 + 199/9600000 > 1/40.
On this SAME graph the global maximum cut has certificate zero. Its true
fractional-half minimum is zero. Thus this is not a counterexample to the
sparse-half conjecture or any positive universal bound.

2. Conventions and retained constructions

A support is a finite simple graph with positive rational vertex masses w_v
of sum 1. An edge is counted once. For selected masses p_v in [0,w_v] with
sum p_v=1/2, its half cost is sum_{uv in E} p_u p_v. Equivalently the usual
profile is z_v=p_v/w_v in [0,1]. For a cut X,Y let
  x=sum_X w_v, y=sum_Y w_v, q=sum_{E(X)} w_u w_v, j=sum_{E(Y)} w_u w_v.
Orient x<=y, so y>=1/2. Put c=1/(2y). Uniform selection on Y costs c^2 j.

The existing completion theorem supplies Q(x)+q when 1/3<=x<=1/2, where
  Q(x)=3x^2/2-5x/4+9/32   if 1/3<=x<=3/8;
  Q(x)=x(1/2-x)/2         if 3/8<=x<=1/2.
The branches agree at 3/8. Below x=1/3 we omit this term. This is a feasible
upper bound, not an asserted cost of every completion procedure.

For y>1/2 put b=y-1/2. For each v in Y put
  d_v=sum_{u in N_Y(v)} w_u,
  s_v=sum_{u in N_Y(v)} w_u d_u.
Since the graph is triangle-free, N_Y(v) is independent. Its incident edge
mass is s_v. The reused neighborhood profiles have costs
  F_v=(j-s_v)/(4(y-d_v)^2)  if d_v<=b;
  F_v=j-b*s_v/d_v          if d_v>b.
In the first branch select mass w_u/[2(y-d_v)] on Y minus N_Y(v).
In the second select w_u off the neighborhood and (1-b/d_v)w_u on it.
Set all selected masses on X to zero. Both profiles have mass 1/2 and remain
within the vertex capacities. The first denominator has y-d_v>=1/2; the
second uses d_v>b>0. For d_v=0 use only the first branch. At d_v=b>0 both
formulas agree. For y=1/2 the uniform-Y half is enough, and no b denominator
or neighborhood branch is used. The scripts check actual profile costs.

The retained certificate is the minimum of these available numerical bounds.
The neighborhood construction is already the negative endpoint in Sarid's
one-step lemma. E6 adds no new construction or constant.

3. Exact graph

Set y=599/1000, x=401/1000, j=5y^2/48=358801/9600000,
and q=2/47-j=2336353/451200000. There are four X vertices and 24 Y vertices.
Their masses are
  w_0=1/20,
  w_1=20q=2336353/22560000,
  w_2=x/2-1/20=301/2000,
  w_3=x/2-20q=2186927/22560000,
  w_v=y/24=599/24000 for 4<=v<=27.
Every mass is positive. Their sum is 1. Both pairs {0,2} and {1,3} have
mass x/2. Both Y groups L={4,...,15}, T={16,...,27} have mass y/2.

The complete edge list is specified by these disjoint rules:
  (0,1);
  (4+i,16+((i+t) mod 12)) for 0<=i<12 and 0<=t<5;
  all edges from {0,2} to T;
  all edges from {1,3} to L.
There are 1+60+48=109 edges. There are no other edges. The global color
classes A={0,2} union L and B={1,3} union T each have mass 1/2. Every edge
joins A to B. Thus the graph is bipartite and triangle-free. The verifier
also checks simplicity and absence of a common neighbor on every edge.

Take the bad cut X={0,1,2,3}, Y={4,...,27}. Its internal X cost is
w_0 w_1=q. The Y subgraph is 5-regular on equal masses y/24, so its cost is
60(y/24)^2=5y^2/48=j. Hence q+j=2/47 exactly.

4. Strict local optimality and certificate failure

For a positive-mass vertex v, moving that vertex to the other cut side cannot
increase crossing edge mass exactly when its internal weighted degree is at
most its external weighted degree. On Y the two degrees are
  internal mu=5y/24=599/4800,
  external x/2=401/2000.
Their difference is 1817/24000>0. On X, vertices 0 and 1 have internal
degrees w_1 and w_0; vertices 2 and 3 have internal degree zero. Every X
vertex has external degree y/2=599/2000. These differences are all positive
and exceed 1817/24000. Thus the cut is strictly locally optimal at all 28
vertices. The verifier checks each degree without relying on symmetry.

For this cut c=500/599. Its uniform cost is
  c^2 j=5/192 > 1/40.
Here x=401/1000>3/8>1/3, so the second completion branch is valid. It gives
  Q(x)+q=56462237/2256000000
         =1/40+62237/2256000000 > 1/40.
Every Y vertex has d_v=mu and s_v=mu^2. Also
  b=99/1000 < mu, with mu-b=619/24000>0.
Thus every neighborhood profile uses the high branch and costs
  F_v=j-b*mu=(5/48)y(1-y)=240199/9600000
       =1/40+199/9600000 > 1/40.
The last cost is less than each of the two previous bounds. It is therefore
the retained certificate, and every entry of that certificate fails the target.

5. True half optima and global maximum cut on the same host

Take the global cut A,B from section 3. It has internal cost zero. Internal
edge costs cannot be negative, so this is a global maximum crossing cut.
It is balanced, and the uniform profile on either side is an independent
half with cost zero. This certifies the global half minimum as zero without
enumerating 2^28 subsets. It also proves the global-cut retained certificate
is zero. No appeal to a maximum-cut search heuristic is involved.

The true half minimum RESTRICTED TO Y is exactly the bad neighborhood value.
To see this, let p be any half supported on Y and let t_v=w_v-p_v on Y.
Its deleted mass is b. Since all Y degrees equal mu, expansion gives
  cost_Y(p)=j-sum_v t_v*d_v+cost_Y(t)=j-b*mu+cost_Y(t)>=j-b*mu.
This lower bound is attained by deleting mass b only in L, which is
independent. Such deletion is possible because b<y/2. The script deletes
whole clusters in order, then one partial cluster. It records the selected
mass at all 28 vertices and checks its actual cost. Hence the failure on Y
is structural; a better Y-only profile cannot meet 1/40 on this graph.

6. Completion-envelope scope

The example refutes the stated retained-envelope/profile test at a locally
optimal cut. It does NOT show that the complete existing deletion/completion
proof has no better witness. In PiecewiseCompletion.lean,
independent_set_completion_even selects a maximum independent set internally;
it is not required to keep the original X in the selected half. On this graph,
after deleting the X edge, the color class A is still an independent half.
It stays independent when the edge (0,1) is restored, because only vertex 0
lies in A. Thus a zero-cost completion witness is available. The scalar
envelope Q(x)+q discards that information. There is no conflict between the
failure of this retained bound and that zero-cost witness.

7. Exact finite simple graph and parity

Let N=22560000 and replace each support vertex by an independent cluster of
Nw_v vertices. The four X cluster sizes are
  1128000, 2336353, 3395280, 2186927;
each Y cluster has 563060 vertices. Join two clusters completely exactly when
their support vertices are adjacent. These positive integer sizes sum to N.
This is an explicit finite simple triangle-free graph, described implicitly;
the large adjacency matrix is not materialized. Every support edge contributes
N^2 w_u w_v edges. Each normalized cut or profile cost above is therefore the
finite graph cost divided by N^2.

N is even. Each global color class has N/2=11280000 vertices. The bad cut has
|X|/N=x and |Y|/N=y and exactly (2/47)N^2 internal edges. For every individual
vertex in a cluster, the internal and external degrees are N times the
weighted degrees. Their minimum difference is N*(1817/24000)=1707980>0.
Thus strict local optimality holds for moving ONE actual finite-graph vertex,
not just moving a whole support cluster. All neighborhood profiles and the
uniform profile reproduce the same normalized certificate.

The zero half and the Y-restricted optimum witness use integer cluster counts
at this N. For the latter, deleting full clusters and a remaining amount bN
uses integer counts, so its cost is also an actual integral-half cost. E6 does
not need the eventual BCL theorem or a rounding theorem to assert its explicit
counterexample: its cut budget and parity are checked directly. The constant
2/47 and normalization are unchanged. No stronger external cut input is used.

8. Exact small-host sanity tests and the enumeration lemma

The script checks K2, C4, C5, C7, Petersen, and Andrasfai supports of orders
8, 11 and 14. For each support it tests the uniform weights and two seeded
positive integer weight vectors with entries 1,...,7, normalized by their sum.
The seed is 2026101806. This is a fixed 24-case harness, not a broad search.
It enumerates all pure-cluster cuts up to complementation: 29838 total.
Of these, 82 are locally optimal and meet the BCL cut budget. None of these
82 has retained certificate above 1/40. Every global maximum pure-cluster cut
on these hosts is also checked; no eligible global-cut certificate fails.
These positive checks are finite evidence only. The separate 28-cluster graph
is the negative result. No universal global-maximum-cut statement is proved.

For completeness the fractional-half minimum on each small host is computed
exactly, not by restricting arbitrarily to integral cluster choices. A minimum
exists on the compact feasible box slice. If two coordinates p_i,p_j are
strictly between their capacities, replace them by p_i+t,p_j-t. The objective
as a function of t has quadratic coefficient -1 if ij is an edge, and zero
otherwise. It is concave or linear on the permitted closed interval. One
endpoint therefore has value no larger and makes at least one coordinate
integral relative to its capacity. Repeating terminates with at most one
partial cluster. This argument works for every finite simple support.

For integer weights a_i with W=sum a_i, enumerate a full subset S and one
possible partial vertex v outside S. Put r=W-2sum_{i in S}a_i. A feasible
partial choice has 0<=r<=2a_v and p_v=r/(2W). Its cost is
  [2 sum_{ij in E(S)}a_i a_j + r sum_{i in S, i~v}a_i] / (2W^2).
The generator enumerates full subsets first; the independent verifier
enumerates the partial vertex first. The latter also checks the recorded
half witness directly. On the blowup with cluster sizes 2a_i and order 2W,
this fractional optimum is an actual integer half. The exact uniform costs
include C5=1/50 and Petersen=1/50; K2 and C4 have optimum zero.

All-cut enumeration here covers cuts constant on support clusters. A global
cut minimum is attained among these: with all other allocations fixed, its
cost is affine in one cluster's allocation, as no intracluster edges exist;
round to an endpoint without increasing cost and repeat. No claim is made
that the enumerated local cuts include every split-cluster local optimum.
The explicit counterexample cut is pure, so that limitation cannot remove it.

9. Verification and limits

All deciding arithmetic uses Python Fraction or integers. The independent
verifier imports no experiment code. It rechecks the full graph, weights,
local degrees, cut costs, each actual neighborhood profile, optimum witnesses,
finite blowup and small-host enumeration. replay.py copies both scripts into
a fresh directory and reruns them. It compares every mathematical output;
only the nondeterministic elapsed-time field is excluded from equality.

Six counter-controls remove the cross edges, add a triangle, forge a profile
cost, change the half mass, falsely label the bad cut a zero-cost global cut,
and forge the C5 optimum. Each is rejected for the relevant mathematical fault.
The first development verifier stopped two controls at an edge-count check.
That weaker output is retained. The final verifier tests local optimality
and triangle-freeness before the exact edge count and records those reasons.

This is a written exact finite counterexample with replayable rational evidence.
No new Lean endpoint or kernel-checked formalization is claimed. Independent
review and acceptance belong to the critic. The local-cut hypothesis is
rejected within its stated retained-certificate scope. A universal positive
statement for global maximum cuts remains open. No further search, optimization,
or structural route is started in this packet.
