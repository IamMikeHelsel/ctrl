Independent graph reconstruction
independent_check.py reconstructs the 28 positive rational masses and all 109
edges from the formulas, without importing either builder script. It compares
them to the frozen data. The color classes {0,2} plus Y-left and {1,3} plus
Y-right each have mass 1/2. Every edge crosses these classes, so simplicity,
triangle-freeness and the zero global half follow directly.

The bad cut has X mass .401 and Y mass .599. Its Y subgraph is a 5-regular
bipartite circulant on 24 equal masses. Internal Y degree is 599/4800 and
external degree is 401/2000. On X, each external degree is 599/2000 and
exceeds its internal degree. This strict inequality is the correct local-cut
condition: moving one vertex changes crossing cost by its positive mass times
(internal degree minus external degree).

The finite blowup has N=22560000 vertices in independent clusters, with complete
links exactly on support edges. I checked every integer cluster size. Every
single ACTUAL vertex has internal and external degrees N times the weighted
degrees. The smallest external-minus-internal difference is 1707980>0. Thus
the example is locally optimal for single finite-graph vertex moves, not just
for moving a whole cluster. Its exact internal edge count divided by N^2 is
2/47. This explicit budget needs no appeal to the eventual BCL theorem.

Completion distinction checked more closely
After deleting edge (0,1), the global color class remains an independent half.
It can also be chosen as a MAXIMUM independent set, as the formal completion
proof requests: a perfect matching exists without that X edge. In each Y
cluster reserve 376940 vertices for matching to the opposite X side. Each X
side has exactly 12 times that many vertices, and all needed cross links are
complete. The 186120 remaining vertices per Y cluster match to the corresponding
opposite Y cluster by the shift-zero link. This covers every finite vertex.
A perfect matching bounds an independent set by N/2, attained by the global
color class. Restoring edge (0,1) adds no selected edge to that class. Thus a
zero-cost output is available within the existing completion logic itself.
E6 must not be relabeled as failure of every completion output.

Enumeration and negative controls
The small-host harness checks 24 seeded weighted cases and 29838 pure-cluster
cuts up to complementation. Its separate verifier re-enumerates all 82 eligible
local cuts and all global cuts and half optima. These finite positive tests
are sanity evidence only. They cannot prove a universal global-cut statement.
The independent verifier imports no experiment module.

The half-enumeration lemma is sound. On the compact capacity box slice, moving
two partial selected masses by +t and -t makes cost concave (edge coefficient
-t^2) or affine (no edge). An endpoint does not increase cost and makes at
least one coordinate a capacity endpoint. Repetition leaves at most one
partial cluster. The enumerated formula counts full-full and full-partial
edges once, with denominator 2W^2. It covers exact half mass even for odd W.
All-cut enumeration covers pure clusters; coordinatewise affine rounding
proves some global cut optimum is pure. It does not enumerate every local
split-cluster optimum, and the proof makes no such claim.

My separate endpoint enumeration reproduces uniform K2 and C4 optimum zero,
and C5 and Petersen optimum 1/50. An independent odd-support control shows
why the partial coordinate is necessary: two nonadjacent full C5 clusters
cost zero but have mass 2/5, not 1/2. Omitting the partial coordinate would
confuse a floor-half of clusters with a fractional half of total mass.
