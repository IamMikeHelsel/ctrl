1. Candidate theorem and unchanged graph conventions

Set R=251/10000=.0251 and I=2/47. The external hypothesis hBCL is exactly
BCLEventual from PublishedBCL.lean:21: there is N such that every finite
simple triangle-free graph H of order at least N has a vertex cut with at
most I|V(H)|^2 internal edges, each edge counted once.

Candidate conclusion: every finite simple triangle-free graph G on n
vertices has floor(n/2) vertices spanning at most R n^2 edges. No regularity,
maximum-degree, optimal-cut or new global density assumption is added.
The coefficient 1/50 is not proved. First we give a rational fractional
half, then use the same all-order and rounding interfaces as E4.

For now let n>0 be even. Take any cut X,Y with |X|<=|Y| and
  e(X)+e(Y)<=I n^2.
Put x=|X|/n, y=|Y|/n, c=1/(2y), q=e(X)/n^2 and j=e(Y)/n^2. Then
  x+y=1, 0<=x<=1/2, 1/2<=c<=1, x=1-1/(2c),
  q>=0, j>=0, q+j<=I, and 0<=j<=I.
A rational fractional half has z_v in [0,1] and sum z_v=n/2. Its normalized
cost is n^(-2) sum_{uv in E(G)} z_u z_v. All graph costs below come from
actual feasible profiles. Scalar parameter points are not graph optima.

2. Existing uniform and completion profiles

The profile c on Y and zero on X has cost U(c,j)=c^2 j.
For c>=3/4, the actual graph theorem deletion_completion_even applies:
x>=1/3, so delete edges inside X, complete the resulting independent set,
and restore the removed edges at cost at most q. This gives cost at most
  Q(x)+q <= Q(x)+I-j,
where
  Q(x)=3x^2/2-5x/4+9/32 for 1/3<=x<=3/8,
  Q(x)=x(1/2-x)/2       for 3/8<=x<=1/2.
The two formulas agree at x=3/8, or c=4/5. The completion theorem needs no
maximum-cut assumption. It selects a maximum independent set internally.
Its graph domain is c>=3/4; the stricter c0 in the old scalar wrapper is
only a choice in the older numerical proof and is not imposed here.

Choosing the cheaper of these two profiles gives the old bound
  E(c)=c^2(I+Q(x))/(1+c^2), c>=3/4.
This follows by taking their weighted average with weights 1 and c^2 and
using q+j<=I. The denominator is positive. We do not assume a minimum over
a compact set of rational profiles. Q decreases with x on both branches,
and x increases with c>0. Thus Q(1-1/(2c)) decreases for c>=3/4.

3. Reused neighborhood construction and exact moments

Give the vertices of Y positive rational masses w_v totaling y>1/2. The
unweighted graph uses w_v=1/n. Let
  b=y-1/2, d_v=sum_{u in N(v)} w_u,
  s_v=sum_{u in N(v)} w_u d_u,
  j=sum_{uv in E(Y)} w_u w_v, mu=2j/y.
Triangle-freeness makes N(v) independent. Therefore s_v is exactly the
edge mass incident to N(v), counted once. Its complementary edge mass is
j-s_v. This cost identity requires neighborhood independence.

For each v, use zero on X and the following profile on Y:
  LOW, d_v<=b: zero on N(v), 1/[2(y-d_v)] elsewhere.
  HIGH, d_v>b: 1-b/d_v on N(v), 1 elsewhere.
Each profile has weighted mass 1/2 and values in [0,1]. Its normalized cost is
  F_v=(j-s_v)/[4(y-d_v)^2] in LOW,
  F_v=j-(b/d_v)s_v        in HIGH.
At d_v=b>0 the profiles agree. At d_v=0 use LOW. Positive masses imply
 d_v<y, and all used denominators are positive. For w_v=1/n the selected
mass is exactly n/2, with rational entries and no degree divisibility issue.
These are Sarid's existing negative feasible endpoint profiles, already
used in E4. They are not a new construction.

With expectation under probabilities w_v/y, double counting gives
  E[d_v]=mu and E[s_v]=E[d_v^2].
Define phi(d) by
  phi_low(d)=j-4R(y-d)^2-d^2,  d<=b;
  phi_high(d)=a d-d^2,        d>=b;
  a=(j-R)/b.
The branches agree at b. If every F_v>R, rearranging the cost formulas
with positive denominators gives s_v<phi(d_v)+d_v^2. Thus
  E[phi(d_v)]>0.                                         (OPPOSITION)
No regularity or global concavity assumption is used.

4. A free affine slope

Let K=4R+1>0 and set
  L0=8R(y-mu)-2mu,
  C=j-4R(y-mu)^2-mu^2.
For any real scalar L, define
  A(L)=C+(L-L0)^2/(4K),
  H(L)=(a-L)^2/4+Lmu.
Completing squares gives identities for all real d:
  phi_low(d)=A(L)+L(d-mu)-K[d-(8Ry-L)/(2K)]^2,
  phi_high(d)=H(L)+L(d-mu)-[d-(a-L)/2]^2.
Consequently, A(L)<=0 and H(L)<=0 imply phi(d)<=L(d-mu) on both branches.
The scalar L may depend on the graph parameters y,j, but is the same for
every vertex v. Therefore E[phi(d_v)]<=L(E[d_v]-mu)=0, contradicting
OPPOSITION. Some actual neighborhood profile must have F_v<=R.

This is pointwise domination followed by expectation, a weak-duality
argument. It uses no strong duality, optimal SDP, interior-moment hypothesis
or theorem about a different objective. The square bounds hold globally;
using them only on their assigned degree branches is conservative.

For a simple parameterization let
  L1=a-2mu, D2=(L1-L0)^2, Hmin=a mu-mu^2,
  L=L0+theta(L1-L0).
Then
  A(L)=C+theta^2 D2/(4K),
  H(L)=Hmin+(1-theta)^2 D2/4.
E5 needs only theta=0 and theta=1. Each accepted cell selects one of these
two rational constants. L0 and L1 are evaluated at the actual parameters
inside that cell, so one fixed numerical L across an entire cell is not
required. In each individual graph the chosen L is independent of v.

mathematical_controls.py checks both square identities, both parameterized
identities, and H(L)=Hmin+(L-L1)^2/4 coefficient by coefficient in a rational
polynomial ring. It clears only the positive denominator 4K where needed.
These checks are exact arithmetic evidence, not Lean kernel proofs.
