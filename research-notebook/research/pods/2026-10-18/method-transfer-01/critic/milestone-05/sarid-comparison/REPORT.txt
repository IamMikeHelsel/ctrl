Finding
The neighborhood construction is already Sarid's construction. The general
idea of bounding a function by an affine function and then taking expectation
is standard, and Sarid's displayed cubic proof itself uses that idea. The
reviewed source does not state the particular threshold-dependent two-branch
criterion A(L)<=0, H(L)<=0 or optimize its free slope. That difference does
not establish novelty. The present step is best described as a new analysis
of a known endpoint construction within this project. Any new graph constant
requires its own complete certificate and wider prior-art comparison.

Exact construction match
Normalize the Y weights by y. Sarid's constant profile is alpha=1/(2y),
normalized degree delta=d/y, and direction 1_N-delta. Its negative endpoint
is alpha-t_minus*(1_N-delta), with
 t_minus=min(alpha/(1-delta),(1-alpha)/delta).
If d<=b=y-1/2, this endpoint is zero on N and 1/[2(y-d)] elsewhere.
If d>=b, it is 1-b/d on N and one elsewhere.
These are exactly the E4/E5 profiles. At d=b the formulas agree; d=0 uses
the low formula without division by d. Triangle-free neighborhood independence
gives costs (j-s)/[4(y-d)^2] and j-(b/d)*s. This is reuse, not a new profile.

Different displayed estimates
Sarid defines normalized edge mass m=sum_edges w_u*w_v, degrees delta_v and
neighbor degree sums e_v. It proves that some v has
 delta_v*(e_v-m*delta_v)>=4*m^3.
The proof normalizes degrees by 2m, uses the harmonic-arithmetic mean
inequality on neighborhoods, and averages with degree-weighted vertex mass.
The final pointwise inequality is
 2r^3/(r^2+1)>=(2r-1),
whose difference is (r-1)^2/(r^2+1). This is already an affine-expectation
argument. Thus a claim that the present project introduced that general
mechanism into the neighborhood proof would be wrong.

Sarid then averages BOTH feasible perturbation endpoints. Their barycentric
weights cancel every linear term, including an arbitrary external objective.
The saving is the product of the two radii times the cubic neighborhood
quantity. It bounds the radius product below by a function of alpha alone.
After a BCL cut, it applies that saving to two baseline profiles and maximizes
the resulting scalar envelope. The source's second baseline is c on Y; its
first includes X and a constant on Y. This is not our deletion-completion Q
baseline, although both arguments use the same BCL input.

Our checked criterion keeps the negative endpoint's exact degree dependence.
Assuming all its costs exceed a threshold R gives a piecewise quadratic
phi after using E[s]=E[d^2]. It bounds both quadratics by the same line
L(d-mu), with mu=E[d]=2j/y, and can choose L. The accepted gate needs only
this double-counting identity and weak pointwise domination. It does not
invoke Sarid's cubic saving, strong duality or an SDP optimizer.
The displayed source does not perform this particular optimization. I have
not proved that no algebraic reformulation of its estimates implies it.

Notation warning
Our mu is the mean AMBIENT degree 2j/y. Sarid's m (called mu in the source)
is the NORMALIZED edge mass j/y^2. Our L is a freely chosen affine slope.
Sarid's L(alpha) is a lower bound on the product of feasible radii. They are
different objects. Conflating them would create a false theorem transfer.
