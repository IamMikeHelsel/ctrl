Counting argument
For each side with 2m vertices and integer degree d<=m, the shifts
0,...,d-1 define a simple bipartite regular circulant. There are d distinct
neighbors at each vertex and no same-class edges. Disjoint union gives the
specified X and Y, no cross edges and a triangle-free graph. The stated
orders and degrees satisfy all divisibility, positivity and degree bounds.
For Y, y=80/133, j=71/1880, mu=d_Y/N=9443/75200 and b=y-1/2=27/266.
X has q=9/1880, so j+q=2/47. The cut itself has no crossing edges.

Let each original vertex mass be w=1/N. For a fractional half z in Y,
put t=w-z. Then t>=0 and sum t=b. Expanding each unordered edge once gives
 e(z)=j-sum_v t_v*(sum_neighbors w)+e(t)=j-b*mu+e(t).
Regularity supplies the same mu at every vertex. Nonnegative masses give
e(t)>=0. Since b<=y/2, all removed mass can lie in one independent
bipartition class. Then e(t)=0. The specified N also permits an integral
removal of exactly 107611200 vertices, so this lower bound is attained.
Every neighborhood profile has d=mu>b and s=mu^2 and attains this value.
The supplied uniform and completion upper envelopes exceed 1/40 as claimed;
this says nothing about optimality of the completion construction itself.

The union of the left classes of X and Y is an independent set of N/2
vertices, so the global optimum is exactly zero. Every positive-degree
vertex can improve the supplied cut by moving to the other side. A maximum
cut has internal cost zero. Therefore this example cannot refute a claim
whose input includes local or global cut optimality. Finite improving moves
from any BCL-admitted cut preserve its internal-cost upper bound, but that
fact alone proves no stronger sparse-half result.
