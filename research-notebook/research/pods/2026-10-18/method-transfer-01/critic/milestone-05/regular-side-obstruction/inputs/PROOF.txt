AN ACTUAL GRAPH EXPLAINS THE NEXT CERTIFICATE LIMIT
Root diagnostic,18 October2026. Written proof pending independent review.

Claim
At c=133/160 and j=71/1880, the obstruction to target R=1/40 is not
only an artificial degree distribution. There is a finite simple triangle-free
graph and a cut (X,Y), with q+j=2/47, for which every fractional half supported
inside Y costs at least3763/150400>1/40. Yet the whole graph has an independent
half. This is a limitation of the chosen side, not a counterexample to Erdos128.

Construction
All vertices have mass1/N. Take N=1060169600. Let Y have637696000 vertices,
degree133127414, and X have422473600 vertices, degree25472160. Construct each
side as a regular bipartite circulant: for2m vertices, split into two parts
indexed by Z/mZ and join left i to right i+s for0<=s<d. Since d<=m, all
neighbors are distinct, the graph is simple, and every vertex has degree d.
There are no edges between X and Y. No adjacency matrix is materialized.
This defines a finite graph directly; the large order is a denominator cost.

The exact check.py verifies both integer degrees, side sizes, and
 y=80/133, x=53/133, j=e(Y)/N^2=71/1880,
 q=e(X)/N^2=9/1880, q+j=2/47,
 mu=d_Y/N=9443/75200, b=y-1/2=27/266, mu>b.
These are exact rational equalities. The graph is bipartite and triangle-free.

All halves confined to Y
For any fractional selected masses0<=z_v<=1/N in Y with sum z_v=1/2,
set t_v=1/N-z_v. Their removed mass is b. Expanding each edge product gives
 e(z)=j-sum_v t_v*mu+e(t)=j-b*mu+e(t)>=j-b*mu,
since t_v>=0 and all edge coefficients are nonnegative. This is valid for
fractional selections, not just integral vertex subsets.
Because b<=y/2, choose the removed mass entirely in one bipartition class of
Y. It is independent, so e(t)=0 and equality is attained. The chosen integer
order also makes an integral removal possible: remove107611200 vertices.
Therefore the exact restricted-half optimum is
 j-b*mu=3763/150400=1/40+3/150400.
Every single-neighborhood profile is in the high branch, since all degrees
are mu>b, and s_v=mu^2. It attains exactly the same restricted optimum.
The scalar uniform and completion envelopes also exceed1/40 at these cut
parameters; check.py records their exact values. The completion envelope
is an upper bound, not the optimum of its underlying construction.

The full graph is easy
Take the union of the left bipartition classes of X and Y. It has exactly
N/2 vertices and no edges, so the actual global half optimum is0. The cut
(X,Y) used above is deliberately poor: its cut edges are0. A maximum cut
has all edges crossing and internal cost0. Every vertex with positive degree
can improve (X,Y) by a single move, so even local cut optimality excludes it.

Meaning for the next test
No better distributional estimate can force a half supported inside this Y
to meet .025. We must change the construction, use the other side, or exploit
a better cut. This does NOT prove that every graph needs a stronger approach,
that .025 is the optimal universal coefficient, or that the moment mechanism
has no other value. E5's .0251 cover is a separate universal conditional result.

One justified next hypothesis is to require a locally maximal cut while
preserving the BCL internal-cost upper bound. Starting from any admitted cut,
finite improving vertex moves never increase its internal cost. Such a cut
satisfies internal degree<=external degree at each vertex. This excludes the
diagnostic but does not yet imply a useful half bound. A later test must find
an actual construction and a complete inequality, or a new exact obstruction.
