The exact claim
For a finite nonempty type V, a simple triangle-free graph G, decidable graph
adjacency, a supplied C : OrientedBCLCut G, and rationals R,L, the theorem
half_from_squared_certificates takes only:
  1/2 < sideMass C, 0 <= R,
  lowCertificate (sideMass C) (sideEdges C) R L <= 0,
  highCertificate (sideMass C) (sideEdges C) R L <= 0.
It concludes HasNormalizedFractionalHalfBoundQ G R: an actual rational
vertex profile in [0,1], with total mass |V|/2 and edge cost / |V|^2 <= R.
Edges are unordered and counted once. The conclusion is existential.

OrientedBCLCut is not a free arbitrary-cut type. It carries a smaller-side
orientation and the explicit budget e(X)+e(Y)<=(2/47)|V|^2. That budget is
part of the supplied input even though the construction does not use its
numerical value. No theorem that every graph has this cut is proved here.
There is no additional global-density, maximum-cut, degree-regularity, C4,
feasible-profile, objective-bound or moment premise. No even-order premise
is needed for this fractional endpoint. Empty V and balanced side y=1/2
are outside this theorem's domain. Integral floor halves remain a later step.

Why the construction has the claimed meaning
Let n=|V|, k=|Y|, y=k/n and j=e(Y)/n^2. Uniform weights 1/k on the actual
induced side graph are strictly positive and total one. The source derives
 edgeMass=j/y^2, weightedDegree(v)=sideDegree(v)/y,
 alpha=1/(2y)=C.c, local target=R/y^2, local slope=L/y.
The completed-square identities imply both local affine bounds, for every
rational degree argument, by multiplying by the positive factor y^2. The
ambient meanDegree is 2j/y, equal to 4cj when c=1/(2y). The low square uses
K=4R+1>0 from R>=0. The high normalization uses y-1/2>0. The domain y>1/2
also gives 0<alpha<1. No denominator argument passes through the balanced
endpoint, where alpha=1 and y-1/2=0.

The actual side graph remains triangle-free by induced-graph inheritance.
The unchanged accepted E8 theorem derives its moments from adjacency and
uses actual feasible neighborhood profiles. E9 supplies its two affine
bounds with the same rational L/y for every side vertex. The global square
bounds are stronger than branch-restricted bounds, which is a conservative
sufficient condition, not a circular graph conclusion. Equality at the degree
branch boundary is allowed by E8's low branch; no degree or vertex is omitted.

The proof extends E8's profile by zero outside Y. It proves
 sum(extension)=k*weightedMean(profile)=k/(2y)=n/2,
 normalizedEdgeCost(extension)=y^2*localObjective(profile)<=R.
The induced-edge count, neighbor sums and factor two between ordered pairs
and unordered edges are proved in the helper extraction. The local degree
scaling lemma is also proved explicitly. No state, plant or controls theorem
is imported; lean-controls appears only as the existing package-cache location.
The result proves existence. It is not an efficient selector implementation.

Nonvacuity and semantic controls
The six-vertex graph has one edge and four isolated vertices. The small cut
side is one isolated vertex, so y=5/6,j=1/36 and c=3/5. A constant 3/5
profile on Y has local edge objective 9/625, ambient mass 3 and normalized
cost 1/100. Its exact square certificates are -1/5850 and -19/22500. The
six_vertex_endpoint proof calls the main theorem with these actual graph
parameters and certificates. The existential output of that call is not
identified with the independently exhibited constant profile.

The same graph has the independent half {2,3,4} with zero cost. Thus the
positive example is a normalization and nonvacuity test, not sharpness.
The critic adds the legal half {0,1,2}, whose normalized cost is 1/36>.01.
This rejects a false universal interpretation of E9's existential conclusion.

The four copied mathematical rejection controls test the actual wrong
alternatives: cost scaled by y instead of y^2; local mass 1/2 instead of
1/(2y); an ambient slope used locally without division by y; and alpha<1 at
the balanced endpoint. Positive compiled control theorems state their exact
counterfacts. The critic's fifth rejection is the false claim that every
fractional half of the six-vertex graph has cost <=.01. Rejection requires
an unsolved mathematical goal, not timeout, syntax or missing-name failure.
