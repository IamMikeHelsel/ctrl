Question and scope
Can the accepted E9 finite-graph bridge and the proposed E10 scalar selector
be joined to the existing constructive completion, blowup and rounding proofs?
Target: for every finite triangle-free simple graph G on n vertices, there
is a set S with |S|=floor(n/2) and e(G[S]) <= (251/10000)*n^2, conditional
only on the precise eventual BCL cut theorem already used in this project.
This is the written E5 result's formal closure, not the conjectured1/50 bound.
No best-known or novelty claim follows. The stronger external cut claim
remains unaccepted and could give a smaller number with the older method.

External premise — keep explicit
Use exactly PublishedBCL.lean's BCLEventual proposition: there exists N such
that every finite triangle-free graph of order at least N has a cut whose
two internal edge counts sum to at most(2/47)*n^2. This is a quantified
hypothesis, not a custom axiom and not a hidden stronger finite theorem.
The cited input is arXiv:2103.14179v1, Theorem2(a), as recorded in the prior
source bundle. This task does not reprove that external graph theorem.
No max-cut optimality, regularity, degree distribution or new density bound
may be added. The generic even-order lemma can take one actual BCL cut.

Source mechanism and object map
E9 supplies an actual ambient fractional half from its two square bounds.
E10 is to select uniform U, completion Q, or moment M for every rational
c in[1/2,1],j in[0,2/47]. Set R=251/10000.
For an actual oriented cut C, use X=C.small,Y=C.large,n=|V|,
y=|Y|/n,c=n/(2|Y|),j=e(Y)/n^2,q=e(X)/n^2,x=|X|/n.
E9 proves c=1/(2y). Derive q,j>=0 and q+j<=2/47 from C.internal_bound.
The oriented side sizes supply c in[1/2,1]. Do not assume these scalar
inequalities independently of C and do not leave them on the final theorem.

First endpoint: positive even order
Let n=2t, t>0. Prove a normalized fractional half at R from any supplied
oriented BCL cut and E10's accepted selector.
U: c^2*j<=R. Invoke uniform_side_fractional_half on Y. Its mass is exactly
t=n/2 and raw cost is c^2*e(Y). Divide by the positive n^2.
Q: c>=3/4 and completion(c)+2/47-j<=R. Derive n/3<=|X|<=n/2 from c and
orientation; equivalently2t<=3|X| and |X|<=t. Invoke the actual theorem
PiecewiseCompletion.deletion_completion_even directly, or use the proved
min witness. It gives raw cost<=completionEnvelope(|X|,t)+e(X).
Prove completion(c)=completionEnvelope(1-1/(2c),1/2) with the actual E10
definition, including c=4/5. Degree-two scaling gives
completionEnvelope(|X|,t)=n^2*completionEnvelope(x,1/2).
Then normalized cost<=completion(c)+q<=completion(c)+2/47-j<=R.
M: c<1 and a common rational L satisfies E9's two certificates at
(y,j,R,L). Derive y>1/2, substitute the actual sideMass and sideEdges,
and invoke half_from_squared_certificates. No even-order condition is
needed inside E9. Do not substitute a different L for each vertex.
The balanced case c=1 must use U or Q; do not divide by y-1/2 there.

Second endpoint: every finite order and integral halves
Preserve the prior construction in PublishedBCL.lean, with its actual proof
sources and attribution. bclCutCertificate_all_of_eventually applies BCL to
a sufficiently large positive balanced blowup and descends the normalized
cut cost. No assumption that the original graph meets the size threshold.
For nonempty G, apply the new even-order result to balancedBlowup G2.
It has2n vertices and remains triangle-free. Use the existing normalized
fractional-half blowup equivalence to descend exactly to G. Keep the
multiplicity positive and the division by n^2 valid. Treat n=0 separately.
Finally apply the unchanged hasSparseHalf_of_hasFractionalHalfBound after
converting normalized cost to raw cost. This rounds odd orders to floor(n/2),
not ceil(n/2), and does not increase the unordered edge count.

Falsifiers and nonvacuity
- Missing n^2, wrong large-side y factor, or counting directed edges must fail.
- At c=1 no M denominator may be used. Test a balanced independent cut.
- At n=0 no positive denominator proof may be invented.
- Test an odd graph, e.g. C5: an integral set of2 can have no edge, while any
  set of3 has an edge and violatesR*25=.6275. This is a real counterexample
  to silently replacing floor by ceil. Separately use an even nonempty graph.
- The exact scalar .025 obstruction remains a restricted-selector obstruction,
  not a graph counterexample to the.025 conjecture.
- A conditional theorem does not prove BCLEventual. Disclose whether a small
  instance invokes the cut-specific endpoint using an explicit cut rather
  than instantiating the external universal hypothesis.
