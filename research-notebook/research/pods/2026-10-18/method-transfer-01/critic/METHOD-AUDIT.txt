Verdict
The catalogue is a useful source of candidate proof moves. It is not evidence
that unusual field combinations caused most of the reported discoveries. Our
four selected cases support a narrower lesson: identify a missing interface,
keep its hypotheses, and test it before importing a large theory. None supplies
a new Erdos 128 bound, a robot guarantee, or a GU operator definition by itself.

The README describes a significance-filtered release from about 4,000 posed
problems. It reports a common procedure for most results and mean compute of
three hours per result, with exceptions. It does not provide an attempt-level
dataset that supports a success-rate estimate. Families, manuscripts, tasks,
and independently verified new theorems are different units. The ten abridged
summaries cannot measure how often any mechanism occurred, how rare a field
combination was, or whether the same route would work on our problems.

This is direct reason to audit dependency closures and statement versions. It
does not establish an error rate for the remaining corpus. The stated 300/719
formalization tally is a repository claim. We did not replay it.

Method filter for this pod
1. Pin the exact source statement. Separate the headline, paper theorem,
   documented formal statement, and a proof we actually replayed.
2. Name our obstruction. Map source objects to target objects before naming
   fields. A matching word is not a map.
3. List each needed assumption as available, newly required, or equivalent to
   the desired conclusion. Reject the last category as circular.
4. Preserve arithmetic, positivity, independence, conditioning, dimensions,
   quantifiers and limit order. State which estimates are uniform.
5. Put parameter choices in dependency order. Every iteration must pay for
   its losses without choosing an earlier parameter from a later conclusion.
6. Run the cheapest exact counter-control. For a graph problem, couple the
   proposed statistics to the actual graph objective, not just a scalar point.
7. Compare under the same contract. Advance only if there is a useful new
   statement, a better bound, or a concrete obstruction removed.
