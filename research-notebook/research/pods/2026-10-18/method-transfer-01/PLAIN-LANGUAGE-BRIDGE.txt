HOW ONE KIND OF MATH CAN HELP ANOTHER
18 October 2026

For Erdos 128, we want to choose half the vertices of a triangle-free graph
while keeping few edges between the chosen vertices. Our first bound kept
only two numbers: the sizes of a split and the edge counts inside it. It
lost information about how the edges meet.

Probability gives us a way to keep some of that information without listing
every graph. Choose one vertex at random. Count its neighbors. Then add the
neighbor counts of those neighbors. These two measurements obey an exact
identity when averaged over the graph.

For example, take the four-vertex path A--B--C--D:

  vertex                    A   B   C   D
  number of neighbors       1   2   2   1
  square of that number     1   4   4   1
  neighbors' counts added   2   3   3   2

The last two rows both sum to 10. This holds for every finite undirected
simple graph, by counting the same contributions in two orders. Our proof
uses the weighted version on one side of the split.

The absence of triangles supplies a second fact: no two neighbors of one
vertex are joined. That gives us actual ways to choose a fractional half.
Here "fractional" means that a vertex can be selected by a fraction between
zero and one. An existing rounding result later gives an ordinary half.

The bridge is this: the graph gives feasible selections, probability gives
an exact relation between their measurements, and a quadratic inequality
shows that at least one selection must be cheap enough. Exact rational
arithmetic can check the parameter ranges. Lean can then check a formal
version of the argument. None of those steps alone proves the conjecture.

The recent matching and moment-matrix papers prompted us to restore joint
information. We did not apply their theorems to this graph. The neighborhood
selection itself comes from existing sparse-half work. This distinction
matters when we assess what, if anything, is new.

In controls, the same question has a different answer: what did the earlier
bound throw away? It threw away part of the actuator's exponential response.
Keeping that term gives a tighter position envelope. Checking its whole peak
can certify clearance that the simpler formula cannot certify. The math is
standard comparison theory; its value is the stronger explicit contract.

A second controls test follows the largest weighted state component. That
component can change as the system moves or a new command takes effect. If
every component that can be largest has enough inward drift, the state must
enter a smaller region by a calculable time. A recent chemical-dynamics proof
uses this pattern. We must establish our own robot-model inequalities.

Our rule for choosing the next idea

1. Identify the information that the present bound loses.
2. Find a method that can retain that information.
3. Translate its assumptions and conclusion exactly.
4. Try a small counterexample before a large search.
5. Compare against existing results under the same assumptions.
6. Prove the full claim and have another agent check it.

This is a map for experiments. It is not evidence that combining more fields
necessarily makes a proof better, or that the current results are novel.
