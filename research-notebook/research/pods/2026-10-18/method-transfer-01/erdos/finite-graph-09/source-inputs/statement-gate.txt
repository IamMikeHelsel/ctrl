Universal algebra and domains
Write z=d-m and K=4R+1. Since R>=0, K>0. Expanding the low difference gives
 C0+(L0-L)z-Kz^2.
Completing its square gives
 A(L)-K*(z-(L0-L)/(2K))^2.
For a=(j-R)/b, the high difference is -d^2+(a-L)d+L*m. It equals
 H(L)-(d-(a-L)/2)^2.
Thus A<=0 and H<=0 imply the two pointwise bounds for every real d, and hence
for the required restricted rational degree domains. There is no optimization,
strong-duality, or attainment premise. One common L is used for all vertices.

Nonempty V gives n>0. A strict larger side gives k>0, y=k/n>1/2, b>0,
and 0<alpha=1/(2y)<1. Also y<=1. The local threshold 1-alpha equals b/y,
so the low/high degree branch conditions transport without changing direction.
The balanced case y=1/2 gives alpha=1 and b=0. It is excluded before division;
Lean's total division cannot make it a valid instance of this argument. The
balanced construction remains a separate endpoint.

Normalization
Under uniform weight 1/k on the induced graph, its edge mass is
 mu=e(Y)/k^2=j/y^2.
Its normalized degree is d/y and its mean degree is 2mu. Put
 T=R/y^2 and L_normalized=L/y.
Then y^2*(d/y-2mu)*(L/y)=L*(d-m), since m=2j/y. For the low expression,
 y^2*(T/alpha^2)*(1-d/y)^2=4R*(y-d)^2.
For the high expression,
 y^2*(d/y)*(mu-T)/(1-alpha)=d*(j-R)/b.
These identities prove the claimed scaling for both differences. They do not
rely on finite numerical tests.

The E8 output has values in [0,1] and weighted mean alpha. Extending it by zero
outside Y preserves the unit interval and gives total mass k*alpha=n/2.
Each ambient edge inside Y is counted once. Edges outside Y contribute zero.
Thus normalized ambient cost equals y^2 times the local weighted objective,
and a local cost <=R/y^2 gives ambient cost <=R. This works for odd n as a
fractional half. It does not itself supply a floor-sized integral half.

Attributed source interface

normalizes edgeCost by n^2; there is no hidden factor of two. WeightedGraph
uses one half of the ordered adjacency sum, which counts each unordered edge
once. LowDensityGraph's C.c is n/(2k), exactly the required alpha. It must not
be confused with y or with the coefficient C0.

Disclosed exact control
check.py independently reconstructs the six-vertex graph arithmetic. There is
one edge and four isolates; Y contains the edge and three isolates. It checks
 y=5/6, j=1/36, mu=1/25, alpha=3/5,
 weighted objective=9/625, ambient mass=3, normalized cost=1/100.
For R=1/100 and L=-1/15, it finds A=-1/5850 and H=-19/22500. The cut also meets
the structure's BCL budget, since 1 <= (2/47)*36. This is a nonzero-cost
normalization and arithmetic nonvacuity example. It is not a sharpness example:
the same graph has an independent half of cost zero.

The exact rejected alternatives are preserved in result.json. Replacing y^2
by y gives 3/250 rather than 1/100. Using alpha=1/2 gives mass 5/2 rather than
3. At d=1/6, omitting the slope scale produces discrepancy 1/900. The balanced
endpoint violates alpha<1 and has b=0. These are arithmetic falsifiers, not
failed Lean builds. The script also checks both quadratic coefficients at the
example and five exact scaling points; those controls supplement the universal
algebra above and do not prove it by sampling.
