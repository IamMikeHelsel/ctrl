E8 EXACT SEMANTIC CONTROLS

All positive statements in this file are proved in ProfileControls.lean.
The replay also tries four false statements and requires Lean to reject
them with mathematical unsolved goals, not parser or missing-name errors.

1. Nonzero full-endpoint instance: K2, weights (1/2,1/2).
   d=1/2, s=1/4, mu=1/4, alpha=4/5, T=3/20, L=-1/2.
   The low certificate holds for 0<=d<=1/5. The high certificate holds for
   every rational d, hence on its required domain. The endpoint is invoked.
   The explicit high profile at root 0 is (1,3/5), with mean 4/5 and
   cost exactly 3/20. This is a nonzero cost example, not an empty graph.

2. Boundary: K2, alpha=1/2, d=1/2=1-alpha.
   Low and high profiles agree. The selected low profile has mean 1/2,
   unit capacities and cost zero. This checks the branch boundary; it is
   not an assertion of the full affine criterion at these parameters.

3. Zero degree: the one-vertex edgeless graph with weight 1.
   Its degree is zero. The low profile at alpha=4/5 is feasible, has mean
   4/5 and has zero cost. No high-branch division is used.

4. Wrong branch: K2 at alpha=4/5.
   The low profile gives value 8/5 at the root, so it is not a unit profile.
   BadBranch.lean asserts feasibility and must be rejected.

5. Missing scaling: on K2, profile (4/5,0) has mean 2/5, not 4/5.
   BadMean.lean asserts the wrong mean and must be rejected.

6. Invalid normalization: two vertices cannot both have weight 1 in a
   RatProbabilityWeight. BadNormalization.lean asserts such a weight
   object exists and must be rejected.

7. Triangle-free premise: K3 with positive weights (1/3,1/3,1/3).
   Set alpha=1/2, mu=1/3, T=0, L=-2/3. The low difference between phi
   and the affine upper bound is -(d-1/3)^2; the high difference is
   -(d-2/3)^2. Both inequalities hold for every rational d and therefore
   on 0<=d<=1/2 and 1/2<d<1, respectively. Nevertheless every unit profile
   of mean 1/2 has cost at least 1/18. Profile (1,1/2,0) attains 1/18.
   BadTriangle.lean asserts existence of cost<=0 and must be rejected.
   This gives a graph counterexample to dropping triangle-freeness. It is
   not just a failed proof tactic or a scalar infeasibility example.
