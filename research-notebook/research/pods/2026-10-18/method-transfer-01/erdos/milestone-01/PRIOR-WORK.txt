1. Amir Sarid, Sparse halves in triangle-free graphs: a bound of131/5000.
Source revision ba85f319ebb33ec2194ef4c2929a12916329e1e7:
https://github.com/aimir/erdos-128-sparse-halves/blob/ba85f319ebb33ec2194ef4c2929a12916329e1e7/sparse_halves_simple.tex
Read the local frozen source: introduction lines20--147, cubic lemma and
onestep proof lines420--550, and cut construction/calculus lines552--650.
The exact profile used by E1 is the negative endpoint in lemma onestep,
lines485--547. Normalize masses by y; set alpha=1/(2y) and delta=d/y.
For d<=y-1/2, t_minus=alpha/(1-delta); it gives zero on the neighborhood
and1/[2(y-d)] elsewhere. For d>=y-1/2, t_minus=(1-alpha)/delta; it gives
1-(y-1/2)/d on the neighborhood and1 elsewhere. The linear term in that
lemma can be zero. E1 selects one of these existing endpoint profiles.

Sarid then selects a vertex through a cubic/harmonic-mean lemma and averages
the two feasible endpoints. E1 instead uses the actual degree first/second
moment identities and one affine majorant of a piecewise quadratic. The
specific C,L,S separator and .02586 conclusion were not found in the read
sections. The entire repository was not audited for all equivalent arguments.
Sarid's stated global coefficient is.0262, with a flag-algebra input as well
as BCL. E1 does not import the flag-algebra certificate or cubic saving lemma.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09406
Author PDF retrieved and hashed under sources/:
https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~razborov/files/halves.pdf
Read the main bound statement, Lemma4.1 on printed pages10--11, and Section4.5
on pages16--18. Lemma4.1 uses a neighborhood-complement fractional half and
another neighborhood-supported profile, then combines their bounds. Section4.5

this very problem. The paper states27/1024, not.02586. These targeted sections
