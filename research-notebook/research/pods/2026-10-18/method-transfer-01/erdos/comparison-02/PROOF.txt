1. Graph and input conventions

Let G be a finite simple triangle-free graph of positive even order n.
Use any cut X,Y with |X|<=|Y| and e(X)+e(Y)<=I n^2. It need not be a maximum
cut. Put y=|Y|/n, c=1/(2y), x=1-y, q=e(X)/n^2, j=e(Y)/n^2. Thus
  1/2<=c<=1, q>=0, j>=0, q+j<=I, 0<=j<=I.
Let beta be the minimum normalized fractional-half cost. Edges are counted
once. The old construction gives beta<=c^2 j. For c>=3/4 it also gives
  beta<=Q(x)+q,
where Q(x)=3x^2/2-5x/4+9/32 for x<=3/8 and
Q(x)=x(1/2-x)/2 for x>=3/8, on the applicable domain x>=1/3.
Therefore the unchanged old envelope is
  beta<=c^2(I+Q(x))/(1+c^2),  c>=3/4.                (OLD)

The BCL input is exactly the previous eventual statement: some N works for
every finite triangle-free graph of order at least N, giving such a cut with
I=2/47. The prior balanced-blowup transport removes N. The two-fold blowup
and fractional-profile descent remove the even-order restriction. The prior
rounding theorem then gives floor(n/2) vertices at the same normalized cost.
The empty graph is immediate. No new BCL theorem or better cut input is used.

2. Correct normalization of Sarid's existing lemma

Define ell(c)=min(4r^2,r(1-r)), where r=min(c,1-c).
The cited one-step lemma takes a finite triangle-free graph H with strictly
positive rational probability weights and edge mass mu. From a constant
profile alpha it constructs a[0,1] profile with the same weighted mean,
saving at least4 ell(alpha) mu^3. An arbitrary linear objective term is
allowed. Here take H=G[Y], weights1/|Y|, alpha=c, and linear term zero.
Y is nonempty because n>0 and |Y|>=n/2.

The edge mass inside this probability space is
  mu=e(Y)/|Y|^2=j/y^2=4c^2 j.
The weighted mean c corresponds to selected mass c|Y|=n/2. Extend the new
profile by zero on X. All X-internal and X--Y contributions vanish. Its
ambient normalized cost is y^2 times its cost in H. Hence the saving is
  y^2 * 4 ell(c) mu^3
    = [1/(4c^2)] * 4 ell(c) * (4c^2 j)^3
    = 64 ell(c)c^4 j^3.
We obtain the genuine feasible-profile bound
  beta<=A_c(j):=c^2 j-64 ell(c)c^4 j^3.              (CUBIC)

There is no global edge-density restriction on this use. Sarid's lemma
onestep precedes his low-density cut section and has no such hypothesis.
Only his later combination with another baseline imposes a density split.
The comparison uses neither that other baseline nor the flag-algebra bound.

3. A safe cap in j, without a calculus assumption

For c in[1/2,1], 0<=ell(c)<=1/4. For 0<=j<=I,
  A_c(I)-A_c(j)
    =(I-j)c^2[1-64 ell(c)c^2(I^2+Ij+j^2)]
    >=(I-j)c^2(1-48I^2)>=0,
because I^2+Ij+j^2<=3I^2, c^2<=1, ell(c)<=1/4, and
  1-48I^2=2017/2209>0.
Thus CUBIC implies beta<=c^2 I-64 ell(c)c^4 I^3. No regularity or equality
of degrees is used. This argument also handles j=0 and j=I.

4. Four exact regions cover every parameter

Set a=779/1000 and h=783/1000. Then
  1/2<a<h<4/5<1 and h>7797/10000>3/4.
The older scalar wrapper only asks for its second witness above7797/10000;
every use of OLD here is above h and satisfies that domain.

A. For1/2<=c<=a, use the unmodified uniform cap:
   beta<=I a^2<R,
   R-I a^2=399/23500000>0.

B. For a<=c<=h, c<=4/5 implies ell(c)=c(1-c). Indeed r=1-c and
   c(1-c)<=4(1-c)^2 precisely when c<=4/5. Therefore
   ell(c)>=a(1-h), c^4>=a^4, c^2<=h^2. Section3 gives
     beta<=I h^2-64a^5(1-h)I^3
          =5228036405019791917/202779296875000000000
          <R,
   with exact margin11780626230208083/202779296875000000000>0.
   The displayed bound is about.0257819042. The decimal is not used to accept.

C. For h<=c<=4/5, use OLD's first Q branch. The numerator of R-OLD is
     P(c)=(R-I-17/32)c^2+7c/8+R-3/8.
   It is concave, with leading coefficient -2575427/4700000. Its minimum
   on the closed interval is at an endpoint. Both endpoint values are positive:
     P(h)=69535997/4700000000000,
     P(4/5)=4217/29375000.
   The denominator1+c^2 is positive.

D. For4/5<=c<=1, use OLD's second Q branch. Its gap numerator is
     Ac^2-Dc+E,
   A=R+1/4-I=34264/146875>0, D=3/8, E=R+1/8,
   4AE-D^2=3846033/29375000000>0.
   Completing the square proves positivity for every real c. The endpoint
   c=1 is included; the cubic saving is zero there and is not needed.

These four closed intervals cover the entire allowed c domain. Their shared
endpoints are harmless. There is no grid, uncovered interval, or hidden
condition on j. They prove beta<R for positive even order. The unchanged
interfaces in section1 give the stated non-strict integral bound for every
finite order, conditional on BCL and the validity of the cited saving lemma.

5. Falsifiers and limits of this comparison

At the old scalar obstruction c=7797/10000 and
j=2172228946875000/51047928060481223, the exact saving is about.000313050624.
The improved cap is about.0255561224, below R. Thus the gain at that point
is much larger than the.00001 change sought by E1. comparison.json stores
the exact fractions and the normalization identity.

The old scalar-only point still exceeds.02586. Omitting the cubic term cannot
establish this comparison. A normalization control records that omitting the
factor y^2 overstates the saving by1/y^2=4c^2 at that point.

At c=.78 and neighborhood degree delta=.01, the actual feasible radii have
product less than4(1-c)^2 but at least ell(c). Hence replacing the min in ell
by its first branch would be wrong. These parameters can occur in a weighted
two-vertex edge. This rejects an incorrect strengthening of the lemma.

Changing only the target to.02583 while keeping this fixed split makes P(h)
negative. That is a failed certificate, not a counterexample to.02583 or a
claim of the optimal coefficient. No smaller constant was searched for.
