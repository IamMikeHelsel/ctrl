1. Statement and external hypothesis

Set R=63/2500=.0252 and I=2/47. Let hBCL mean: there is an integer N such
that every finite simple triangle-free graph H of order at least N has a
vertex cut with at most I|V(H)|^2 internal edges. An internal edge has both
ends on the same side, and each edge is counted once. This is exactly the
BCLEventual proposition in PublishedBCL.lean:21. It remains a hypothesis.

Candidate theorem: under hBCL, every finite simple triangle-free graph G
on n vertices has a set S of floor(n/2) vertices with e_G(S)<=R n^2.
There are no regularity, maximum-degree, or optimal-cut assumptions. The
conjectured coefficient 1/50 is not proved. The construction first gives a
rational fractional half. Section 7 states the unchanged parity, blowup,
and rounding steps which give the integral conclusion.

First take n>0 even and any cut X,Y with |X|<=|Y| and
  e(X)+e(Y)<=I n^2.
Write
  x=|X|/n, y=|Y|/n, c=1/(2y), q=e(X)/n^2, j=e(Y)/n^2.
Then x+y=1, 0<=x<=1/2, 1/2<=y<=1, 1/2<=c<=1,
x=1-1/(2c), q>=0, j>=0, q+j<=I. Thus 0<=j<=I.
A rational fractional half is a profile z_v in[0,1] with sum z_v=n/2.
Its normalized cost is n^(-2) sum_{uv in E(G)} z_u z_v.
All costs below are costs of such profiles. We do not identify a scalar
feasible value with the minimum over actual graph profiles.

2. Existing feasible bounds and their domains

The profile c on Y and zero on X is a fractional half of cost
  U(c,j)=c^2 j.                                            (UNIFORM)
If c>=3/4, then x>=1/3. Delete edges inside X, apply the existing independent
set completion construction, and restore those edges at cost at most q.
This produces a fractional half of cost at most
  Q(x)+q <= Q(x)+I-j,                                     (COMPLETION)
where
  Q(x)=3x^2/2-5x/4+9/32,  for 1/3<=x<=3/8;
  Q(x)=x(1/2-x)/2,        for 3/8<=x<=1/2.
The formulas agree at x=3/8, which corresponds to c=4/5.

The source theorem deletion_completion_even in PiecewiseCompletion.lean
requires only n/3<=|X|<=n/2, triangle-freeness and even order. Its proof
selects a maximum independent set internally after deletion; X need not
be maximum, and the cut need not be optimal. The old CutScalar wrapper
uses c0=7797/10000 to prove its older numerical target. That restriction
is not a premise of deletion_completion_even. We use the actual graph
construction directly wherever c>=3/4, including c<c0.

Choosing the cheaper of UNIFORM and COMPLETION also gives a profile with
cost at most
  E(c)=c^2(I+Q(x))/(1+c^2),  for c>=3/4.                  (OLD)
Indeed the minimum of two numbers is at most their weighted average, with
weights 1 and c^2. The average of U and c^2(Q+q) is at most E(c), since
q+j<=I. The denominator 1+c^2 is positive. This step selects between actual
profiles; no minimum over a rational compact set is assumed.

The function Q decreases with x on[1/3,1/2]. On the first branch its
slope is 3x-5/4<0; on the second it is 1/4-x<0. The branches meet. Also
x=1-1/(2c) increases with c>0. Thus Q(1-1/(2c)) decreases with c>=3/4.
This is also the normalized form of completionEnvelope_antitone in the
existing source. It justifies the cell bound used in section 5.

3. The reused neighborhood profile and graph moments

Consider the induced graph on Y. More generally, give its vertices positive
rational masses w_v with sum y>1/2. For the unweighted graph use w_v=1/n.
Let
  b=y-1/2,
  j=sum_{uv in E(Y)} w_u w_v,
  d_v=sum_{u in N_Y(v)} w_u,
  s_v=sum_{u in N_Y(v)} w_u d_u,
  mu=2j/y.
Triangle-freeness makes N_Y(v) independent. Hence s_v is exactly the edge
mass incident to N_Y(v), counted once, and j-s_v is the edge mass outside
that neighborhood. This is the only cost identity in this step which
needs triangle-freeness. Double counting alone does not imply it.

For each v construct a profile, zero on X, by these rules:

LOW, when d_v<=b: put 0 on N_Y(v) and 1/[2(y-d_v)] elsewhere in Y.
Its mass is 1/2. Since y-d_v>=1/2, its values are in[0,1]. Its cost is
  F_v=(j-s_v)/[4(y-d_v)^2].

HIGH, when d_v>b: put 1-b/d_v on N_Y(v) and 1 elsewhere in Y.
Here 0<b/d_v<1. Its mass is y-b=1/2, and its cost is
  F_v=j-(b/d_v)s_v.

At d_v=b>0 the two profiles and costs agree. At d_v=0 use LOW, so no
reciprocal of zero occurs. Positive masses imply d_v<y because a vertex
is not its own neighbor. The case y=1/2 does not enter this lemma; the old
bound in section 5 covers it. For w_v=1/n, mass1/2 means sum z_v=n/2.
All profile entries are rational. No degree divisibility is needed.

These are the negative feasible endpoint profiles from Sarid's existing
lemma onestep. They are reused, not a new construction. The normalization
is w'_v=w_v/y, alpha=1/(2y), delta=d_v/y; the negative endpoint radius is
min(alpha/(1-delta),(1-alpha)/delta). The two cases reduce to LOW and HIGH.
Razborov's 2021 Lemma 4.1 also uses a neighborhood-complement fractional
half. The candidate change here is the refined moment test and its cover.

Take expectation over v with probabilities w_v/y. Finite double counting
gives the exact identities
  E[d_v]=2j/y=mu,
  E[s_v]=(1/y) sum_v w_v sum_{u~v} w_u d_u
        =(1/y) sum_u w_u d_u sum_{v~u} w_v
        =E[d_v^2].                                      (MOMENTS)
No regularity assumption is used.

4. Refined moment implication: C<=0 and T=C+S<=0

For d<=b define psi(d)=j-4R(y-d)^2; for d>=b define
psi(d)=(j-R)d/b. The branches agree at b. Put phi(d)=psi(d)-d^2.
If every neighborhood profile has F_v>R, positive denominators give
s_v<psi(d_v) in both branches. The weights are positive and Y is nonempty,
so MOMENTS implies E[phi(d_v)]>0.

Define
  L=8R(y-mu)-2mu,
  C=j-4R(y-mu)^2-mu^2,
  B=(j-R)/(y-1/2)-L,
  T=B^2/4+Lmu.
For comparison with E1's notation, S=T-C, so T=C+S. Direct expansion gives
  j-4R(y-d)^2-d^2
    =C+L(d-mu)-(4R+1)(d-mu)^2;                         (ID-LOW)
  (j-R)d/(y-1/2)-d^2
    =T+L(d-mu)-(d-B/2)^2.                              (ID-HIGH)
Since R>0, if C<=0 and T<=0 both branches satisfy
  phi(d)<=L(d-mu).
Taking expectations gives E[phi(d_v)]<=0, a contradiction. Therefore some
actual neighborhood profile has cost F_v<=R.

Strict C<0 is not required. Also S<=0 is not required. E1 imposed that
stronger sufficient condition. This proof uses C and T directly. It does
not use Jensen or a global concavity claim; phi can have an upward jump in
its derivative at b. mathematical_controls.py verifies both identities
coefficient by coefficient in a rational polynomial ring, before any point
or graph tests. The upper check uses B+L=(j-R)/b.

7. All orders and integral halves: unchanged interfaces

The even-order construction above is for any eligible cut, so apply it to
the positive balanced two-fold blowup G[2]. Triangle-freeness is preserved
and its order is even. hBCL gives an eligible cut by the existing theorem
bclCutCertificate_all_of_eventually in PublishedBCL.lean:47. That theorem
first takes a large enough positive balanced blowup to meet N and descends
the normalized internal-cut bound. The descent is exact and has no error
term, density assumption or new external input.

Average a fractional profile over each independent twin class. This
preserves selected mass and normalized edge cost, yielding a rational
fractional half of G at normalized cost<=R. The exact existing interface is
  Erdos128.hasNormalizedFractionalHalfBoundQ_balancedBlowup_iff,
BalancedBlowup.lean:417, for any positive blowup size and rational bound.
The even-order use above therefore covers odd original n as well.

Finally invoke
  Erdos128.hasSparseHalf_of_hasFractionalHalfBound,
RoundingFinal.lean:84. Moving mass between two fractional coordinates
makes the edge objective concave in the transfer, so some feasible endpoint
does not increase cost. Iteration leaves at most one fractional coordinate.
For even n there is none; for odd n its value is1/2 and removing it does not
increase cost. The resulting set has floor(n/2) vertices. For n=0 choose
the empty set. The normalization divides by n^2 only when n>0.

These exact interfaces were already used by the conditional .02587 Lean
endpoint. E4 changes none of them. This packet gives a written argument and
