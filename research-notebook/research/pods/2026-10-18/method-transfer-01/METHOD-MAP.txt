Observed bridges and proposed uses

087. Convex geometry + complex analysis + probability.
The symmetric Mahler paper uses a sum of basis-feasibility probabilities as a
shared quantity. A holomorphic mass argument bounds it from below. A geometric
simplex identity relates it to the volume product. Its published search summary
reports failed entropy, tensor and induction routes before this construction.
Lesson for us: seek one quantity that both sides can control. No holomorphic
object has been supplied for our graph problem; importing that machinery now
would be speculation. This is a source-reported route, not our verified proof.

113. Graph combinatorics + entropy + polytope geometry.
The inspected perfect-matching argument keeps a joint law and its covariance,
then uses the geometry of the relevant face. Individual averages are not the
whole object. Our candidate uses a different, elementary identity: for a
weighted random vertex, the mean neighbor-degree sum equals the degree second
moment. That connects an actual half construction to an affine majorant.
The matching theorem is not applied to triangle-free graphs. It prompted the
question; the graph construction itself is already in sparse-half literature.

095. Real algebraic geometry + moment matrices + matrix lifts.
The inspected cone papers separate failure in the original coordinates from
failure of every finite lift. The construction uses a truncated functional
with a positive degree-two moment matrix; it need not come from an actual
measure. Lesson: enrich a relaxation with relevant information and
check realizability. One failed scalar bound does not prove the target false.
Our negative control is stronger: the old graph densities are realizable, but
their asserted sparse-half cost is not the optimum of that realizing graph.
Thus mere realizability of those densities cannot remove our obstruction.

197. Coding-inspired finite-field identities + geometric group theory.
The inspected pairing argument uses interpolation and cancellation on affine
lines. Characteristic two is essential to the cited cancellation mechanism.
Our critic checked small exact field identities and a real-number obstruction.
This does not transfer to GU's real or complex matrices: a sum of real positive
outer products cannot cancel unless every vector vanishes. The full group-ring
construction was not independently checked here. This route is rejected for GU.

221. Statistical physics + probability + uniform approximation bounds.
The inspected stability argument controls errors uniformly before taking an
infimum or a limit. Its separate averaged depth estimate is not a pointwise
bound. For controls, keep the exact response envelope and measure the cost of
simplifying it. Do not turn an average latency or average motion error into an
all-time safety claim. This is an audit pattern, not a plant theorem imported
from spin glasses.

149. Chemical dynamics + active affine branches.
The inspected permanence argument tracks whichever finite affine branch is
active. A related proof pattern could turn our holding invariant into a finite
entry time for a smaller set. Chemical positivity and reaction assumptions do
not hold for the robot state. We must supply our own derivative inequalities.
Our initial weighted-box calculation is now a finite-entry test, but its position
band is too wide to claim precision standstill. Generic polyhedral comparison
methods also predate this release. This route is now the second controls milestone, after the recovery builder freeze.

Two useful rejection tests
097 chooses signs or an order after a finite vector family is known. It cannot
directly solve causal replanning. An adversary can choose each next planar unit
vector perpendicular to the current sum, forcing its norm to grow as sqrt(N)
regardless of the chosen sign. Preserve the clock and information available.
282 assumes physical fields, products, domains and Ward identities before its
operator reconstruction. Our finite Clifford model does not supply them. It
does not choose GU's missing normalized operators or variation conventions.
