Controls: a full finite handoff
The result constructs one continuous physical path from supplied braking
motion, through the first qualifying sensor reading, the pending command,
and later digital holding motion. It keeps the actual acceleration and the
original sample/arrival clocks. Later readings come from the new path.
It derives the future execution and its forward bound; it does not assume
an invariant, a future trajectory or an aggregate feedback-error budget.

For every admitted finite horizon, position stays at or below
3013722449/20100000000 m, about 149.93644 mm, at every real time. This includes
the command delay and dispatch boundaries. The margin below 150 mm is only
about 63.56 micrometres. The theorem is finite; it does not prove an infinite
physical execution or maintained rest.

The supplied braking input uses the stronger C6 contract. It has initial
position <= 1 mm, speed between 0.39 and 0.41 m/s, and acceleration between -1.22
and 0.1 m/s². Its recovered response rate lies between 8 and 12 s⁻¹, with effective
braking target between -1.22 and -1.2 m/s². The exact loss-phase, continuity and
sensor assumptions remain in controls/constructed-handoff-08/CLAIMS.txt.
Future digital cells have bounded constant rates and target errors; these
may change between cells. Samples and fixed command delay are each 0.5 ms.
Sensor error bounds are 1 mm, 10 mm/s and 0.05 m/s².

The combined example switches at 0.3165 s while moving forward at 10.2 mm/s.
Acceleration is continuous; the command changes and later noisy readings
follow the new path. This example has a constant old braking target. It
shows a valid handoff, not an actual loss/restoration event or settling test.

The policy allows retreat. At the nominal 140 mm reading, its target is
95.73033 mm, about 44.27 mm back. Its late position band can have 45.9 mm half-width.
It does not protect a lower travel limit or establish precision standstill.
Franka documents 1 kHz measurements; the present proof needs 2 kHz fresh samples.
There is no coupled-arm, contact, finite-precision or hardware acceptance.

Erdos128: the complete conditional graph result

internal edges. Its only external mathematical premise is the transparent

a cut with total internal edges at most (2/47)*n².
