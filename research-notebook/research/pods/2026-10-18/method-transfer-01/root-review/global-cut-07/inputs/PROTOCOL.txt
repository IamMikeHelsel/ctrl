18 October 2026. Finite evidence only. No universal theorem or new constant.

1. Fixed question and quantifiers

The candidate statement is: every finite simple triangle-free graph whose
minimum internal cut cost is at most (2/47)n^2 has SOME global maximum cut,
with a permitted orientation, whose retained certificate is at most .025n^2.
We test the corresponding rational weighted supports. A failure would require
the BEST certificate over all tied maximum cuts to exceed .025. Failure of only
the worst tied certificate would show a tie-selection issue, not refute this
existential statement. E7 reports both extrema and every tied cut mask.

The retained certificate is the unchanged minimum of uniform-Y cost, the
completion envelope on its proved domain, and all neighborhood-profile costs.
It does not denote the best possible output of the full completion theorem.
E6 showed why that distinction matters. All edges here are counted once.

3. Exact cut and profile conventions

For a cut X,Y orient the smaller mass as X. If the masses are equal, evaluate
BOTH orientations. With x=sum_X w, y=sum_Y w, put
 q=sum_{uv in E(X)}w_u w_v, j=sum_{uv in E(Y)}w_u w_v, c=1/(2y).
The uniform-Y half costs c^2 j. Here y>=1/2, so its selected masses c*w_v
lie within capacities and sum to1/2. The completion envelope is available
exactly when1/3<=x<=1/2:
 Q(x)=3x^2/2-5x/4+9/32 for1/3<=x<=3/8;
 Q(x)=x(1/2-x)/2 for3/8<=x<=1/2.
Use Q(x)+q only on that domain; otherwise omit it. The branches agree at3/8.
Q(x)+q is a feasible upper bound. It is not asserted to be the cost of every
completion witness or the true half optimum.

For y>1/2 put b=y-1/2. For v in Y define
 d_v=sum_{u in N_Y(v)}w_u, s_v=sum_{u in N_Y(v)}w_u*d_u.
Triangle-freeness makes N_Y(v) independent. The two neighborhood costs are
 F_v=(j-s_v)/(4(y-d_v)^2) if d_v<=b;
 F_v=j-b*s_v/d_v if d_v>b.
The first profile selects w_u/[2(y-d_v)] on Y minus N_Y(v), zero elsewhere.
Its denominator has y-d_v>=1/2. The second selects w_u off the neighborhood
inside Y, and (1-b/d_v)w_u on the neighborhood; it uses d_v>b>0.
Both profiles have mass1/2 and capacities0<=p_u<=w_u. At d_v=0 use the first
branch; at d_v=b>0 the formulas agree. At y=1/2 use the uniform/completion
terms and skip neighborhood profiles. No division by zero is performed.
The verifier computes actual edge costs from the selected masses.

The retained certificate C(X,Y) is the minimum of those available bounds.
The graph-level neighborhood formula is reused work, not a new E7 construction.

4. All ties, local comparison and true half minimum

The screen enumerates all2^(n-1) support cuts up to complementation by requiring
vertex0 to lie outside the stored mask. It computes their exact integer
internal cost before any division by W^2. Every mask attaining the minimum is
retained. No random max-cut heuristic or tolerance is used. For each mask the
permitted orientation rule is then applied. The filter for the proposed
conclusion is47*minimum_internal_numerator<=2W^2. Excluded cases would remain
listed with their cost and reason. There are none in this fixed sample.

For comparison, all single-vertex-locally-optimal support cuts are identified
by internal weighted degree<=external weighted degree at each vertex. Every
eligible local orientation has its retained certificate evaluated. This is
finite comparison data; E6 already supplies an exact contrary local example
outside this fixed sample.

The true fractional-half minimum is computed on every case, not just on a
candidate failure. Selected masses p_v satisfy0<=p_v<=w_v, sum p_v=1/2,
with cost sum_{uv in E}p_u*p_v. A minimum exists on the compact box slice.
If two coordinates i,j are fractional, transfer t between them. The cost is
concave or linear in t: its quadratic coefficient is-1 if ij is an edge,
and0 otherwise. One endpoint has no larger cost and makes at least one
coordinate equal0 or its capacity. Repeating produces a minimum with at most
one partial cluster. This argument does not assume regularity.

Thus enumerate a full subset S and a possible partial vertex v outside it.
Let r=W-2sum_{u in S}a_u. For0<=r<=2a_v the selected partial mass is r/(2W),
and the cost is
 [2sum_{ij in E(S)}a_i*a_j + r*sum_{i in S,i~v}a_i]/(2W^2).
The generator visits full subsets first. The independent verifier visits the
partial vertex first. Endpoint duplicates do not affect the minimum. Every
reported optimum includes its full selected-mass vector and is checked by
direct edge summation. These are exact rational minima, not relaxation guesses.

5. Finite simple realization and what was enumerated

For each case replace vertex v by an independent cluster of2a_v vertices and
each support edge by all edges between its endpoint clusters. The resulting
simple triangle-free graph has even order N=2W. Every support cut has exactly
N^2 times its normalized internal edge cost. Its selected half witness has
integer counts N*p_v and total N/2. The output records these cluster sizes and
counts for all42 cases.

A global cut minimum in this blowup is attained by a pure-cluster cut. With
all other cluster allocations fixed, the cut cost is affine in the number of
vertices of one cluster placed on a side, because there are no intracluster
edges. Move that allocation to an endpoint without increasing cost, and repeat.
The final pure-cluster cut has no larger cost. Hence a global optimum from the
support enumeration is also global in the full blowup.

Likewise any actual half maps to a feasible weighted mass profile, while the
minimum extreme profile above has integer counts at N=2W. The recorded
fractional minimum therefore equals the normalized actual half minimum in
this even blowup. This is a direct proof; no unverified rounding assumption
is substituted.

The tie list is complete for the ATOMIC WEIGHTED SUPPORT. Split-cluster global
ties in the blowup are not enumerated. This does not weaken a passing existential
case: its exhibited pure-cluster cut is global in the blowup and has the stated
certificate. A putative failure on all pure ties would need an additional check
of split-cluster global ties before it could refute the full finite-graph
existential statement. No such failure occurred, and none is claimed.
