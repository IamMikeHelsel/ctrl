Read abstract/introduction and sections2.1-3 through Lemma14 and its proof.
Not a full-paper audit; output-feedback and long-delay extensions were only
identified from their introductory scope statements.

The paper treats asynchronous reference delivery to a stable LTI closed-loop
plant. Lemma10 gives an exact discrete transition: nominal evolution plus a
delay matrix times the reference increment. Lemma14 encloses that term by a
polytope and checks its vertices against the admissible set. Retaining the
previous reference supplies recursive feasibility. The early development
assumes instantaneous state information; later sections address other cases.
Remark9 discusses adding intersampling output checks at a finer period.
The paper also treats convergence of the reference to a feasible request.

Implications for this project
Reference adjustment, explicit delay errors, robust constraints and fallback
are established architecture. Their combination alone is not our novelty.
Our current C5 proposal chooses a center once and proves a scalar continuous-
time enclosure under rate uncertainty and measured-history bounds. It does
not restore a desired reference or match the paper's full functionality.
The exact LTI transition cannot simply replace our uncertain-rate dynamics.
A fair benchmark needs the same uncertainty, sensors, timing and target task.
The current old-center comparison is useful but is not a benchmark against
this published network governor. No superiority or novelty verdict follows.

Next decision
Complete the bounded proof to test the proposed useful contract. Before a
paper claim, add a matched reference-governor baseline or explain exactly
which formal interface or certificate is contributed. A small Lean case

separate from successful compilation and from hardware performance.
