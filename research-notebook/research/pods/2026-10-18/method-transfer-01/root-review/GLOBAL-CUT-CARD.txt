E7 CANDIDATE: DOES GLOBAL CUT SELECTION REMOVE THE RETAINED-BOUND FAILURE?
Root card,18 October2026. Not dispatched. Requires E6 independent acceptance.

Source and object map
E6 constructs an actual rational weighted triangle-free graph for which strict
one-vertex cut optimality fails the retained .025 certificate. Its global cut
has certificate zero. Thus local search alone cannot supply the needed cut
property. This is a proved obstruction if independent review accepts E6.
The BCL max-cut source also motivates structured triangle-free test hosts:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14179, section2.1 (extended abstract/proof sketch).
This card imports no unverified theorem from the ten process summaries.

Fixed test
For each disclosed positive rational weighting of a small triangle-free host,
find ALL globally maximum crossing cuts with exact arithmetic. For each cut
evaluate the unchanged retained certificate. Orient the smaller side X; at
equal masses permit both orientations. Report the best AND worst certificate
among the tied global cuts. Restrict the proposed conclusion to graphs whose
minimum internal cost is <=2/47. Report excluded cases explicitly.

The hypothesis to challenge is existential: some globally maximum cut has
retained certificate <=1/40. A graph whose best tied certificate exceeds
1/40 refutes this. A failure of only the worst tied certificate shows that
tie selection matters, not that the existential hypothesis is false.

Use a fixed small family: Clebsch(16 vertices), Mycielski(C5)(11), Petersen(10)
and selected existing Andrasfai hosts of at most17 vertices. Define adjacency
explicitly and check triangle-freeness. For Clebsch one convenient construction
is the binary4-cube plus complement edges: xor differences1,2,4,8,15.
Use uniform weights, a few declared degree/symmetry-breaking weight patterns,
and a seed-fixed bounded sample of positive integer weights. Choose the sample
count and seed before running. Do not keep increasing them after a null result.

Baseline and falsifier
On the same hosts retain E6's local-cut comparison, uniform selection, completion
envelope Q(x)+q on its actual domain and every neighborhood profile. For any
candidate failure, compute the true fractional-half optimum exactly with E6's
at-most-one-partial-cluster enumeration lemma, and provide a finite simple
integer blowup. Distinguish failure of this retained bound from failure of all
completion witnesses. No general sparse-half conclusion follows from a scan.

Stop and scope
An exact counterexample closes this test. A complete null screen is useful
evidence about those hosts only. It does not prove the universal hypothesis or
justify endless random search. After either outcome, grade whether a specific
structural lemma is plausible or whether effort should move to the accepted
E5 graph-level Lean bridge. No new numerical constant is the goal.
