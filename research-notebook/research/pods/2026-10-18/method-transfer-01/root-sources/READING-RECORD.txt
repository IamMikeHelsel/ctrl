087: symmetric Mahler
Read introduction through the beginning of the functional consequence, and the
boundary-feasibility lemma/proof in feasibility.tex. Read published reasoning
summary pages1–7 and the opening of page8; the rest of page8, later pages and
the full equality proof have not been reviewed.
The summary reports failed induction, entropy and tensor routes before a complex
boundary-law construction. The paper joins convex geometry, a conformal map with
a specified boundary distribution, a holomorphic mass bound, and a simplex-volume
identity. The exact bridge is a sum of basis feasibility probabilities: the
analytic argument bounds it below, while the geometric identity bounds it by
the desired volume product. This is a source-reported mechanism, not our replay.
Its prior complex-analytic and geometric work is explicitly cited by the authors.
We have not checked the entire theorem, its formalization or novelty.
Transfer status: useful pattern, no direct theorem application to Erdos128.
No corresponding holomorphic construction for our graph problem has been given.
The general-body reasoning also records failed scalar-covariance shortcuts.
It motivates testing joint graph information, not assuming a transport exists.

095: hyperbolicity cones and semidefinite lifts
Read introduction, obstruction section through the initial scaled-Gram argument,
and the separate explicit-lift paper introduction/main statement.
The source distinguishes failure in original coordinates from failure of every
finite lift. One explicit23-coordinate cone has a100-by100 lift with307 auxiliary
variables, whereas a separate high-dimensional construction claims no finite
lift. Its mechanism joins real algebraic geometry, truncated moment functionals,
Hankel matrices, finite-field rank certificates, and analytic Gram-factor jets.
The constructed degree-two moment matrix is positive. The functional need not
come from an actual probability measure. This is highly relevant
to interpretation: a feasible relaxation point is not automatically a graph or
physical trajectory. No result here proves that our graph problem needs, admits,
or forbids a particular SDP lift. The full source proof has not been replayed.
Transfer status: use richer variables and exact separating certificates; reject
the inference that one failed relaxation proves the target problem impossible.
