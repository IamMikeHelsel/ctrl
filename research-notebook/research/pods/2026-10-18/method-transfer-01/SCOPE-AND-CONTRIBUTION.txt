INTERNAL PAPER REVIEW — WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS WORTH PUBLISHING?
18 October 2026. C8 and E11 have passed independent final review.

Verdict
There is substantial formal integration work. There is not yet enough
evidence for a new controls-method claim or a new best graph bound. The two
proof joins are complete; test the contribution against the gates below.
Expanding the paper by adding unrelated theorems would not close the gap.

A coherent controls paper could answer one question:
When a slow or uncertain actuator recovers, can a controller detect an
admissible handoff and keep a forward travel limit through delayed execution?
The useful chain is recovery -> measured admission -> delayed handoff ->
constructed finite motion -> all-time forward bound. The clock and measured
state must survive every interface. C8 closes this join for the declared supplied-input finite scalar class.

The strongest present contribution candidate is the explicit proof contract
and its checked composition. The recovery comparison, reference adjustment,
polyhedral enclosure and delay treatment are established ideas. We must state
what the formal composition catches that a normal implementation could miss:
old readings reused after a path changes; sample age reset at dispatch;
incoming and outgoing commands confused; first zero called held rest; or
existence of the switched path quietly assumed. Preserved negative controls
illustrate several of these errors. They do not establish that such errors
are common in deployed products.

The current case is too narrow for a broad robot-safety claim. It supplies a
one-dimensional lag model and a finite-horizon theorem. It permits a large
retreat and a wide eventual band. It does not protect a lower travel limit.
Its 2 kHz fresh-sample contract differs from the intended 1 kHz Franka interface.
There is no coupled-arm model, actuator identification, contact result,
finite-precision implementation or physical test. A 149.93644 mm ceiling leaves
only about 63.56 micrometres below the illustrative 150 mm limit.

Useful scope expansion follows the same chain:
1. Make sample age an explicit parameter and test the intended interface.
2. Make recovery parameters explicit, with a checked certificate generator.
3. Compare against an established method on one common nonempty contract.
4. Add finite-precision error and a replayable implementation only after the
   mathematical and timing budgets are feasible.
These additions can form one paper because they test the same handoff claim.
They are next-round work. No new route is authorized by this review note.

A graph paper would need a separate contribution argument.
The present route combines known sparse-half constructions with a refined
moment certificate, exact full coverage and formal graph normalization.
The accepted endpoint keeps the eventual BCL assumption visible and chooses
floor(n/2) vertices. It does not prove the conjectural 0.02 coefficient.
A smaller coefficient than our earlier notes is not automatically new in
the literature. Resolve the stronger external cut claim and make comparisons
under the same assumptions before describing numerical novelty. A reusable
formal certificate framework could be a different contribution, but requires
a clear account of what it adds beyond prior formal and computational work.

The research-method study should stay an exploratory appendix or notebook.
We examined a selected corpus subset and used proof patterns to form tests.
There was no controlled comparison and no unbiased sample of all attempts.
Our two successful-looking chains cannot establish that combining fields
is generally more effective. A prospective paired study is a separate task.

GU does not belong in either paper without a new source-defined bridge.
The missing operators, domains and variation choices remain the obstruction.
Adding standard algebra would enlarge the manuscript without answering it.

Review-ready versus submission-ready
A team can review the exact claims, source files, failures and independent
checks now. Submission readiness additionally requires a credible related-work comparison, a defined contribution and a
manuscript whose headline matches its demonstrated scope. The related-work and contribution gates remain
open. No submission, external contact or public release is authorized.
