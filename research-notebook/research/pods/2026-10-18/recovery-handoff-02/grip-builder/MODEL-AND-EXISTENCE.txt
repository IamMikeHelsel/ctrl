MODEL AND EXISTENCE BOUNDARY

The state f=(x,v,a) is physical position, velocity and acceleration. Time is
in seconds; x in metres, v in m/s, a and target q in m/s^2, rate k in 1/s.
The physical equation is a'=r(q-a). q is an effective acceleration target
after available braking authority and disturbance effects. A commanded motor
effort alone does not establish q<=-b.

This models restored effective braking with lag. Physical acceleration remains
continuous across T. Its derivative may jump at T because the target or rate
may change there. It excludes impact-like acceleration jumps. It is not a
model of instantaneous loss of shoe or tire contact force. Grip recovery is
motivation unless a mechanical mapping supplies the target and rate bounds.
The recovery deadline T, lost-phase cap A, recovered magnitude b and rate floor
k must be justified outside this theorem.

There is one continuous f on [0,H]. The first phase has an ODE on (0,T);
the second phase has an ODE on (0,S) for f(T+s). Both phase state predicates
include their endpoints. Rate continuity is required within each closed phase;
no agreement of the two rates at T is required. Targets may differ at T.
No physical coordinate is reset. The witness shows q=0 before T=1 and q=-1
after T=1 with the identical boundary state [1,1,0].

The theorem is a universal statement about supplied executions satisfying
these premises. General existence for every bounded or irregular target is
not proved. The exact piecewise witness supplies one nontrivial instance.
The guarantees on the recovered target and rate are required throughout the
computed S, even if the first zero occurs earlier or before T. Stopping the
ODE at a guard and removing the post-event continuation premise is a separate
extension. A physical controller that changes mode at zero needs a matching
composition argument; this result does not supply it.

The first zero can equal zero if initial velocity is zero. Otherwise the
returned prefix has positive velocity. Continuing a negative target after
that zero can produce rearward motion. No maintained rest or lower position
bound is claimed. The result is not yet connected to a detector, measured
handoff guard, sampled hold or a complete stop-and-hold operation.

The scalar comparison proof uses an exponential supersolution g. It does not
assert the generally false inequality a'<=-k(a+b) for actual a below -b.
Instead, g+b>=0 and
 a'-g'+r(a-g)=r(q+b)-(r-k)(g+b)<=0.
This permits r above k without any upper rate bound. The loss-phase comparison
likewise derives a<=A from a'+r(a-A)=r(q-A)<=0.
