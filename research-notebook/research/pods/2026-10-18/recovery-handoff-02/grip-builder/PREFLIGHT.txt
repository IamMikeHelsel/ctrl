TEMPORARY BRAKING LOSS — ANALYTIC PREFLIGHT
18 October 2026. Informal argument; no new Lean build or acceptance yet.

Finding
The root's proposed bound is sound under the precise conditions below.
The missing input is effective physical braking, not a larger response rate.
The bound is useful as a cheap conservative admission test. It can be very
loose near zero speed. The existing local comparison results already contain
the main method; this extension is a new contract/composition, not established
new control theory.

Physical contract, SI units
One continuous physical state satisfies x'=v, v'=a and a'=r(t)(q(t)-a).
q is the effective acceleration target after all authority loss and error.
It is not merely the command sent to a drive. r has units 1/s and controls
the response speed. It cannot create a negative q when friction/force is absent.
The proposed model is an effective scalar model. It is not a derived tire,
contact, slip, normal-force, impact or shoe-grip model.
Because actual a remains continuous, changing q to zero models a lagged loss
of acceleration. It does not represent an abrupt contact-force jump unless
a separate physical argument justifies that abstraction.

Let T>=0 be a known recovery deadline; A>=0, v0>=0, b>0, lambda>0.
Assume 0<=v(0)<=v0 and a(0)<=A. Before T, require q(t)<=A and r(t)>=0.
After T, require q(t)<=-b and r(t)>=lambda. Recovery may occur earlier.
The recovered authority must persist through the stopping argument. A brief
return of grip or a negative command without effective force does not suffice.
No physical state is reset at T. A command/rate jump may change a', not a.

For a first formal pass, supply a trajectory continuous on [0,H], classical
on each open phase, with a continuous rate on each closed phase and the ODE
there. The phases share x(T),v(T),a(T); a' need not exist across T. This is a
conditional theorem, not general existence for arbitrary signals. No derivative
bound on r and no post-recovery upper rate are needed for this upper bound.
The proposed whole-horizon theorem takes H=T+V/b below and requires braking
through H. An event-limited version would only require it before the first
zero; that is a separate formal scope improvement, not a silent assumption.

Derivation from the ODE
1. Compare a with the constant A on [0,T]. At initial time a(0)<=A, and
   a'+r(a-A)=r(q-A)<=0. The positive integrating factor gives a(t)<=A.
   Thus v(t)<=v0+At and x(t)-x(0)<=v0*t+A*t^2/2 for 0<=t<=T.
   Define P=v0*T+A*T^2/2 and U=v0+A*T. In particular a(T)<=A and v(T)<=U.

2. For s>=0 after recovery, set g(s)=-b+(A+b)exp(-lambda*s).
   Then g+b>=0 and g'=-lambda(g+b). Along the actual ODE,
   a'-g'+r(a-g)=r(q+b)-(r-lambda)(g+b)<=0.
   Initial order is a(T)<=g(0)=A. Scalar comparison therefore gives
      a(T+s)<=-b+(A+b)exp(-lambda*s).
   Do NOT argue a'<=-lambda(a+b) for every actual a. That inequality need
   not hold when a<-b. Comparison against the upper curve avoids this error.

3. Put C=(A+b)/lambda and V=U+C. Integration gives the tighter envelopes
      v(T+s)<=U-b*s+C*(1-exp(-lambda*s))
              =V-b*s-C*exp(-lambda*s)<=V-b*s,
      x(T+s)-x(0)<=P+V*s-b*s^2/2
                           -(C/lambda)*(1-exp(-lambda*s)).
   Hence x(T+s)-x(0)<=P+V^2/(2b), by completing the square. This bounds
   all intermediate positions, not just a deadline position.

4. V>=0 and b>0, so S=V/b>=0. The velocity envelope is nonpositive at S.
   Continuity and v(0)>=0 give an earliest zero tau in [0,T+V/b], with
   positive velocity before tau. If v(0)=0, tau=0. If the first zero occurs
   during the loss phase, it is still covered. The path before tau satisfies
      x(t)-x(0)<=L,  L=v0*T+A*T^2/2+(v0+A*T+(A+b)/lambda)^2/(2b).
   Before the first zero, this displacement is the forward travel. The result
   does not bound total path length after reversals or all future motion.

Practical looseness
The simple velocity bound inserts the entire lag allowance C at recovery,
although the tighter envelope starts at U. The position bound also discards
a nonpositive exponential term. These are sufficient relaxations, not a
worst-case physical trajectory. The tighter envelope is attained by the
idealized trajectory a=A,q=A before T and q=-b,r=lambda after T, with
v(0)=v0 and no earlier first zero when v0>0. Numerical roots of that envelope
can quantify looseness, but are not kernel-certified safety limits.

As v0 tends to zero with A=T=0, the simple bound tends to b/(2*lambda^2),
while actual first-zero travel tends to zero. At v0=0 the first zero is
already time zero. Thus a finite residual bound near rest can be an artifact
of the enclosure. A failed L<=clearance test does not establish unsafe motion.
See numerical-screen.json for fixed illustrative cases; no calibrated plant
data or comparison against another controller is supplied.

Required comparison with the OLD exact illustrative contract
x(0)<=.001 m, v(0)<=.41 m/s, A=.1 m/s^2, T=.05 s, b=1.2 m/s^2,
lambda=8/s gives V=.5775 m/s. The simple reference-position bound is exactly
.1605859375 m. It FAILS the old .15 m certificate already proved by the
sharper exponential argument. The tighter-envelope peak is about .140710783 m
by numerical evaluation only. This new symbolic route cannot be advertised
as a stronger clearance result. Its possible value is an explicit parameter
trade and an ODE-derived loss-phase envelope, at a known cost in conservatism.

Exact failure witnesses, currently informal
No recovery: q=0,r=1,a=0,v=1,x=t satisfies the ODE for every t>=0.
Velocity never reaches zero and forward travel is unbounded. Positive response
rate alone gives no braking authority.

No uniform positive authority: q(t)=-exp(-t)/2, r=1,
  a(t)=-t*exp(-t)/2,
  v(t)=1/2+(t+1)*exp(-t)/2,
  x(t)=t/2+1-(t+2)*exp(-t)/2.
Direct differentiation gives the same ODE, x(0)=0,v(0)=1,a(0)=0.
q is strictly negative, but v(t)>1/2 for all t>=0. Pointwise negative
authority is insufficient when it decays and supplies too little impulse.

No uniform positive rate: q=-1, r(t)=exp(-t)/4, a(0)=1 gives
  a(t)=-1+2*exp(-(1-exp(-t))/4)>0 for t>=0.
With v(0)>0 the plant never stops, despite q<0 and r(t)>0 at all times.
A positive lower rate, or an adequate cumulative-response condition, matters.

No known recovery deadline: coasting at v=1 for an arbitrary loss duration N
already travels N. Later recovery does not yield one uniform finite distance
bound when N has no upper bound.

Boundaries and proposed build gate
The model does not prove that grip recovers, estimate T/b/lambda, or guarantee
that the chosen drive command yields q<=-b. External loads must already be
included in q. Continuing braking after the first zero can drive backward.
There is no permanent-rest, lower-travel, detector or digital-hold conclusion.

After root releases construction, use the existing scalar comparison and
first-zero helpers with their source bytes and pins preserved. First derive
the pre-recovery acceleration bound, then compose the shared-state phases,
prove the parametric travel/deadline inequality, and include a checked coast
witness. Add a nonzero-delay numeric instance and failed-clearance test.
Do not build until root releases this second milestone. The independent
critic, not this builder, must accept the final actual statement/evidence.
