Delayed handoff: main challenges
The accepted digital theorem starts with sample=dispatch=0. Merely shifting its
clock to a later dispatch D turns the initial reading timestamp into -D. That
violates its causal history contract. A new bridge must cover the actual old
read and all physical motion before and after dispatch, on one physical state.

The builder proposes global time0 at the initial reading, pending command q=-1,
and a delayed first digital command using that reading. This can address the
history gap if the following facts are proved from the stated initial box and
physical ODE. They must not be assumptions of the final admission theorem.
- Startup position, velocity and acceleration bounds, hence startup cube safety.
- A startup feedback-slope bound under the pending command, not under digital
  feedback. The old continuous-hold slope estimate cannot be reused here.
- A common slope bound across startup and digital intervals, so a later reading
  may refer to a time before D without an uncovered integration interval.
- The pending command on the left of D and the digital command on the right.
  Physical x, v and a must remain continuous through that change.
- Admission from the measured state and error box; no assumed endpoint cube,
  global mismatch bound or unproved trajectory envelope in the exported result.

The proposed slope7 route gives .093+.01+.001*7=.110 m/s^2, below2R=.11105.
It has only .00105 m/s^2 of slack. That number is an arithmetic check, not an
acceptance of the mixed-mode proof. q=-1 is an explicit illustrative restriction.
For the nominal measured state and r=12,d=0, a pending q=-2 gives k'=12.08,
which exceeds7. The same state with q=-1 gives6.08. A slope7 certificate cannot
be advertised as covering every pending brake command in [-2,2]. Failure of
that sufficient guard is not a physical unsafe-trajectory claim.

Sharp first-cell timing issue
If the old reading is stamped0 and first dispatch is D=1 ms, its total age is
already exhausted. A strictly later next dispatch leaves a nonempty interval
where that read is older than1 ms. Thus D=1 ms cannot initialize a positive-
duration first hold cell under this schedule. H=D is only an endpoint case.
Use D<1 ms, an explicit smaller startup limit, or a different justified clock
contract. If D=.5 ms, the initial command has at most another.5 ms of legal
holding before a fresh enough reading must replace it. A1 ms sample period
after D would be too late. The old5 ms capture allowance does not transfer.

Require a D>0 example that satisfies the entire clock and physical contract,
including a nonempty digital interval. Require a rejected delay or pending-
command case. State whether the rejection is a guard failure or unsafe motion.

Temporary braking loss: main challenges
Effective braking authority and response rate are distinct. A positive rate r
only makes acceleration approach its target; it does not make that target
negative. A finite stopping claim needs a bounded recovery deadline, a bound
on positive acceleration/motion during loss, a sustained post-recovery target
at most -b with b>0, and enough post-recovery response. If a uniform response
lower bound is used, expose it rather than hide it in the word recovery.

The target bound must persist through the claimed stopping horizon. A momentary
return of braking authority is insufficient. Any travel bound must include the
loss interval and the transient while acceleration still points forward after
recovery. Carry the same physical state across recovery; do not reset velocity
or acceleration to a convenient recovered value.

Counterexample targets to require
- No recovery: q=0,a(0)=0,v(0)=v0>0 gives a=0 and x=x0+v0*t. Finite response
  rate does not prevent unbounded forward travel.
- No uniform minimum authority: for r=8, q(t)=-epsilon*exp(-t), a(0)=0, the
  total braking impulse is epsilon. The exact acceleration is
  -(8*epsilon/7)*(exp(-t)-exp(-8t)). If v0>epsilon, velocity stays positive.
  A target can be strictly negative at every time and still fail to stop.
  This is an informal analytic counterexample at preflight, not a Lean result.
- If the proof only concludes first zero/nonpositive velocity, continued
  negative braking can then make velocity negative. Do not claim permanent
  rest, rearward-travel safety or a later holding law from that event alone.

Physical interpretation
A continuous acceleration state with a first-order lag describes an abstract
lagged effective-target loss/recovery. It does not by itself model abrupt loss
of mechanical contact, changing normal force, slip, Coulomb friction or contact
restitution. State that boundary. The present review does not validate a grip
mechanism or calibrated force model.
