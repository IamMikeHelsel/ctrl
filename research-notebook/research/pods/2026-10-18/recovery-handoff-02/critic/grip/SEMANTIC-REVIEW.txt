RECOVERY SEMANTIC REVIEW — independent critic
18 October 2026. This note records statement inspection; replay status is separate.

The main theorem is conditional on a supplied physical trajectory. Its state is
f=(x,v,a), continuous through recovery. The loss equation uses f(t); recovery
uses that same f(T+s). There is no state reset or assumed terminal safety set.
Each phase requires the classical ODE only on its open time interval. State
continuity supplies endpoints. The two rates may differ at T, but each rate is
continuous on its own closed phase. No derivative at T is assumed. Thus a target
change and an acceleration-derivative jump are compatible with the interface.
General existence for arbitrary irregular targets is not proved.

For T>=0, the loss lemma derives a<=A from a(0)<=A, qLoss<=A and rLoss>=0.
This is an ODE comparison, not an assumed acceleration envelope. The complete
stopping theorem adds A>=0, v0>=0 and 0<=v(0)<=v0, with x(0)<=X. It has no
lower x, acceleration or target bound. Those omissions are intentional for an
upper-position result, but prevent treating this as a full operating region.

After recovery, qBrake<=-b and rBrake>=k must hold throughout the computed
horizon, with b,k>0. q denotes effective physical acceleration authority after
disturbance effects. A motor command, positive rate, or momentary return of grip
does not establish this premise. The theorem does not require an upper rate.
Its comparison uses g=-b+(z_a+b)exp(-k*s), with z_a>=-b. It proves the correct
inequality a'-g'+r(a-g)<=0. It does not multiply a possibly negative a+b by a
rate floor. The proposed shortcut a'<=-k(a+b) is false when a<-b and r>k.

Let V=v0+A*T+(A+b)/k, S=V/b and H=T+S. The theorem returns a first zero tau
in [0,H], positive velocity on [0,tau), and x(t)<=X+L through tau, where
L=v0*T+A*T^2/2+V^2/(2*b). With X=x(0), L is a displacement limit. For a loose
upper X it is a reference-position limit. Recovery assumptions persist through
H even if tau occurs earlier. General event-triggered mode composition is open.
The zero may be time zero. No permanent rest or lower-travel safety follows.

The exact witness is genuinely nonempty and includes a real loss phase:
x=t, v=1, a=0 with q=0 and r=1 until T=1. It then uses the exact lag flow with
q=-1 and r=1 from [1,1,0]. All three physical coordinates are continuous.
The instantiated horizon is H=3; the theorem gives first zero by 3 and position
at most 3 through that event. This is one analytic execution, not a general
existence result. In fact v(3)=-exp(-2)<0, so continued braking reverses motion.

The no-recovery example is also an actual ODE execution: r=1, q=0, a=0,
v=1, x=t. It stays forward-moving and exceeds any finite position bound.
This rejects rate-only recovery. Decaying-authority examples in the analytic
preflight remain informal; they are not counted as checked theorems.

The simple formula is conservative. Its exact old-contract position bound is
4111/25600=0.1605859375 m, greater than 0.15 m. The previous sharper exponential
certificate already proves 0.15 m under its recorded contract. At v0=.01,
A=T=0,k=8, raising b from 1 to 2 raises the simple bound from 0.0091125 m to
0.0169 m. At v0=0,A=T=0,b=1,k=8, the formula is 1/128 m although the first
zero is already time zero. These facts limit use of the relaxed certificate;
they do not show that stronger physical braking is worse.

Internal overlap is explicit. ActualStop and EventStop already cover delayed
first zero and travel with fixed constants and a supplied loss acceleration
bound. This packet derives that bound from a loss-phase target/rate equation,
exposes symbolic parameters, removes the recovered rate ceiling, and supplies
actual coast/recovery and no-recovery witnesses. This is a checked extension
and reuse of local work. It is not evidence of new friction mechanics, field
benefit, literature dominance or publication merit.
