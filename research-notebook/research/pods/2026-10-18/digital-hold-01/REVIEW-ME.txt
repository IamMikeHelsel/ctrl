KEEPING AN AXIS INSIDE A LIMIT WITH OLD READINGS
Digital hold review packet — 18 October 2026
STATUS: Accepted bounded proof milestone. Ready for team review.
The builder, independent critic and progress reviewer have finished.

The result in plain language
The controller uses a measurement that is already old. The proof accounts for
how far the actual state can move while that measurement stays in use. Under
the exact conditions below, the axis stays within 55.525 mm of its target at
every instant. The proof covers the times between command updates as well.

This closes the fixed 1 ms conditional holding test. It does not yet close the
brake-to-hold operation. It does not show that the axis becomes still. The
allowed speed is up to 0.4442 m/s and acceleration up to 3.5536 m/s^2. Those
bands are too broad to call this a precision stop without a task requirement.

What changed
The earlier 1 ms calculation assumed a motion bound while checking that bound.
The new proof removes that loop. It considers a possible first boundary
crossing, derives the error bound from the preceding motion, and proves the
crossing cannot occur. The initial safe set remains an explicit condition.

One checked example also changes the command at 0.5 ms. The physical state
stays continuous, but the acceleration derivative jumps. This shows that the
formal execution model admits a real digital update. The example uses an
exact exponential trajectory, not a numerical simulation.

The exact contract, in SI units
State: position x, velocity v, acceleration a. Fixed target: c.
Plant: x'=v, v'=a, a'=r*(clip(k(measurement))+d-a).
Controller: k=-8(x-c)-6v-a/2. Clip range: [-2,2] m/s^2.
Response rate: 8 <= r <= 12 per second; it can change with time.
Physical target error: |d| <= 0.01 m/s^2, added AFTER clipping.
Sensor errors: <=0.001 m, <=0.01 m/s, <=0.05 m/s^2, relative to the actual
state at the measurement timestamp.

The initial values of all three transformed coordinates must lie in [-R,R]:
  (x-c, x-c+v/4, x-c+v/2+a/16), where R=2221/40000 m.
Position alone does not establish this initial condition.

There is one continuous physical state through all updates. Dispatches are
strictly ordered and only finitely many occur in each finite time interval.
Each command stays fixed between dispatches. The left and right derivatives
obey the plant equations, including at updates. These derivative assumptions
are explicit; arbitrary irregular signals are not promised an execution.

The first measurement and its command dispatch are both at time zero.
Every later reading is causal and its TOTAL age stays at or below 1 ms on
both sides of each update. A 1 ms sample period alone does not establish this.

Illustration of total age during a later hold; these are example clock times:
  sample at 10.0 ms -> dispatch at 10.2 ms -> replace at 10.9 ms
  age just before replacement: 0.9 ms; this meets the age condition.
  replace instead at 11.3 ms -> age 1.3 ms; the theorem no longer applies.
Failure of that condition does not by itself prove unsafe motion.

What Lean proves under those conditions
For any supplied execution and any finite horizon H>=0, at every real time:
  |x-c| <= 0.055525 m; |v| <= 0.4442 m/s; |a| <= 3.5536 m/s^2.
  The whole transformed cube is preserved, not just these separate bands.
  |raw measured command| <= 1.4256 m/s^2, so clipping is inactive.
  |effective acceleration target| <= 1.4356 m/s^2.
The last bound is on the target in the lag equation, not actual acceleration.

The derived feedback mismatch bound is 0.1090800376 m/s^2. The available
inward-pointing budget is 0.11105 m/s^2. Their difference is
0.0019699624 m/s^2. This is proof slack, not a measured robustness margin or
an optimal timing limit. The first, coarser age bound was too large; one
further refinement closed the fixed test without changing its parameters.

What remains open
General existence is not kernel checked. The theorem assumes a supplied
execution. A written construction derives measurements from fixed clocks,
errors and the evolving state; it does not allow arbitrary measurements to
be fixed independently of that state. The checked witness has narrower scope.

The previous delayed capture reading cannot directly start this theorem.
A real handoff must prove safety during the fresh read and dispatch delay,
or extend the theorem to cover the existing command and measurement history.
Position, velocity, acceleration and the pending command must carry through.

No matched comparison or hardware test was run. These are illustrative
parameters, not measured axis limits. Controller rounding and real clock
implementation are outside this result. Novelty and practical value remain
open. The packet is for team review; it is not ready for paper submission.

Failures are preserved
The first coarse bound was insufficient. Source changes to a 5 ms age or a
50 mm radius fail this proof route; these are not plant counterexamples.
Compiler failures and an earlier replay during a source change are retained
in builder/attempts/. The final frozen source is the acceptance target.
