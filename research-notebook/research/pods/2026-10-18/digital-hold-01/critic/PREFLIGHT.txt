INDEPENDENT DIGITAL HOLD PREFLIGHT
18 October 2026. Critic: digital_hold_critic_01.
Scope: proposed 1 ms digital holding bridge. This is not final acceptance.

Result
The original 1 ms arithmetic screen is correct. It is conditional and does not
close sampled invariance. I found no arithmetic obstruction at 1 ms. The main
risks are the execution contract and a circular use of the mismatch budget.

Exact independent checks
The feedback derivative coefficients have absolute sum 128-4r for 8<=r<=12.
Thus with cube radius K and mismatch bound B, a valid slope bound is
  max(96K+4B, 80K+6B).
At B=2R this gives 104R=5.7746 m/s^3. A separate rational test checks the physical
and transformed derivative identities at 48 interior/nonvertex points.
The original 1 ms screen is .1087746 m/s^2 < .11105, with slack .0022754.
These checks prove scalar arithmetic, not the trajectory bridge.

I challenged the builder's tentative 92K coefficient. At r=8, z=(-K,K,0), w=0,
the absolute derivative is 96K. The builder was told to use at least 96K in an
r-independent bound. Its proposed two-refinement route still has slack:
  L0 = 208R + 6(M+D) = 12.1672 m/s^3;
  W0 = M+D + h L0 = .1151672 m/s^2;
  L1 = 96R + 6W0 = 6.0214032 m/s^3;
  W1 = M+D + h L1 = .1090214032 m/s^2 < .11105.
Here M=.093, D=.01 and h=.001 in SI units. W0 exceeds the desired budget, so it
cannot directly feed the old cube theorem. W1 has slack .0020285968. The prefix
argument, time integration and first-contact step still need proof.

A separate possible route uses a bounded prefix supremum B, with
  B <= M+D+h(96K+6B).
It gives B <=(.103+.096K)/.994. At K=R this is .108984305835... <2R. This is only
an algebraic route suggestion. A proof must first establish finite B without
assuming the desired mismatch bound. It must not integrate across an interval
for which its prefix hypothesis is unavailable.

Concrete failure controls and contract requirements
1. Causality: t-s<=h alone admits t=0,s=1. Require 0<=s<=dispatch<=t for the
   active command. At a dispatch boundary, state which command is left-active
   and which is right-active. Timestamp monotonicity is not a substitute for
   these pointwise inequalities. All sample states must lie in the proved past.
2. First command/history: a bound from t=0 needs a fresh initial reading and
   dispatch, or a certified prehistory and pending command. If the initial
   state is (x-c,v,a)=(R,0,0) and the pending target is +2 with r=12, then
   a(t)=2(1-exp(-12t))>0 for t>0. It follows that x(t)>c+R immediately. This
   violates containment despite bounded authority. At 1 ms the position excess
   is about 3.9880e-9 m. The analytic sign argument, not this number, establishes
   the counterexample to omitting the initial command law.
3. Derivative jumps: q=0 before a dispatch and q=.093 after it, with r=8 and
   zero state at dispatch, has left a'=0 and right a'=.744. The physical state
   is continuous but a has no two-sided derivative there. The inherited
   IsTrajectory requires a classical derivative at every interior time. It
   cannot be applied unchanged to this legal digital command jump.
4. Jumping mismatch: held commands and physical error can make w_total jump.
   A first-exit proof on |w_total| needs appropriate regularity or must avoid
   that argument. State continuity alone is not continuity of w_total.
5. State linkage: one physical state must span all command updates. Equality of
   position alone is insufficient. Velocity and acceleration cannot be reset.
6. Saturation: state whether the plant sees clip(q)+d, clip(q+d), or another
   signal. Under the proved cube, |q|<=1.4256 and |q+d|<=1.4356, both below2.
   This can show the chosen clipping is inactive. An unconstrained derivative
   premise is not itself a saturated implementation.
7. Existence: bounded arbitrary r and d plus a classical trajectory assumption
   do not prove trajectories exist. A finite/piecewise or one-sided contract
   can support conditional safety; general existence and uniqueness remain
   separate claims. A constant equilibrium witness proves nonemptiness only.
8. Finite dispatches and endpoints: cover horizon zero, dispatch at0, final
   horizon, exact age equality, sample=dispatch, and sample before the current
   interval. Exclude or handle Zeno dispatch accumulation explicitly.
9. Vacuity: a predicate requiring classical dynamics through incompatible
   jumps can make the theorem true on an unrealistically small execution class.
   Inspect a real held-command witness, not only an equilibrium witness.
10. Circularity: the final execution predicate must not assume cube invariance,
    a global feedback-slope bound, or the desired aggregate mismatch bound.
    Prefix cube assumptions are acceptable only inside a discharged barrier
    proof, not in the exported safety theorem's hypotheses.

Acceptance boundary
A checked theorem may establish finite-horizon cube containment for executions
of an explicit real-valued digital contract. Conditional-on-existence scope must
be visible. This would not establish a complete braking operation, eventual
sample acceptance, real-time implementation, finite-precision arithmetic, hardware
bounds, precision standstill, comparative merit, novelty or publication value.
The cube permits |v|<=.4442 m/s and |a|<=3.5536 m/s^2. These are coarse guaranteed
bands, not predicted motion or a precision-stop claim.
