What the dispatch witness adds
It materially reduces a specific vacuity concern. A legal sensor error at
0.5 ms changes the held command from zero to 0.001 m/s^2. One continuous
physical state then follows a nonconstant exact exponential flow. The
acceleration derivative is zero on the left and 0.008 m/s^3 on the right;
the source proves that no classical derivative exists there. Thus the
predicate admits a behavior that the old classical trajectory class excludes.

It is still only a two-cell, 1 ms witness from the zero state, with r=8/s,
zero physical target error and one small measurement error. It does not
show existence for every cube state, repeated long operation, delayed first
dispatch, changing-rate signals, or operation near the error/authority limits.
Those omissions limit the existence evidence, not the quantified safety claim
for any supplied execution that meets the predicate.

The handoff gap matters
The theorem requires a fresh measurement AND its applied holding command at
the new time zero. Its model has no first-command measurement/dispatch delay.
The earlier noisy capture theorem permits a reading up to 5 ms before the
actual switch. That reading cannot directly initialize this fresh-start tail.
A complete operation needs either a fresh-read/dispatch bridge whose whole
path is safe, or a proved initial-history extension. It must preserve x,v,a
and the actual pending command through that bridge.
