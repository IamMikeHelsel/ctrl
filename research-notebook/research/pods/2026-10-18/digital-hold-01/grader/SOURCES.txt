S1. Singletary, Chen, Ames, Control Barrier Functions for Sampled-Data
Systems with Input Delays (2020), arXiv v1.
https://arxiv.org/html/2005.06418v1
Inspected Sections II-IV, Proposition 1, Theorem 1, delay Assumptions 1-2,
Algorithm 1, Theorem 2 and Corollary 1.
Scope: zero-order held backup control, uncertainty sets, and known input
delay with an input-history buffer are prior work. The delay section assumes
an integer multiple of the sample period and an admissible initial zero-input
prefix. State-uncertainty treatment uses incremental stability. The paper
also discusses nonsmooth sampled flows and almost-everywhere conditions.
Boundary: this inspection does not establish a ready application to arbitrary
changing actuator response, the present sensor bounds, or this exact cube.
Inference: a fair comparison must expose those adaptations and initialization.

S2. van Wijk et al., Disturbance-Robust Backup Control Barrier Functions:
Safety Under Uncertain Dynamics (2024), arXiv v1.
https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07700v1
Inspected Section II-B and Section III through Theorem 2, including the
disturbed model, Assumption 1, tightened flow set and Lemma 3.
Scope: the paper assumes a robustly invariant backup set under a backup law.
It bounds deviation from a nominal flow under additive bounded disturbance.
Its stated existence discussion uses piecewise continuous disturbance and
locally Lipschitz feedback. This supports the overlap warning for terminal
backup composition; it does not itself supply our sampled hold certificate.
Boundary: the local multiplicative response error would need a justified
enclosure before being treated as a bounded additive disturbance. Sampled
feedback and arbitrary age are not silently inherited from this inspection.

S3. Hellwig et al., From Zonotopes to Proof Certificates: A Formal Pipeline
for Safe Control Envelopes (2025), arXiv v1.
https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20301v1
Inspected Section 2, Definition 2, Section 4 evaluation setup and the start
of the double-integrator example.
Scope: the paper states one-step invariance, intersample safety and command
admissibility as control-envelope obligations and formally links them to
safety. It connects numerical enclosure candidates with deductive checking.
Boundary: we did not execute its artifact or establish support for our
changing rate and stale-measurement history. The proposed direct-enclosure
benchmark follows these obligations and must document its own adaptations.

Conclusion
The fixed hold bridge can have value without a new controller. The inspected
sources prevent a novelty claim based only on held input, uncertain state,
delay, backup composition or formal intersample safety. They do not establish
that the exact planned interface is duplicated or that it offers an advance.
