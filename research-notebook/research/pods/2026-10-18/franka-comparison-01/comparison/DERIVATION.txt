B1 — DELAYED SAMPLED-DATA GOVERNOR ADAPTATION
18 October 2026. Builder derivation, pending independent review.

1. What is implemented

This is a bounded, explicitly disclosed adaptation of the reference-governor
architecture in Di Cairano, Kalabic and Kolmanovsky (2015), not a reproduction
of their continuous closed-loop plant or their spacecraft experiment.

Primary source: https://www.merl.com/publications/docs/TR2015-154.pdf
DOI: 10.1016/j.automatica.2015.09.006. Pinned PDF in inputs/.
Read equations (3), (5), (9)-(19), (23)-(30), Assumptions 2, 3, 12, 17, 18,
Lemma 10, Lemma 14, and the relevant proofs. Sections 2-4, printed pp.3-7
(PDF pp.5-9). Their (15) selects the closest requested reference subject to
next-state admissibility under a known delay. Equation (19) handles unknown
short delay by robust vertices. The unchanged-reference fallback removes
their delay-increment uncertainty. Their early model has exact state data;
Section 4 supplies an observer-error treatment. Remark 9 adds finite checks
between sampling times. It does not alone establish every-real-time safety.

Own adaptation: a known fixed command delay enters a sampled feedback loop,
with persistent measurement and target errors. We derive its exact augmented
map. A persistent-error block-invariant tail replaces an unjustified reuse
of the paper's no-change argument. Our estimator is a fresh measurement box,
not their observer. We use a finite prediction with a conservative terminal
subset and retain the last accepted constant-reference plan when a new box
is infeasible. We do not claim their recursive QP feasibility or convergence
theorem. This is existing reference-governor reasoning, not a novelty claim.

2. Common experiment contract

Use SI units and one physical state p=(x,v,a):
  x'=v; v'=a; a'=r(u+d-a), with known constant r=8 /s.
  |u|<=2 m/s^2. Clipping is BEFORE d. |d|<=.01 m/s^2.
  T=.001 s. Sample k at kT; compute from that sample; dispatch at kT+D,
  D=.0005 s. The old command remains active until dispatch.
  Pending u_-1=-1.21. The controller knows this value exactly.
  m_k=p(kT)+e_k; |e_k|<=(.001,.01,.05) in corresponding SI units.
  u_k=clip(8*c_k-[8,6,.5]m_k,[-2,2]).

For THIS experiment d is an unknown independent CONSTANT on each of the two
subintervals [kT,kT+D) and [kT+D,(k+1)T). Values can differ between halves.
This is an explicit common subclass of the older arbitrary-bounded-d model.
It is not valid to replace an arbitrary time-varying d by one scalar average
in each half; controls.py contains a counter-control for that reduction.

The controller receives the sample immediately; only its computed command
has the known D delay. No sensor-channel delay, dropout, packet overtaking,
variable response rate or unknown delay is included. At actuation, the held
sample age ranges from D to T+D=1.5 ms, with the old sample used on the left
of dispatch. Neither arm gets the true state. Both get the same exogenous
sensor-error and disturbance sequences, indexed by the same physical time.
Their measured values differ after their physical trajectories separate.

Both arms use the same requested position .14 m, travel limits [-.05,.15] m,
initial states, commands and clocks. The lower travel limit is an explicit
experimental restriction; the old C5 theorem only addressed forward travel.
The RG uses an auxiliary sample-speed certificate |v|<=.5 m/s. This is an
internal conservative check, not privileged plant information.

The C5 arm selects its center once from its actual first measurement, using
the exact policy in the copied ClearancePolicy.lean, and retains that center.
The requested target remains .14 m when computing all tracking errors. Its
center is not redefined as the requested target. Its old proof has total
age<=1 ms. The numerical extension here has age<=1.5 ms, so that old theorem
is not a justification for these runs. A separate T1 theorem, if accepted,
must be identified separately; it is not silently imported here.

3. Exact two-subinterval map

Let P(t)=exp(A_o*t), b(t)=integral_0^t exp(A_o*s) B_o ds, where
  A_o=[[0,1,0],[0,0,1],[0,0,-8]], B_o=[0,0,8]^T.
Let R=T-D, J=P(R)b(D), K=[8,6,1/2], E=[1,0,0,0]^T.
For unclipped feedback and eta=-K*e, augmented state X=[p,u_old] satisfies
  X_next=F*X+B*c+G*[eta,d_before,d_after],
  F=[[P(T)-b(R)K, J],[-K,0]], B=[8*b(R);8],
  G=[[b(R),J,b(R)],[1,0,0]].
Here |eta|<=.093, independently of |d_before|,|d_after|<=.01. The three-input
box is an outer bound on all allowed sensor errors. This transformation does
not remove the old-command memory. F*E+B=E, so equilibrium is X=E*c.

At the current decision the computed u is known from m, so it must not be
assigned a second sensor-error input. With epsilon=p-m, the FIRST step is
  X_1=F*[m,u_old]+B*c+H*epsilon+G0*[d_before,d_after],
  H=[P(T);0], G0=[[J,b(R)],[0,0]].
This is not F*[epsilon,0]. The first command depends on m, not hidden p.
controls.py compares both maps against separate analytic two-phase flows.

For a proposed constant c, at later sample h>=1 the nominal state is
  X_h=F^h*[m,u_old]+(E-F^h*E)*c.
For any row C, its uncertainty support is
  |C F^(h-1) H|*sensor_bounds
  + .01*sum |C F^(h-1) G0|
  + sum_(i=0)^(h-2) |C F^i G|*[.093,.01,.01].
Absolute values are componentwise; empty sums are zero. This formula retains
the first measurement uncertainty, both first physical disturbances and all
fresh errors at later samples.

Within a later cell, for 0<=tau<=D, use old=b(tau), new=0. For D<tau<=T,
use old=P(tau-D)b(D), new=b(tau-D). Physical state is
  p(tau)=[P(tau)-new*K,old]X_h+8*new*c+new*eta_h
         +old*d_before+new*d_after.
Before D the last term's new coefficient is zero. The same independent
constant disturbance in a half is correlated with its endpoint, but separate
support maxima remain sound upper bounds. In the current cell, epsilon
propagates by P(tau), and eta_h is absent because u(m) is already known.

4. Exact persistent-error terminal construction

Define z=S*(X-E*c), where
  z0=x-c; z1=x-c+v/4; z2=x-c+v/2+a/16;
  z3=(u_old+8*(x-c)+6*v+a/2)/24.
The fourth coordinate is essential. All numerical scalings use SI values.
Let A=S F S^-1, W={S G nu: |nu|<=(.093,.01,.01)} and B0=[-.014,.014]^4.
Set m=2000. For 0<=j<=m define the exact reachable polytope
  R_j=A^j B0 + sum_(i=0)^(j-1) A^i W.
Its coordinate support is
  b_j=|A^j|*[.014,.014,.014,.014]
      +sum_(i=0)^(j-1) |A^i S G|*[.093,.01,.01].
The integer-interval checker proves b_m<.014 componentwise for the exact
matrix, and bounds all b_j for 0<=j<m. Thus R_m is contained in B0.
The convex hull Omega=conv(union_(j=0)^(m-1) R_j) is robust positively
invariant: A*Omega+W is contained in conv(union R_(j+1)), which is contained
in Omega because R_m is contained in R_0. This is a finite polytope; we do
not claim it is the maximal admissible set. Repeated blocks also directly
show the all-future constant-reference property starting from B0.

terminal_interval.py uses integer outward intervals with quantum 10^-24.
It encloses exp(-.004) and exp(-.008) by the alternating Taylor terms 12 and
13, then encloses every matrix product and support sum with floor/ceiling
integer division. No floating exp or matrix product enters this check.
terminal-exact.json records exact rational endpoints and all eight tests.
The proof of the enclosure rules is written here, not formalized in Lean.
Independent review of the script and argument remains necessary.

Approximate values, for orientation only:
  b_m <= (.012892,.012879,.012875,.003912).
  max_j b_j <= (.014001,.014002,.014057,.014001).
The exact JSON bounds are authoritative. The block infinity norm is <1,
which also proves the exact sampled F is Schur after similarity transform.
This is stronger evidence than only checking eigenvalues of a binary64 F.

For c in Gamma=[-.03,.135], Omega obeys the travel and command requirements.
At sample times |v|<=4*(b0+b1), |a|<=16*(b0+2*b1+b2), and the next raw
command has magnitude <=8*(b0+b1+b2)+.093. The checker establishes these
are respectively below .5, 2.01 and 2. Thus clipping is inactive in the tail.
It also establishes c+/-b0 with the interpolation margin inside travel limits.

5. Finite prediction, intersample travel and fallback

The online program requires N=2000 steps of constant-reference prediction,
using the support formula above. It checks position at tau=0,T/4,T/2,3T/4,
sample speed |v|<=.5, and every raw new command in [-2,2]. Each position bound
is tightened by .000126 m. At N it requires the whole uncertain z_N in B0.
All constraints are affine inequalities in the ONE reference c. Their
intersection with Gamma is an interval. Select the projection of the SAME
requested .14 onto that interval; this solves the scalar squared-distance
objective. If the interval is empty after an earlier acceptance, keep the
last accepted constant reference. If the first interval is empty, fail the
case; do not invent a safe initial reference.

The command constraints make clipping inactive for every trajectory in an
exactly checked plan. Given |a(0)|<=2.01 and |u+d|<=2.01, the scalar lag ODE
keeps |a|<=2.01. Sample-speed bounds then give |v(t)|<=.50201 over each cell.
The maximum gap between position checks is T/4; the possible travel in one
gap is at most .50201/4000=.0001255025<.000126 m. This covers the real-time
intervals, including the last partial cell, not only the checked instants.
The invariant tail supplies the remaining samples and their intervening
intervals. This written argument is separate from numerical peak finding.

If every finite inequality is checked soundly, each accepted constant plan
is safe for all future allowed disturbances. Retaining that plan is safe even
if a freshly reset measurement box fails a later query. A new accepted plan
contains the actual state by the sensor bounds and known u_old. Induction on
accepted plans gives the same safety statement. We do NOT assert that the
fresh-box optimization itself is recursively feasible at every sample.

Critical implementation boundary: compare.py constructs and evaluates the
finite prediction in numpy/scipy binary64, with a 1e-10 inequality reserve.
That reserve has NOT been proved to enclose all construction/evaluation error.
Only the terminal block is independently enclosed with integer arithmetic.
Therefore this packet does NOT prove robust all-time safety of the executable
controller, despite supplying a complete mathematical terminal construction.
A validated finite-prediction evaluator is the exact remaining numerical
trust obligation. No Lean theorem for this adaptation is claimed.

6. Simulation and meaningful limits

The simulator uses the closed-form constant-input physical solution on each
half interval. Position extrema are sought at endpoints and every v=0 root.
Since a(t) has at most one zero in a constant-input segment, splitting there
brackets all velocity roots. scipy brentq locates these roots numerically.
This is more informative than a 1 kHz peak sample, but is still binary64
numerical evidence. It is not a worst-case proof over the uncertainty class.

The 12 case definitions were fixed before the first sanity run. No case was
added after a result. All scores are retained. In ALL 12 cases the RG chooses
c=.135 throughout. Its dynamic inequalities are evaluated but not binding;
Gamma's upper endpoint binds. These results do not measure the advantage of
online state adaptation. A separate OUTSIDE-C5-GUARD semantic witness
m=(.13,.08,-.2) makes c<=about .12807855 necessary for this certificate and
rejects the generic .135 clamp. That diagnostic is not a scored comparison.

The fixed-rate and subinterval-constant-d restrictions are intentional. A
varying-rate flow and a within-half d sign switch both give counter-controls
against pretending the exact augmented map covers a larger class. The
standstill target is also limited: constant eta+d=.103 allows equilibrium
position offset .103/8=.012875 m from c. Neither this controller nor C5 proves
precision standstill under those unchanged worst-case biases.
