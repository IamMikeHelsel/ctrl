PRECISION LIMIT OF THE PRESENT FIXED-REFERENCE HOLDING LAW
18 October 2026. Written algebra and exact rational witness; not a Lean theorem.

At a stationary unclipped equilibrium, the present feedback law gives
  x - c = -e_x - (3/4)e_v - (1/16)e_a + d/8.
With the declared independent sensor and target-error bounds, the right side
can be 0.012875 m. holding_bias_witness.py constructs such an equilibrium:
actual velocity and acceleration are zero, the command is -0.01 m/s², and
the target error +0.01 m/s² cancels it for every positive response rate.
Clipping is inactive. The arbitrary sample age has no effect on constant data.

This is a limitation of the current law and uncertainty description. It is
not a universal lower bound for all controllers or observers. It is not a
claim that this equilibrium is reachable from the recovery admission guard:
the example's acceleration reading does not satisfy that guard. It does show
why a tight stationary band around the chosen fixed reference does not follow
from the holding law and sensor bounds alone. Reducing the 45.9 mm proof band
to a small assembly tolerance needs more than a sharper enclosure: investigate
measurement assumptions, disturbance estimation, feedback design or adaptation.

Both sides of the comparison must retain the same requested target and error
bounds. A reference governor may change its auxiliary reference to recover the
requested target. That capability must be reported separately from the common
inner feedback law. Neither nominal trajectories nor this equilibrium witness
are a complete robust performance comparison.
