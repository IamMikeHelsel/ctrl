Exact saturated held-input actuator-lag bridge
============================================

Source: lean-controls/LeanControls/SaturatedLag.lean
Namespace: LeanControls.SaturatedLag

Model and semantics
-------------------
State order is [position x, velocity v, acceleration a]. The normalized actuator
time constant is one. The sampled feedback request is input(z)=-x-3v-2a.
During one interval, the applied input is u=clip[-1,1](input(z))+w, with both
the sampled request and additive disturbance w held constant. The exact model
is x'=v, v'=a, a'=-a+u. The disturbance is added after clipping: the clipped
command is limited to one, while total input including w need not be.

heldSegment is the analytic exponential solution for an arbitrary held u.
segment uses the actual clipped request plus w. Component derivative theorems
and segment_ode establish the equations for that exact model. segment_zero
establishes its initial state. These facts impose no discretization error.

response(t)=[t^2/2-t+1-exp(-t), t-1+exp(-t), 1-exp(-t)].
heldSegment_add_input proves the exact disturbance response identity. Under
|input(z)|<=4, segment_sector_decomposition chooses one alpha in [1/4,1]
such that, simultaneously for every t and w,

  segment(t,z,w)=nominalSegment(t,z,alpha)+w*response(t).

The chosen alpha depends on the initial request, not on elapsed time or w.
The actual saturated feedback is retained. Requests between one and four in
magnitude can genuinely clip; no inactive-saturation assumption is made.

Four-vertex bridge
------------------
segmentInExp exposes a common scalar exponential parameter e in all three
coordinates. Its dependence is affine in e at fixed alpha and affine in alpha
at fixed e. Four exact coordinate identities connect (alpha,e) equal to
(1/4,1-t), (1/4,1-t+t^2), (1,1-t), (1,1-t+t^2) to the four generated Travel
transition matrices. These identities are symbolic Lean equalities.

ActuatorLag.exp_interpolation supplies exp(-t) between 1-t and 1-t+t^2 on
0<=t<=1, including t=0. nominal_enclosed then supplies a nested convex
combination of the four transitions for every alpha in [1/4,1]. The gain
interpolation parameter is (4*alpha-1)/3. Both parameters are proved in [0,1].

All four generated matrices share exactly the same positive quadratic P:
  [[38/5,257/25,5], [257/25,1319/50,769/50], [5,769/50,278/25]].
Using their checked decrease certificates and quadratic convexity,
nominal_energy_decrease proves, for all initial z, all alpha in [1/4,1],
and all t in [0,1/5],

  energy(nominalSegment(t,z,alpha)) <= energy(z)-t*squaredNorm(z).

nominal_energy_nonexpansion is the resulting upper bound by initial energy.
This is no overshoot relative to the initial sample. It does not assert
pairwise monotonicity between arbitrary times within the interval.

Scope and remaining composition
-------------------------------
This module alone does not establish a disturbed invariant, position limit,
all-real-time closed-loop bound, or safety under arbitrary varying continuous
disturbances. Those require bounds on response, ellipsoid geometry, recurrence
and sampling-clock coverage. It exposes the exact equalities needed for that
composition. There is no quantization, sensing noise, floating-point error,
hardware calibration, changing lag constant, or actuator rate limit here.
