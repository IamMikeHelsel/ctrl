THINK OFTEN, SWITCH SMOOTHLY
A bounded Lean result for frequent robot replanning
17 October 2026

The useful distinction is between planning a new motion and committing it to the
robot. A planner can update often. The executor should accept an update only if
it joins the motion already in progress and satisfies the motion limits.

A minimum wait between updates is insufficient. After any wait, the next plan can
still demand an acceleration jump. Conversely, a matching replacement can keep
a fixed jerk bound without a minimum wait. Jerk is the rate of acceleration change.

WHAT WE NOW CHECK

SmoothReplan.lean is a scalar command-level result. A segment has position q,
velocity v, acceleration a and constant jerk j. Its exact motion is

  q(t) = q0 + v0 t + a0 t²/2 + j t³/6,
  v(t) = v0 + a0 t + j t²/2,
  a(t) = a0 + j t.

Admission checks a positive duration h and ten finite conditions: a jerk limit,
four position control points, three velocity control points and two acceleration
control points. The position controls are

  q0,
  q0 + v0 h/3,
  q0 + 2 v0 h/3 + a0 h²/6,
  q(h).

If these four numbers are inside the position interval, the entire cubic segment
is inside it. This follows from the Bernstein convex-hull property. Parallel
checks give the velocity and acceleration bounds. These are sufficient checks;
failing a control-point check does not always mean a trajectory is unsafe.

The Lean statements establish:

- Exact derivatives for the segment.
- Continuous-time position, velocity, acceleration and jerk bounds from Admission.
- Replacement from the current executed command state matches q, v and a.
- If two motions match q, v and a at their switch, then q'=v and v'=a still hold
  at every time, including the switch, and acceleration stays continuous.
- Matching acceleration preserves |a(t)-a(s)| <= J |t-s|. The same J survives
  any finite certified replacement history. Jerk itself may jump.
- A replacement leaves its committed prefix unchanged.
- A separately established tracking-error bound transfers a shrunken command
  position interval to the actual position interval.
- A single acceleration jump after any waiting time violates every finite jerk
  bound. Thus a minimum dwell time alone does not solve this problem.

This is a finite-history theorem. It does not cover infinitely many switches in
finite time. A fixed positive servo period is one way to exclude that behavior.
For prefix preservation, the switch must occur at or after the current execution
time. The history theorem alone does not impose chronological scheduling.

A CONCRETE ACCEPT/REJECT PAIR

These are illustrative translation units, not identified robot limits.
Start with q=0 m, v=0.1 m/s, a=0 m/s² and h=0.020 s. Hold the same limits for all
candidates: q in [-0.01,0.01] m, |v|<=0.2 m/s, |a|<=1 m/s², |j|<=10 m/s³.

  Candidate                                  Decision
  j=10; endpoint 0.002013333... m             Accepted in Lean
  Next 20 ms: j=-10 from the executed state   Accepted in Lean
  New endpoint 0.0025 m over the first 20 ms  Rejected in Lean

The last target requires j=375 m/s³ under this constant-jerk construction.
It fits the position interval, but it violates the unchanged jerk limit.
The second accepted segment reverses jerk without resetting acceleration. Its
final state is q=0.00408 m, v=0.104 m/s, a=0. This arithmetic is exact rational
arithmetic in examples.json and the segment admissions are Lean checked.

For one constant-jerk segment, Lean also proves the exact endpoint condition

  |q(h) - (q0 + v0 h + a0 h²/2)| <= J h³/6.

This sets a motion horizon for a target correction. It does not force the planner
to wait that long before thinking again. The theorem is not general reachability
for prescribed final position, velocity and acceleration.

WHAT ALREADY EXISTS

1. RTC, Black, Galliker and Levine (2025), runs policy inference while the robot
   executes the current action chunk. It conditions the next chunk on the prefix
   already committed during inference delay. Its soft mask improves continuation;
   Section 4.2 says hard-prefix guidance can still permit strategy changes.
   It has a minimum execution horizon and reports robot experiments. Our result
   adds an exact, conditional execution check; it does not replace RTC or prove
   RTC unsafe. We did not run its policy or audit its implementation.
   Paper: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07339v1
   Authors' code: https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/real-time-chunking-kinetix

2. Training-time RTC, Black et al. (2025), conditions on action prefixes during
   training to remove inference-time inpainting cost. Continuation itself is
   therefore not an unexplored idea.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05964

3. Legato, Liu et al. (2026), trains native continuation. Its stride ablation
   separates boundary agreement from whole-trajectory smoothness: more frequent
   updates can improve overlap consistency while worsening global smoothness.
   Its smoothness metrics evaluate model commands, not actual robot states.
   This supports our caution that a local jerk bound does not prove decisive,
   efficient, human-like task behavior.
   https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12978v1

4. Adaptive Action Chunking, Liang et al. (CVPR 2026), uses action entropy to vary
   the execution horizon. It targets the responsiveness/consistency tradeoff.
   It is not a proof that a minimum wait implies bounded jerk.
   https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04161v1

5. VLASH, Tang et al. (2025 preprint), predicts the future execution-time state
   to address the difference between state at inference and state at execution.
   Our splice must also use the state at the actual switch. A stale start state
   does not meet the matching premise.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01031
   Authors' code: https://github.com/mit-han-lab/vlash

6. Ruckig, Berscheid and Kroeger (RSS 2021), already generates jerk-limited motion
   from arbitrary initial and target position, velocity and acceleration. The
   present constant-jerk certificate is much narrower. We do not claim a new
   trajectory generator, optimality, or equivalence to Ruckig's implementation.
   https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss17/p015.pdf
   Authors' code and documented usage: https://github.com/pantor/ruckig

7. Bernstein trajectory constraint checking is established. The BeBOT work gives
   continuous-time constraints using Bernstein geometry. Our small Lean proof is
   a checked instance of that mathematics, not a new convex-hull theorem.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09992
   Authors' code: https://github.com/caslabuiowa/BeBOT

PRACTICAL INTERPRETATION AND NEXT WORK

A low-level executor could receive candidate VLA targets, preserve commands that
are already committed, create a matching motion segment, and admit that segment
only if the certificate passes. A rejected target can be delayed, modified, or
replaced by a previously certified backup. This admission layer can complement
RTC, Legato, or another planner.

No fallback is automatically safe just because the new target was rejected.
The old motion must retain a valid backup or stop reserve. An emergency should
preempt a dwell preference. It still needs its own physical bounds. If emergency
motion uses a larger jerk bound, the original smoothness guarantee no longer
applies during that mode.

Our command proof is not a proof of actual end-effector motion. Physical tracking
error must be bounded separately. Rotation, inverse kinematics, joint limits,
singularities, contact force, friction, torque and tactile response are open.
Applying independent scalar bounds to coordinates does not prove collision
avoidance or safe dexterous contact. Matching commanded acceleration also does
not establish that the motor has that actual acceleration.

Bounded jerk can still allow small repeated reversals and indecision. We have
not proved task progress, grasp persistence, optimality, or human-like control.
A plausible next step is a progress test or mode hysteresis above this admission
layer, with an explicit emergency exception. That needs a task-specific merit
function; a generic timer cannot establish progress.

The publication opportunity is not yet established. A useful next experiment is
an identical recorded stream of asynchronous proposed updates, including delay,
state error, rapid target reversals and emergency triggers. Compare raw swapping,
minimum dwell, RTC-style continuation, Ruckig, and certified admission. Measure
constraint violations, response latency, task completion, rejected updates and
backup use. The exact certificate should be checked on the same executed trace.
A contribution may emerge from uncertainty-aware admission plus a certified stop
reserve. That connection has not yet been proved for the existing braking plant.
