Why a braking model needs more than velocity

For x₀=0 and v₀=a₀=U=τ=1, immediate maximum braking gives x(1)=1/2+2/e>1/2. The familiar instantaneous-acceleration guard v₀²/(2U)=1/2 is insufficient for this lagged actuator. This is a model mismatch, not a refutation of the double-integrator formula. The numerical stopping-distance diagnostic is about 2.6051; that decimal is not an interval-certified bound.

Delay adds another issue: a fast actuator can worsen forward acceleration while a command is pending, whereas a slow actuator worsens subsequent braking. A constant unknown lag must retain the correlation between those phases. Zero velocity also leaves residual acceleration, so one-sided stopping clearance is not by itself a two-sided, all-time safety controller.

Prior work constrains the contribution claim

Backup-set methods already turn backup flows and terminal sets into safety mechanisms. Robust backup methods already address uncertainty. Our potential contribution is a complete checked instance with a useful admitted region, explicit implementation assumptions and a reproducible comparison—not the invention of backup control.
