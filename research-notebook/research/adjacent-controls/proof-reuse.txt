COMMAND AGE / DELAY: MORE USEFUL, WITH A REAL ADMISSION GAP
Proposed model at actuator-update time T_n: the held request is computed from
z(T_n-a_n), with age a_n>=0, plus a held request error eta_n. Drive error d_n is
after clipping. Relative to current-state feedback, the extra held request
error is e_n=k(z(T_n-a_n))-k(z(T_n))+eta_n. The existing theorem applies ONLY
after proving |e_n|+|d_n|<=the budget. Assuming this inequality as an input and
calling it a delay theorem would add little.

Concrete next theorem: establish a timestamp-based admission rule and initial
history contract that imply that error inequality, then transfer safety for
all continuous solutions of the delayed sampled equations. The current code
has neither history/timestamp semantics nor a proved request-change estimate.
Feedback delay during a hold creates two command regimes and must be modeled
at actual actuator update times, rather than silently changing the held model.

A conservative feasibility calculation (not a Lean theorem here): k'=-v+
(2r-3)a-2r*u. For V<=12, dual quadratic geometry gives a bound on the first
state-dependent term via 12*L_r*P^-1*L_r^T, L_r=(0,-1,2r-3). Its endpoint
values are3643746/1391479 and1444626/1391479; convexity in r bounds the entire
rate interval. With |u|<=1+1/90 this implies |k'|<4. Thus a sufficient candidate
is 4*a_n+|eta_n|+|d_n|<=1/100. If all .01 is assigned to age, a_n<=1/400=.0025
normalized time; at nominal tau0=.1s this is only .25ms. This is a WARNING about
conservatism/usefulness, not a verified engineering delay margin. Initial
history must obey the region/slope conditions, or acquisition must begin with
fresh state. A proof should use already-safe earlier segments to bound age
error at the next update, avoiding an assumption of the desired invariant.

Credible development: improve this to state-dependent admission using the
actual clipped-request difference or a checked local reachable set. Saturation
can make stale requests equal after clipping even when raw requests differ.
Prove the time/measurement/error transfer, not merely a norm inequality. Obtain
one explicit nonzero delay or useful age region, one rejected over-age example,
and compare conservatism against a published baseline and numerical reachability.
Next block: 1-2 sessions for history/admission semantics and conservative bound;
then bounded certificate design for usefulness. Stop/pivot if only sub-millisecond
bounds survive at plausible model scales, or if improvement requires shrinking
the admitted region so far that active saturation disappears. No novelty claim
is justified before targeted prior-work review of sampled saturation with delay.

DROPOUTS: SEPARATE THE EASY COROLLARY FROM A NEW MODEL
If packet success means fresh feedback is applied immediately, the maximum
successful-update gap is<=.2, and r is held over that whole gap, dropouts are
already irregular sampling. For nominal update period p, at most m consecutive
misses gives gap<=(m+1)p; e.g. p=.05 and m=3 yields .2. Formalizing this schedule
arithmetic is useful but routine. Do not call h<=.2 alone a packet-loss model.
If actuator rate changes at missed base-clock updates while the old command
persists, the current held-rate segment theorem does not directly apply.
Likewise delayed delivery, stale packets, reordering and networked measurements
need explicit command/timestamp semantics. A combined age/gap admission theorem
with a tested packet policy is a more credible artifact than another jitter
corollary. No stochastic packet-loss or probability guarantee is implied.

EVENT-TRIGGERED SAFETY: A POLICY MUST EARN ITS TIMING GUARANTEE
Current safety is conditional on hmin<=h_n<=.2. A state-dependent choice inside
that interval is already allowed, but this does not prove that an event trigger
exists, is implementable, or cannot produce Zeno updates. A timeout alone gives
the upper bound; a cooldown/base polling clock can supply the lower bound.
A trigger that could require an update before cooldown expires needs a proved
safe waiting interval, rather than assuming its event times satisfy hmin.

Candidate useful theorem: define a sampled trigger using a local energy/age
certificate, a mandatory timeout, and a positive minimum polling interval;
prove its recursion is total, the held-error admission condition at every
accepted event, non-Zeno timing, and all-time safety. A policy selecting the
largest certified next hold could also quantify fewer updates on a specified
trajectory family. Compare with periodic .2 under the SAME errors/region/rate
box; a larger theorem interface or a simulated mean event rate alone is not a
contribution. Polling/max-gap wrappers alone are routine. A checked causal rule
with less conservative holds and a useful delay/dropout contract is the more
promising package. Continuous monitoring/instant actuation must not be implied
for a digital polling implementation.

RECOMMENDATION
Use sample-varying held rates as a small verified extension, not the paper's
main novelty. Prioritize a deterministic timestamp/command-age admission layer,
then optional polling-trigger/dropout scheduling on that same layer. This reuses
the verified plant/sector/energy/uniqueness work while closing a real execution
assumption gap. Publication value remains conditional on useful constants,
clear comparison and literature differentiation; this assessment is not a
prior-art clearance or a claim of new control mathematics.
