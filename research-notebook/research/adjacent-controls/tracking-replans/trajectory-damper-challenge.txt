Trajectory damper: independent mathematical challenge
17 October 2026

The useful claim
A stateful command smoother can remove jumps in the requested acceleration
target. It does not automatically remove physical jerk, tracking lag, or
oscillation. The next contract should state which signal becomes smoother,
what uncertainty regularity is required, and how much tracking budget is spent.
This note proposes mathematics. It does not alter the frozen tracking proof.

Signals and the exact jump condition
The current plant is x'=v, v'=a, a'=r(t)(u+w-a). Write b=u+w-a and physical
jerk j_a=a'=r*b. A filter state u with u'=nu and |nu|<=L_u is continuous and
Lipschitz. At a switch where u and a are continuous and one-sided limits exist,
  jump(j_a) = (r_plus-r_minus)*(u+w_minus-a)
              + r_plus*(w_plus-w_minus).
Thus continuous u alone cannot ensure continuous physical jerk. The exact
no-jump condition is r_plus*(u+w_plus-a)=r_minus*(u+w_minus-a).
Continuous r and w are sufficient, but not individually necessary: their
changes could cancel. A useful uncertainty contract must say how this product
is controlled rather than assuming continuity of the desired jerk conclusion.

A full counterexample under the present piecewise-classical scope
Take u=0 and r=8. Before time zero, take w=a=v=x=0. After zero, let w=1 and
  a(t)=1-exp(-8t),
  v(t)=t-(1-exp(-8t))/8,
  x(t)=t^2/2-t/8+(1-exp(-8t))/64.
The physical state is continuous at zero and solves the ODE on both open
sides. The command is perfectly smooth. One-sided physical jerk changes from
0 to 8. This is an admissible finite switch under a magnitude-only disturbance
contract. A single globally classical ODE at that switch would exclude the
example by assumption; the current finite-segment semantics do not.
Changing r from 8 to 12 while b is a nonzero constant at a switch gives the
same issue without a disturbance jump: jerk changes by 4*b.

Three levels of useful regularity
1. Bounded jerk magnitude. Given |r|<=r_max and |u+w-a|<=B, the ODE gives
|j_a|<=r_max*B. No continuity of r or w is needed on open segment interiors.
A possible explicit B is U+W+A when |u|<=U, |w|<=W and |a|<=A. With a
nonnegative rate and a bounded effective target, ordinary lag comparison can
use A=max(|a(0)|,U+W). This magnitude result does not bound jerk jumps or snap.

2. Continuous jerk. Continuous u, r, w and a give continuous r*(u+w-a).
For matching piecewise trajectories, this extends a' continuously across the
switch. Position then has a continuous third derivative. A clipped derivative
nu may jump, so the fourth derivative need not be continuous.

3. Bounded jerk variation. Suppose r, u and w are Lipschitz, with constants
L_r, L_u and L_w, and the above bounds r_max and B hold. Bounded jerk implies
that a is Lipschitz with constant J_a=r_max*B. The product inequality gives
  |j_a(t)-j_a(s)| <= S*|t-s|,
  S = L_r*B + r_max*(L_u+L_w+r_max*B).
This bounds snap wherever the derivatives exist; for Lipschitz signals that
means almost everywhere. If the signals are differentiable at the time of
interest, the identity is
  a''=r'*(u+w-a)+r*(u'+w'-a').
Continuity alone supplies no numerical values for L_r or L_w. The filter's
slew limit supplies L_u only.

A weaker finite-variation contract is also possible. If r and w have bounded
total variation on a time interval of length T, then
  TV(j_a) <= B*TV(r)+r_max*(L_u*T+TV(w)+r_max*B*T).
This permits jumps and bounds their accumulated magnitude. It does not bound
instantaneous snap or make jerk continuous. It may fit switching uncertainty
better than assuming bounded derivatives everywhere.

Smooth bounded disturbances still defeat any universal snap bound
Take u=0, constant r>0, a(0)=0, and w(t)=W*sin(omega*t), W>0.
The exact smooth acceleration is
  a(t)=r*W/(r^2+omega^2)*
       (r*sin(omega*t)-omega*cos(omega*t)+omega*exp(-r*t)).
It satisfies a'(0)=0 and a''(0)=r*W*omega. Increasing omega makes snap
arbitrarily large without changing the disturbance magnitude W. Thus even
smoothness of each individual signal does not give a uniform snap bound over
the allowed family. Quantitative frequency, derivative, or variation bounds
are needed. This counterexample is mathematical here, not Lean-formalized.

The filter changes the proved controller
Let u_star be the already proved feedback/feedforward target and let the plant
receive a continuous filtered u. Then d=u-u_star acts as another additive
target error. The old tracking proof can be reused only if a justified bound
|d|<=D is charged to its budget:
  W+D+J/(48*sigma) <= 2*sigma^2*R.
Assuming small d would not prove the filter safe. A standalone conditional
composition is useful, but it must expose that unresolved premise.

For the un-clipped first-order filter tau*u'=u_star-u, tau>0, suppose u_star
has derivative magnitude at most L between finitely many known jumps.
Starting after any initial jump, the error formula gives
  |d(t)| <= exp(-t/tau)*|d(0)| + tau*L*(1-exp(-t/tau))
            + sum_k exp(-(t-t_k)/tau)*|jump(u_star,t_k)|,
where the sum includes positive switch times t_k<=t. This is a transient bound.
It requires a justified L. In feedback, u_star depends on the physical state,
so using an assumed tracking cube to obtain L can be circular. An augmented
invariant or a closed bootstrap argument must resolve that dependence.
A clipped slew limiter has different error dynamics; the same exponential
formula cannot be silently reused once clipping is active.

A state-dependent replan admission rule
When physical state and reference position, velocity and acceleration match
at a switch, u_star changes only by kappa*Delta(j). A continuous filter state
therefore gives the exact mismatch transfer
  d_plus=d_minus-kappa*Delta(j).
A new jerk can be admitted only when the chosen mismatch budget still holds:
|d_minus-kappa*Delta(j)|<=D. Admissibility on both sides necessarily requires
|kappa*Delta(j)|<=2D. This is a state-dependent feasibility test, not a timer.
It does not prove that |d|<=D is maintained between switches.

The existing precision example exposes the cost
For sigma=10, R=0.2 mm, W=0.01 m/s^2 and J=10 m/s^3,
  D_max=1/25-1/100-1/48=11/1200 m/s^2.
The jerk coefficient is kappa=1/96 s. A flip from -10 to +10 m/s^3 changes
u_star by 5/24 m/s^2. This exceeds 2D_max=11/600 m/s^2.
No continuous filter state can lie within D_max of both one-sided targets.
The best instantaneous centering still requires mismatch at least 5/48 m/s^2.
The same pointwise sufficient tube certificate therefore cannot pass through
this filter unchanged. This does not prove that physical position leaves the
tube. A transient-aware certificate may allow brief, larger mismatch.

Which kind of smoothness is being tested?
A smooth time trace can still oscillate indefinitely. A jerk limit alone does
not identify unwanted motion or prove useful progress. A geometric path can
look straight while its speed changes sharply; smooth timing and path shape
are different properties. A small sampled RMS error is also different from a
uniform worst-case bound. Report the reference, filtered input, actual motion,
tracking lag, jerk magnitude and jerk variation separately.
The scalar model has no flexible modes or contact impulses. It also does not
represent a free-floating base and arm as a coupled mechanical system. Those
contexts require additional dynamics, even if a common smoothing architecture
is useful. No universal arm, human-neurology, or new-method claim follows here.
