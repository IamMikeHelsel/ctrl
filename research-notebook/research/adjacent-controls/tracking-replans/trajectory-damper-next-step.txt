WHEN THE PLAN CHANGES, KEEP THE MOTION
Research question and next proof — 17 October 2026

The candidate gap is a checked connection between frequent new plans and smooth
physical execution. Industrial robots already shape paths and filter motion.
Our question is how to retain those benefits when targets, timing and actuator
response change, while keeping a stated tracking bound and a feasible stop.
This is a research hypothesis. We have not established that the combination is new.

A concrete task is a robot carrying a part toward a fixture. Vision keeps revising
the fixture location. The robot should absorb small corrections without repeated
jolts. It must also respond to a large correction and retain room to stop. Strong
filtering can improve motion while adding delay or rounding the path. A useful
method must account for each cost. Our present proof covers one modeled axis;
it does not yet cover the full arm, contact, perception or network execution.

WHAT THE EXISTING WORK ESTABLISHES

The finished tracking package has 34 checked statements, including helper lemmas.
It bounds actual tracking error under changing actuator response and matched
finite replans. The five-page note, numerical comparison and animation remain a
separate frozen artifact. Their verification record is verification.json.

CommandSmoothing.lean adds seven checked statements. The new verification record
is smoothing-verification.json. Fresh source builds, the axiom audit and separate
kernel replay passed. A false doubled-allowance statement was rejected. The
independent challenge is in trajectory-damper-challenge.txt.

The main new result is conditional. Let u* be the original acceleration command,
u the filtered command, and D a bound on |u-u*| throughout the segment. Then the
earlier physical tracking bound still holds if

    W + D + J/(48 sigma) <= 2 sigma^2 R.

W bounds the actuator disturbance in acceleration units. J bounds planned jerk.
R is the position-error radius. The actual response rate must lie between
8 sigma and 12 sigma. The initial transformed error must lie in the same cube as
before. The proof also retains the original trajectory and derivative conditions.
The source gives the exact statement. This result does not prove that a chosen
filter maintains D.

At a matched replan, the filter state must survive. If the planned jerk changes
by delta-j, the old command law changes by

    delta-u* = 5 delta-j/(48 sigma).

A continuous filter cannot follow that jump instantly. Its mismatch transfers as

    d+ = d- - delta-u*.

This is why a smooth reference alone is insufficient. A controller can reintroduce
a command jump through jerk feedforward. Resetting the filter at each replan would
remove the very continuity we want to preserve.

The 0.2 mm illustration makes the conflict concrete. At sigma=10, W=0.01 m/s^2
and J=10 m/s^3, the remaining pointwise filter budget is only 11/1200 m/s^2.
A planned-jerk reversal from -10 to +10 changes u* by 5/24 m/s^2. No continuous
filter state fits that same small mismatch allowance on both sides. Lean checks
this incompatibility. It rejects transfer of that sufficient certificate; it
does not show that actual position leaves the allowed region. A bound that uses
the duration of the transient could permit more than this pointwise bound.

Continuous command also does not ensure continuous physical jerk if response rate
or disturbance can jump. A quantitative jerk-variation bound needs a bound on their
combined effect. Separate bounds on rate and disturbance variation are one
sufficient approach. The Lean file checks the exact jump identity. The challenge
note also gives a mathematical trajectory example; that full example is not formalized.

WHAT THE INDUSTRIAL SOURCES ADD

FANUC's official driver documentation identifies timing jitter as a possible
cause of drift or vibration. Its stated remedies include more buffering or a
lower update rate. Yaskawa's external-motion guide distinguishes native buffered
planning from incremental control and warns about overly dense target points.
Its SMOOTH guide describes motion filtering with path and response tradeoffs.
These are specific interface constraints, not evidence of inferior research arms.

The six official sources, their limits and the proposed fair comparison are in
industrial-motion-baselines.txt and industrial-motion-sources.json. Existing
academic baselines are in trajectory-damper-prior.txt. DMPs, online jerk-limited
generation and command filtering already address parts of this problem. Human
motion sources guide useful requirements; they do not identify a single neural
damper that can be copied into a robot controller.

THE NEXT THEOREM TO ATTEMPT

Prove one retained-state motion layer, with a precise rule for accepting a new
plan. Start with an augmented state: physical tracking error plus filter error.
Prove a bound between updates and a bound at each accepted update. The update
rule must use the current filter error; a fixed minimum replan interval alone
does not describe how large a new command may be.

For the present certificate, a necessary handoff test is

    |d- - 5 delta-j/(48 sigma)| <= D.

Passing that test alone is not enough. The filter dynamics must keep the bound
until the next update. Deriving a command-rate bound by assuming the tracking
bound we are trying to prove would be circular. Use a joint invariant or an
explicit continuation argument. A slew-clipped filter needs its own dynamics.

Then include commands already committed to a buffer and a bound on their age.
The fallback must preserve a feasible stop if a plan arrives late or is rejected.
Our separate stopping work is a candidate component, but its assumptions and
handoff must be proved compatible. Progress toward a goal needs an assumption
such as a persistent feasible target; arbitrary target changes cannot guarantee
arrival. These parts remain open.

THE TEST THAT CAN SUPPORT OR REJECT THE IDEA

Use the same plant, task, load, limits and measurements for each method. Compare
a competent native blended baseline, an established online motion generator, and
our retained-state layer. On an industrial arm, distinguish native buffered
planning from externally generated increments. Do not manufacture a weak baseline
with stop-between-points calls or missing tool data.

Use a fixed repeated path, an unseen correction/reversal, delayed or irregular
updates, and an approach to a travel limit with a rejected plan. Report actual
path error, settling time, vibration, jerk, command age, buffer occupancy, rejected
updates and remaining stop margin. Define measurement bandwidth and differentiation
before comparing jerk. Separate simulation evidence from hardware evidence.

A publication claim needs a clear gain or a useful limitation that existing
methods do not already cover. If an established baseline meets the same contract
with equal or better results, narrow the contribution to the verification method
or stop claiming a new control benefit. The current negative filter-budget result
is useful because it tells us exactly what the next construction must overcome.
