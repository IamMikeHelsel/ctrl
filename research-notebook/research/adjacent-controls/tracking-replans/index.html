When plans change,
how far can a robot stray?

A smooth plan says where we want the axis to move. The real axis can lag behind. We have proved a bound on that difference for a scalar actuator whose response changes inside a known band. The bound survives any finite sequence of properly joined plans.

Finished: 34 checked Lean statements, a 144-case comparison, an independent challenge, and the illustrated note below. This is a conditional model proof. The real actuator's bounds still need measurement.

What the proof lets us say

If the rate, disturbance, initial error and planned jerk stay within the stated bounds, actual position stays within a radius R of planned position. Keep the plan at least R away from a travel limit and the actual axis also respects that limit. Each new reference must join the old reference in position, speed and acceleration. The actual state does not reset.

One illustrative specification permits a 0.2 mm position tube with actual lag rate 80 to 120 per second, target error at most 0.01 m/s², and planned jerk at most 10 m/s³. Those are assumptions to test. Increasing gains on a slow actuator does not establish the faster response band.

The comparison does not give a universal winner
