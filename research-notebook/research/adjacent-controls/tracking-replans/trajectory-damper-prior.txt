TRAJECTORY DAMPERS: EXISTING BASELINES AND THE NEXT PROOF GAP
Primary-source check — 17 October 2026

A stateful trajectory damper is a useful component, and close versions already
exist. It is not missing from all generated motion. The stronger research question
is what a damper can guarantee when new plans keep arriving, the physical response
is uncertain, and a safe stop must remain possible. This check does not establish
that nobody has solved that combined problem.

FOUR PRIMARY BASELINES

1. Critically damped target dynamics and dynamic movement primitives (DMPs).
Ijspeert, Nakanishi, Hoffmann, Pastor and Schaal, 2013, Dynamical Movement
Primitives: Learning Attractor Models for Motor Behaviors, Neural Computation.

Section 2.1.1 starts with a second-order spring attractor and chooses critical
damping. A learned forcing term adds movement shape. Section 2.1.8 is especially
close: changing the goal can create an acceleration jump. Equation 2.15 filters
the new goal through a first-order state, tau*g_dot=alpha_g*(g0-g), so g remains
continuous. This is prior work on the proposed stateful motion layer. Basic DMP
attractor behavior does not itself supply our hard actuator, travel, contact-force
or uncertain-plant certificate. Section 3.4 also discusses generalization problems,
including motions that risk joint limits.
Primary paper, sections 2.1.1, 2.1.8 and 3.4:
https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/7874487/NECO_a_00393.pdf

2. Acceleration- and jerk-limited online generation: Ruckig.
Berscheid and Kroeger, 2021, Jerk-limited Real-time Trajectory Generation with
Arbitrary Target States, Robotics: Science and Systems.

Ruckig computes synchronized motion from complete initial states to target
position, velocity and acceleration, subject to velocity, acceleration and jerk
limits. Its model is explicitly kinematic. Section VI demonstrates online reactions
and robot use. The maintained API carries the current generated state forward
with pass_to_input; a changed input can trigger a new trajectory. It is a strong
baseline for repeated target changes, not just a fixed offline curve. Its listed
kinematic guarantees do not by themselves prove bounded physical tracking error,
contact force or safe progress under arbitrary target reversals.
Primary paper, sections III and VI:
https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss17/p015.pdf
Author-maintained implementation, control-loop usage:
https://github.com/pantor/ruckig

3. Reference filters and command-filtered control.
Farrell, Polycarpou, Sharma and Dong, 2008 conference paper; 2009 journal paper,
Command Filtered Backstepping.

The conference introduction already treats bounded, possibly discontinuous
planner output as input to a stable stateful prefilter. Its states provide smooth
reference derivatives; bandwidth sets a response tradeoff. The paper then studies
filters inside a feedback design, including compensation for filter error. The
journal paper analyzes stability and performance. Thus adding a low-pass filter
is established, and treating its lag as harmless is not justified. A filter at the
reference input and a filter on the actuator command change different equations.
Primary conference paper, introduction and filter analysis:
https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/acc08/data/papers/0104.pdf
Primary journal abstract and metadata:
https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2009.2015562

4. Physical interaction: impedance control.
Hogan, 1985, Impedance Control: An Approach to Manipulation, Part I: Theory.

Impedance control regulates the dynamic relation between motion and interaction
force. The paper separates motion control from impedance and explains why position
or force alone is insufficient for manipulation. It is not equivalent to filtering
a target path. A virtual spring in a reference generator becomes an interaction
law only when a controller and plant implement that relation at the contact port.
Primary author-lab copy, abstract and introduction inspected:
https://newmanlab.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/1985-impedance-control-an-approach-to-manipulation-part-I-theory.pdf
DOI: 10.1115/1.3140702

THE IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

The following points are deductions for our proposed model, not novelty claims
attributed to those papers.

A simple target damper may have
  q_ddot + 2*omega*q_dot + omega^2*(q-g) = 0.
It retains position and velocity when g changes. But a goal step changes q_ddot
by omega^2*Delta_g. Critical damping alone does not prevent that acceleration
jump. It also does not promise monotone motion from every moving initial state.
Filtering g, using more state, or using a constrained generator changes the
regularity and response time. Those costs must appear in the comparison.

Our present matched-segment result retains q, q_dot and q_ddot. Reference jerk
may jump. In the controller u=q_ddot+feedback+kappa*j, a jerk jump produces
Delta_u=kappa*Delta_j when the matched reference and physical state are fixed.
If r and w remain continuous, actual jerk a_dot=r*(u+w-a) can then jump by
r*kappa*Delta_j. A bound on jerk is different from continuity of jerk; a bounded
snap or continuous jerk contract is a stronger request. Snap is the rate of jerk
change. The existing bound must not be described as jerk continuity.

Filtering u could remove that particular command jump. It also adds controller
state and tracking lag, so the current error-cube proof no longer applies without
an augmented-system argument. Further, bounds on r and w alone allow jumps in
those inputs. Even a continuous u cannot guarantee continuous actual jerk under
that broader disturbance contract. The desired regularity needs corresponding
assumptions on the physical response and disturbance.

The current scalar plant has no contact-force output. For a fixed free-space
mass, net force equals mass times acceleration. Contact force is a different
quantity and needs a contact, load and sensing model. We cannot infer safe or
smooth contact force from the current reference jerk bound.

A PLAUSIBLE NEXT CONTRACT

Use an existing generator as the proposer. Retain a stateful execution layer and
prove a small, explicit interface:

- New proposals can arrive at each declared servo update. Replacement retains
  the required reference and filter states. A failed proposal leaves a valid
  motion or stop available.
- Reference and command limits imply a conditional physical tracking tube for
  the uncertain actuator model, including the added filter state. State the
  command-authority, sampling and delay assumptions.
- State the precise physical smoothness target: bounded acceleration, bounded
  jerk, continuous jerk, or bounded snap. Do not use one as a synonym for another.
- Tighten the travel corridor by the tracking tube and reserve a feasible stop.
  Show that executing the admitted prefix preserves the conditions for the next
  decision. This is stronger than testing a single safe segment.
- Make progress conditional on a feasible goal that persists long enough, or on
  a declared bound on target motion. Arbitrary repeated reversals cannot support
  an unconditional arrival guarantee. A latency or settling bound is needed to
  show that the damper does more than make the robot slow.

This is a candidate composition and verification problem. Neither VLA branding
nor putting an existing damper into Lean establishes control novelty. A useful
artifact could prove the exact interface, give a replayable checker, and expose
when smoothness, response time and stopping reserve are incompatible.

FAIR NEXT COMPARISON

Use the same initial states, repeated target stream, plant uncertainty, physical
limits and final goal across: a critically damped target filter; a goal-filtered
DMP or equivalent attractor; Ruckig; and our proposed stateful checked layer.
Do not change bandwidth or limits separately after seeing the result. Compare
held-goal noise, a sustained new goal, slow intended motion, rapid reversals and
a travel-boundary stop. Report physical position error, actual acceleration and
jerk, command jumps, arrival time, rejected updates and remaining stop margin.
Contact force needs a separate model and experiment. The prior numerical
comparison remains frozen and is not evidence for these new baselines.
