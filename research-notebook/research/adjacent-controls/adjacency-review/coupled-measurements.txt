Coupled wheel/driveline measurements: attribution before control
Primary-source screen, 2026-10-17. No experiment, implementation, or claim about
the user's particular robot. Updated for the clarification that the issue was
wheel/tire imbalance versus contributions from other drivetrain components.

Assessment
----------
This is a credible connection to our uncertain-transient work at the level of
reasoning: several plausible causes can explain the measured response, and an
action must remain safe across the causes that the evidence has not excluded.
It is not the same plant model. Our first-order actuator-lag proof does not model
rotating sources, suspension modes, or source attribution.

The closest source found is unusually direct. SAE paper 2005-01-2307 says that
overlapping driveline orders and wheel-order harmonics can prevent repeatable
imbalance-sensitivity measurements. It uses small differences in wheel-roll
speeds on a chassis dynamometer to separate rotating-component orders [1]. Thus
the source-separation problem is established prior art, not a new discovery.

Three different problems must stay separate
------------------------------------------
1. Source/parameter nonidentifiability: two different assignments of wheel and
   drivetrain forcing can produce the same observations. Extra copies of those
   observations do not distinguish the assignments. Small differences relative
   to measurement/model error cause practical ill-conditioning even when exact
   identifiability holds.
2. Shared information: sensors or processed features can contain a common force,
   reference, calibration error, prior, or filter history. Treating dependent
   estimates as independent can overstate confidence. Correlation alone does
   not mean a channel should be discarded; its distinct transfer path may help.
3. Mechanical overconstraint: incompatible or redundant physical constraints
   can create loads or alter compliance. Nothing in the user's clarification
   establishes this diagnosis. More measurements than unknowns is an
   overdetermined inverse problem, not necessarily mechanical overconstraint.

For this account, item 1 is the main issue. Item 2 may coexist but should not be
inferred from the word "deduplicated." Suspension is a support/transfer path;
it need not be a rotating imbalance source itself.

A small model exposes the ambiguity (our analysis, not a fitted vehicle model)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
At one tracked frequency, use complex amplitude/phase observations:

    y(omega) = H_w(omega, theta) U_w
             + H_d(omega, theta) U_d + e.

Here U_w and U_d denote source amplitudes under a stated convention (the usual
speed-squared imbalance factor can be included in H); theta contains support
and coupling parameters. If H_w and H_d are proportional across all available
channels, only a combination of U_w and U_d is identifiable. For example, with
H_w = H_d, replacing (U_w,U_d) by (U_w+c,U_d-c) changes no observation. Perfect
noise-free sensing does not fix that ambiguity.

An ordinary common speed sweep helps only if it changes distinguishable source
orders or transfer-path signatures. Sources locked to the same order can stay
confounded throughout the sweep. Phase references, load and coupling state must
also be tracked; subtracting amplitudes without phase is not vector subtraction.

A known trial mass is an intervention on one source. Under an unchanged linear
transfer path and unchanged nuisance forcing,

    delta y = H_w delta U_w.

This identifies wheel sensitivity. It DOES NOT by itself identify the baseline
wheel imbalance: y = H_w U_w + b_d still has two unknown contributions. Removing
total measured vibration with a wheel correction could compensate another
source at one operating condition rather than isolate the wheel's imbalance.
An additional independent signature or intervention is needed for attribution.
Nonlinear response or a changed support/load state can invalidate the subtraction
assumptions [3]. This is the main trap to avoid in the proposed research.

[1] Braun, Meinhardt, Walter, Steyer and Voight (2005), "Driveline Imbalance
    Sensitivity Testing Methodology," SAE 2005-01-2307.
    https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/driveline-imbalance-sensitivity-testing-methodology-2005-01-2307
    https://doi.org/10.4271/2005-01-2307
    Inspected: official publisher abstract/metadata, not paywalled full paper.
    Direct vehicle evidence for overlapping orders, known-added-imbalance
    sensitivity tests, and differential roll-speed separation. Its particular
    dynamometer method is not automatically available on an on-vehicle robot.

[2] I. A. Craighead (1997), "Sensing tyre pressure, damper condition and wheel
    balance from vibration measurements," Proc. IMechE Part D 211(4), 257-265.
    https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1243/0954407971526416
    Inspected: official publisher abstract/metadata, not full paper. Proposes
    extracting tire, damper and wheel-balance information from wheel/axle
    vertical vibration during driving. Supports the coupled-measurement setting;
    the abstract does not establish uniqueness of source separation.

[3] "Investigation into the influence of bearings nonlinear forces in unbalance

    https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsv.2020.115807
    https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022460X20306362
    Inspected: publisher-indexed abstract and introduction; direct open returned
    an access error, so no full-paper claim. Discusses trial-mass influence
    coefficients and the limits of assuming linear rotor response with nonlinear
    bearings. Rotor/bearing research, not validation of a vehicle suspension.

[4] Murat Uney, Simon J. Julier and Simon Maskell (2025), "Generalising
    Covariance Intersection for multiple posterior densities in multi-sensor
    fusion," IEEE SSP, DOI 10.1109/SSP64130.2025.11073391.
    https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10217482/
    https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10217482/1/SSP-2025-GeneralisedCovarianceIntersection-2.1.pdf
    Inspected: author accepted manuscript, introduction and formulation. Treats
    fusion with shared information/unknown correlations. This addresses excess
    statistical confidence, not physical source identifiability. A covariance
    bound is also not automatically a deterministic error bound for a Lean
    all-trajectories safety theorem.

[5] Johannes Koehler, Peter Koetting, Raffaele Soloperto, Frank Allgoewer and
    Matthias A. Mueller (2020), "A robust adaptive model predictive control
    framework for nonlinear uncertain systems," IJ Robust Nonlinear Control.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02899v2
    https://doi.org/10.1002/rnc.5147
    Inspected: author-posted abstract and version/journal metadata. Connects
    set-membership parameter bounds with robust constraints and recursive
    feasibility. This establishes the broad identification-to-safe-control
    approach as prior art; it does not certify our proposed rotating system.

One falsifiable research question
--------------------------------
Can a small, explicitly bounded intervention distinguish wheel contribution
from synchronous drivetrain forcing enough to certify a safe next spin/test
action, without requiring unique estimates of every mechanical parameter?

Proposed first test, not work already performed:
- Fix a public low-order coupled model, a finite operating range, and a joint
  bounded error model. Include at least two rotating sources, unknown support
  stiffness/damping and an explicit phase reference. State what is measured and
  which wheel/shaft speed ratios can actually be changed.
- Construct an indistinguishable-source case first. A common speed sweep must
  not produce false certainty in that case. Repeating the exact same bounded
  observation must not shrink the feasible set.
- Compare the same baseline data with one predeclared intervention: a known
  small trial mass plus an independently separating operating condition, or a
  permissible change in relative rotating speeds. Mechanical decoupling is only
  an option if feasible; changing it also changes the transfer model.
- Retain every source/plant/error tuple consistent with all observations.
  Check whether its worst-case vibration/travel envelope permits a predeclared
  next action that the baseline uncertainty set could not certify. Check the
  envelope on held-out speed/load conditions. No arbitrary post-hoc threshold.

Candidate theorem: assuming the true plant and bounded errors are in the initial
joint set, the update preserves that tuple; an action certified for every tuple
in the updated set respects the stated limit throughout the action and its
specified safe termination. Duplicate constraints leave the exact set unchanged.
The theorem must include the intervention dynamics and support-model changes,
not merely perform an interval fit and assume it contains the real plant.

Stop gates: stop or narrow the claim if two source assignments remain compatible
but require conflicting corrections; if held-out data refute the assumed error
or model bounds; or if safe excitation cannot provide enough separation. A
correct outcome may be "attribution unresolved; this action remains safe" rather
than a falsely precise wheel-imbalance estimate.

What could be useful for publication
-----------------------------------
A reproducible benchmark that exposes same-order ambiguity, followed by a
machine-checked bounded-attribution-to-safe-action result, could be useful.
Source separation, trial masses, robust adaptation, and conservative fusion are
already known. We have not established novelty for their combination.

Reusable local work: exact interval acceptance, uncertainty-preserving bounds,
continuous-segment safety, and explicit handoff obligations. New work: coupled
oscillatory dynamics, a sound measurement/phase/error model, intervention
identifiability and bounded source attribution. No current Lean result solves
the user's balancing setup, and no hardware or proprietary-data claim is made.
