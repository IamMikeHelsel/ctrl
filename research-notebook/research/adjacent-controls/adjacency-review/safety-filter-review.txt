SAFETY FILTER ADJACENCY REVIEW — 17 October 2026
Independent primary-source screen; no proof builds or paper edits

Recommendation: pivot the research question, retain the proof infrastructure.
The user's concern is justified. Three references did not expose the strongest
prior work. More references alone will not make the case study meaningful.
Safe recovery into a terminal invariant, sampled backup control, actuator-lag
robustness, online safety filtering, and verified controller implementations
all have substantial precedents. Our concrete Lean proof does not establish a
new control method, and there is no identified public open problem that it solves.

The model is a plausible abstraction of one acceleration-controlled axis. It
is not a calibrated industrial axis. Its present value is a transparent test
case for verification machinery. No evidence yet shows that it changes a real
design decision or makes verification substantially easier for another person.
The exact instance being locally new is weak evidence of research novelty.

What the primary literature already supplies

[1] Singletary, Chen and Ames, Control Barrier Functions for Sampled-Data
Systems with Input Delays (2020).
https://arxiv.org/html/2005.06418v1
Full text inspected: Sections II-IV and simulation description. The paper
already combines zero-order-hold backup flow, whole-hold safety, terminal safe
sets and state uncertainty. Its tractable state-uncertainty construction uses
incremental stability. The delay treatment assumes a known integer multiple of
a fixed controller period and an initial safety condition. Its demonstration is
a high-fidelity Segway simulation, not a hardware experiment. This subsumes the
generic claim that sampled backup recovery with delay is a new idea. It does
not establish equivalence to our variable hold sequence, exact numerical region
or Lean theorem. A matched comparison must preserve its delay and stability
assumptions instead of presenting it as an arbitrary-delay result.

[2] van Wijk et al., Disturbance-Robust Backup Control Barrier Functions:
Safety Under Uncertain Dynamics (2024).
https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07700v2
Full text inspected: Assumption 1, flow bounds, feasibility remark and examples.
The method encloses disturbed backup trajectories around a nominal flow and
uses a robustly invariant terminal backup set. The disturbance bound includes
piecewise-continuous forcing; the spacecraft simulation uses time-varying
disturbances. Examples include a double integrator and rigid-body rotation.
The QP need not remain feasible; the specified robust backup supplies the
fallback. Its prediction-grid tightening must not be confused with a proof
for every arbitrary implementation hold. Our exact zero-error braking prefix
is less physically robust than this disturbance class. The local addition is
mechanical checking of a specific flow, terminal certificate and execution
composition, not discovery of recovery under uncertainty.

[3] Seiler, Jankovic and Hellstrom, Control Barrier Functions With Unmodeled
Dynamics Using Integral Quadratic Constraints (2021).
https://arxiv.org/html/2108.10491v1
Full text inspected: input uncertainty model, Examples 1-2, robust constraint
and simulation. It explicitly models delays and first-order actuator dynamics
A(s)=p/(s+p). Input-output IQC bounds account for the dependence of the error on
the command history. The safety condition requires a suitable robust barrier
and feasible controls; the paper notes feasibility difficulties. Its example
is a simulated point mass with uncertain delay, not calibrated servo hardware.
Thus "we account for actuator lag" cannot carry novelty. Our explicit lag
state and exact held flow give a different, narrower certificate route. There
is no demonstrated quantitative superiority over an IQC approach at matched
assumptions.

[4] Abate et al., Run Time Assurance for Spacecraft Attitude Control Under
Nondeterministic Assumptions, IEEE TCST (2024).
https://coogan.ece.gatech.edu/papers/pdf/abate2022runtime.pdf
https://coogan.ece.gatech.edu/papers/abate2022runtime.html
Full author manuscript inspected: model, algorithm assumptions, terminal set,
reachability and hardware sections. The filter checks a desired action followed
by backup reachability into a terminal set, with disturbance torques and bounded
actuation. It can operate outside the terminal set. It assumes negligible RTA
computation time relative to the update step. ASTROS experiments use human
commands; the reported update rate is about 10 Hz and filter computation about
1 ms. This is a much stronger application demonstration than our fixed braking
maneuver. It is not a Lean/kernel certificate of its implementation. Our work
currently has no analogous online admission rule, recursive fallback guarantee,
measured computation deadline or hardware demonstration.

[5] Bohrer et al., VeriPhy: Verified Controller Executables from Verified
Cyber-Physical System Models, PLDI (2018).
https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~smitsch/pdf/VeriPhy.pdf
Full text inspected: pipeline, machine-code theorem and experimental section.
It connects verified hybrid models, monitor/fallback code, sound machine-word
interval arithmetic and CakeML compilation. It includes commodity robot tests.
The end-to-end guarantee is conditional on sound sensing/external interactions,
compiler assumptions and resource conditions. Model violation detection does
not make arbitrary violating physics safe. This is a direct warning against
claiming that adding fixed-point code or a model monitor would itself be new.
Our all-time Lean execution theorem has not reached this implementation scope.
A Lean integration might still be useful, but must demonstrate a distinct
supported contract or a practical workflow advantage.

[6] Hellwig et al., From Zonotopes to Proof Certificates: A Formal Pipeline
for Safe Control Envelopes, iFM 2025 proceedings published 2026.
https://logic.kastel.kit.edu/ap/pub/zonotope-proofs.pdf
Full text inspected: problem definition, invariant-to-safety link and certificate
architecture. It develops a formal pipeline combining numerical reachability,
control-envelope proofs, Taylor error bounds and containment witnesses.
Correction after a Section4 read: its double-integrator example uses visual
containment verification and states that a formal proof can be derived. Do not
describe the paper's examples as fully mechanically replayed on that evidence.
Its sampled formulation uses a fixed period. Whole-step invariance, intersample
safety and input admissibility are explicit obligations. Thus an untrusted
numerical producer plus a small checker, or finite-step-to-all-time composition,
is not a new architecture here. Our potentially useful difference is a Lean
artifact with explicit actual-ODE uniqueness, saturated own-state sampling and
bounded variable tail holds. This screen does not show that those properties
are unavailable in the prior framework, nor that our method scales better.

[7] Lange and Suppa, Trajectory Generation for Immediate Path-Accurate
Jerk-Limited Stopping of Industrial Robots, ICRA (2015).
https://elib.dlr.de/96525/2/stop_ICRA15_header.pdf
Full text inspected: motivation and stopping constraints; repository metadata
and experiment summary cross-checked at https://elib.dlr.de/96525/.
The work considers unexpected controlled stops, acceleration and jerk limits,
path preservation and prevention of rebound when velocity reaches zero. It
reports KUKA robot experiments. This directly predates the practical motivation
that zero velocity alone is not an adequate stopping endpoint. Its trajectory
generation problem is not our uncertain first-order acceleration-servo model.
It shows, however, that a useful axis contribution must account for commanded
path behavior and actuator constraints, not merely prove a selected rail box.

[8] Berscheid and Kroger, Jerk-limited Real-time Trajectory Generation with
Arbitrary Target States, RSS (2021), Ruckig.
https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss17/p015.pdf
Full primary paper available; abstract, problem definition and evaluation scope
inspected. It generates synchronized trajectories with velocity, acceleration
and jerk constraints, including arbitrary target acceleration. It reports a
large random-trajectory test suite and real-world use. That is kinematic
trajectory generation, not a guarantee that uncertain lagged hardware exactly
tracks the reference. Our local first-order lag model might help check tracking
envelopes, but the current held-command checker is not already a checker for
Ruckig's piecewise-polynomial references. Any such adapter needs its own input
and tracking semantics. Do not advertise compatibility without proving it.

[9] A Backup Control Barrier Function Approach for Safety-Critical Control of
Mechanical Systems Under Multiple Constraints, IEEE/ASME TMECH,
DOI 10.1109/TMECH.2024.3504573; published online 2024, volume 30(6), 2025.
https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10787265/
Access limit: publisher abstract/index only; direct page did not expose full
text. It describes a feasibility-guaranteed backup filter, multiple state/input
constraints and experiments. Its abstract explicitly does not guarantee all
aggressive time-varying constraints. This is an overlap lead that must be read
fully before making a novelty claim about mechanical-system backup filtering.
I do not infer its delay, lag, numeric verification or hardware-error guarantees.

What is actually different in the local artifact

Source review, not an inference from those papers: we have explicit proofs for
the selected third-order plant, uncertain but fixed r, clipped sampled feedback,
exact flow, rational enclosures, actual executions, and continuous handoff.
The original backup box has a checked zero-error recovery prefix and a robust
tail. PilotContinuation now gives all-time direct-feedback safety for one
positive-volume initial subbox and one rate cell: the first 25 holds are .05
with zero errors, then holds may vary up to .2 with combined held command error
at most 1/90. A complete direct-feedback parameter covering is not yet checked.
GeneralHandoff permits any continuous prefix but deliberately leaves its plant
dynamics, prefix safety and terminal energy to the caller.

Those are useful proof components. They do not yet constitute an online safety
filter, an industrial safe-stop function, deployed code assurance, a calibrated
servo model, or a result for arbitrary within-hold disturbances. Adding more
small boxes will not by itself change those boundaries. Our earlier family
comparison did not prove a true safe-region advantage for backup over direct
feedback; a failed enclosure is not a failed controller.

Three worthwhile, falsifiable directions

A. Independent certificate checking on an existing public controls benchmark.
Practical relevance 4/5; contribution runway 3/5.
Use an external producer and a preselected public problem. Check both the
continuous trajectory tube and terminal invariant against actual execution
semantics. Compare accepted region, proof/checking time, manual proof effort,
certificate size and trusted components with [6], on the same plant and
uncertainty assumptions. Include deliberately corrupted certificates and one
independent user replay. The smallest useful result is a reproducible adapter
that verifies a result its producer cannot certify, with a measured advantage
or a clearly useful additional contract. Stop the paper claim if the only
difference is prover choice, or if each new plant needs another bespoke proof.
The existing full direct-feedback cover is a pilot gate, not the contribution.

B. A measured single-axis operating contract for recovery and continued motion.
Practical relevance 5/5; contribution runway 3/5, conditional on real plant access.
Choose a real axis and an engineering decision: maximum update/measurement age,
admitted approach speed, or required distance from a software travel limit.
Specify acceleration estimation, lag range, command saturation, computation
and actuation timing, and model residual. Make the proof accept nonzero prefix
error and a physically justified disturbance class. Produce a conservative
design envelope, an intentionally rejected operating point, and repeatable
hardware evidence under declared loads. Tests support the model assessment;
they do not prove a universal residual bound. Stop if only ideal zero-error
prefixes pass, or if the model cannot explain observed stopping behavior. This
is an application/verification contribution, not a new backup-control method.

C. Checked acceptance and fallback for commands from an untrusted planner.
Practical relevance 5/5; contribution runway 2/5 because [4]-[6] already cover
much of the concept. Specify a finite command certificate, its entire execution
window, and the state/timing uncertainty at reception. Prove that accepted
commands preserve recoverability and that rejection or timeout has a safe
fallback available. Connect the checker to actual controller arithmetic and
measured execution cost. Stop if fallback feasibility is simply assumed, if
the check completes after the command must start, or if the integration adds
nothing beyond an existing runtime-assurance pipeline. A fixed brake followed
once by feedback does not satisfy this deliverable.

Recommended next decision

Pause expansion of the current control example. Select A if the intended
audience is formal methods; select B if the intended audience is robotics or
controls. Do not attempt all three at once. Before another large proof effort,
write a one-page matched comparison and obtain an external consumer or concrete
axis requirement. The current work is worth retaining as a regression example
and proof library. It is not yet evidence for a strong standalone research paper.
