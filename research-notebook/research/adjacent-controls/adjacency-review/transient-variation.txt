Response variation across units and repeated stops
Primary-source addendum, 2026-10-17. Assessment only; no new Lean proof.

RECOMMENDATION
The useful target is an envelope for the actual stopping response over a stated
operating domain, not one fitted time constant. Start with a powered axis and
extend the scalar model to permit both response-rate variation and a bounded
effective acceleration target during each hold. This is a small mathematical
change with a meaningful physical distinction. Do not call it a thermal friction-
brake model until a specific brake/axis and its data support that abstraction.

WHAT THE PRIMARY SOURCES ESTABLISH
ABB's robot stopping-distance specification, revision AE (2026), explicitly
notes variation with additional load and, for category 0 stops, individual
brakes and friction. It also notes that acceleration during the stop delay can
make a higher-load case stop in less distance than a lower-load case. Thus
“test maximum payload once” and “slowest lag is always worst” are not sound
universal shortcuts. Access limitation: the official indexed PDF text was
available, but a subsequent direct fetch returned 403; the full document was
not downloaded in this pass.
https://library.e.abb.com/public/af537e5df1d84c32956ed2aea9317287/3HAC048645%20PS%20Robot%20stopping%20distances-en.pdf

Universal Robots' UR30 manual publishes stopping data for stated payloads,
motions and orientations, and describes safety-rated stopping-time/distance
limits that adjust program speed. This establishes an existing industrial
need and solution class. It does not give us a universal temperature law or
permission to transfer its envelopes to another robot.
https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW10_9/Content/prod-usr-man/complianceUR30/H_g5_sections/stopping_time_n_distances_g5/stoppingTimeAndDistance_UR30_en.htm

NHTSA's EA15-005 investigation includes physical repeated-braking and hot-soak
tests, including deliberately induced friction fade or fluid vaporization.
The largest temperatures at some components occurred during hot soak, not
necessarily during active braking. This is direct evidence that duty history
and measurement location matter; it concerns vehicle hydraulic friction brakes,
not the electromechanical motor lag of our current model. Do not import its
failure thresholds or vehicle-specific conclusions into a robot-axis claim.
https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/inv/2015/INCLA-EA15005-6165.PDF

Mayr describes brake monitoring of switching state, temperature, wear and
magnetic force reserve, plus serial-number-linked production measurements. It
also describes a specified cold-climate brake variant. This is evidence that
condition and unit traceability already matter in brake engineering. It is a
manufacturer account, not independent validation of our proposed model. An
engagement indication alone does not establish the full dynamic braking torque
or stopping envelope.
https://www.mayr.com/en/news/press-releases/always-keeping-an-eye-on-the-brake~1847

Reachset-conformant identification and uncertainty-aware safety control are
already established approaches; see actuator-motion.txt and its primary papers.
The candidate contribution here would be a useful, machine-checked envelope
and clear evidence contract for one real mechanism. No novelty claim follows
from adding temperature labels or changing a constant to a function.

PRECISE MAPPING FROM THE CURRENT RESULT
Current normalized dynamics are x'=v, v'=a, a'=r*(u-a), with constant unknown
r in [0.9,1.1]. The physical relation is r=tau0/tau, hence this rate interval
means tau/tau0 in [10/11,10/9], not +/-10% in tau.

- Different units or runs may use different constant r. The theorem is uniform
  over that choice, provided every other model assumption still holds.
- An isolated checked spike, proof-spike/SampleVaryingRate.lean, permits r_n
  to change at sample boundaries and be constant inside each hold. Its common
  energy induction proves the constructed trajectory safe. It does not prove
  arbitrary within-hold drift or a new physical execution contract.
- Continuously varying r(t) is not already covered by the constant-r exponential
  formula or by selecting whichever constant-r trajectory looks most adverse.
- Varying r changes approach speed to the commanded acceleration. It does NOT
  change the steady acceleration under a constant command. Thermal derating,
  payload/gravity, reduced friction torque or supply constraints can change that
  acceleration, engagement time, or the ability to hold. They need extra terms
  or a different model, not merely a wider r interval.

Motor servo deceleration and friction braking must be separated. A powered
servo can command signed acceleration and may regenerate energy. A friction
brake supplies direction-dependent opposing torque, can engage after a delay,
and can stick at rest. Holding brakes, service stops and power-loss stops need
not use the same mechanism. Constant negative command in our model eventually
moves the axis backward; it does not reproduce frictional rest and hold.

MOST FEASIBLE NEXT THEOREM: WITHIN-HOLD RESPONSE ENVELOPE
Proposed, not yet implemented:
  x'=v, v'=a, a'=r(t)*(b(t)-a),
  0<r_lo<=r(t)<=r_hi, b_lo<=b(t)<=b_hi,
  (x0,v0,a0) in a specified box, on 0<=t<=H.
Initially require continuous r,b and a classical solution; piecewise-continuous
or measurable signals can be a later extension with explicit solution semantics.
Here b is the effective acceleration target. It may enclose a held request,
gain variation and command-equivalent residual. In physical time r has units
1/second; b has acceleration units. This is an inclusion contract, not a claim
that every possible thermal mechanism has this form.

An explicit upper comparison uses initial upper bounds (x_hi,v_hi,a_hi),
constant target b_hi, and rate
  R_hi = r_lo if a_hi>=b_hi, otherwise r_hi.
A lower comparison uses (x_lo,v_lo,a_lo), target b_lo, and
  R_lo = r_hi if a_lo>=b_lo, otherwise r_lo.
Then each actual coordinate lies between the corresponding constant-parameter
heldSolution coordinates for every t in [0,H]. The rate choices are justified
by the sign of b-a: faster response can increase OR decrease acceleration.
Scalar ODE comparison proves the acceleration bounds; integration proves the
velocity and position bounds. This uses the existing exact held solution but
requires a new comparison proof. No derivative bound on r is needed under the
stated pointwise bounds and continuity assumption.

This theorem directly supports a finite sequence of checked stopping envelopes
with uncertain within-step response. A bounded external jerk j can be represented
by b=u+j/r when a'=r*(u-a)+j, giving |b-u|<=J/r_lo if |j|<=J. Gain loss needs
an explicit interval for b, e.g. b=g(t)*u+d(t); it is not automatically covered
by the old small disturbance budget. A data-backed lower braking capability
is essential for a useful stopping guarantee.

Important limit: the existing feedback energy certificate proves constant-r
segments. A box envelope for variable r does not automatically preserve that
same ellipsoid or all-time tail theorem. First prove finite-horizon travel
and terminal enclosure. A time-varying tail invariant, or a valid separate
rest/hold mode, is another obligation. Likewise this envelope does not by itself
prove the maximum position occurs at an endpoint; check the full interval.

EXACT MODEL-MISMATCH EXAMPLE
Take x0=0,v0=1,a0=-1 and request u=-1, in normalized illustrative units.
For every possible positive r(t), the old model has a(t)=-1, so x(1)=1/2.
If available braking changes to effective b=-1/2 with r=1, then
  a(t)=-1/2-exp(-t)/2,
  v(t)=1/2-t/2+exp(-t)/2,
  x(1)=3/4-exp(-1)/2 > 1/2.
Thus a travel wall at 1/2 is violated despite an arbitrarily broad positive
rate interval. This is an analytic counterexample, not a newly compiled Lean
lemma or a measurement of a particular hot brake. It isolates amplitude loss
from response-time variation.

MINIMUM DATA AND ACCEPTANCE OBLIGATIONS
1. Select the actual stop mechanism and mode: controlled servo stop, mechanical
   emergency braking, or holding. Record units, firmware and command processing.
2. Record requested and applied commands with timestamps, position/velocity,
   acceleration estimates with error bounds, payload/inertia/configuration,
   relevant component temperatures, supply state and stop/dwell history.
3. Challenge multiple units and repeated runs: cold start, warmed operation,
   repeated-stop duty cycles, cooling/hot-soak periods, load/configuration changes
   and allowed wear states. Do not assume “warm is worse” across all conditions.
4. Fit rate AND effective-target envelopes, including engagement delay. Check
   held-out units and complete runs; random point-level splits hide shared
   thermal history. A good mean fit or percentile is not a deterministic bound.
5. State exactly how between-sample behavior and measurement uncertainty are
   bounded. Finite measurements alone do not prove all future dynamics. Retain
   the physical conformance assumption and define an out-of-envelope response.

Before a thermal-state model, use a conservative envelope over a declared
condition domain. Temperature-conditioned narrower envelopes are a later useful
step only if sensing uncertainty, thermal lag and movement between temperature
bins are bounded over the whole stop. A temperature reading at stop initiation
cannot silently freeze the brake state throughout that stop.

PL d/e CONTEXT
This theorem can support the deterministic performance argument for a specified
safety function. It does not establish a Performance Level. IFA guidance treats
architecture, dangerous failures, diagnostic coverage, common causes, software,
systematic faults and validation as additional parts of ISO 13849 assessment.
Its current resource page points to a revised 2025 German report; the English
2017e report reflects an older standard edition. No compliance assessment or
complete normative-standard review was performed here.

STOP GATE
Proceed if a real axis can be enclosed by this small model with useful braking
margin under the declared conditions. If gain loss, delay, friction sign changes
or holding dominate and cannot be conservatively bounded, use an explicit brake
hybrid model instead. Do not spend time proving a tighter lag theorem for a
mechanism whose main variation is not lag.
