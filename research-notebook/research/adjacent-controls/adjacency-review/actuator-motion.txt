Actuator-aware stopping and safe robot motion: bounded adjacency review
Checked 2026-10-17. Primary sources only. No proof or benchmark expansion.

RECOMMENDATION
Do not make a wider initial box the main research claim. The current model
proof is a useful component, but safe braking, fallback composition, sampling,
input delay, uncertain actuation, and checked ODE numerics all have substantial
prior work. The strongest next target is a small, auditable contract between a
motion planner and one physical axis: accept a proposed motion only when its
actual actuator response, sensing uncertainty, command age, and fallback remain
inside a travel limit. This is a candidate artifact contribution, not an
established open control-theory problem or a claim of first formalization.

Practical question: “May this axis execute one more commanded step, given its
current velocity AND acceleration, stale measurements, lag uncertainty, and
remaining clearance, while retaining a safe continuation?” This is broader and
more useful than certifying another particular feedback trajectory. A single
axis can represent a carriage or a conservative joint-coordinate subproblem.
It does not by itself guarantee manipulator collision avoidance, contact safety,
or autonomous-road-vehicle safety.

WHAT THE CLOSEST WORK ALREADY DOES

1. Motion generation and stopping are mature.
Berscheid/Kroeger, Ruckig (RSS 2021), generates synchronized jerk-limited
trajectories with arbitrary position, velocity and acceleration targets.
Its paper reports large random testing and real-world applications. The current
official tutorial supports stopping through its velocity interface and exposes
position extrema; it also documents positional-limit failure results. These are
kinematic trajectory guarantees, not evidence that our uncertain physical lag
plant tracks an arbitrary output trajectory. A useful comparison must use the
same plant and limits, with an explicit tracking-error contract. We should not
portray Ruckig as unaware of acceleration or stopping.
https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss17/p015.html
https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss17/p015.pdf
https://docs.ruckig.com/tutorial.html

Lange/Suppa (ICRA 2015) already addresses path-accurate acceleration/jerk-limited
stopping, including avoidance of rebound at zero velocity, with KUKA hardware
experiments. Our recovery handoff can retain nonzero velocity/acceleration; it
must not be sold as an improved stop-and-rest algorithm.
https://elib.dlr.de/96525/

2. A proposed step plus a verified fallback is established architecture.
ARMTD (RSS 2020) constructs reachable sets for manipulator trajectories that
include braking, with six-DoF Fetch hardware examples. This already answers the
broad “safe motion with a backup when planning fails” question.
https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss16/p100.pdf

SARA shield (TRO 2026; inspected arXiv v2 and official repository) combines an
intended step with a stopping fallback and checks reachable occupancies/contact
energy. Its Section III-D explicitly assumes bounded tracking error, joint
motion limits, sensing error and delay. Its physical study uses a Schunk LWA 4P
at 166.7 Hz. The official deployment instructions ask users to provide a
conservative tracking margin, for example through reachset-conformance
identification. This gives a concrete integration boundary for a smaller
verified actuator model; it does not establish that their method lacks such
modeling or that our artifact would improve their performance. We have not
reproduced their implementation or hardware experiments.
https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10180v2
https://github.com/TUMcps/sara-shield

3. Sampling, delay, uncertainty and model monitoring are not new ingredients.
Singletary/Chen/Ames (2020) develops sampled backup CBFs with state uncertainty
and known input delay. Its delay construction uses an input history, an integer
multiple of the sample period, and a safe startup condition. Evaluation is a
high-fidelity Segway simulation, not a hardware experiment in that paper.
Its robust prediction interface is close to the command-history enclosure we
would need. A claim of newly handling delayed sampled backups would be false.
https://arxiv.org/html/2005.06418v1

Seiler/Jankovic/Hellstrom (2021) handles unmodeled plant-input dynamics via
integral quadratic constraints, explicitly including first-order actuators and
delay. The input perturbation is dynamically correlated with past commands;
the example uses a point-mass simulation. This rules out a broad claim that
actuator-aware safety is missing. It also warns against treating all actuator
mismatch as an independently chosen small constant error.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.10491

ModelPlex work already derives sound runtime monitors with sensor uncertainty,
actuator disturbance and fallback controllers. Isabelle/HOL's ODE development
already includes verified numerical enclosures using Runge-Kutta and affine
arithmetic. A Lean implementation or exact interval arithmetic alone is not a
new conceptual assurance architecture.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.06502
https://isa-afp.org/entries/Ordinary_Differential_Equations.html

4. Hardware conformance is an existing research field, and remains our gap.
Liu/Schuermann/Althoff (2021) combines reachset-conformant identification with
controller synthesis and robot experiments. Luetzow/Althoff (2024) extends
identification across nonlinear and input-output models, with simulated and
real-world data. These are comparison and method sources, not authority to
assume our first-order lag matches a robot. Containing finite recorded behavior
does not, by itself, prove every future physical trajectory satisfies an ODE
and uncertainty bound. Our theorem must keep that assumption visible.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01626
https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11692

Manufacturer evidence supports inspecting the actual command path. Franka's
FCI documentation describes a first-order command filter and derivative rate
limiting; the API can alter the requested signal before the robot executes it.
The narrative overview and current API reference differ about the rate-limiter
default, so an experiment must pin the library version and explicitly set both
options. Neither document implies that the entire joint is our acceleration-lag
plant. Packet losses, filtering and rate limiting cannot simply be folded into
our current nominal-tau symbol without a model argument.
https://frankarobotics.github.io/docs/doc/libfranka/docs/overview.html
https://frankarobotics.github.io/libfranka/latest/classfranka_1_1Robot.html

EXACT BOUNDED TARGET AND WHAT IS STILL MISSING

Proposed target (not proved by this review): a certificate-acceptance theorem
for x'=v, v'=a, a'=r(u-a)+j(t), with one fixed unknown positive r in a specified
interval. Commands u are piecewise held after explicitly timed actuator updates.
The initial state lies in a measured box; measurements have timestamps and
bounded errors. A supplied command history encloses the state at the actual
update time. A certificate encloses every point of each accepted segment and
places its terminal box in the already verified tail region. Then every
continuous/appropriate absolutely-continuous execution satisfying that contract
respects both travel limits through the prefix and the robust tail.

Crucial new obligations:
- Disturbance must have the declared channel. Current held error d enters
  r*(u+d-a); external jerk j enters outside r. A continuously varying bounded
  j(t) needs an integral/comparison enclosure, not the existing exact constant-
  command formula with a convenient nominal d substituted.
- A delayed command has old-command and new-command subintervals. Account for
  both and the same fixed r; do not treat delayed delivery as merely a longer
  hold of a fresh command. Packet order/expiry policy and startup history need
  explicit semantics.
- The monitor needs a previously certified fallback during computation or
  rejection. Detection after an unsafe interval is not preventive assurance.
- Measurement membership, including acceleration uncertainty, must be supplied
  or derived from an observer contract. An acceleration estimate is not exact
  merely because it is present in a log.
- A reusable prefix certificate must not assume the very path-safety conclusion
  it is meant to establish. The existing general handoff only composes supplied
  prefix safety and terminal membership; it does not establish arbitrary prefix
  dynamics or safety.

Reusable now: exact exponential held flow and uniqueness; clipping; rational
coefficient and finite-trace checking; intersample position estimates; fixed-
unknown-rate feedback safety; continuous handoff and physical-coordinate
scaling. These are a substantial base. The proposed disturbance, delay/history,
and monitor decision contracts are not already proved by those components.

A reasoned order of work:
A. First deliver a reusable accepted/rejected motion certificate for bounded
   nonzero prefix error and explicit measurement boxes, using fixed timed holds.
   Evaluate a family parameterized by clearance, speed, acceleration and lag;
   avoid choosing the original tiny box as the only favorable example.
B. Add bounded command age with an explicit two-part hold and pending-command
   history. Produce a useful nonzero age margin and a rejected over-age case.
C. Only then expose an executable online acceptance interface or integrate with
   an existing planner. Current Lean certificate compilation is offline evidence,
   not a demonstrated real-time monitor. A fast runtime table/decision routine
   needs its own proved lookup/arithmetic semantics and measured worst-case cost.

This remains a focused one-axis artifact. Multi-axis collision geometry,
configuration-dependent torque limits, coupling, elasticity, friction and
contact dynamics are separate work. They make a full manipulator theorem much
larger than multiplying three independent axis proofs.

COMPARISON AND STOP GATES
Use identical model, input limits, disturbance channel and timing for every
comparison. Compare (i) instantaneous-braking approximation, (ii) a properly
lag-aware numerical reachability baseline, and (iii) the checked certificate.
Ruckig can supply planned kinematic profiles, but only through an explicit
tracking model; its kinematic result must not be scored as a physical violation.
Report admitted region, clearance margin, rejection causes, offline proof cost,
and runtime cost separately. Include failure from stale state, underestimated
lag, nonzero disturbance, and velocity-zero with residual acceleration.

For a hardware-facing result, obtain traces of applied commands, update times,
position and velocity, and an identified acceleration/error envelope on a
restricted operating domain. Use held-out trajectories and parameter/load
changes to challenge that envelope. Record empirical conformance separately
from the conditional mathematical theorem. No hardware is selected or validated
in the current work.

Advance only if one nonzero error/age contract remains useful on a declared
axis/task and the artifact eliminates a real unchecked numerical or semantic
assumption in that workflow. If robust margins collapse, if first-order lag
fails the intended data, or if the only differentiation is a new proof language
and more certified boxes, retain this as an educational/reproducibility artifact
and choose a different problem. The present sources do not justify claiming an
unsolved general stopping problem, a new backup-control method, or superiority
to the cited systems. A systematic novelty check remains open.
