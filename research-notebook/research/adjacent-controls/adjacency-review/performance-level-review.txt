PERFORMANCE LEVEL AND TRANSIENT ENVELOPE REVIEW — 17 October 2026
Independent addendum; no compliance claim, proof edits or hardware validation

The useful claim is a verified bound on the behavior of a specified stopping
function, conditional on a justified plant and implementation contract. A Lean
proof alone does not establish PL d or PL e. Population and repeated-condition
variation could make a stopping-envelope study useful, but these concerns are
already recognized in official industrial guidance.

Three different questions must remain separate

1. Functional behavior: once a valid stop request arrives, does the axis remain
within its permitted displacement, reach the specified stopped condition by
the deadline, and remain there? A mathematical transient proof can address
this question under explicit assumptions. It must include sensing, processing,
communication, actuation delay, braking, and the post-stop behavior relevant
to that particular function.

2. Dangerous failure frequency: how often can the implemented safety function
fail dangerously? The published PFH bands are PL d: 10^-7 <= PFH < 10^-6 per
hour; PL e: 10^-8 <= PFH < 10^-7 per hour. These are not percentages of trials
that stop successfully. The frequency criterion is only part of a PL claim.
Source: German electrotechnical standards organization DKE, functional-safety
overview and PFH comparison table:
https://www.dke.de/de/arbeitsfelder/core-safety/funktionale-sicherheit

3. Design and validation: architecture, component reliability, diagnostic
coverage, common-cause failures, systematic failures, software development,
integration and validation also matter. A good transient result supplies none
of the missing component failure data by itself. IFA Report 1/2025 explicitly
separates the quantitative PFH aspects from software/systematic requirements.
It lists formal mathematical/model verification as one V&V method, alongside
other analysis and testing. Thus formal proof has a legitimate evidence role,
but not an automatic PL assignment or exemption from the remaining work.
Source: IFA Report 1/2025, especially Chapters 7-10, pp. 83 and 108:
https://publikationen.dguv.de/widgets/pdf/download/article/5091

ISO's current public catalog identifies ISO 13849-1:2023 as the fourth edition.
It covers safety-related control-system design, including software, for high
demand and continuous modes. It does not itself prescribe the safety function
or required PL for each application. The catalog still lists ISO 13849-2:2012
as current, with a replacement draft under development; its scope is validation
by analysis and testing of safety functions, categories and achieved PL.
These scope statements are not a clause-by-clause conformity assessment.
https://www.iso.org/standard/73481.html
https://www.iso.org/standard/53640.html

An up-to-date quantitative reference is ISO/TR 13849-3:2026. Its public preview
describes Markov-based PFH calculations using architecture, channel MTTFD,
diagnostic coverage, common-cause factor and mission time. Our deterministic
ODE theorem has no such failure process or reliability parameters.
https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:tr:13849:-3:ed-1:v1:en

The strongest disconfirming evidence for novelty

DGUV Information 209-074, Industrial robots, already distinguishes reaction
time from braking time and discusses load, speed and extension. For stop
category 0 it explicitly adds brake temperature, wear and contamination. For
the category-1 arrangement it describes, the mechanical brake holds the axis
after controlled deceleration and does not determine the braking time. The
guide also says manufacturer stopping values need validation on the real
system. Therefore "warm brakes differ from cold brakes" and "nominal stop
data need testing" are established engineering concerns, not research novelty.
Source: pp. 24-26 of this older official guide; cited as prior engineering
knowledge, not as a current robot-standard compliance checklist:
https://publikationen.dguv.de/widgets/pdf/download/article/3150

First choose the mechanism. Our present u=-1 maneuver is a powered acceleration
command with first-order lag. It is not a friction-brake engagement model, a
power-loss stop, or a vertical-axis load-holding model. Substituting a wider lag
interval does not automatically capture brake slip, engagement delay, friction
fade, gravity, loss of power or mechanical failure. The proper distinction
depends on the actual drive/brake design, not merely the word "braking."

What the local Lean results honestly support

They prove bounded position, recovery into an invariant and continued bounded
motion for a stated model. They do not prove standstill by a deadline, a
maintained stop, fault detection, or safety after loss of power. The feedback
tail may continue moving within the admitted region.

The universal fixed-rate result can cover different units or cold/warm runs
if each entire execution really has one fixed rate inside the proved interval
and satisfies all other assumptions. It assigns no probability to those
assumptions. Thermal evolution during a run, aging outside that interval and
arbitrary within-hold forcing are not covered by declaring r "uncertain."
The current recovery and direct pilot prefixes also assume zero command error.

A future proof could support a traceable functional requirement, conservative
distance/time margins, parameter choices and a monitor's logical detection
property. A correct monitor can contribute to a diagnostic design, but its
mathematical detection theorem is not the numerical DC value: fault modes,
failure rates, test interval and the complete diagnostic path still matter.
Code correspondence, scheduling and sensor/actuator assumptions must be linked
to the implementation; mathematical real-number arithmetic is not deployed code.

Population evidence is useful, but has a different quantifier

Repeated stops on one warm unit estimate within-unit behavior under those
conditions. They do not establish production-unit variation, cold starts,
wear states or rare faults. Repeated observations can be correlated through
temperature, wear, supply conditions and calibration. A fitted mean or a
high empirical percentile is not a bound for all admissible trajectories.

If a statistical tolerance envelope is proposed, state its population,
coverage, confidence, independence assumptions and extrapolation limits.
Keep that statistical statement separate from the conditional Lean theorem.
Do not convert a per-stop exceedance probability to PFH without a justified
operational/demand and failure model. Do not silently add an empirical tail
probability to a component PFH calculation as if the units and events matched.

Smallest useful and falsifiable research gate

Select one actual axis and one safety function. Declare its start trigger,
allowed travel, stopping deadline, stopped-state tolerance, hold duration and
fault/energy conditions. Preselect a test matrix with multiple units and
repeats spanning cold start, thermal steady state, repeated stops, declared
load/speed, supply conditions and permitted wear. Use independently measured
motion and timing with measurement uncertainty. Separate model fitting from
the units/conditions used to challenge the bound.

Build a conditional envelope that an engineer can use to select approach
speed or required clearance. Give it nonzero sensing/timing/model-error margins.
Prove whole-transient safety and the actual required stop/hold condition.
Compare it with the manufacturer's bound and a simple conservative engineering
bound on exactly the same conditions. The desired result is a useful certified
design decision or a falsified inadequate model, not a claim that more repeated
tests alone demonstrate PL e.

Stop or revise if a held-out trace escapes the envelope after accounting for
measurement uncertainty; if obtaining a valid bound removes the useful operating
region; if warm/cold behavior requires a different mechanism than the model;
or if the proposed decision can already be made equally well with the existing
manufacturer bound. Before claiming diagnostic benefit, demonstrate that the
fault is detected early enough for a proved fallback to remain available.

Judgment: practical relevance of a measured transient envelope, 5/5; current
evidence that our proof establishes PL d/e, none; research contribution runway,
3/5 conditional on a real axis, a useful decision and a matched baseline. The
claim should be "formal verification evidence for a specified safety function,"
never "Lean proves this machine is PL d/e."

Access limits: official ISO catalog/preview material and official DGUV/DKE
sources were checked live. The full purchased ISO standards were not reviewed.
Selected sections of the freely available 2025 IFA report were inspected;
this is not a complete standards audit or a conformity assessment. No external
communication, certification request, proof modification or test was performed.

This is a mathematical source review, not a Lean proof or novelty assessment.

The proposed endpoint-rate choices are correct. Write the upper acceleration
as U(t)=b_hi+(a_hi-b_hi)*exp(-R_hi*t). Its sign relative to b_hi never changes.
At the comparison boundary a=U,
  r(t)*(b(t)-U) <= r(t)*(b_hi-U) <= R_hi*(b_hi-U)=U'.
The second inequality uses R_hi=r_lo when a_hi>=b_hi and R_hi=r_hi otherwise.
For L(t)=b_lo+(a_lo-b_lo)*exp(-R_lo*t), the inequalities reverse with the
stated lower-rate choices. Equality a_hi=b_hi or a_lo=b_lo is harmless.
Comparison therefore bounds every actual acceleration, even if the actual
acceleration crosses either target bound. The rate choice depends on the
comparison curve's sign, not on an unjustified ordering of all actual rates.

An integrating-factor proof is available: the difference a-U satisfies
(a-U)' + r(t)*(a-U) <= 0 and starts nonpositive. The lower difference has the
opposite inequality. No derivative bound on r or b is needed. Arbitrary
continuous b(t) in the stated constant interval is permitted; b need not be
held or independent of r for this pointwise comparison.

Use H>=0 and ordered initial/target/rate intervals. Require full-state
continuity on [0,H] and the three derivative equalities on (0,H), with the
specified initial bounds. This includes H and avoids an endpoint-reset gap.
Continuous r,b on the closed finite interval give bounded coefficients and
the usual linear-ODE existence/uniqueness construction; if the new theorem
claims execution existence, supply that construction separately. A conditional
comparison theorem alone does not establish existence. The measurable-signal
extension needs absolute continuity and almost-everywhere ODE semantics.

Integrating twice preserves the order for t>=0, despite negative acceleration
or velocity values. For each comparison rate R and endpoint data x0,v0,a0,b,
  V(t)=v0+b*t+(a0-b)*(1-exp(-R*t))/R,
  X(t)=x0+v0*t+b*t^2/2+(a0-b)*(t/R-(1-exp(-R*t))/R^2).
These match the held-flow form. Use the upper initial x,v,a values together
for the upper curve and the lower values for the lower curve. This safely
encloses a correlated initial set by its box, but later box propagation can
lose correlations and become conservative. Check the whole X(t) interval:
terminal position containment alone does not bound an interior maximum.

Composition needs one further distinction. A finite sequence can use these
envelopes one hold at a time, with full-state continuity at each boundary and
a box containing the previous terminal state. This is a piecewise classical
execution argument; it is not a single globally C1 trajectory when targets
or rates jump. The existing sample-invariant energy proof does not transfer
automatically to the resulting variable-rate dynamics.

The existing GeneralHandoff interface can nevertheless close a finite variable-
rate prefix with the old fixed-rate robust tail. Required facts are: prefix
travel safety on [0,H], V(z)<=12 for every z in the terminal box, unchanged
actual full state at H, and the actual post-H sampled ODE with one fixed
admissible tail rate and the old dwell/error bounds. The prefix rate need not
equal the tail rate in that abstract interface. Persistent thermal drift or
effective-target uncertainty in the tail must instead receive its own invariant
proof or a separate physically justified stop/hold mode. Do not infer that
the rate becomes fixed merely because the mathematical maneuver ends.

The jerk-to-target conversion b=u+j/r is valid under r>=r_lo>0 and |j|<=J,
giving |b-u|<=J/r_lo. The displayed amplitude-loss counterexample also checks
algebraically: x(1)=3/4-exp(-1)/2>1/2. Neither result establishes a friction
brake model or a maintained stop. No blocking correction to the proposed scalar
envelope is needed; the regularity, endpoint and tail boundaries above should
be explicit in the theorem statement and its application contract.
