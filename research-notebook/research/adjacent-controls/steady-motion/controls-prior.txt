STEADY MOTION UNDER CONTINUOUS REPLANNING
Independent controls adjacency review — 17 October 2026

The useful research question is: how little unwanted motion can we guarantee,
while still reacting to a real change in the task within a stated time?

“Any arm dead steady” is not a possible unconditional contract. An arm can lack
the force, bandwidth, sensing, or stable contact needed to oppose a disturbance.
A command trace also does not label which motion is intended. The promise needs
a robot model, a disturbance envelope, and a definition of intended motion.

THREE DIFFERENT PROBLEMS

1. Planner dithering. Successive plans disagree although the task is unchanged.
   A smooth splice can remove acceleration jumps while retaining repeated small
   reversals. A jerk bound alone therefore does not establish steadiness.
   A target uncertainty set, commitment rule, and task progress condition belong
   above the trajectory generator.

2. Physical motion error. Loads, friction, resonance, contact, and actuator error
   move the arm away from its command. Command smoothing cannot by itself bound
   that error. A measured response envelope or physical tracking theorem is
   needed. Flexible modes and impact require more than our current lag model.

3. Feedback oscillation. Fast corrections can interact with sensing, sampling,
   computation, and actuator delay. Raising gains or observer bandwidth is not
   an unrestricted remedy. Stability and noise sensitivity must be checked for
   the actual discrete execution loop [5].

These mechanisms can coexist. A single motion spectrum does not identify which
one caused the oscillation. Report command motion and actual motion separately.

EIGHT PRIMARY SOURCES

[1] Burdet, Osu, Franklin, Milner and Kawato, Nature 414 (2001), 446–449.
    The central nervous system stabilizes unstable dynamics by learning optimal
    impedance. https://doi.org/10.1038/35106566
    In a robotic force-field experiment, people adapted the geometry of arm
    impedance to stabilize unstable motion. This supports direction-dependent
    mechanical resistance as a useful design idea. It does not show that all
    human steadiness comes from one neural rule, or that maximal stiffness is
    best. Publisher abstract and supplementary description inspected; main
    article is subscription content.

[2] Ferraguti, Secchi and Fantuzzi, ICRA 2013, 4948–4953.
    A Tank-Based Approach to Impedance Control with Variable Stiffness.
    https://iris.unimore.it/handle/11380/1012933
    The controller accounts for exchanged energy to preserve passivity while
    stiffness varies. Thus safe online stiffness adaptation is established
    territory. The institutional abstract was inspected; it provides no full
    paper. Do not infer a complete moving-reference or sampled-VLA contract
    from this abstract. DOI: 10.1109/ICRA.2013.6631284.

[3] Panzirsch, Singh, Sierotowicz and Dietrich, IEEE RA-L, accepted January 2024.
    Extension of the Deflection-Domain Passivity Approach for Variable
    Stiffnesses to SO(3).
    https://elib.dlr.de/206285/1/panzirsch24ral1_copyright.pdf
    The author's manuscript describes an important limitation of time-based
    energy controls: an empty tank can cause sudden force attenuation and
    zero-stiffness periods. Their method distributes the required attenuation
    over spring deflection, including coupled rotations, with teleoperation
    experiments. Passivity alone is not a lower bound on task authority or a
    proof of accurate tracking. Abstract and opening discussion inspected via
    the indexed author PDF; direct PDF opening returned an internal error.

[4] Riviere, Rader and Thakor, IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering
    45(7), July 1998, 839–846. Adaptive Canceling of Physiological Tremor for
    Improved Precision in Microsurgery.
    https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/pub_files/pub3/riviere_cameron_1998_1/riviere_cameron_1998_1.pdf
    An adaptive frequency estimator drives active cancellation on a benchtop
    instrument. Recordings from simulated surgery provide the input. This is
    direct evidence that a narrow disturbance model can help. Intended motion
    was not measured independently; an offline low-pass estimate was used for
    one error metric. The discussion reports sensor noise and beam resonance
    as limits. Full eight-page manuscript inspected.

[5] Sariyildiz, Hangai, Uzunovic, Nozaki and Ohnishi, 2020.
    Stability and Robustness of the Disturbance Observer-based Motion Control
    Systems in Discrete-Time Domain.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08075
    The authors derive bounds on observer tuning from stability, performance,
    and noise sensitivity, and report experiments. Their result warns against
    assuming more bandwidth always gives better rejection. Measurement choice
    matters; their position/velocity and acceleration cases have different
    constraints. Primary abstract inspected, not a full implementation audit.

[6] Muramatsu and Katsura, IEEE Transactions on Industrial Informatics 14(10),
    2018, 4446–4456. An Adaptive Periodic-Disturbance Observer for
    Periodic-Disturbance Suppression.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00487v2
    A disturbance observer uses a delay element to reject a fundamental and
    harmonics. An adaptive notch filter estimates changing frequency. The paper
    reports multi-axis manipulator experiments. Thus periodic rejection and
    adaptive notch estimation are existing baselines, not a new consequence of
    our Lean work. Primary abstract inspected. The arXiv upload is later than
    the 2018 journal publication. DOI: 10.1109/TII.2018.2804338.

[7] DeWolf, Jaworski and Eliasmith, Frontiers in Neurorobotics 14 (2020), 568359.
    Nengo and Low-Power AI Hardware for Robust, Embedded Neurorobotics.
    https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2020.568359/full
    A neural adaptive component augments an operational-space controller on a
    physical Kinova Jaco arm with an unexpected load. CPU, GPU, and Loihi
    implementations are compared. This is evidence for a practical adaptive
    implementation. Spiking hardware does not remove observability, authority,
    delay, or stability premises. The primary article and arm-control section
    were inspected; code was not run.

[8] Liu et al., Robotics: Science and Systems 2026.
    Learning Native Continuation for Action Chunking Flow Policies (Legato).
    https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss22/p058.html
    The policy learns continuation across chunks and conditions for varying
    inference delay. Reported real-robot results address smoothness, multimodal
    switching, hesitation, and completion time. Those objectives already have
    close VLA prior work. A proof proposal must distinguish bounded physical
    error and worst-case response from measured command smoothness. Official
    proceedings abstract inspected; earlier project review also read v1 text.

THREE ESSENTIAL EXISTING BASELINES

RTC already preserves a committed prefix while inference runs:
https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07339v1
Its full text was inspected again. Learned continuation and multirate execution
are established. Neither a new splice rule nor a faster planner is sufficient
by itself to claim novelty.

Ruckig already constructs jerk-limited trajectories from general initial and
target position, velocity, and acceleration:
https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss17/p015.pdf
The primary paper was inspected again. Our constant-jerk segment is narrower.

Reference governors already admit or modify references to enforce constraints.
Bemporad's 1998 nonlinear result includes stability and tracking conditions:
https://cse.lab.imtlucca.it/~bemporad/publications/papers/ieeetac-rgnl.pdf
The author's primary manuscript abstract was inspected. Adding an admission
gate alone is not a new control architecture.

SMALLEST USEFUL CONTRACT TO PROVE

Start with a scalar translation axis. Separate four contracts: task observation,
command generation, physical response, and backup. This keeps assumptions visible.

Task observation. During a hold task, let c be the fixed intended position.
Each planner proposal p_k obeys |p_k-c| <= epsilon. The retained anchor a also
obeys |a-c| <= epsilon. Do not treat this as a property supplied by a VLA unless
there is evidence for it. Set an update threshold H >= 2 epsilon.

Noise rejection. Admit a target change only when |p_k-a| > H, followed by the
feasibility checks below. Then bounded proposal noise cannot cause an update:
|p_k-a| <= |p_k-c| + |c-a| <= 2 epsilon <= H.
The execution remains at its current hold policy. This prevents planner-driven
motion, while the physical hold theorem still permits its stated error tube.

Response to a real change. Suppose the intended target changes and remains at
c'. All subsequent valid proposals obey |p_k-c'| <= epsilon. If
|c'-c| > H + 2 epsilon, then the next valid proposal crosses the threshold.
This follows from |p_k-a| >= |c'-c| - |p_k-c'| - |a-c|.
The theorem must state what happens for smaller changes: they may be ignored.
A threshold creates a resolution/response tradeoff, not a free improvement.

Timing and feasibility. Suppose a valid proposal arrives within P seconds,
admission computation finishes within D seconds, and a feasible continuation
can begin within S seconds. Then acceptance and onset occur within P+D+S.
This is an onset bound, not an arrival bound. A declared motion duration T and
tracking guarantee are needed to bound target acquisition by P+D+S+T.
If no feasible path exists, the theorem must not promise response. The supervisor
must remain in the already certified hold or stopping set.

Motion. Use the executed state at the actual switch, preserve the committed
prefix, and match position, velocity, and acceleration. Reuse SmoothReplan's
finite-history jerk and interval checks. Its scalar checks do not certify
collision avoidance, orientation, contact force, or inverse kinematics.

Physical authority. Reuse RobustHold only with its actual premises. In that
model, |w| <= 2R, 8 <= r <= 12, and the invariant initial cube give |x-a| <= R.
The target bound is 26R in the stated units. If an actuator bound U is used to
rule out saturation, require 26R <= U and prove that the implemented saturation
is inactive. Sample, sensor, and delay errors consume the disturbance budget.
For a fixed intended c, this composes to |x-c| <= R+epsilon while holding.

Backup. Every admitted motion must retain a stop or hold reserve through the
next decision deadline, including a missed update. This recursive feasibility
step is not supplied by rejecting a candidate or by a jerk bound. It is the
main missing composition with our existing braking results.

No hidden progress claim. A noise bound does not prove the planner proposes a
useful target. A nonempty feasible set does not force the supervisor to use it.
Task progress needs a persistent feasible proposal, a selection rule, and a
task-specific progress measure. Emergency handling needs its own timing and
motion limits, rather than silently bypassing a smoothness theorem.

This contract is a proposed research target. It is not yet a Lean result.
Its elementary threshold and timing lemmas are not publication novelty.

TWO NECESSARY LIMITS TO INCLUDE IN THE PROOF PACKAGE

Intent ambiguity. For the same observed trace y(t), one admissible explanation
can be intended motion y(t) with zero disturbance. Another can be intended rest
with disturbance y(t). An algorithm receiving only that trace has the same
output in both cases. It cannot guarantee exact tracking in the first and exact
cancellation in the second when y is nonzero. Frequency overlap is one way this
ambiguity arises. Extra task labels, force/contact data, or a separation model
can change the problem. They must become explicit premises.

Finite authority. If a disturbance requires more opposing input than the arm
can deliver, exact rejection is impossible. Even within authority, unmeasured
disturbance onset and delay can cause nonzero transient error. A useful theorem
therefore bounds motion and response time. It does not assert zero motion for
all arms, loads, contacts, or disturbances.

FAIR EXPERIMENT AND NOVELTY SCREEN

Freeze a common sequence of proposed targets, timestamps, inference delays,
measurement errors, and emergency triggers before tuning controllers. Include:
bounded alternating proposal noise; a sustained true step; a slow intended
drift; an intentional oscillation inside the rejection frequency band; changing
periodic disturbance; a payload change; and a missed update. Use known intended
motion in simulation rather than defining truth with the tested filter.

Compare raw replacement, minimum dwell, low-pass filtering, hysteresis alone,
Ruckig with current-state initialization, and our certified admission. Use the
same actuator, sensor, delay, position, velocity, acceleration, and jerk limits.
If actual RTC or Legato is not run, label a prefix-preserving surrogate as a
surrogate. Compare physical disturbance observers in a separate layer so a
better low-level controller is not mistaken for a better planner policy.

Measure maximum and RMS actual hold error, command error, unintended reversals,
actual response delay, target acquisition time, constraint violations, input
saturation, rejected proposals, backup use, and completion of the task. Report
frequency-band error separately from whole-trajectory error. An algorithm that
holds forever can score well on jitter while failing every motion task.

Use three test levels. First, exact scalar command checks. Second, the declared
lagged plant with bounded errors and disturbances. Third, a flexible/contact
simulation as an external challenge. Passing level three is numerical evidence;
it is not covered by the scalar lag theorem. Physical tests remain a separate
stage. Record all failed and out-of-contract cases.

Potential contribution: a small replayable certificate that jointly bounds
unwanted physical motion, response time to a distinguishable feasible update,
and retained stopping reserve under asynchronous execution. The paper needs a
clear benefit over reference governors, robust filtering, trajectory generation,
and current VLA continuation methods. This search identifies a plausible place
to test for that benefit; it does not establish a literature gap or novelty.

A low-pass equation, a jerk-splice lemma, or an energy tank on its own is not
enough. A clean negative result about conflicting specifications can also be
useful, but it must go beyond the elementary ambiguity example above.
