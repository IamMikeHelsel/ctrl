Independent challenge: steadiness under repeated planning
17 October 2026

Scope reviewed
SmoothReplan.lean proves command-level C2 switching and bounded jerk for a finite
replacement history. It does not prove that the command stops oscillating.
RobustHold.lean proves a physical invariant position tube for the stated scalar
lag model. It does not prove exact rest, convergence, or rejection of every
periodic disturbance. Both results are useful. Neither supports “any arm dead
steady.” This is a mathematical review, not a neurology claim.

A precise next theorem
Let p_k be a scalar task target at observation k. Let y_k be its noisy proposal.
Assume |y_k-p_k| <= epsilon. This is an input contract; ordinary VLA outputs do
not acquire a deterministic error bound merely by being called predictions.
Let c be the last committed proposal. A gate updates c to y_k only when
|y_k-c| > H. It may also require N consecutive proposals beyond the same side.
Use H >= 2 epsilon for noise rejection and H > 2 epsilon for a finite switch
budget. Distinguish the candidate target from the executed trajectory.

1. Noise cannot repeatedly change a fixed target.
If p is constant and c is one observation of p, then |y_k-c| <= 2 epsilon.
The gate never fires when H >= 2 epsilon. If c is arbitrary at initialization,
this statement needs |c-p| <= epsilon, or it only holds after the first accepted
observation. Equality at H is rejected by the strict gate.

2. A persistent separated target is eventually admitted by the decision gate.
If |p_new-c| > H+epsilon throughout the evidence window, each valid y lies
beyond H on the same side of c. N consecutive observations therefore trigger
acceptance by the Nth arrival. This is a decision-gate result. It does not say
a safe trajectory exists to reach the target. A separate failed motion-admission
check must not be hidden behind a claim of guaranteed acceptance.
If observation gaps are at most Ts and total compute/handoff delay is at most
tau, detection and commitment take at most N*Ts+tau after a change just missed
an observation. Ts is a maximum inter-arrival time, not an average frequency.

3. True-target variation pays for accepted switches.
At two successive acceptance indices i<j, the old commitment is y_i and the
new one is y_j. The gate gives H < |y_j-y_i|. The error contract gives
|y_j-y_i| <= 2 epsilon + |p_j-p_i|. Thus H-2 epsilon < |p_j-p_i|.
For M accepted targets, the disjoint intervals between those indices give
(M-1)*(H-2 epsilon) < total variation of p, when M>=2. With a non-strict
total-variation bound V, (M-1)*(H-2 epsilon) < V. For M=1 use the trivial
non-strict bound instead; do not state 0<V when V may be zero.
A weak version uses <= and covers all M. This bound is independent of how
many proposals were evaluated. It needs H>2 epsilon to bound switch count.
This is a stronger claim than a minimum timer. It still permits repeated
switches if the actual task target has enough variation.

4. Rejected proposals give a useful target error bound.
For the simple one-sample gate, no update implies |y_k-c| <= H, hence
|p_k-c| <= H+epsilon. With an N-sample evidence gate, this bound is not true
while a separated target is awaiting sufficient evidence. Give that pending
state a latency bound instead. Do not silently reuse the single-sample bound.
After acceptance, |p_k-c_new| <= epsilon.

5. Bounded target speed gives a useful delay price.
If |p(t)-p(s)| <= L*|t-s| and execution uses a commitment delayed by at most
Delta, then the target error can increase by at most L*Delta during the delay.
This bounds target staleness, not physical tracking error. The latter requires
a separate dynamics model or a proved tracking-error estimate.

Reserve room to reject or wait safely
A decision gate cannot delay a safety override unless a fallback reserve covers
the wait. Suppose remaining decision/actuation delay is Delta, current forward
speed <= V and forward acceleration <= A throughout that delay. Then extra
forward travel <= V*Delta + A*Delta^2/2 and terminal speed <= V+A*Delta.
Add the certified stopping distance from that terminal state. In the current
lag model, preserve the acceleration coordinate; stopping distance depends on
it as well as speed. Do not substitute a velocity-only bound without proof.
If only initial acceleration A0 and jerk bound J are available, the delay
bounds become V*Delta+A0*Delta^2/2+J*Delta^3/6 for travel,
V+A0*Delta+J*Delta^2/2 for speed, and A0+J*Delta for acceleration.
These are one-sided envelopes derived by integration. The initial bounds and
nonnegative Delta must be stated. They do not cover contact impulses.

Counterexamples the theorem must survive
A. Bounded jerk can oscillate forever.
For any A>0 and omega>0, q(t)=A sin(omega*t) has jerk magnitude at most
A*omega^3. Choosing A*omega^3<=J satisfies a positive jerk limit while motion
never settles. Even a fixed command can coexist with unmodelled flex motion.
Therefore command smoothness, low switch count and physical steadiness are
three separate properties.

B. A minimum wait alone does not make a switch smooth.
The existing dwell_alone_not_jerk_limited theorem already proves this for an
acceleration step after any wait. A dwell limit also permits a periodic
alternation with a period longer than the wait. Deadband and dwell do not
rule out disturbance-driven mechanical vibration.

C. Intent and artifact may be observationally identical.
Let y(t)=A sin(omega*t) with 0<A<=epsilon. World 1 has intentional target
p=y and zero observation error. World 2 has fixed target p=0 and error y.
Both satisfy |y-p|<=epsilon and present identical observations. A causal
deterministic controller receives identical histories and must produce the
same command. It cannot both track p exactly in world 1 and hold zero exactly
in world 2. Frequency, task intent, force sensing, a plant model, or another
assumption is needed to distinguish them. Calling one waveform tremor does
not supply that information.

D. Exact rest cannot be robust to an unknown instantaneous acceleration force.
At a common state and history in x''=u+d, the same causal command u must act
in two allowed worlds d=+D and d=-D. For D>0, u+D and u-D cannot both be zero.
This rules out a universal exact-rest claim under that information pattern.
It does not rule out disturbance measurement, bounded error, adaptation,
compliance control, or useful practical suppression.

E. The current holding model has an explicit persistent-error floor.
At equilibrium, v=a=0 and u=-8*(x-c). Its dynamics require u+w=0, so
x-c=w/8. A nonzero constant target error w therefore permits a displaced
equilibrium. Time-varying w can sustain motion. The existing invariant cube
is a robust tube, not a theorem that the arm stops at c.

Connection to the existing proofs
For a new reference c_new=c_old+delta, every existing transformed holding
coordinate changes by -delta at the same physical state. Thus an old cube
of radius R_old fits a new cube of radius R_new whenever
R_old+|delta| <= R_new. This is a direct handoff geometry check. A sharper
check evaluates all three coordinates at the actual switch. It must not
reset position, velocity, or acceleration.
A command-level matched splice preserves C2 and the jerk bound. A separate
physical handoff certificate checks the measured-state uncertainty, delay,
control authority and reserve. Their composition must identify which signal
is a commanded position and which signal is an acceleration target.

Recommended scope
Prove a proposal gate with noise rejection, a finite variation switch budget,
and a quantified response delay. Show exact counterexamples for a timer-only
policy and a smooth but oscillatory command. Then connect accepted switches
to an actual jerk-limited segment and a braking/holding reserve. Compare
against matched smoothing alone and a timer with the same delay budget.
Do not call an assumed tracking-error bound a new physical tracking proof.
Neurology can motivate the architecture; it cannot establish the robot model.
Novelty is not established by this note.

Sharper variation and detectability results
For the single-observation gate selected for implementation, let n be the
number of accepted switches, S the sum of accepted target step magnitudes,
and V the true-target total variation over the same interval. Include the
initial commitment as a valid epsilon-observation. The triangle inequality
and gate give S<=V+2*epsilon*n and H*n<=S. Hence, when H>2*epsilon,
  n <= V/(H-2*epsilon),
  S <= H*V/(H-2*epsilon).
These weak inequalities include zero switches and V=0. They give a useful
bound on requested target motion. They do not bound the arc length of the
executed trajectory. That needs trajectory/control properties. The factor
diverges as H decreases to 2*epsilon, which states a real design tradeoff.

A change relative to the committed estimate is not the same as a true step.
The guaranteed response condition |p_new-c|>H+epsilon becomes the sufficient
true-step condition |p_new-p_old|>H+2*epsilon when |c-p_old|<=epsilon.
Keep these two statements distinct in examples and prose.

There is an observation-level limit even for a more elaborate causal policy.
For 0<=delta<=2*epsilon, the same reading y=delta/2 is epsilon-consistent with
true target 0 and with true target delta. Construct two histories that agree
before the change and have this common reading thereafter: one true target
remains zero, the other steps to delta. Both obey the error bound. A causal
gate sees identical observations. A requirement to never respond in the
first world contradicts a requirement to always respond in the second.
This is a witness of missing information, not a proof that every small real
change is undetectable in practice. Additional assumptions can separate them.

Tight examples for the selected scalar gate
The no-update error H+epsilon is attained by c=0, y=H, p=H+epsilon.
The gate rejects equality. The true-step guarantee H+2*epsilon cannot be
lowered for this gate: p_old=0, c=epsilon, p_new=H+2*epsilon,
y_new=H+epsilon gives |y_new-c|=H and is rejected.
The variation amplification is a sharp supremum when epsilon>0 and H>2*epsilon.
Take p0=0, c0=-epsilon, p1=H-2*epsilon+eta, c1=p1+epsilon with eta>0.
Then V=H-2*epsilon+eta and S=H+eta. Their ratio tends to H/(H-2*epsilon)
as eta decreases to zero. This is sharpness for this gate, not a claim that
it is the best possible policy with all possible extra information.

Physical handoff and data-age cautions
Reference-shift cube inclusion must also check the new physical interval:
c_new-R_new>=L and c_new+R_new<=U. Radius inflation alone can exceed a travel
limit. Check command authority again for that new radius.
For delayed proposals, the error contract must apply at admission time.
If a stale proposal has model error epsilon_model, age at most Delta, and
the true target moves at speed at most L, then epsilon_model+L*Delta is an
error bound at admission. Without a target-speed or prediction-error bound,
age alone does not supply one. A VLA confidence score is not such a bound.
The gate argument uses only a triangle inequality and can extend to a metric
space. Pose control needs a defined distance and consistent translation/
rotation scales. The extension does not establish dynamics or contact safety.

Source review: CommitmentGate.lean
Reviewed all 14 theorem statements and proofs after the first compile repair.
No mathematical defect found. The finite-history premises are valid anchors
and accepted step lengths; they do not assume the desired variation bound.
The file does not yet construct an accepted subhistory from the implemented
history recursion. Its finite-variation theorem is therefore a certificate
for histories satisfying those two input properties, not that extraction.
The overlap theorem proves a common-observation witness. It correctly does
not claim a formal impossibility theorem for arbitrary causal algorithms.
The source states that the physical tracking transfer is conditional.

The main integration caveat is the boundary after the proposal gate. The
selected_target_error theorem describes the gate output. If a downstream
safe-motion check rejects this target and keeps an older one, that retained
target need not satisfy H+epsilon error. A composed controller must expose
this case. Safety does not imply task progress.

A useful next composition would put the gated reference directly into the
actual plant ODE as c(t)=history y c H (sampleCount t). For a constant true
target and uniformly valid observations, the history theorem makes c(t)=c.
Then the existing physical hold certificate gives |x-p|<=R+epsilon without
assuming that final target-relative bound. This would check the interface.
It would remain a bounded-tube theorem, not an exact-rest or tremor-removal
result. Adding only a theorem whose ODE already assumes c fixed would add
little beyond a transparent triangle inequality.
