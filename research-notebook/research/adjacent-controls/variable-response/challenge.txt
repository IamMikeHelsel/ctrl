VARIABLE RESPONSE — INDEPENDENT CHALLENGE
17 October 2026. Review only; no proof source edits or Lean builds.

Early design check
The scalar comparison design is sound. An upper acceleration comparison with
target b_hi chooses r_lo when its initial acceleration is at least b_hi, and
r_hi otherwise. The comparison curve stays on the same side of its target.
This permits arbitrary continuous rates and targets within their stated bounds.
The proposed IsTrajectory contract includes full-state continuity on [0,H]
and the actual interior ODE, without an analytic-flow equality or path-safety
premise. Final compiled sources and theorem closures remain to be reviewed.

Required first-stop claim
State an actual first time T_stop with v(T_stop)=0 and v(t)>0 for 0<=t<T_stop,
for initial v(0)>0. Prove it exists before a stated finite deadline. Then bound
the maximum forward displacement up to that first zero. A root assumed in a
premise, or a generic IVT theorem without a discharged final-velocity bound,
does not yet establish that the proposed braking model stops in time.

Concrete challenges
1. A negative effective braking capability is essential. If a0=0 and b(t)=0,
   positive r(t) of any size leaves acceleration zero and v0>0 never stops.
   A wider lag interval alone cannot represent or solve lost braking strength.
2. Endpoint position is insufficient. With x0=0,v0=1,a0=b=-1, every positive
   rate gives x(t)=t-t^2/2. At H=2, x(H)=0, but the first zero of velocity is
   at1 and the forward excursion is1/2. An endpoint-only .25 travel check
   would pass incorrectly. Bound the full interval or prove the comparison
   curve's maximum occurs at its first velocity zero.
3. First zero can occur during a bounded command delay. Do not assume positive
   velocity at engagement merely because initial velocity is positive. A global
   continuity/IVT argument from initial positive to final nonpositive velocity
   can establish existence despite earlier zeros.
4. At stop velocity zero, acceleration may remain negative. Continuing the
   command can reverse motion. First-zero travel is not maintained stopping,
   two-sided safety after stopping, a power-loss stop, or a holding guarantee.

Delay and measurement contract
For 0<=delta<=D, x0<=X, v0<=V with V>=0, and a(t)<=A with A>=0 throughout
the pending interval, the upper engagement bounds
  x(delta)<=X+V*D+A*D^2/2; v(delta)<=V+A*D; a(delta)<=A
are valid. The last bound must include the engagement endpoint and delta=0.
These are upper bounds, not equalities of the actual engagement state.
If the stated candidate approach speed is measured, use its upper uncertainty
bound. Position error alone does not account for velocity/acceleration error.
An acceleration bound during delay is a plant/controller assumption that needs
its own evidence; it must not be inferred from the desired brake command.

Composition boundary
Per-hold boxes can enclose a finite prefix, provided the actual state is
continuous at phase changes. Reinitializing a comparison box is permissible;
reinitializing the actual state is not. Unknown delay can be bounded by a common
engagement box, but total deadline must include delay plus braking duration.
GeneralHandoff can attach a checked terminal box to the old robust tail only
if the actual tail obeys that fixed-rate sampled ODE and the whole terminal box
satisfies V<=12. Thermal drift continuing into the tail remains outside it.

No publication novelty follows from this design check. The potentially useful
extension is the changed forcing class and a concrete first-stop envelope under
nonzero uncertainty, tied to an explicit application contract.

Remaining event boundary, being addressed separately
ActualStop requires the braking ODE and target upper bound for the entire .5 s
after engagement, even when the first zero occurs earlier. It does not directly
cover a real controller that changes its target to hold at that event. An
event-terminated contract must remove both the post-zero ODE and the negative
target requirement. A guard saying only that all EARLIER velocities are positive
is insufficient: it remains true at the first-zero instant itself. Including
current positive velocity avoids requiring the old command at the switching
instant; the state bound at that instant must follow by continuity. EventStop
is in development and is not covered by this frozen-source audit yet.

Event-terminated stopping audit, 17 October 2026
Reviewed frozen EventStop.lean SHA-256

Its local compile log reports all three public closures with only the three
standard axioms and no warnings. The five-module replay above predates this
addition; parent-owned expanded replay is pending at this review point.

The event boundary identified above is resolved in this new theorem. BeforeStop
includes the current instant, so the guard is false at the first zero itself.
Both the actual braking ODE and the negative-target requirement use that guard.
The auxiliary bCut agrees with the real target strictly before the endpoint,
then supplies an artificial endpoint value only to apply the closed-interval
comparison. It imposes no value on the actual command at the first zero and
does not reset or replace the actual state. Closed state continuity carries the
bound to the event. An initial first zero during the delay remains covered.

Existence is not assumed: first_zero_or_positive gives either a first zero or
positive velocity throughout the horizon. The latter case activates braking
throughout the required comparison interval and contradicts the proved final
nonpositive velocity. The position bound is then derived up to the first zero.
No safety or terminal conclusion has been hidden in the input contract. I found
no blocking mathematical defect in this frozen source.

Remaining assumptions are explicit: global full-state continuity, bounded
continuous rate across the finite horizon, delay kinematics and acceleration
bound across the whole delay interval, and sufficient effective braking while
the true first-zero event has not happened. Later motion is unspecified. This
supports a first-stop bound; it proves neither maintained rest nor a safe hold
controller, and does not attach the old fixed-rate all-time invariant.

The guard is a mathematical event condition, not an implemented stop detector.
An estimated/sampled detector that switches early while actual velocity is
still positive can violate the guarded negative-target premise. Initial
measurement uncertainty is covered; continuing sensor error, event detection,
sampling delay and mode-switch implementation still need their own contract.
This is the next useful composition proof if a real stopping controller is the
goal. Calibrating the rate, strength, delay and pending-acceleration bounds on
the intended mechanism remains the larger engineering evidence gate.

Current judgment
The bundle now proves a concrete conditional first-stop property with variable
response, nonzero initial uncertainty and bounded delay. This is more useful
than the original fixed-lag academic case. It is still a synthetic formalization
of established comparison/first-event ideas, not evidence of a new control law,
an unsolved named problem, an identified physical model, or PL d/e compliance.
The scalar attribution example and this dynamic stopping theorem remain separate
contracts until a sound measurement-to-dynamics interface is provided.
