Variable-response coordinate envelope: checked scope

Module: ComparisonEnvelope.lean
Namespace: LeanControls.VariableResponse

The independent actual-trajectory predicate IsTrajectory H r b f requires:
- full-state continuity on the closed interval [0,H];
- interior derivatives x'=v, v'=a, a'=r(t)*(b(t)-a).
It does not assume an analytic solution, a state bound, a stopping event,
or equality with a selected comparison trajectory.

The public coordinate_enclosure theorem assumes H>=0, r continuous on [0,H],
0<rlo<=r(t)<=rhi, blo<=b(t)<=bhi, and componentwise initial-state bounds.
It proves lower and upper bounds for all three state components at EVERY t in
[0,H]. The bounds are the existing exact heldSolution at two fixed comparison
rates and targets. This includes the zero-length interval and both endpoints.
No derivative at either endpoint is required. b need not be separately assumed
continuous; the given classical trajectory and the stated ODE must still exist.

Upper comparison:
  initial state zHi, target bHi;
  upperRate = rlo when bHi<=zHi[2], otherwise rhi.
Lower comparison:
  initial state zLo, target bLo;
  lowerRate = rhi when bLo<=zLo[2], otherwise rlo.

The rate choices follow the sign of target minus acceleration. Faster response
increases acceleration when below its target and decreases it when above.
The scalar integrating-factor theorem linear_comparison proves acceleration
order. derivative_comparison then lifts that order to velocity and position.
These are genuine differential-equation arguments; there is no sampling grid,
numerical integration acceptance, hidden uniqueness premise, or native decision.

upper_flow_bound argument order:
  hH hrc hrlo0 hrlo hrhi hb hf hi t ht i
where hb is the upper target bound, hi the upper initial-state bound,
ht : t in Icc 0 H, and i : Fin 3. lower_flow_bound is analogous.
The final combined theorem takes both target and initial bounds.

The theorem permits arbitrarily varying continuous rates within the interval;
there is no bound on r'. It does not cover discontinuous rates by this one
contract, or arbitrary absolutely-continuous solutions with almost-everywhere
ODEs. Such extensions require explicit concatenation or solution semantics.
The effective target b can account for a declared command/gain/error envelope,
but the theorem does not identify or validate that envelope from hardware data.

This is finite-horizon enclosure. It does not itself prove that velocity first
reaches zero, that the axis remains at rest after reaching zero, or that a
friction brake is modeled correctly. It also does not transfer the old fixed-
rate all-time feedback invariant to time-varying rates. Parent-owned FirstStop
and StoppingCertificate build separate stopping-event consequences.
