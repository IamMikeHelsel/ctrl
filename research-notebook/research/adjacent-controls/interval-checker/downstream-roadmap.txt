Proof dependencies and a coherent paper scope
17 October 2026

One research claim can require several theorems. Each theorem should either
support that claim, remove a stated restriction, or enable an independent reuse.
A larger theorem count is not a publication result.

Proved interfaces
1. Rational interval operations and held-flow containment.
2. Finite endpoint composition with outward rounding.
3. Exact exponential coefficient bounds on sixteen rate cells for h=0.05.
4. Endpoint correspondence with actual continuous executions of the fixed-rate model.
5. One concrete 25-step direct-feedback cell reaches V<=12 at T=1.25.
   Holds are 0.05 and errors are zero globally. This is not the full 1024-cell proof.
6. Existing brake proof: continuous path, terminal entry, robust sampled tail,
   continuous handoff, execution existence/uniqueness, and physical scaling.

Next dependency, highest reuse value
A. Whole-hold position enclosure from an initial box and bounded command.
   It must cover the complete interval, not just endpoints. For nonzero
   post-clamp error d, do not assume |u|<=1; include its bound.
   Unlocks: path safety for every accepted finite trace using these flow bounds.
B. Handoff at sample N with assumptions restricted to the finite prefix.
   Unlocks: the existing robust invariant tail after a checked direct-feedback,
   braking, or other admitted prefix. The present pilot assumes zero errors globally.
C. Efficient certificate acceptance and a proved covering of all starting cells.
   Unlocks: fair whole-region controller comparisons and repeated certificate search.
   The single pilot compile cost is a performance warning, not a full-run benchmark.
D. Application-selected error model and admission rule.
   Unlocks: a claim about a specific axis only if its real behavior meets the contract.
   Choose the next robustness extension from measured needs: estimation error,
   timing error, bounded residual forces, or lag variation. Do not add all by default.

Paper boundary
Keep checker soundness, path/terminal composition, one matched comparison, and
reproducibility together if they support one central verification claim.
A hardware-conformance study or a materially broader disturbance theorem may be
a later paper. Erdős results do not belong in this controls paper unless there is
a real mathematical dependency; none is established here.

Evidence boundary
The current model is an idealized carriage on a rail with first-order acceleration
lag. An acceleration-controlled horizontal servo stage is a plausible target.
No hardware validation, broader true recovery region, or publication novelty is
established. Independent review found no circular terminal premise, but flagged
both the missing continuous path and the globally restricted pilot execution.
