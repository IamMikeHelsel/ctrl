Naming and dependency boundary
FiniteHandoff is finite-prefix safety composition for the existing sampled
saturated-feedback law. Its global IsExecution predicate fixes that law before
and after sampleN. Unrestricted prefix errors do not make it an implemented
arbitrary prefix controller. Calling it a general controller-switching theorem
would exceed its contract. A controller-independent handoff interface can be
added, but it must state how the actual prefix is specified and validated.

The reusable chain is now: sound coefficient/interval step -> finite trace ->
actual sampled endpoints -> whole-hold path -> terminal invariant -> tail.
The pilot demonstrates this chain on one closed parameter cell. Efficiently
accepting a complete parameter covering is the next comparison gap. It is not
necessary to label the basic composition principle new control theory.

Result: no blocking mathematical or contract mismatch found in this pass.
IsContinuation has full-state continuity on nonnegative time and the actual
own-state feedback ODE on each open tail hold. Its first tail sample is f(T).
Translation preserves that feedback and obtains the old execution contract;
uniqueness then identifies the actual tail with the analytic state. Safety
requires T>=0, fixed r in[.9,1.1], positive lower dwell, holds<=.2, and the
combined held command-error budget<=1/90. The prefix safety and terminal
V(f(T))<=12 conditions are separate visible premises. They are not hidden in
the execution definition or the existence theorem.

The splice construction agrees with the supplied prefix through T, including
all three state components. Its tail starts from p(T), with no state reset.
Derivatives are required inside holds; the command can change at the handoff
and sample boundaries. This does not assert differentiability across those
boundaries. The construction for negative T is mathematically harmless; the
safety and shifted-execution theorems explicitly require nonnegative T.

The source and its scope note correctly disclose the central boundary:
an arbitrary continuous prefix is not necessarily a plant trajectory. For
example, the constant state curve p(t)=(0,1,0) is continuous but violates
x'=v. This is not a counterexample to the theorem; it shows why the existence
theorem must not be called existence of a physical whole-run execution.
A caller must separately establish prefix dynamics and any plant-parameter
consistency. The module also proves tail uniqueness from a common terminal
state, not uniqueness of arbitrary prefixes. It does not certify a switching
guard, repeated mode changes, state-estimation errors, switch-time uncertainty,
time-varying lag, or hardware conformance.

Naming: "general prefix handoff to the fixed sampled controller" is accurate.
"General hybrid safety controller" or "arbitrary controller safety" would
overstate it. FiniteHandoff/PilotContinuation remain the more specific result:
finite sampled-feedback prefix composition, with all-time actual-execution
safety for one positive-volume initial cell and one fixed-rate cell. The two
interfaces are complementary, not evidence of a new backup-control principle.
