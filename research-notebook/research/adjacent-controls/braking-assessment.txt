Backup stopping under actuator lag: bounded assessment
2026-10-17. Analytical derivation and numerical diagnostic, NOT a Lean proof.
No current paper, controls source or upstream repository was changed.

RECOMMENDATION
Good adjacent theorem target: a velocity- AND acceleration-dependent forward
travel guard for a bounded first-order actuator, followed by a separately
certified handoff into the existing invariant ellipsoid. This would address
an actual limitation of a fixed initial ellipsoid: available clearance and
motion toward the boundary can be used directly, while inadmissible states
can be identified by a necessary stopping-distance condition.
The stopping formula and backup-CBF idea are established control concepts.
The credible contribution would be a complete machine-checked composition
of actuator semantics, reaction delay, uncertainty, saturation, guard and
terminal handoff, with quantified conservatism. It is not new control theory
merely because the formula is formalized.

EXACT MODEL AND FIRST CANDIDATE THEOREM
One normalized axis:
  x'=v, v'=a, tau*a'=u-a;  U>0, tau>0, -U<=u<=U.
For the first theorem take v0>0 and 0<=a0<=U, one upper travel limit L,
no disturbance, no reaction delay, exact real states and constant known tau.
Apply u=-U. Write A=a0+U. Then
  a(t)=-U+A*exp(-t/tau),
  v(t)=v0-U*t+A*tau*(1-exp(-t/tau)),
  x(t)-x0=v0*t-U*t^2/2+A*tau*(t-tau*(1-exp(-t/tau))).
There is exactly one positive zero T of v, with
  v0/U < T < (v0+A*tau)/U.
(The non-strict versions cover degenerate boundary cases.) Acceleration
crosses zero once; velocity first rises then falls, and x reaches its global
maximum at T. Define D(v0,a0,tau,U)=x(T)-x0.

Candidate theorem: under these assumptions, an admissible input can keep
x(t)<=L for every t>=0 if and only if x0+D<=L; the constant backup u=-U
realizes the sufficient direction. This is an exact one-sided viability
boundary, not just an arbitrary sufficient ellipsoid.
Proof of necessity: the actuator is order preserving in its input. The
variation-of-constants kernel is nonnegative, so every u>=-U gives
x_u(t)>=x_brake(t) for every t. If x0+D>L, every admissible controller violates
the upper wall no later than its position at the backup's peak time T.
Prove this comparison explicitly; it cannot be inferred from simulations.

This theorem does NOT stop and hold the axis: at T, v=0 but a<0; continuing
u=-U moves indefinitely backward. It proves a one-sided forward-clearance
property. A two-sided travel guarantee requires a terminal handoff or a
separate finite-time arrest/hold maneuver. Merely switching to u=0 at T
still leaves negative velocity as the residual acceleration decays.

EXACT COUNTEREXAMPLE TO THE NAIVE GUARD
Take x0=0,v0=a0=U=tau=1 and immediate full braking u=-1. The instantaneous-
acceleration rule v0^2/(2U) says clearance1/2 suffices. In the actual lag model,
  x(1)=1/2+2*exp(-1)>1/2,
so that guard fails even with zero reaction delay and perfect measurements.
This inequality is exact (exp(-1)>0), although not yet Lean-checked here.
Numerical closed-form/root-bisection diagnostics give T approximately
2.8887033562 and D approximately2.6051031723. With a0=0 the distance is still
approximately1.1460182573. Thus accounting for positive a0 matters, and even
zero initial acceleration does not recover instantaneous braking.
With v0=a0=U=tau=1 and reaction delay0.2, holding u=+1 during reaction gives
x_delta=0.22,v_delta=1.2,a_delta=1 and total distance approximately3.4251522603.
These decimals are diagnostic only, not interval-certified bounds.

BOUNDED LAG AND REACTION DELAY: CORRELATION MATTERS
For braking from a FIXED initial state with a0>=-U, a(t) increases with tau;
therefore the worst braking distance on tau in[tau_min,tau_max] is attained
at tau_max. This comparison also has a direct differential-inequality form.

It is invalid to carry this conclusion blindly across a reaction interval.
When u=+U and a0<U, a smaller tau accelerates faster during that interval.
Our numerical diagnostic found, for v0=0.5,a0=-1,U=1,delay0.2, total forward
peak distances approximately0.26934,0.20965,0.16514,0.14338 for constant tau
0.25,0.5,1,2 respectively. The largest-lag shortcut underestimates this case.
This is numerical evidence against a proposed monotonic shortcut, not a
formal theorem or an exhaustive uncertain-parameter optimum computation.

A clean robust SUFFICIENT construction, initially with v0>=0 and 0<=a0<=U:
1. During a known reaction interval delta, allow arbitrary u in[-U,U].
   Use the upper comparison trajectory u=+U,tau=tau_min to obtain
     a_plus=U+(a0-U)*exp(-delta/tau_min),
     v_plus=v0+U*delta+(a0-U)*tau_min*(1-exp(-delta/tau_min)),
     d_plus=v0*delta+U*delta^2/2+
       (a0-U)*tau_min*(delta-tau_min*(1-exp(-delta/tau_min))).
2. Brake with u=-U and upper comparison initial(v_plus,a_plus),tau=tau_max.
3. Require L-x0 >= d_plus+D(v_plus,a_plus,tau_max,U).

The first segment is nondecreasing in position under these initial sign
restrictions. Both segments dominate the corresponding actual trajectory.
This comparison is sufficient for fixed unknown tau, and allows bounded
piecewise-continuous time-varying tau with the appropriate ODE semantics.
For freely varying tau, the fast-reaction/slow-braking endpoint pattern is
attainable. For a SINGLE unknown constant tau, combining the two different
endpoints is conservative; do not label this exact worst-case stopping
without optimizing the correlated whole trajectory over the same tau.
A known held pre-braking command can be modeled exactly instead of replacing
it by +U. A sampled trigger needs delta to include the worst intersample wait,
computation, communication and actuation latency under explicit assumptions.

A simpler conservative no-root bound for the braking segment is
  D(v,a,tau,U) <= (v+(a+U)*tau)^2/(2U).
Indeed v_brake(t)<=v+(a+U)*tau-U*t; integrate that positive line up to its
zero, noting the true forward peak occurs earlier. It is generally quite
conservative: the first example gives4.5 versus actual approximately2.6051.
This polynomial bound could be a small first Lean milestone before a verified
implicit-root enclosure. Avoid Lambert W as an unnecessary first dependency.

DISTURBANCE, SENSING AND TERMINAL SET
Do not transplant the current W=0.01 or 1/90 budget without its channel.
For command-equivalent disturbance w inside tau*a'=u+w-a, |w|<=W<U,
worst braking command becomes -(U-W), while the reaction upper input is U+W.
The acceleration invariant interval and initial upper bound change too.
For external jerk a'=(u-a)/tau+d, the equivalent command disturbance is tau*d;
a bound D_j becomes tau_max*D_j before comparison. Those are distinct models.
If |x-xhat|<=eps_x, etc., monotonicity supports evaluating an upper guard at
xhat+eps_x,vhat+eps_v,ahat+eps_a within the theorem's domain. That extension
still needs explicit proof, particularly near sign boundaries.

To obtain an ALL-TIME TWO-SIDED robot-axis result, certify the whole backup
segment and a terminal state inside the existing travel-invariant ellipsoid;
then invoke the existing uncertain sampled controller theorem after the
switch. A zero-velocity event alone does not imply ellipsoid membership or
zero acceleration. The handoff guard must check the full(x,v,a) state and
both travel boundaries. No arbitrary initial position-only claim follows.

REUSE VS NEW WORK
Reuse: exact held exponential solution/derivative lemmas, rate-to-physical-tau
scaling, explicit disturbance-channel definitions, clipping bounds, sampling
clock and all-real-time execution infrastructure, existing ellipsoid as a
terminal invariant set. The current quadratic vertex certificate proves a
particular feedback's energy decrease; it does not by itself prove extremal
stopping under constant maximum braking.
New proof work: input-order comparison, unique velocity-zero/max-position
characterization, stopping-distance dependence on a and tau, reaction-phase
comparison, sampled switching semantics, validated root bounds if used, and
a substantive terminal-set entry/handoff proof. Two-sided exact viability
or finite-time rest under lag is a further optimal-control problem, not a
free corollary of the one-sided theorem.

   https://arxiv.org/html/2104.11332v1
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11332
   Full HTML inspected, including the double-integrator stopping-distance
   example and backup-flow interpretation. It already presents the standard
   v^2/(2u_max) guard for an instantaneous-input double integrator and compares
   backup sets with Hamilton-Jacobi and SOS approaches. Our lag augmentation
   changes the model; the underlying stopping/backup idea is not new.

   Barrier Functions: Safety Under Uncertain Dynamics" (2024).
   https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07700v1
   The robust backup literature already bounds disturbed flow around a nominal
   backup trajectory. A claim that we invented uncertainty-aware backup
   safety would be indefensible. Primary HTML and abstract inspected; no
   reproduction of their simulation/controller was performed.
3. Gacsi, Kiss, Molnar, "Braking within Barriers: Constructive Safety-Critical
   Control for Input-Constrained Vehicles via the Backup Set Method" (2025).
   https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15797v1
   Full relevant setup inspected: a valid backup controller/set pair needs
   input admissibility and forward invariance; safe finite-horizon rollout
   must terminate in that backup set. Their ellipsoid construction provides
   close conceptual precedent. The vehicle lateral-braking application is not
   the same one-axis lag model, and its particular theorem was not reproduced.
4. Singletary, Chen, Ames, "Control Barrier Functions for Sampled-Data Systems
   with Input Delays" (2020), https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.06418
   Primary abstract checked; sampling and delay safety are established topics.

   https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20842 (Robust Adaptive Backup CBFs,2026),

NEXT EXPERIMENT / STOP GATE
Advance only a small first theorem: known fixed tau, immediate full braking,
exact one-sided stopping guard and the exact naive-guard counterexample.
Then prove the conservative bounded-lag/reaction comparison and compare its
admitted state region against the current ellipsoid on identical model and
limits. Success needs demonstrable useful states outside the ellipsoid plus
a valid terminal handoff for any claimed two-sided/all-time application.
Stop or downgrade to a tutorial artifact if the extension is only the old
v^2/(2U) identity, if safety is checked only at sample times, if the handoff
assumes instantaneous a=0 at v=0, or if keeping constant-tau correlation and
bounded disturbances cannot be justified. Do not start a large general CBF
framework before this model-specific obligation is closed.
