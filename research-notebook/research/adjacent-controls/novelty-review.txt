ADJACENT CONTROLS — SKEPTICAL NOVELTY SCREEN
17 October 2026; bounded literature screen, not a systematic review

Recommendation

The strongest nearby path is a checked implementation-to-safety composition, or
a genuine runtime safety shield with a certified backup policy. Adding delay,
dropout, actuator uncertainty or rounding as a label is not a novelty claim:
primary papers already address each. None of the four items below is established
here as an unsolved control-theory problem. They are proposed formal-methods or
application contributions whose distinct value must be demonstrated.

The current manuscript's advantage is its exact held-flow/model connection and
all-time guarantee for arbitrary admitted ODE executions. Its weakest publication
point remains whether another person can use the artifact to answer a practical
question. A second carefully chosen workflow is more valuable than another
numeric uncertainty interval or another theorem count.

Scores are ordinal editorial judgments, not probabilities: 1=weak, 2=limited,
3=plausible, 4=strong, 5=very strong. "Contribution runway" means a useful bounded
research deliverable appears available relative to the present artifact. It does
not mean the theorem is new, easy, likely to be solved, or likely to be accepted.

1. A checked fixed-point command implementation feeding the safety theorem

Closest overlap: Flessing, Devadze and Streif [F] already formalize fixed-point
controller computations and overflow freedom in Minlog, including polynomial
functions. Their paper explicitly targets the mathematical-controller/software
gap. Therefore "first verified fixed-point controller" and "overflow-free control"
are unavailable contribution claims. The present controller's integer gains
also make its arithmetic unusually easy; merely proving a rounding inequality
would be too small.

Smallest differentiating deliverable: specify one concrete signed-bitvector
program for sampling/encoding x,v,a, forming -x-3v-2a, clipping, and encoding the
actuator command. Fix widths, scales, rounding and saturation order. Prove both
absence of intermediate overflow and a command-error bound on the full admitted
ellipsoid. Compose that bound with the uncertain-lag, active-clipping, all-time
execution theorem. Include the actual state-dependent quantizer's recursive
execution, rather than assuming its error sequence is already admissible. The
user-visible output is a checked table of feasible sensor/output resolutions and
word widths for one physically scaled axis.

Smallest evidence that makes the composition meaningful: at least one admitted
fixed-point format and one intentionally inadequate format that is rejected for
a concrete overflow or error-budget reason; a runnable controller whose logical
arithmetic is exactly the function proved; and a second-person replay of the
format/parameter choice. Tests of emitted C do not establish C/assembly semantic
correspondence. State the compiler boundary unless a verified translation or
separate source-code proof closes it.

Falsification/stop gate: first derive actual geometric ranges and the combined
sensor+command error budget. Stop the proposed contribution if it reduces to
wrapping existing ideal rounding in a new theorem name, if the claimed deployed
code lacks semantic correspondence, or if no nontrivial format boundary can be
shown. If the pipeline is just [F] instantiated without a new useful composition,
frame it as replication/interoperability or change direction. New control theory
is unlikely here; a bounded implementation/refinement case study is credible.

2. A lag-aware backup shield that admits states beyond the existing ellipsoid

Closest overlap: disturbance-robust backup CBFs already use tubes around nominal
backup flows [B]. Their Assumption 1 requires a backup policy and a robustly
invariant backup set. Sampled-data backup safety with state uncertainty and input
delay also predates this work [D]. Input/actuator dynamics are already treated
through IQC-based safety design [I]. Thus neither "backup braking with lag" nor
"robust sampled safety filter" is, by itself, a new research question.

Smallest differentiating deliverable: take the existing ellipsoid as a verified
terminal set. Construct a certified backward-reachable collar under one explicit
brake-then-stabilize backup policy. Prove that a bounded arbitrary requested
command may be admitted for one sample only when its entire held segment stays
inside the position limits and its endpoint remains recoverable. Otherwise use
the backup. Prove recursive availability of a safe action and all-time safety
with uncertain fixed lag and the specified error budget. Start with one axis and
one boundary/direction; do not promise optimal stopping distance or full viability.

Required differentiator: exhibit an open region of initial states outside the
current ellipsoid that the shield admits, active saturation, and at least one
requested command that is modified. A position/velocity/acceleration slice and
comparison against the existing controller should show the added capability.
The interesting contribution is checking the complete admission/backup/terminal
composition, including intersample behavior and feasibility.

Falsification/stop gate: before formalizing the full shield, search for a state
in the proposed recoverable collar whose backup crosses the position boundary
before recovery. Such a trace rejects that collar/policy; it does not disprove all
backup methods. Stop if the certified collar is empty or wholly inside the old
set, if safety depends on assuming feasibility of the filter, or if the final
artifact cannot distinguish the proposed shield from existing backup frameworks.
An exact checked implementation of an established framework may still support an
application paper; a new control theorem would need a sharper demonstrated gap.

3. Timestamped sensing with stale measurements and missed updates

Closest overlap: [D] establishes sampled-data invariance with state uncertainty
and input delay; its delay construction assumes a known integer multiple of a
fixed controller period, with a startup safety condition. The recent weakly-hard
paper [W] handles window-bounded failures through graph-based barrier functions.
These are direct overlap warnings, not evidence that a particular Lean execution
contract is already available.

Smallest differentiating deliverable: define acquisition time, receive/update
time, held output and a deterministic maximum measurement age for one axis. The
controller must use the trajectory's state at the acquisition timestamp, not an
independent bounded-error oracle. Derive the actual stale-feedback error from the
lag dynamics and prove an all-time safety contract with one explicit fallback
when freshness expires. Produce a checkable trade-off between sensor resolution,
sample duration and admissible age. Keep sensor age, network delay and missed
actuator updates separate in the model.

Why this might matter: it turns a controller's abstract error budget into a
testable scheduling/sensing requirement. That is stronger application evidence
than asserting an unexplained held error. A finite miss-pattern automaton is an
optional later extension; it should not be the first deliverable.

Falsification/stop gate: if missed packets merely enlarge the time between
successful updates while the old held input remains active, and that interval
already satisfies h<=1/5, the current theorem already covers it. Stop calling
that a new problem. For genuinely stale samples, stop if the error bound assumes
the very safety invariant it is supposed to establish without a valid bootstrap
argument, or if useful sensor age collapses to effectively zero. Before claiming
better tolerance than [W], compare the same hold/fallback semantics and failure
language. Otherwise this remains an engineering contract/formalization task.

4. Disturbances varying during a hold, with fixed actuator rate initially

Closest overlap: robust backup safety already accommodates bounded disturbances
[B]; robust CBF treatment of time-varying nonlinear parameter uncertainty is
established [P]. Extending the present constant-drive response formula is useful
for its model fidelity but not evidence of a novel robustness principle.

Smallest differentiating deliverable: keep r fixed in the existing interval and
the request sampled, but permit a continuous bounded drive disturbance d(t)
inside r*(u-a). Prove an appropriate convolution/tube energy bound, then an
all-time invariant for every admitted forcing function. State regularity and ODE
semantics explicitly. Continuous forcing is the smaller first target; measurable
forcing requires an almost-everywhere/Caratheodory execution contract and should
not be hidden under the current pointwise classical derivative assumptions.

Required evidence: show the changed disturbance class, a nonzero certified bound,
and a forcing example not represented by the old held-error theorem. Compare the
usable budget/conservatism with the old bound. Do not assume that the constant
input response estimate also bounds arbitrary input waveforms: cancellation
within a constant response can conceal a larger worst-case varying response.

Falsification/stop gate: use adversarial sign-varying forcing to test a proposed
bound before investing in formal analysis. Stop if the extension produces only
an unusably small disturbance budget or a standard integral inequality with no
reusable interface/application evidence. Time-varying r(t) is a second problem:
the old exponential flow/finite-vertex identity no longer applies, even when each
instantaneous rate lies in the old box. Do not combine both extensions at once.

Why not prioritize multi-axis composition now?

Independent Cartesian copies with separate limits give a product theorem, which
adds little scientific content. Coupled collision geometry, shared actuation or
cross-axis errors could matter, but they replace the present simple geometry and
require a concrete application plus separate literature review. The current
bounded screen does not establish novelty there. Treat it as a later application
test of an interface, not a fifth claimed open problem.

Proposed decision order

First compare [F] with an exact bitvector-to-uncertain-execution claim and obtain
one independent artifact consumer. If that differentiator is too thin, test the
backup collar/shield, whose added user capability is easier to display. Keep the
freshness contract as an alternative when real timing/sensor constraints exist.
Use within-hold forcing to remove a demonstrated modeling limitation, rather
than selecting it solely because it is mathematically adjacent. Do not combine
all four into a larger unreviewable paper.

Practical braking refinement after the parallel primary-paper screen

For a robot-facing deliverable, prioritize a braking admission guard parameterized
by current acceleration, lag and reaction delay; it has a clearer added behavior
than another robustness constant. Keep the complete backup shield as a later
milestone. A small first result can be a certified sufficient stopping envelope
and a terminal-recovery obligation, explicitly short of an implemented shield.

A useful diagnostic is the nominal full-brake trajectory with U=tau=v0=a0=1.
For held command -1, a(t)=-1+2 exp(-t), v(t)=3-t-2 exp(-t), and displacement
at t=1 is 1/2+2/e>1/2. Thus the familiar instantaneous-braking distance v0^2/(2U)
is already exceeded before stopping. This follows directly from the current
held-flow formula. It demonstrates the specification error of ignoring lag; it
is not a new control-theory result or a completed braking-safety proof.

The decisive gate is terminal behavior: the first v=0 state generally has
negative acceleration, so continuing the brake causes reverse travel. A two-sided
travel theorem must also bound that recovery or establish entry into a verified
terminal invariant. Reaction-delay motion, initial acceleration of either sign,
uncertain rates, and the opposite travel boundary must be part of the acceptance
test. Do not label a one-sided stopping-distance lemma a complete safety filter.

The parent independently inspected [W]'s discrete-time zero/hold dropout models
and [D]'s delay assumptions; those reinforce the overlap warnings. Switching r
only between holds also appears to reuse the existing uniform local invariant
through routine composition. It still needs an execution proof, but is unlikely
to be a differentiating paper contribution. This is distinct from r(t) varying
inside a hold, which invalidates the current exact-flow representation.

The choice between braking and fixed-point work should reflect the target:
braking offers the clearest new robot guard; arithmetic refinement offers a
focused formal-methods composition. Neither should be sold as a known open
mathematical problem or a likely acceptance without further comparison.

search hit does not establish novelty. No numerical solve or acceptance
