Empirical recovery comparison: useful certification extension, modest behavior change
2026-10-17

VERDICT
The experiment does not establish that the backup is needed for safety of
these tested trajectories. The original saturated sampled controller also
stayed within the travel limit and reached V<=12 in every tested case.
The backup gives a small peak-travel reduction, mixed terminal-energy results,
and increased command variation. Its demonstrated practical benefit is the
new proof route from an initial region outside the old certified ellipsoid.
Do not describe this as rescuing an unsafe baseline or uniformly improving
control performance.

REPRODUCE
From the workspace root:
  .tools/research-python/bin/python research/adjacent-controls/recovery-proof/experiment.py
Only the Python standard library is used; no installation, simulator or
service is needed. The script writes experiment-results.json, including
source hashes, parameters, per-run metrics, paired differences and normalized
state traces. Python 3.14 was used. All three files are outside the frozen
proof and paper directories. No proof files were edited.

MATCHED COMPARISON
There are 24 pairs: the 8 vertices of the specified initial box, each with
r=0.9,1.0,1.1. The controller gains remain fixed for every rate.
Initial box: x in [.99,1.01],v in [.49,.51],a in [-.01,.01].
Plant: x'=v,v'=a,a'=r(u-a), fixed r per run.
Direct method: u=clip(-x-3 v-2 a,-1,1) held for 0.1, beginning at time 0.
Recovery method: u=-1 for T=1.25, then the SAME sampled feedback with a new
sampling clock beginning at T. State is preserved at the switch.
Both are evaluated to normalized time 20 with zero measurement, quantization,
disturbance, timing and command error. Actual rate affects the simulated
plant, not the controller gains. No uncertain-model or sensor estimator is
implemented by this experiment.

Every interval uses the exact closed-form held solution evaluated in binary
floating point, with expm1 to reduce cancellation. There is no numerical ODE
integration. Position extrema are obtained numerically from endpoints and
velocity-zero roots, bracketed using acceleration's possible zero and bisected
60 times. These are numerical extrema, NOT rigorously rounded bounds.
Energy maxima are sampled at spacing<=.005, with all segment endpoints and
T included. Refining to .0025 changed the measured post-T peak V by 0 on these
runs; that sensitivity check is not a rigorous error estimate.

In both methods the post-T sampled energy maximum occurred at T. Terminal
energy is evaluated at exactly T even when this is inside a direct-control
hold. V at the initial state exceeds 12 in all cases, but that fact only says
the old initial-region theorem does not apply there. It does not show unsafe
motion. The analytic all-box lower bound is separately proved in RecoveryPath;
the empirical corner range above is not a replacement for that theorem.

PAIRED EFFECTS AND FAIRNESS
Backup peak |x| was lower in all 24 pairs, by 0.001090--0.002215 normalized units.
This is small compared with the roughly 0.57 remaining travel margin in the
worst direct case. Terminal V improved in 13 pairs and worsened in 11.
At r=.9, backup-minus-direct terminal V ranges from-.187059 to-.118842;
at r=1, from-.098852 to+.080151; at r=1.1, from+.105914 to+.407151.
Command total variation increased in every pair, by .047304--.472645.
Fewer command changes partly reflect holding a fixed command longer and
therefore fewer feedback intervals; they are not evidence of smoother control.
A change is counted when adjacent held values differ by more than 1 e-12;
the unspecified pre-initial command is excluded in both methods.

T=1.25 is not aligned with the baseline's 0.1 sampling clock, so the backup
also changes the feedback phase. A small hold-sensitivity comparison is saved:
  hold .05 (aligned): peak-travel improvement .001410--.002722;
                     terminal-V difference-.230739--+.359250.
  hold .10:           peak-travel improvement .001090--.002215;
                     terminal-V difference-.187059--+.407151.
  hold .20:           peak-travel improvement .000695--.001734;
                     terminal-V difference-.133026--+.461230.
No observed travel violations occurred for either method in these checks.
Thus the modest peak reduction is not solely an alignment artifact, but
terminal-energy dominance still fails. This remains a finite parameter check.

PUBLICATION USEFULNESS
Useful as an honest illustration accompanying a proved recovery theorem:
- It demonstrates nonvacuous starting states outside the original certificate.
- It shows how the actual backup and continuing controller behave without
  replacing the mathematical proof by plots or discretized acceptance.
- It exposes a real tradeoff: a slightly smaller forward excursion costs more
  sustained braking and command variation, with no uniform energy benefit.

A skeptical reviewer can reasonably ask whether it is simpler to certify the
original controller's finite prefix from this same box. That is an alternative
proof-development task, not something this experiment settles. The paper should
frame the result as a certified recovery composition and report the benign
baseline comparison rather than claim a superior controller.
A stronger application evaluation would compare certified region size and
verification effort against a matched direct-controller extension or existing
backup method, and quantify sensor/backup-error margin. No priority claim or
such broad comparison follows from these 48 main trajectories.

BOUNDARIES
The box corners do not establish behavior for every interior state under a
nonlinear sampled clamp, and three rate values do not verify the whole interval.
Finite horizon 20 does not prove all-future safety. Both phases have ZERO error
here; this does not test the post-handoff error allowance or any disturbance
robustness during backup. The existing formal statements, when fully composed,
have their own explicitly stated assumptions and are the safety evidence.
If nominal time scale is .1 s, the backup is 125 ms and horizon 20 is 2 s; this is an
illustrative conversion only. Reported axes and states are normalized. No
hardware data, calibrated motor model, tracking task, payload dynamics, energy
consumption, comfort/jerk optimization, emergency-stop compliance or deployment
acceptance was measured.

No additional SVG file was produced because this task's write ownership was
restricted to experiment.py, experiment-results.json and this review. The JSON
contains downsampled state/energy traces for a later normalized-axis plot.
