Eric Weinstein: what can we check in Lean?
Assessment date: 2026-10-17

Answer

Yes. We have checked one exact algebraic ingredient used in Geometric Unity.
We have not proved Geometric Unity, a new physical law, or a new research result.
The full theory is not a sensible short proof target in its present public form.

The result we checked

The Geometric Unity draft separates a symmetric tensor into its trace part and
its trace-free part. It chooses the sign of the trace part. That choice matters
for the signature used later in the construction [1, printed pp. 19 and 22].

A signature counts independent positive and negative directions in a quadratic
form. This is a property of a mathematical space. It does not count particles.

We wrote the calculation explicitly for every symmetric real 4 by 4 matrix A.
Let G = diag(1,-1,-1,-1). The chosen form is

  Q(A) = tr(G A G A) - tr(G A)^2 / 2.

We prove that this formula reverses the trace contribution and keeps the
trace-free contribution. We give an invertible linear change of ten coordinates.
It turns Q into

  -y0^2 + y1^2 + y2^2 + y3^2
  -2 y4^2 -2 y5^2 -2 y6^2 +2 y7^2 +2 y8^2 +2 y9^2.

The formal result is six positive directions and four negative directions.
This checks the local trace-sign choice behind the draft's ordered pair (4,6).
We report both sign counts to avoid a convention ambiguity. The opposite overall
sign would exchange the counts.

What Lean checked

MetricSignature.lean has 11 proved statements. They include the matrix identity,
the trace split, both inverse identities, coverage of all symmetric matrices,
and the positive and negative counts using mathlib's signature definitions.
The count is not just arithmetic on an assumed diagonal form. The proof links
that form to the matrix contraction by an explicit linear equivalence.

The file compiled with Lean 4.35.0. leanchecker replay also passed. Its theorem
axioms are only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. No sorry or extra
axiom was used. Pinned mathlib build artifacts were reused; this is not an
independent rebuild of Lean or of all mathlib. See verification.json and the logs.
Run: python3 research/weinstein-lean/verify.py

This is established linear algebra applied to a claim that the draft actually
uses. It is a useful feasibility check. It is not a publishable breakthrough by
itself, and it does not verify the global geometric construction or its physics.

What else is in reach?

1. A stronger Geometric Unity check.
   Target: write a precise finite-dimensional version of the operator in equation
   (9.3), with all domains and group actions stated, then check its transformation
   law. A preliminary gate is to establish that all terms have matching types.
   The draft's section 8.2 says that the preferred operator selection argument
   has not been reconstructed [1, printed pp. 41-44]. A proof must not fill that
   gap by assuming the desired property. This is a much larger project than our
   signature check. It would test a central mathematical claim if the exact
   operator and hypotheses can first be fixed.

2. The Malaney-Weinstein welfare construction.
   The 2021 paper has two explicit conjectures about comparing welfare when
   preferences change [2, section 9]. This is a more compact formalization target
   than a full field theory. A useful first statement is the relabeling action:
   if C(X(u))=u and g is an invertible relabeling, then
   (g inverse composed with C)(X(g(u)))=u. This checks equation (3.3), but is
   elementary and would not settle either conjecture.
   The harder target is uniqueness of the welfare comparison. Before a Lean
   statement is credible, 'constructible from' must become an exact condition.
   The allowed function spaces, regularity, and existence of complete flows
   must also be fixed. Formalization can reveal these choices; it cannot decide
   which choice best measures human welfare.

3. An audit of the welfare conjectures and their assumptions.
   Nguyen's 2021 response argues that the uniqueness conjecture needs clarification
   and treats the structure conjecture through standard connection theory [3].
   Pfeffer's August 2026 draft claims proofs with explicit observed-basket and
   completeness conditions [4]. I read the relevant original statements and the
   response sections. I have not verified either full response. Their hypotheses
   must be compared before claiming that the original conjectures are solved,
   false, or still open. A Lean audit of that comparison could be useful.

Mathlib already available in our pinned checkout

- QuadraticForm/Signature.lean provides positive/negative signature counts and
  the needed invariance under linear isometry. This was used above.
- Matrix.Trace, bilinear forms and quadratic forms provide the local algebra.
- CliffordAlgebra/SpinGroup.lean defines Pin and Spin groups. Its own notes list
  a remaining comparison between Lipschitz-group definitions. This is not a
  ready-made classification of all representations used by Geometric Unity.
- Geometry/Manifold provides manifolds and vector bundles. The LeviCivita file
  constructs and proves uniqueness for a Riemannian connection. That alone does
  not discharge every indefinite-metric construction in the draft.
- ExteriorPower/HodgeStar.lean defines a star operator for a nondegenerate
  bilinear form and volume choice. Its notes still list missing identities.

These are verified source observations for mathlib commit
56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679. They are not a complete search of every
external Lean library. The main work would be assembling the required structures
and proving the bridges between them.

Recommendation

Keep the controls program as the main effort. Keep this as a bounded mathematics
side project. The next useful decision is whether to audit a specific GU operator
or the exact assumptions behind a welfare uniqueness theorem. Do not spend a large
budget on a statement that still contains undefined terms.

Plain-language takeaway

We can ask Lean to check exact mathematical parts of Weinstein's ideas. We did
one such check, and it passed. That means the calculation is sound. It does not
mean nature follows the proposed theory. Larger checks need sharper statements
first. The most useful next step is to identify one disputed or missing claim
whose proof would change our assessment.
