Abstract
We formalize Eric Weinstein’s Geometric Unity (GU) as typed Kleis structures with Z3 
satisfiability checking, based on the April 2021 author’s draft and Nguyen/Polya’s 2021 
critique. We go beyond auditing to executable verification: 46 Z3 queries across three 
files produce a complete diagnostic. Layer 1 confirms the core framework (observerse, 
gauge group, Clifford algebra) is internally consistent (16/16 SAT). Layer 2 machine-
verifies each Nguyen obstruction as a logical consequence of stated premises (8/8 verified). 
Layer 3 — the novel contribution — establishes formal INDEPENDENCE results: the 
theory’s predictions (3 generations, SM quantum numbers, Lorentz group, SM gauge 
group SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1), Einstein recovery, Shiab selection, coupling constants) are all 
logically independent of its stated axioms. Both the claimed value and its negation are 
consistent with the framework. This is stronger than ‘the derivation is missing’ — it 
establishes that no derivation exists within the stated formalization. The complete verdict: 
the published formulation is obstructed and underdetermined. The central mechanism (the 
Shiab operator) is a machine-verified mathematical impossibility, and the stated axioms 
do not determine the predictions. The 14-dimensional observerse is a valid mathematical 
object; the claim that it unifies gauge theory with gravity via the Shiab is not supported 
by the published axioms.
Keywords: Geometric Unity, formal verification, gap analysis, differential geometry, gauge theory, Kleis, Z3, Shiab 
operator, complexification
1 Introduction
Geometric Unity (GU) is a proposed unified field theory presented by Eric Weinstein in a 2013 
lecture at Oxford and elaborated in a 69-page author’s draft released April 1, 2021 [1]. The theory 
claims to recover the Standard Model Lagrangian, the Lorentz group, three generations of fermions, 
and Einstein’s field equations from a single geometric construction on a 14-dimensional ‘observerse’ 
manifold built over a bare 4-dimensional topological manifold 𝑋4.
The theory has been criticized as incomplete and mathematically ill-defined. Nguyen and Polya 
[2] identified four specific objections: a complexification error in the Shiab operator, a chiral 
anomaly in the gauge group, supersymmetry constraints incompatible with the stated structure 
group, and numerous missing computations. The broader mathematical physics community has 
largely not engaged. The most detailed public exposition [8] — a technically competent 3-hour 
presentation by a science communicator with graduate training in mathematical physics — explains

GU’s constructions step by step but does not attempt to verify whether the claimed derivation 
chain is logically valid. Understanding what each step says is distinct from establishing that each 
step follows.
We take a different approach. Rather than arguing correctness, we ask: what has GU actually 
specified, and where precisely does it fail to compile?  Using the Kleis formal language [3] with

classification:
• Specified: Components with explicit definitions and formulas that can be typed as Kleis 
structures
• Self-acknowledged gaps: Components Weinstein himself marks as incomplete in his draft
• Obstructed: Components where Nguyen/Polya demonstrate mathematical impossibility re -
sults
Beyond classification, we perform executable Z3 verification: 46 queries producing a complete 
logical diagnostic. The novel finding (Section 10) is that GU’s predictions are formally independent 
of its axioms — the theory does not logically entail the physical content it claims.
The purpose is to convert ‘the theory is vague’ into machine-verifiable logical verdicts. The 
verdict is not vague: the theory’s central mechanism is mathematically impossible, and its predic-
tions do not follow from its stated framework.

2.2 Formalization Strategy
For each component, we apply a three-stage test:
1. Can it be typed? Does the component have a well-defined mathematical type?
2. Can it be axiomatized? Are there stated properties expressible as first-order axioms?
3. Is it obstructed? Does Nguyen’s analysis demonstrate that the axioms are unsatisfiable or 
contradictory?
Components passing stages 1-2 without obstruction become satisfiable Kleis structures. Compo -
nents failing at any stage receive a named gap with severity classification:
• Type gap: The object lacks a well-defined mathematical type

2.3 Z3 Verification Construction
The executable verification uses three Kleis theory files, each self-contained with its own 
structures and examples. The construction employs several techniques to make SMT verification 
tractable:
Ground instances over universal quantifiers.  Universal quantification ( ∀𝑥.𝑃(𝑥)) over 
complex algebraic structures causes Z3 memory explosion. Instead, we declare named elements 
(e.g., g1, g2 for gauge group elements) and assert axioms over these specific witnesses. This trades 
generality for decidability — the properties hold for the declared elements, which suffices for 
satisfiability and entailment checking.
Element-scoped structures. Each Kleis structure defines axioms only over its own named 
elements. This prevents global axiom conflicts when multiple structures coexist in the same file. For 
example, Model_3_Generations defines gen_count(m3) = 3 where m3 is local to that structure, while 
Model_4_Generations defines gen_count(m4) = 4 with its own element — both are simultaneously 
satisfiable because they make claims about different elements.
Entailment semantics. Kleis assert checks logical entailment: given the axioms loaded by 
the example block, does the assertion follow as a logical consequence? For Layer 2 (obstructions), 
we encode Nguyen’s premises as axioms and assert the impossibility conclusion — Z3 confirms 
it is forced by the premises (UNSAT for the negation). This is stronger than satisfiability: the 
obstruction is a theorem, not merely consistent.
Independence via the Cantor/Cohen method. To prove a prediction 𝑃 is independent of 
axioms 𝑇, we construct two separate structures: one where 𝑇∪{𝑃} is satisfiable, another where 
𝑇∪{¬𝑃} is satisfiable. If Z3 confirms both (SAT), then 𝑃 is undecidable from 𝑇. This adapts 
the forcing/inner-model technique from set theory to SMT — the same method used to prove the 
Continuum Hypothesis independent of ZFC [3].
Three-file separation.  Layers 1, 2, and 3 are isolated into separate files 
(geometric_unity.kleis, geometric_unity_obstructions.kleis, geometric_unity_skolem.kleis). 
This is not merely organizational — loading conflicting axioms in a single file would produce 
global inconsistency, making all assertions trivially true. Separation ensures each layer’s verdict is 
genuine.
2.4 Two-Level Presentation
The structures shown in Sections 3–9 of this paper are intended formalizations — they express 
the mathematical content with full generality (polymorphic type parameters, universal quantifiers, 
rich operation signatures). These serve as specifications: what the theory means to say.
The executable verification files use a deliberately different representation: ground instances, 
integer encodings, and element-scoped axioms. This is not an oversight — it is required by

SMT tractability. Z3 cannot decide arbitrary first-order theories with nested quantifiers over 
uninterpreted sorts; it can decide ground theories in milliseconds. The ground-instance versions 
are conservative approximations of the intended formalizations: any property that fails to hold 
even over named witnesses certainly fails in general, and any independence result over ground 
terms implies independence over the full theory (if the axioms cannot force a value even for specific 
elements, they cannot force it universally).
The intended formalizations in this paper establish what we are testing . The executable files 
establish that we tested it. Readers wishing to verify the results should consult the .kleis theory 
files directly.
2.5 Completeness of the Axiom Set
A natural question: did we include enough axioms? If additional structure (the deformation 
complex, branching rules, topological constraints) were formalized, might the predictions become 
derivable?
We included every formally stated axiom  in the April 2021 draft: dimensional constraints, 
signature, Lie algebra dimension, spinor dimension, gauge group algebraic properties, Clifford 
decomposition sums, and tilt homomorphism behavior. What we did NOT include are:
• The Shiab operator’s action — because it is mathematically impossible (Layer 2, Obstruction 
1). Including an inconsistent axiom would make ALL assertions trivially true, which is the 
opposite of a meaningful test.
• The Shiab selection criterion — because Weinstein self-acknowledges it is lost (‘unable to locate 
the notes from decades ago,’ Section 8.2).
• The deformation complex calculation yielding 3 generations — because it is never exhibited 
in the draft. Section 10 states the conclusion but does not show the representation-theoretic 
derivation.
In each case, the axiom is absent from our formalization because it is absent from the theory . 
The independence results are not an artifact of incomplete formalization — they reflect genuinely 
missing structure.
Moreover, the missing axioms cannot rescue the theory even in principle. The Shiab operator — 
which is the mechanism that would connect geometry to predictions — is not merely unformalized; 
it is machine-verified as mathematically impossible (Layer 2). Any additional axiom invoking 
the Shiab inherits this impossibility. The derivation chain from observerse geometry to physical 
predictions passes through a proven obstruction. Adding downstream axioms to a blocked pipeline 
does not unblock it.
Should alternative axioms ever be proposed that bypass the Shiab entirely (constituting a 
different theory), they can be added to the .kleis files and tested. The framework is extensible: 
provide the axiom, get the verdict.

5.3 Obstruction (Nguyen Section 3.2)
GAP-04 (Obstruction, Critical): The gauge group 𝑈(128) contains a central 𝑈(1) subgroup 
whose associated gauge connection induces an abelian chiral anomaly [2, Section 3.2]. This breaks 
gauge invariance in the quantum theory. Switching to Spin(14) removes the anomaly but makes 
the Shiab operator impossible to define (the representation spaces have incompatible dimensions). 
Nguyen’s conclusion: ‘it is impossible to both remove the gauge anomaly and have a well-defined 
shiab operator, thereby rendering GU inconsistent.’

6.1 What Is Specified (Section 8, eq. 9.3, Weinstein)
The Shiab operator is explicitly defined. It acts on ad-valued 2-forms Ξ∈Ω2(𝑌,ad) as:

The operator is structured as an Einsteinian contraction generalized to preserve gauge covari -

• The first term is Ricci-like (analogous to contracting the Riemann tensor to Ricci)
• The second term is Ricci-scalar-like (the further trace)
• The Weyl curvature is annihilated, exactly as in Einstein’s case

naive projection, which breaks gauge invariance — the core problem GU claims to solve).

6.3 The Nguyen Complexification Obstruction (Section 3.1)
The Shiab requires identifying elements of the adjoint bundle Ad(𝑃) with elements of the exterior 
algebra Λ•(𝑇∗𝑈). This identification requires:
Ad(𝑃)⊗ℂ≃Λ•(𝑇∗𝑈)⊗ℂ
This isomorphism holds only after complexification . Without complexification, 𝑢(128) (skew-
Hermitian 128×128 matrices) is not isomorphic to Clℝ(14) (real 128×128 matrices).
Nguyen’s dilemma:
• Without complexification: The Shiab is mathematically undefined. The ‘pure trace’ identi-
fication fails.
• With complexification: Following Witten [5], complexifying the space of connections leads 
to a quantum theory that is either non-unitary or has energy unbounded below. Neither is 
physically acceptable.
GAP-06 (Obstruction, Critical): The Shiab operator as defined requires a bundle isomorphism 
that does not exist over the reals. The only resolution (complexification) destroys the physical 
viability of the quantum theory. This is the central mathematical obstruction in GU.

9 The Nguyen Obstructions
Nguyen and Polya [2] identify three independent mathematical obstructions, each of which 
would individually prevent GU from functioning as stated. Together they form a trilemma with 
no apparent resolution.
9.1 Obstruction 1: The Complexification Dilemma (Section 3.1)
The Shiab operator requires identifying the adjoint bundle Ad(𝑃) with the exterior algebra 
bundle Λ•(𝑇∗𝑈). This requires:
Ad(𝑃)≃Λ•(𝑇∗𝑈)
But Ad(𝑃) has fibers 𝑢(128) (skew-Hermitian matrices) while Λ•(𝑇∗𝑈) has fibers isomorphic to 
Clℝ(14)≃𝑀128(ℝ) (real matrices). These are not isomorphic as real vector spaces.
After complexification both become 𝑀128(ℂ) and the isomorphism holds:
Ad(𝑃)⊗ℂ≃Λ•(𝑇∗𝑈)⊗ℂ
But complexifying the gauge theory leads (following Witten [5]) to non-unitary quantum mechanics 
or unbounded Hamiltonians.
Verdict: The Shiab is either undefined (no complexification) or unphysical (with complexifi -
cation).
9.2 Obstruction 2: The Anomaly-Shiab Incompatibility (Section 3.2)
𝑈(128) as gauge group induces a chiral anomaly via its central 𝑈(1) subgroup. Resolution: 
replace 𝑈(128) with Spin(14).
But Spin(14) has the wrong dimension to support the bundle isomorphism needed for the Shiab 
operator. The Shiab requires the full 𝑈(128) representation theory.
Verdict: Anomaly freedom and Shiab existence are mutually exclusive.

9.3 Obstruction 3: The Supersymmetry Tower (Section 3.3)
GU claims supersymmetric extension of the inhomogeneous gauge group. In 𝐷≥12 dimensions:
• SUSY representations necessarily contain spin-3 fields (Nahm [6])
• Interacting spin-3 fields require an infinite tower of higher-spin fields
• Higher-spin gauge theories require infinite-dimensional structure groups
This contradicts the finite structure group 𝑈(128).
Verdict: The claimed supersymmetry is incompatible with the stated gauge structure.
9.4 The Trilemma Formalized
structure NguyenTrilemma(Theory) {
    operation has_shiab : Theory → Boolean
    operation is_anomaly_free : Theory → Boolean

This structure is satisfiable only if at least one of (Shiab, anomaly-freedom, unitarity, SUSY) is 
abandoned:
• Dropping the Shiab abandons the theory’s central mechanism — the ‘unity’ in Geometric 
Unity.
• Accepting anomalies means the quantum theory is inconsistent (gauge symmetry is broken 
at loop level, rendering predictions unreliable).
• Accepting non-unitarity  means negative probabilities — the theory does not describe 
physics.
• Dropping SUSY is the least immediately fatal option. Without supersymmetry, the theory 
loses its claimed mechanism for relating bosonic and fermionic sectors in the inhomogeneous 
gauge group. However, the deeper issue is that in 𝐷=14 with signature (7,7), SUSY was the 
only proposed mechanism for controlling the ghost sectors that arise from the indefinite metric. 
Without it, one must provide an alternative ghost-decoupling mechanism — which GU does 
not supply. Dropping SUSY is therefore not independently viable without additional structure 
that Weinstein has not specified.
The escape routes are not equally costly, but none preserves GU’s claims as stated.
