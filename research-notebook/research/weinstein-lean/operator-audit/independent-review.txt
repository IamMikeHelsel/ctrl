The sign finding is faithful to the printed formulas

Equation (5.9) defines right-factor conjugation in the semidirect product.
Equations (6.2), (6.4) and (6.13) then fix the relevant action and negative
shift. Equation (7.3) subtracts that same shift in the torsion. These are the
choices encoded in GaugeSignAudit.lean, lines 25-29 and 58-68.

The pointwise product rule gives s(e h) = C_h(s(e)) + s(h). Substitution therefore
gives C_h(T_minus(e,w)) - 2 s(h). The first expansion after (7.4) changes the
printed negative shift to a positive one. This change is visible in the PDF;
it is not a text-extraction error. Its next derivative expansion also prints
h^-1 where the product rule requires e. The Lean proof uses the correct product
rule, which is already used in the draft's (6.5).

A uniform reversal of the derivative sign does not cancel the defect. A
consistent change of action, torsion, or connection conventions can repair the
formula. Thus this is a mismatch among the displayed conventions, not an
obstruction to every possible interpretation of the construction. Footnote 7
on page 36 already warns that the draft combines conflicting conventions.

The real 3 by 3 witness is nontrivial: s(h) = -2 A and the mixed-convention
torsion equals 4 A instead of zero. Its orthogonality and determinant checks
exclude a merely singular or non-orthogonal example. It is still a finite
matrix example. No embedding into the specified GU spinor bundle is proved.
The exact general conclusion is conditional: any allowed nonzero real shift
prevents this mixed-convention covariance identity. The pointwise algebra
does not require the illustrative 3 by 3 realization.

Both repairs are correctly limited

The positive tilt with T_minus, and the negative tilt with T_plus, each give
the claimed local covariance. The positive tilt generally fails to stabilize
the selected base connection under the unchanged printed action (6.2).
The T_plus repair changes the difference of connections supplied by (6.18)-
(6.19) and (7.1)-(7.3). Neither is a replacement that preserves every downstream
definition. A convention ledger must precede further geometric construction.

The coefficient covariance theorem proves its stated law

The assumptions are a real associative algebra R, units e and h, fixed real
coefficient arrays C, and fixed arrays p1 and p2. The gauge action acts only on
the algebra-valued coefficients, by conjugation. The same C, p1 and p2 occur on
both sides. Their conjugated versions change through e becoming e h.

Real coefficients are central, so finite real linear combinations commute with
conjugation. Ordered multiplication also commutes with simultaneous conjugation.
The source proves these facts and composes them. It does not assume the final
covariance law, or hide it in a structure field.

The nesting and 1/2 factor match the ordered-product reading of (9.3). The form
index sizes and the degrees 2 -> 12 -> 13 and 2 -> 12 -> 14 -> 0 -> 1 -> 13 are
correct. The theorem is a valid generic algebraic construction, not a vacuous
implication from an assumed equivariance property.

Remaining mathematical gaps

The fields named star and wedge are arbitrary coefficient arrays. Their sizes
alone do not make them a Hodge star or exterior product. No metric, orientation,
exterior-basis bridge, star-square identity, or coordinate-change law is proved.
The Phi arrays are arbitrary; the invariant tensors in Definition 8.1 have not
been selected. The commutator/anticommutator and factors of i described near
(8.1) are not implemented by the present ordered product. Lie-algebra output
closure, a global bundle operator, Weyl annihilation, and the relevant Bianchi
identity remain open. None of these follows from covariance alone.

The current report exposes these gaps. Keep its phrase "fixed coefficient
version" in any summary. Do not shorten the result to "the GU Shiab operator is
formalized." This review makes no judgment about physics and no novelty claim.
