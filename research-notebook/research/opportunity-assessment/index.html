A credible niche in proofs that engineers can use

The strongest opportunity is to turn a meaningful engineering requirement into an explicit model, a compact exact certificate, and a Lean proof that connects the two. Our controls work gives this idea a concrete starting point. Repeatable prize income remains unestablished.

Mike's role would include choosing useful requirements, challenging the model assumptions, interpreting results and taking responsibility for the mathematics. Agent-assisted search and formalization can accelerate that work. The valuable output is a correct, reusable result whose scope a reader can understand.

Decision: lead with robust sampled-control safety. Keep one bounded coding-theory project and one small discrete-mathematics exploration. Treat prizes as possible upside while testing publication value and reuse.

The controls result is a real next step

The existing paper already proves travel safety for its stated nominal sampled-actuator execution. The new experiment keeps the controller gains, quadratic energy function, initial region E ≤ 12 and maximum normalized hold time h ≤ 1/5. It targets unknown rate r ∈ [9/10, 11/10].

This means physical time constant τ/τ₀ ∈ [10/11, 10/9] when the controller is normalized using nominal τ₀. It is a ±10% rate interval; it is not a ±10% time-constant interval.

A tighter clipping sector, valid on the same energy region, made the certificate feasible. The generated vertex proofs establish exact polynomial inequalities. The proof still needs to connect every admissible plant and held interval to those vertices, establish the disturbance response bound, and compose the result into all-time travel safety.

Disturbance units matter: command-equivalent error inside the actuator-rate factor differs from additive disturbance outside it. The scratch now checks the held-input ODE and the conversion budget in Lean: an outside-rate error budget of 0.01 requires an effective bound of 1/90, which still fits the proposed invariant budget. The complete response and trajectory proof remains open. Neither a numerical grid nor successful vertex proofs alone establish the full uncertain-plant result.

A stronger paper could contribute a reusable certificate interface and a fully checked model-to-execution proof, demonstrated across meaningful dynamics. Robust-control techniques themselves are established; we have not demonstrated a new general control theorem or physical robot validation.

What the prize investigations taught us

The current inspected Proximity candidates report a 68.15 / 116.13-bit interval. Our probes rule out score-only improvements and the tested fibre-count tuning. These scores describe a particular spot-check term, not whole-system cryptographic security.

A parameterized construction is a useful learning and tooling milestone. Winning requires a better mathematical argument and acceptance under the applicable program rules.

A bounded pilot with concrete decision gates

Suggested allocation: 70% controls, 20% Proximity, 10% discrete exploration. These are proposed shares of research effort. A two-week pilot is a planning cap, not a promise to finish an open problem.

  1. Close the controls extension. State the uncertain-rate model and disturbance units precisely; prove all convex-enclosure and response obligations; assemble sample and between-sample safety; replay the final theorem and audit its assumptions.
  2. Test reuse. Apply the certificate machinery to a second model requiring a meaningful change in dynamics or uncertainty. Record what reuses unchanged, what needs new mathematics and how much proof effort it takes. Changing units alone does not pass this test.
  3. Test research value. Compare the exact contribution with prior literature. Prepare a reviewable paper and artifact. The next external milestone is informed human review; no outreach or submission has been made.
  4. Bound the prize branch. Reproduce the smaller Proximity upper construction in its pinned environment. Give one motivated refinement two or three focused days to produce a better exact inequality. Stop that refinement if it only reproduces existing parameters.
  5. Choose the discrete branch by structure. Attempt a small #128 graph interface or a Hankel recurrence. Stop if the work produces only more brute-force examples, duplicates an existing proof or conceals the hard step in a new assumption.

Research success: a useful new scoped result or reusable formalization, correct semantics, reproducible evidence and a defensible explanation of its contribution. Niche validation: someone outside this project can use it, or a defined verification deliverable attracts funding. Neither has been demonstrated yet.

Publication and income are separate tests

Current evidence supports pursuing a publication. It does not support forecasting prize income. Even successful peer review and formal verification do not automatically entitle a Proximity entrant to payment; the program evaluates significance and makes discretionary awards. Official Proximity terms.

There is a concrete funding precedent: the Ethereum Foundation's Q4 2025 allocation includes Rust-to-Lean verification tooling and STIR/WHIR formalization in ArkLib. That shows related work has been funded. It does not establish a customer, price or eligibility for us. Official allocation record.

Its current applicant guidance calls for Ethereum-aligned, open-source work and milestone-based grants, normally paid in ETH. This is a separate route requiring a relevant proposal; the current controls project has not been shown to fit it. Applicant guidance.

My assessment: applied proof engineering is worth a disciplined pilot; publication potential is promising but unconfirmed; prizes are speculative upside. No probability of solving a major open problem or earning sustainable income is justified by this evidence.
