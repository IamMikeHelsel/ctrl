1. CONJECTURES.IO: ORDINARY MATHEMATICAL PROOFS / DEFECT AWARDS

Live intake, not a fixed-deadline paper award. The guide prices one proof
verification attempt at 0.25 TAO, with a free preliminary file check. Rewards
are Subnet 66 Alpha; displayed dollar amounts are conversion estimates.
An account and eventual payout wallet are required. No student/university-only
restriction was found in the reviewed guide or terms; full jurisdictional
eligibility was not established.
https://conjectures.io/how-it-works
https://conjectures.io/terms

Current submission contract is v11, effective 14 October 2026, review policy v4.
A conventional established proof of the same direct problem predating
acceptance disqualifies a new Lean proof: independent rediscovery and first
formalization do not restore eligibility. Original AI chat logs are mandatory
for open problems. Accepted logs can be retained, sold and licensed for AI
training under a permanent nonexclusive grant. This is a substantive decision
about our work, not merely a proof-upload requirement.

Lean acceptance, reward approval and payment are separate. The exact pinned
target, dependency/axiom limits and chronology matter. Defect awards under
v3/v4 are capped at the smaller of $750 converted to Alpha at payout-record
creation and the locked task bounty; they replace the full bounty. New tasks
can start at 2.5% of available treasury and rise to 12.5%; older tasks can have
different starting shares. Thus an apparent 10% versus 2.5% discrepancy with
older README text is explained by task cohorts, not a universal current rate.
https://conjectures.io/submission-terms

Erdos 939 is a historical example, NOT a still-available $750 target. Its
operator result record says a $750-equivalent defect award was paid in Alpha.
The Lean predicate omitted positivity and accepted zero as powerful, allowing
{0,1}; this did not settle the intended mathematical problem. The live dollar
display changes with token value and should not be quoted as a fixed award.
https://conjectures.io/results/91915fc3-9040-4a31-8da5-b49d4e2cc2fb

Fit judgment: C for our immediate project. Good incentive to audit exact
statements, but not an avenue for collecting money simply by formalizing a
known control theorem. Build any candidate locally and establish novelty
before considering a paid submission; no assumption of guaranteed reward.

2. CONJECTURES.IO: VERIFIED FLOAT-KERNEL OPTIMIZATION

The live page shows competition activity on 17 October 2026. Entrants provide
binary32 add/multiply plus sum/batched matrix multiplication with Lean
equivalence proofs. A successful challenger must beat the current champion by
at least 3%, with the specified statistical gate. The last three champions
receive 60/30/10 shares of the competition's allocation, itself 20% of validator
weight. This is token/network reward allocation, not a guaranteed dollar purse.

Entry requires Subnet 66 registration and one hotkey; every stored attempt uses
its registration even if unsuccessful. The required environment is Linux
x86_64. The local gate can benchmark before proofs are complete, then checks
proofs, policy and axioms. Do not assume the ordinary site's 0.25-TAO credit
price also describes this distinct registration path; its current registration
cost was not established here. No closing date was advertised on the inspected
page. No student-only condition was found.
https://conjectures.io/competitions/floatlib

Fit judgment: B for a bounded software-verification experiment; C for advancing
the current controls paper. A useful first milestone is a locally reproduced
baseline and one proved kernel transformation. The reward is competitive and
can end when newer champions displace an entry. No evidence yet that we can
beat the current champion.

The related DEFLATE contest is CLOSED: its page says 28 September–12 October
2026. Its generic introductory text still reads like an invitation, so use the
explicit ended banner. It is a useful example of proving decode(encode(x))=x,
not an open opportunity today.
https://conjectures.io/competitions/deflate

3. THE JUSTIN SUN PRIZE: FORMALIZATION AS A SEPARATE CONTRIBUTION

The official repository explicitly welcomes independent contributors and
AI-assisted work, and separates mathematical discovery from formalization.
No student or academic-affiliation requirement appears in these reviewed rules.
https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/blob/main/docs/about.md

The legacy route remains available for unmigrated problems: a complete
original-problem Lean formalization, reviewed supporting mathematics, public
priority/authorship evidence, accepted contribution PR, personal claim and
identity confirmation, 14-day public review, and award confirmation. The
formalizer can differ from the solver and need not wait for the solver to
claim. It promises prize money and a medal after confirmation; the inspected
process does not establish a currency, fixed amount, funded escrow or a
guaranteed award for every accepted proof. This is more promising for known
mathematics than Conjectures' v4 novelty rule, but must be screened per target.
https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/blob/main/docs/award-process.md

The new 49-problem MathDB pilot is explicitly marked PREPARED, awaiting an
activation announcement and configured official actor. It is not established
as live automatic intake. It includes very difficult problems, not a practical
shortlist for us.
https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/blob/main/problems/mathdb-pilot.md

Prepared pilot rules allocate an announced problem reward 50% Solution, 30%
Lean, 20% Exposition. Solution and Lean require their own original complete
result to have been public after 2026-01-01 and earliest-valid-contributor
evidence. Exposition can explain an open problem but requires committee
selection. The repository publishes no reward estimates. These are conditional
category shares, not payments for every upload. Personal confirmation remains
required; an agent cannot sign or collect on the contributor's behalf.
https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/blob/main/docs/award-process-mathdb.md

The public register contains legacy announced awards and says no MathDB pilot
awards have yet been announced. It expressly separates announcement from paid
status. I did not establish payment for those legacy records or a funded new
promise of any specific amount.
https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/blob/main/awards/README.md
https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/blob/main/docs/grading.md

Fit judgment: B- as a target-by-target watchlist, not a financial plan. The useful
next step would be finding an unmigrated, mathematically solved catalog problem
with no earlier complete Lean formalization and checking its exact award rules.
Our existing certificate artifact is not itself a listed target.

DECISION BOUNDARY

Do not turn legacy dollar announcements, displayed token conversions, an
announced award, a catalog flag, or a successful build into a claim of a payable
cash prize. The above grades assess project fit, not probability of winning.
We can study and prove offline without buying crypto, licensing chat logs or
submitting anything. Those later actions need an explicit user decision.
