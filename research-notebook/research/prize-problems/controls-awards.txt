CONTROLS / ROBOTICS PRIZE ROUTES — checked 2026-10-17

Scope: six selected routes relevant to formal control safety or an applied robotics
extension. Official society, organizer, university, or agency sources only. No
registration, contact, nomination, or submission was performed. This is not a
claim that the present Lean artifact is novel, accepted, or award-competitive.

Assessment: no currently open, modest, theorem-only controls cash bounty was
verified in this search. The closest route for our current artifact is a research
paper/tool paper; the substantial cash competitions require working robotics
systems. Fit grades below are judgments about connection to our existing work,
not probabilities of acceptance or winning.

PAPER / ARTIFACT ROUTES

1. HSCC+ICCPS 2027 — OPEN PAPER CALL; AWARD DETAILS NOT YET ANNOUNCED
   Fit: 4/5. Best immediate research venue match.

   Required abstract: October 29, 2026. Paper: November 5, 2026. Both close
   23:59:59 Anywhere on Earth (UTC-12). Conference: May 17–20, 2027, Boulder.
   The call explicitly includes formal verification, stability, reachability,
   control theory, and high-assurance robotics. Industrial/government experience
   is welcomed when it contributes generalizable technical research.
   Regular papers: 10 pages excluding references. Short papers: 6 including
   references. Tool papers may choose either length, use single-anonymous review,
   and include author identities; regular/short research papers omit them.
   Work must be original and not concurrently submitted elsewhere.
   Source: https://hscc-iccps2027.taylortjohnson.com/call-for-papers/

   The official awards page expressly leaves 2027 categories, eligibility, and
   procedures unannounced and says not to infer them from earlier HSCC/ICCPS
   editions. Thus: do not advertise a confirmed 2027 best-paper, artifact, or
   cash prize. Industry authors can submit; award eligibility remains unknown.
   Source: https://hscc-iccps2027.taylortjohnson.com/awards/

   Accepted research/tool papers may later submit an optional artifact for
   repeatability evaluation. The tentative artifact deadline is February 18,
   2027; this is separate from paper review and is not a cash competition.
   Source: https://hscc-iccps2027.taylortjohnson.com/authors/repeatability-evaluation/

   Concrete direction (our assessment): a carefully scoped tool/research paper
   connecting rational certificates to the actual clipped, sampled, continuous
   trajectory, with reusable interfaces, measured-error semantics, and independently
   reproducible benchmarks. The remaining hurdle is a clear contribution relative
   to prior formal-control work, not another count of compiled theorems. The short
   deadline is not a reason to overstate novelty or skip comparison.

2. IEEE CSS Roberto Tempo Best CDC Paper Award — ESTABLISHED, AFTER PUBLICATION
   Fit: 3/5 for a new controls result with the formal artifact as evidence.

   Prize: $1,000 TOTAL divided among authors, plus a plaque for each. At most one
   paper annually. Eligible papers were presented at CDC in the preceding year
   and published in its official IEEE Xplore proceedings. Nomination is due
   May 15 of the following year through the CSS awards system. Judging considers
   originality, potential control-theory/technology/implementation impact, and
   clarity. Published eligibility states no student or academic-employment
   restriction, so industry authors are not excluded by those criteria.
   The current unpublished project is not eligible. A future CDC presentation
   is the prerequisite; a future conference submission deadline was not verified.
   This is an award for the selected paper, not payment for solving a listed
   problem. A CDC/L-CSS paper cannot receive both corresponding awards.
   Source: https://ieeecss.org/awards/roberto-tempo-best-cdc-paper-award

   Concrete direction: genuinely stronger control bounds, synthesis conditions,
   or a general certificate-to-trajectory result supported by the Lean artifact.
   Formalizing a standard calculation alone is unlikely to supply that result.

3. IEEE CSS Best CCTA Paper Award — ESTABLISHED, AFTER CONFERENCE PRESENTATION
   Fit: 3/5 if we add a convincing application and measured implementation.

   Established in 2025. Prize: $1,000 TOTAL divided among authors, individual
   plaques, and travel for one author to the award-year CCTA. At most one award
   annually. Eligibility is authors of a CCTA paper presented in the immediately
   preceding year, with no stated student or academic-employment restriction.
   Nomination forms and reference letters are due January 15 of the next year
   through CSS PaperPlaza. Thus the next rule-based deadline is January 15, 2027
   for papers already presented at CCTA 2026; our new draft is not eligible.
   Selection includes translational promise, rigor, novelty, and community impact.
   A future conference submission window was not independently verified here.
   Source: https://ieeecss.org/awards/ieee-control-systems-society-best-ccta-paper-award

   Concrete direction: a useful, calibrated actuator/axis safety implementation
   that handles observed timing and sensing errors and demonstrates what its
   certificate changes in practice. Our symbolic physical scaling is a starting
   point, not evidence of a validated robot or industrial deployment.

4. IEEE CSS George S. Axelby Outstanding Paper Award — LONGER-TERM JOURNAL ROUTE
   Fit: 2/5 at the current stage; relevant destination for deeper original theory.

   Authors of papers published in IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control in the
   two preceding calendar years qualify; journal volume date governs, not online
   posting date. Nominations are due May 15. At most one award is presented each
   year. The published criteria do not require student status or academic
   employment. The prize is a plaque for each recipient plus specified CDC
   travel/registration/hotel expenses for one recipient. NO CASH HONORARIUM is
   stated on the official page; do not invent one.
   Source: https://ieeecss.org/awards/george-s-axelby-outstanding-paper-award

   Concrete direction: establish an original general theorem about constrained
   sampled control, uncertainty, or certificate completeness/conservatism, then
   develop a journal article. Publication itself must precede award eligibility.
   This is a recognition route, not an open challenge with a prescribed target.

APPLIED / SOFTWARE COMPETITIONS

5. League of Robot Runners 2026 — CLOSED; GOOD FUTURE SOFTWARE-COMPETITION WATCH
   Fit: 2/5 for our present continuous-axis proof; 4/5 for a separate robust
   multi-robot execution algorithm project. These are topic-fit judgments.

   Organizer's 2026 page: worldwide individuals and teams of any background may
   participate. Execution track first/second prizes: USD 2,500 / 500; scheduling:
   USD 2,500 / 500; combined: USD 5,000 / 1,000 / 500. The 2026 finish was
   July 22, with results August 7; these are historical prizes, not currently
   available entry windows. No 2027 competition/rules were verified.
   Robots perform grid tasks under stochastic execution delays. The execution
   track supplies scheduling and planning, leaving a bounded algorithmic target.
   Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MAPF-Competition/web_resource/main/landing_page.md
   Public site: https://www.leagueofrobotrunners.org/

   Rules exclude organizers, sponsors, and advisers from participation or helping
   submissions. Submitted code becomes public under a participant-selected open
   license. Only permitted starter-code edits and official APIs are allowed;
   tampering with state or evaluation is prohibited. There is no general ban on
   industry entrants in the published rules. Google's industry status alone is
   not the sponsor exclusion, but eligibility depends on actual involvement.
   Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MAPF-Competition/web_resource/main/rules.md

   Concrete direction: develop a collision-safe execution policy robust to delayed
   moves, benchmark throughput against provided defaults, and formalize the
   safety invariant. Existing scalar lag proofs do not directly solve MAPF.
   A future season would need its own verified rules before committing to entry.

6. DARPA Surgical Competition — OPEN QUALIFICATION; LARGE HARDWARE-TEAM PROJECT
   Fit: 1/5 for this artifact alone; potentially a component within a major team.

   Agency page advertises $3.5 million TOTAL prizes, qualification closing
   October 30, 2026 at 4 p.m. ET, January 15–24, 2027 workshop and March 20–26
   scored event. It seeks autonomous/collaborative trauma surgery systems.
   Source: https://www.darpa.mil/research/challenges/surgical

   Rules allocate $1 million / $400,000 / $100,000 per surgical lane, plus
   component-gate awards; prizes require qualifying, competing, and scoring,
   and depend on appropriated funds. Organizations can be prize recipients.
   Source: https://www.darpa.mil/sites/default/files/attachment/2026-09/darpa-competition-surgical-rules.pdf

   Individuals/team members of all nationalities may participate subject to
   screening and exclusions. DARPA employees/support contractors and specified
   household members are excluded; U.S. government organizations/FFRDCs may
   participate but cannot win money. Sanctioned persons/organizations are excluded.
   Cash recipients need a U.S. SSN or taxpayer ID. Corporate status itself is not
   prohibited. Relevant organizational conflicts require mitigation review.
   Self-funded teams retain intellectual ownership and publishing rights.
   Source: https://www.darpa.mil/sites/default/files/attachment/2026-09/darpa-competition-terms-conditions.pdf

   Qualification requires a technical narrative and videos of safe robotic motion,
   hardware emergency stop, and three surgical capabilities. Up to ten teams are
   selected. The guide contains apparent stale event-year typos and uses EST
   where the main agency page says ET; use the live agency timeline and resolve
   exact timing before any real submission. We have made no inquiry.
   Source: https://www.darpa.mil/sites/default/files/attachment/2026-09/darpa-competition-surgical-qualification-guide.pdf

   Concrete direction: a rigorously bounded low-level controller could be one
   safety component, but perception, instruments, tissue interaction, hardware,
   autonomy, and integration dominate. A Lean proof alone cannot qualify. This
   is not a sensible near-term pivot solely for the cash prize.

EXCLUSIONS / STATUS TRAPS

* Eremenko's controls-related mathematical problem list remains useful research
  material, but his official page says he withdrew monetary prizes in summer
  2026. Do not describe those problems as active cash bounties.
  Source: https://www.math.purdue.edu/~eremenko/uns1.html

* PhyRC 2027 is running simulation submissions through December 15, 2026, but
  its only registration deadline was October 15, already past as of this review.
  The advertised grand prize is a Stretch 4 ROBOT, not a specified cash award.
  Teams can change members, so joining an already registered team is a distinct
  possible route; no team or organizer was contacted. The page does not impose
  academic affiliation and evaluates solutions rather than CVs. Still, no new
  unregistered entrant should infer eligibility from the 'open now' banner.
  Source: https://emprise.cs.cornell.edu/phyrc/challenge-2027/

Industry eligibility above is competition/publication eligibility only. It does
not establish employer authorization, ownership, or permission to publish employer
work. No employer resources, private research, or representation are assumed.

Recommended order (assessment): keep developing the coherent paper/artifact;
evaluate the HSCC+ICCPS call if a defensible contribution is ready; treat CDC/CCTA
awards as later recognition, not an expected return; use a future Robot Runners
execution track only if we deliberately choose a separate algorithmic project.
