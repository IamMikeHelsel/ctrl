HAGER REVERSE-MARKOV PRIZE: STATUS SCREEN
17 October 2026. Primary-source literature screen, not a new proof claim.

Decision
--------
REMOVE the original 10,000-yen problem from the open-prize shortlist. Hager's
own later paper explicitly identifies it as solved in 2017. A still-accessible
2015 prize PDF and a 2026 site-migration listing do not establish that a prize
remains open or payable. The sharper Gauss bound is a separate status question;
this screen did not locate its resolution, but also did not establish that it
is currently open or carries a reward. No sponsor was contacted.

Decisive primary evidence
------------------------
1. W. Chen, W. Du, W. W. Hager, L. Yang, Bounds for integration matrices that
   arise in Gauss and Radau collocation, Computational Optimization and
   Applications 74 (2019), 259-273, DOI 10.1007/s10589-019-00099-5.
   Primary manuscript: https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10093307
   Journal p.262, Historical Note, identifies property P1 as Hager's 2015 prize
   problem and states: "it was first solved in 2017 [11]".
   It says that proof in fact bounds |p(t)| by 2 throughout [-1,1]. This paper
   also proves the weighted Euclidean bounds and their adjoint counterparts.
   The listed reference [11] is the 2018 SIAM paper below; 2017 is the authors'
   stated solution year, not its journal publication year.

2. W. W. Hager, J. Liu, S. Mohapatra, A. V. Rao, X.-S. Wang, Convergence rate
   for a Gauss collocation method applied to constrained optimal control,
   SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization 56 (2018), 1386-1411.
   Author-hosted primary paper:
   https://people.clas.ufl.edu/hager/files/gaussconstraints-1.pdf
   Appendix 1, Proposition 9.1 (pp.1407-1408) proves the continuous-interval
   bound for Gauss nodes. Corollary 9.2 (pp.1408-1409) proves the corresponding
   bound for the right-endpoint Radau nodes. The appendix derives the inverse
   differentiation-matrix interpretation and uses polynomial interpolation,
   orthogonality, and Cauchy-Schwarz. Thus both branches of the original
   constant-2 assertion have an explicit published proof, not merely a later
   unsupported claim that someone solved it.

What the historical offer actually asks
--------------------------------------
The dated statement is 2 September 2015:
https://people.clas.ufl.edu/hager/files/prize.pdf
For degree at most n, p(-1)=0 and bounded derivative samples at Gauss nodes,
or right-endpoint Radau nodes, it asks for nodal |p| <= 2; it additionally
states the sharper Gauss target 1+tau_n. The linear polynomial 1+t supplies
the appropriate extremal value. The offer is first-correct-solution only.
There is a harmless introductory endpoint typo: the opening strict tau_n<1
does not apply to the subsequent Radau definition tau_n=1.

The current official migration inventory still lists prize.pdf, but it is an
accessibility/migration record, not a mathematical status update:
https://people.clas.ufl.edu/hager/content-removed/
The inventory says the links were redirected on 19 March 2026. A third-party
ledger still labeling this open is contradicted by the coauthor's historical
note and the actual published proofs. Do not use that ledger as status evidence.

Exact matrix equivalence
------------------------
Let ell_j be the degree-(n-1) cardinal polynomials at the n derivative nodes.
Define B_ij = integral from -1 to tau_i of ell_j(s) ds. For any nodal vector y,
q=sum_j y_j ell_j has degree <= n-1; its anchored antiderivative p has degree
<= n, p(-1)=0, and derivative data y. Hence p_nodes = B y. If Dbar is the
square differentiation block obtained by deleting the initial-state column,
then B=Dbar^{-1}.

For each row i, the exact worst value over |y_j|<=1 is sum_j |B_ij|: choose
y_j equal to the sign of B_ij to attain it. Thus the original nodal statement
is precisely ||B||_infinity <= 2. The sharp Gauss target is precisely
||B||_infinity <= 1+tau_n. This equivalence is established in the cited
literature; it is not a new reduction of ours.

Useful independent sanity check of the constant-2 result
-------------------------------------------------------
This short calculation explains why the historical result is believable.
Positive Gauss/Radau quadrature is exact for q^2 when deg(q)<=n-1, so the
derivative hypothesis gives integral[-1,1] q^2 = sum w_j q(tau_j)^2 <= 2.
For -1<=t<=1, Cauchy-Schwarz then gives
  |p(t)|^2 = |integral[-1,t] q|^2
           <= (1+t) integral[-1,t] q^2 <= 2(1+t) <= 4.
This is a restatement/check of established mathematics, not an original
solution and not a basis for claiming the historical reward. It does not
establish the sharper Gauss constant at the largest node.

Sharper Gauss statement: status remains unverified here
------------------------------------------------------
W. Gautschi, A Discrete Top-Down Markov Problem in Approximation Theory,
Frontiers in Orthogonal Polynomials and q-Series (2018), pp.267-289,
author-hosted reprint in Selected Works vol.4:
https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/wxg/SelectedWorks/WGvol4.pdf
The chapter's Theorem 2.1 gives the absolute-integral-sum extremal formula.
Sections 2 and 4 investigate the canonical sign pattern and monotonicity of
these sharp row maxima computationally. It discusses 1+tau_n explicitly.
Those numerical observations must not be promoted to all-degree proofs.
The 2019 weighted-row theorems settle P1-P4, but their stated norm bounds do
not by themselves prove the sharp 1+tau_n inequality. A bounded primary-source
search did not find a later sharp-bound resolution. This is an absence of
located evidence, not a certified open-problem status.

One bounded follow-up, only if the sharp variant is independently confirmed open
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A focused analytic target is positivity of the LAST Gauss integration row:
  integral[-1,tau_n] ell_j(s) ds >= 0 for every j and n.
If proved, its absolute row sum equals the ordinary row sum 1+tau_n.
For earlier rows, the simple Cauchy-Schwarz estimate above suffices whenever
  2(1+tau_{n-1}) <= (1+tau_n)^2  (n>=2).
Thus a bounded session can check the literature for these two precise lemmas,
then attempt only the last-row positivity lemma, using a Legendre-kernel or
quadrature argument. Handle n=1 separately. Do not attempt a general new
collocation convergence theory or assume the full canonical sign pattern.
If the two lemmas are unavailable after a short targeted pass, stop and record
the gap; small-degree algebra or high-precision samples would only be scouting.

This is a proposed sufficient route, not a verified proof of the sharp result.
The original constant-2 theorem would make a reasonable Lean learning target,
but its formalization alone is neither an open-problem solution nor evidence
of prize eligibility. Given the status correction, it has lower priority than
the active application paper unless the sharp variant acquires a clear status
and relevance argument.
