Let $G$ be a graph with $n$ vertices such that every induced subgraph on $\geq \lfloor n/2\rfloor$ vertices has more than $n^2/50$ edges. Must $G$ contain a triangle?
#128: [Er93,p.344][ErRo93][Er97b]
graph theory
A problem of Erdős and Rousseau. The constant $50$ would be best possible as witnessed by a blow-up of $C_5$ or the Petersen graph.

Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau, and Schelp [EFRS94] proved that this is true with $50$ replaced by $16$. More generally, they prove that, for any $0<\alpha<1$, if every set of $\geq \alpha n$ vertices contains $>\alpha^3n^2/2$ edges then $G$ contains a triangle.

Krivelevich [Kr95] has proved this with $n/2$ replaced by $3n/5$ (and $50$ replaced by $25$).

Keevash and Sudakov [KeSu06] have proved this under the additional assumption that either $G$ has at most $n^2/12$ edges, or that $G$ has at least $n^2/5$ edges. Norin and Yepremyan [NoYe15] proved that this is true if $G$ has at least $(1/5-c)n^2$ edges, for some constant $c>0$.

Razborov [Ra22] proved this is true if $\frac{1}{50}$ is replaced by $\frac{27}{1024}$.

See also the entry in the graphs problem collection.

  • We ran a systematic computational search for a counterexample and found none; since some of the intermediate values seem absent from the literature, we record them here.

    For a triangle-free $d$-regular pattern graph $H$ on $h$ vertices, the balanced-blowup local density at one half satisfies $(d+\lambda{\min})/8h \le f(H) \le d/8h$, with the floor attained iff $H$ has a bisection where every vertex keeps exactly $(d+\lambda{\min})/2$ neighbours on its own side. Applying this to all seven known triangle-free strongly regular graphs (constructions verified programmatically): $C_5$ and Petersen give the extremal $1/50$; Clebsch $= 1/64$, Hoffman-Singleton $= 1/100$, Gewirtz $= 3/224$, Higman-Sims $= 7/400$, all exact via explicit bisection certificates; M22 lies in $(10/616, 0.01771]$ (floor unattainable, 77 odd). In particular Gewirtz bisects into two 3-regular halves and Hoffman-Singleton into two 2-regular halves, which we have not found stated in the literature. None of the sporadic SRGs approaches $1/50$ — consistent with Brandt's observation that Higman-Sims only overtakes $C_5$-blowups at subset ratios $\ge 17/30$.

    A calibrated weighted-blowup max-min search over all connected triangle-free graphs on $\le 7$ vertices plus 35 named sporadics found nothing above $1/50$, and exact-objective simulated annealing over raw triangle-free graphs on $n \le 26$ vertices likewise found no graph above $n^2/50$ (best at $n{=}22$: 8 vs 10 needed; $n{=}24$: 10 vs 12; $n{=}26$: 10 vs 14); $1/50$ is attained only when $10 \mid n$, via $C_5$-blowups.

    Code and hand-checkable certificates: https://github.com/cormundus/erdos-128-census

  • (1): $n/2$ should be $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ in the statement. Otherwise five-cycle is a counterexample.

    (2): In the given reference, Keevash and Sudakov also proved it under the additional assumption that $G$ has $\geq \tfrac{n^2}{5}$ edges. ( The phrasing in their introduction suggests that this was the hard part, compared to proving it for $G$ with $\leq \tfrac{n^2}{12}$ edges. ) Later, Norin and Yepremyan (2015) proved $\geq (\tfrac{1}{5}- \gamma) n^2$ is enough, for some small but absolute $\gamma>0$.

    (3) Razborov (2021) proved it is true when $\tfrac{1}{50}$ is replaced with $\tfrac{27}{1024}$. According to Balogh, Cremen, Lidicky (2024), that is the most recent progress for this type of relaxation.
