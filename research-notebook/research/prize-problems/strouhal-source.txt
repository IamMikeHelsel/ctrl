1 Introduction
Flow around a cylinder is a well established problem of interest in fluid dynamics [3, 4]. This
has been examined extensively both experimentally [8, Figure 14.15] and computationally
(see references in [5, 6]). It is widely believed that a Hopf bifurcation occurs near Reynolds
number 50 [2, 7], where steady flow evolves into the Karm´ an vortex street [1, 11].
The temporal periodicity of the vortex street was first observed (see [1]) experimentally
by Strouhal in 1878 [10], although footnote 1 in [1] suggests that a complete history may
trace back to Leonardo da Vinci, as is the case with many areas of fluid mechanics.
The papers [9, 12] suggest that the vortex shedding in the Karm´ an vortex street is
periodic in time, beginning around Reynolds number 50 (coinciding with the believed Hopf
bifurcation [7, 2]) and continuing this periodicity up to Reynolds number 1000, but that this
periodicity noticeably begins to break down above that.
The paper [9] also demonstrates that there are easily computable, steady solutions of the
Navier–Stokes equations for Reynolds numbers above 50. These are also trivially periodic in
time, since they are constant in time. But we are interested in non-constant-in-time, periodic
solutions.
We know of no mathematical proof of the existence of a non-constant, periodic-in-time
solution at the Reynolds numbers indicated, and we offer a prize of$ 1,000 for the publication
of such a proof, or for a proof that no such periodic solutions exist.

For incompressible fluid flow, the Navier–Stokes equations are as follows,

1.3 Solution regularity
We are seeking a solution that conforms closely to what is observed in common experience,
formal experiments, and computational simulations, namely a smooth classical solution.
Since the boundary is smooth, there is no reason to expect any singularities. For example,
it is sufficient if it is proved that u in C 2 in space and p in C 1 in space, with C 1 regularity
in time. We expect u and p to be C ∞ in both space and time, and any deviations from this
would be interesting to report.
