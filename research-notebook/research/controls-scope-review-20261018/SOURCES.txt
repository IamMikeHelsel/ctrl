PRIMARY-SOURCE OVERLAP CHECK — 18 OCTOBER 2026
Targeted review, not an exhaustive novelty search or reproduced comparison.
Paraphrases below state only the inspected scope. No absence claims were inferred
from a search failure. The proposed joint contract has not had a code-level
overlap audit against these artifacts.

1. Singletary, Chen, Ames (2020), Control Barrier Functions for Sampled-Data
Systems with Input Delays.
https://arxiv.org/html/2005.06418v1
Read contributions, Sections III-IV, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1. The work already
addresses held control, state uncertainty and input delay, with a Segway
simulation. Thus these features alone are not our novelty. Compare assumptions,
the actual execution contract and checked artifact, not feature names.

2. van Wijk et al. (2024), Disturbance-Robust Backup Control Barrier Functions:
Safety Under Uncertain Dynamics.
https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07700v1
Read the backup-set construction, Assumption 1 and Lemma 1. A safe backup flow,
terminal invariant and uncertainty tightening are existing ideas. Our terminal
handoff does not introduce that principle. Their setup offers a relevant
comparative framework, but it has not been instantiated on our model here.

3. Hellwig et al. (2025 arXiv version), From Zonotopes to Proof Certificates:
A Formal Pipeline for Safe Control Envelopes.
https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20301v1
Read Definition 2 and the evaluation setup. It already formally connects
one-step invariance, intersample safety and command admissibility. Reproducing
those obligations in Lean can have artifact value, but is not a first formal
control envelope. No matched performance or proof-effort comparison was run.

4. Doll and Shames (2026), Foundations of Machine-Checked Control Theory in Lean.
https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19727v1
Read abstract and introduction. Lean Lyapunov and small-gain foundations exist.
The paper also identifies faithful definitions as a distinct obligation from
kernel checking. Our scope should be compared with those abstractions at the
interface level. This pass did not audit their full library implementation.

5. Gacsi, Kiss, Molnar (2025), Braking within Barriers: Constructive
Safety-Critical Control for Input-Constrained Vehicles via the Backup Set Method.
https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15797v1
Read backup-set setup and the input-constrained construction. Backup braking,
terminal sets and input bounds already have a substantial literature. This
vehicle construction is not a result for our three-state actuator; no claim of
identical assumptions or direct theorem substitution is made.

6. Das et al. (2026), Robust Adaptive Backup Control Barrier Functions.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20842
Abstract-only scope check. The authors describe certified parameter-estimation
bounds and backup safety with uncertain drift and actuation. This is a further
overlap warning for a broad "adaptive response-aware safety" claim. The full
assumptions and implementation remain unread in this review. Do not infer that
it covers arbitrary time-varying lag, our digital detector, or our Lean artifact.

Assessment from these sources
An engineering problem can remain useful when the main methods are established.
Our research claim must then identify and demonstrate a specific implementation,
composition, conservatism or reuse improvement. The current evidence does not
establish that improvement. Broadening the problem description does not establish
a gap in the literature.
