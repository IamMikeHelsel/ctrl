INTERNAL ASSESSMENT: DOES THE CONTROLS PAPER EARN A LARGER SCOPE?
18 October 2026. Root assistant self-review, not independent peer review.

Decision
Expand the engineering question, but do not expand the paper by collecting more
small theorems. Keep the original fixed-lag paper as a verified case study. The
preferred research direction is one complete digital braking-to-holding operation
under changing actuator response. First test whether that operation has a useful
timing and sensing budget. Publication novelty is still unestablished.

Mike's concern is supported. We have stronger evidence for correctness than for
usefulness. The old paper does not yet show a new control method, a useful design
decision for a measured axis, less verification work, or an advantage over another
complete verification pipeline. That does not make its checked mathematics wrong.

What survives this review
The fixed-lag result connects a real-valued sampled execution, clipping, held
errors and exact certificates to safety between every sample. It includes
existence and uniqueness. The recovery result adds a full-state handoff from a
box outside the invariant ellipsoid. Those are substantive verification tasks.
Their primary evidence remains frozen; this review adds no Lean theorem.

The variable-response work already proves a delayed first velocity zero and
one-sided travel bound. A separate result admits a noisy reading, a command
delay, and continuous-feedback holding without a state reset. The later motion
work derives terminal speed and acceleration bands after a reference stops.
These are different contracts. They are not one sampled stop-and-hold theorem.

What we should correct about our own process
1. We let the next provable lemma set the research agenda. The missing test was:
   which engineering decision changes when this proof is available?
2. Proof reviewers checked statements, assumptions and evidence well. Passing
   those checks did not answer whether the result was new or worth using.
3. We spent effort on papers and animations before fixing the comparative claim.
   Those tools explain results; they do not supply their research value.
4. We accumulated model variants. Fixed lag, varying lag, held feedback and
   continuous feedback now support different conclusions. Combining summaries
   would conceal real interface work.
5. Some reports lag the proofs. The recovery manuscript still describes an
   endpoint-only direct pilot. The later pilot includes between-sample safety
   and a robust tail, but only for one parameter cell. Neither description
   supplies a full matched comparison. Use the evidence map in COMMENTS.txt.

Separate decision gates
Formal correctness: strong recorded evidence within the declared contracts.
This pass inspected statements and records; it did not rerun the Lean stack.
New control theory: not established; the methods have close published precedents.
Lean contribution: plausible verification composition; independent reuse untested.
Engineering usefulness: untested against a calibrated task or a useful design trade.
Team review: ready, with these objections attached.
Submission as a new controls solution: hold pending a value test and overlap audit.

Recommended question
Given remaining clearance, noisy position/velocity/acceleration, bounded sample
age, and changing actuator response, when can a digital controller brake, switch
to holding, and remain within the travel limits without resetting the state?

The useful output is a decision: admit the maneuver, demand a smaller sample
age or speed, or decline the guarantee. The first research result can be a tight
description of this trade, even if no new controller is invented. Bounds on
residual speed and acceleration must be explicit whenever we say "stopped."

There is a concrete next test
The existing 5.5525 cm hold cube permits 0.11105 m/s^2 total target mismatch.
Its example sensor bounds alone cost 0.093 m/s^2 under the hold gains. A new
exact-arithmetic screening calculation leaves a total information-age ceiling
of about 1.394 ms if physical target error also uses 0.01 m/s^2. A 1 ms age
fits that sufficient calculation; 5 ms does not. This is not a sampled safety
proof or a necessary timing limit. See AGE-BUDGET.txt for the assumptions.

This is useful direction-setting evidence: digital execution is a real missing
obligation, and there is a nonempty candidate budget. It is not evidence that
our method is novel, that 5 ms is unsafe, or that hardware can meet these bounds.
The existing one-time 5 ms capture delay is a separate, still-valid contract.

Scope choices
A. More ellipsoids and parameter cells: do not make this the main expansion.
   They complete comparisons, but do not establish value by themselves.
B. Digital changing-response braking and holding: preferred application route.
   It addresses Mike's original question and exposes a practical timing trade.
C. Reusable Lean verification library: viable alternative if a different plant
   uses the unchanged generic interface and the saved work is demonstrated.
   Physical rescaling of the same plant is not enough evidence of reuse.
D. Full Franka, contact, grip loss, PL d/e, or human motor control: separate work.
   Adding these to this paper now would introduce unsupported claims and models.

What would change the decision
Complete one operation on one common model. Compare it with a credible robust
sampled-data or backup-control baseline under the same limits. Show a useful
admitted region, a less conservative budget, less verification effort, or a
clear checked interface that another user can apply. Record negative results.
Correctness plus a different proof assistant is not sufficient by itself.

If the digital bridge only supports unusable sensing/timing, or the comparison
offers no benefit and independent reuse fails, keep the result as a documented
library example or tutorial. Do not broaden the claims to rescue the paper.
