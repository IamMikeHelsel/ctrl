1. A larger certified sampling interval

The normalized double integrator obeys ẋ = v, v̇ = u. At each sample we choose u = −2x − 3v and hold it constant. The exact sample update is

x⁺ = x + h v + (h²/2)(−2x − 3v)
v⁺ = v + h(−2x − 3v).

With V(x,v) = 5x² + 2xv + v², Lean now proves

0 ≤ h ≤ 1/2  ⇒  V(x⁺,v⁺) − V(x,v) ≤ −h(x² + v²)
                    V(x⁺,v⁺) ≤ (1 − h/6)V(x,v).

Consequently arbitrary sample-to-sample variation within [h_min, 1/2] gives V_n ≤ (1 − h_min/6)ⁿ V_0. A positive h_min gives geometric decay. The upper limit is three times our earlier sufficient bound of 1/6; it is not claimed optimal. Time and state are normalized, not calibrated physical units.

The proof expresses the matrix polynomial (P − A(h)ᵀPA(h))/h in a Bernstein basis. Every coefficient matrix exceeds the identity. Nonnegative basis weights that sum to one then give a uniform bound across the entire interval. Symbolic algebra discovers the coefficients; the Lean proof independently checks the polynomial inequalities.

2. Grid rounding, clipping, and disturbances

Define qδ(z) = δ round(z/δ), and command u = clip(−2qδ(x) − 3qδ(v), −1, 1). The held acceleration is u + w. Lean proves nearest-grid error ≤ δ/2 and bounds the resulting control error. It also proves clipping cannot increase error relative to an in-range nominal command.

Each sample interval h_n1/20 ≤ h_n ≤ 1/2
Grid spacing δ1/10000

Initial conditionV(x_0,v_0) ≤ 1/10

Conclusion: V(x_n,v_n) ≤ 1/10 at every sample. This includes, for example, the initial state (0.1, 0), whose energy is 0.05. The recurrence is exact and disturbances may vary adversarially from interval to interval.

This is ideal real-number rounding, not a proof of floating-point firmware. The theorem includes the clipped update but is local to the stated energy region; it is not a global result for strongly saturated trajectories. The new rounded-controller theorem proves sample-time safety, not an independent between-sample safety envelope or convergence to zero under persistent disturbance.

3. The original-research track

For L_A(X) = AX + XAᵀ, the remaining order-six question asks whether a symmetric X always achieves the largest Frobenius-norm amplification. The primary paper reports truth through dimension five and counterexamples from seven onward, leaving six open. Kressner–Vandereycken, 2026.

We have checked the diagonal-A case in Lean in any finite dimension, given an index of maximal diagonal magnitude. A single diagonal-entry witness attains the upper bound. This is an elementary known case and a baseline for subsequent formalization, not a new contribution to the open case.

The earlier 12-minute numerical search found no counterexample in 5,004 optimization restarts. Its best positive gap was approximately 3×10⁻¹⁵, at floating-point noise scale. A separate search uses diagonal-plus-skew coordinates: an orthogonal change of basis diagonalizes the symmetric part of any real matrix, preserving both restricted Frobenius operator norms. That reduction is mathematically justified and numerically cross-checked, but has not yet been formalized here. The two-minute structured search completed 1,245 restarts and likewise found no counterexample; the best gap was about 2×10⁻¹⁵. Numerical searches prove no universal statement.
