APPLICATION PAPER: ADVERSARIAL CLAIM REVIEW
Reviewed 17 October 2026. Read-only review of manuscript and proof sources.

Verdict
-------
No blocking mathematical overclaim found in the current focused draft. The
measured-request theorem, physical scaling, active-clipping example, and
position-only counterexample agree with the implemented definitions and
statements. The two minor issues raised during review have been corrected:
division by elapsed time is explicitly restricted to positive elapsed time,
and the claim map now names measured-execution continuity and ODE theorems.

Claim fidelity: A-. Publication contribution: plausible focused formalization
case study, not established new control theory. This is a qualitative review
grade, not a probability of acceptance. The remaining substantive publication
risk is relevance and breadth, rather than a known mismatch between the paper
and the checked artifact. No source or manuscript changes were made by this
reviewer. PDF typography and an independent full hand-proof audit were outside
this pass.

Claim-by-claim findings
----------------------
1. Plant and disturbance channels: ACCEPT.
   The paper's e enters the scalar requested command before clipping; d enters
   after clipping, as a held acceleration-command error. MeasuredTravel.segment
   implements exactly heldSegment t z (saturate 1 (input z + e) + d).
   The distinction between |u| <= 1 and a possibly larger |u+d| is explicit.
   This is neither a force disturbance nor an arbitrary within-interval signal.

2. Main all-time theorem: ACCEPT.
   MeasuredTravel.state_invariant and travel_limit have the advertised positive
   lower sampling bound, upper bound 1/5, combined |e_n|+|d_n| <= 1/100, initial
   V <= 12, and arbitrary real t >= 0. state_at_sample,
   state_continuousOn_nonnegative, and state_ode_interior supply the other
   conclusions. The latter proves componentwise derivatives on open sample
   intervals with command computed from that interval's recursive sample.
   No assumption of an already-safe trajectory or ODE uniqueness is hidden.
   The draft correctly identifies a defined execution and does not claim
   differentiability at switches or uniqueness of every abstract ODE solution.

3. Nonvacuity and clipping: ACCEPT.
   MeasuredTravel.active_initial proves V(0,0,1)=278/25 < 12 and clipping of
   request -399/200 to -1 with e=d=1/200. active_example_travel supplies an
   all-time instantiation with period 1/10 and these nonzero errors. Therefore
   active saturation is admitted, not excluded by an inactive-clipping region.
   The text correctly refrains from claiming perpetual saturation or attainment
   of every sector/enclosure extreme.

4. Region and weakened-contract counterexample: ACCEPT.
   P in the manuscript equals TravelGeometry.P. The coordinate and gain
   inequalities used are x^2 <= V/3 and k^2 <= V/2; the upper energy constant is
   451/10. They are sufficient geometric bounds, not exact support values.
   TravelScope.position_only_initial, position_only_escapes, and
   outside_certified_region match Proposition 1. The initial energy is exactly
   2322/25 > 12. At elapsed time 1/5, position equals 32/25+exp(-1/5), which is
   at least 52/25 > 2. The admitted time statement does not require the unsafe
   initial state to be admitted by the main ellipsoid theorem.

5. Analytic-to-polynomial bridge: ACCEPT.
   The held formulas and F0-alpha*g*K signs agree with SaturatedLag. The gain
   is selected from the sampled state and held, not recomputed continuously.
   A shared exponential interpolation variable preserves coordinate dependence.
   Separate affine dependence in gain and exponential justifies the four
   vertices without assuming those uncertain quantities are independent random
   variables or dynamically free. Convexity applies to a positive quadratic
   form. The proof uses enclosure of the actual exponential; the vertices are
   not misidentified as the actual dynamics. The zero-time case is explicit.
   Decay is relative to the sample state, not pairwise monotonicity in time.

6. Certificate scope and trust: ACCEPT.
   Four travel cases have cubic residuals, one interval [0,1/5], and four
   Bernstein coefficient matrices. The exact identity and coefficient LDL
   witnesses are checked. Model-to-vertex identities are separate. The producer
   is correctly outside the trust boundary. The text does not infer new control
   theory, optimality, or truth of a physical model from compilation.

7. Response bound and invariant budget: ACCEPT.
   Independently recomputed using Python Fraction arithmetic:
     endpoint energies: 278/25, 3049/250, 7474/625;
     eta = 5/451;
     direct D = 1469/12500 = 0.11752;
     region budget = 60/451 = 0.13303769401330376;
     strict slack = 87481/5637500 > 0.
   TravelDisturbance.response_energy_bound concerns the response without w;
   disturbance_energy_bound supplies the w^2 form shown in equation (7).
   The claim-map label is broadly accurate; optionally list both declarations
   if the intent is to point directly to that exact displayed inequality.
   StepScaledRobustness uses a parameter proportional to elapsed time, with
   the zero-time identity handled separately. The D expression and invariance
   arithmetic match the source.

8. Measured-request reduction: ACCEPT.
   Clipping nonexpansiveness yields the combined scalar error bound globally.
   effectiveErrors is computed from the recursively defined actual samples;
   samples_eq_effective and state_eq_effective prove equality to the base
   execution. There is no circular construction assuming safe sample states.

9. Rounding: ACCEPT WITH THE DRAFT'S EXISTING LIMITATION.
   roundedRequestError_bound has delta_x/2 + 3 delta_v/2 + delta_a, with each
   grid width positive. roundedSamples computes measurements from its own
   evolving samples. Equal grids 1/1000 consume 3/1000, leaving 7/1000 for d.
   rounded_active_example confirms the initial state is represented exactly
   and clips. Mathlib's round uses half-integer ties toward positive infinity.
   The draft correctly limits this corollary to ideal real-valued sensing,
   safety, and sample agreement; it does not silently claim that a separate
   rounded global execution calculus theorem was proved, or that floating
   point, register width, overflow, sensor range, or physical grids are verified.

10. Physical interpretation: ACCEPT.
    PhysicalTravel normalizes time by tau and state by (U*tau^2,U*tau,U).
    Its measured_travel_limit, measuredState_at_sample,
    measuredState_continuousOn_nonnegative, and measuredState_ode_interior
    establish the stated dimensional model and envelope. Positive tau and U
    are retained. Controller gain scaling and post-clamp disturbance scaling
    are correct. For tau=1/10 and U=2 the limits are 20 ms, 0.02 m/s^2 combined
    error, and 0.04 m position; initial acceleration 2 requests -4 and clips
    to -2. The table is explicitly illustrative, not calibrated hardware.
    Varying known tau is not robustness to uncertain tau; the draft says so.

11. Response-bound ablation: ACCEPT.
    Exact recomputation gives C=9471/200=47.355 and
    D_coarse=1070223/2500000=0.4280892. The final rational comparisons are the
    TravelScope theorems. The paper explicitly distinguishes their checked
    status from the explanatory coarse-bound derivation and from an actual
    unsafe trajectory. No maximality claim or comparative solver claim occurs.

12. Mutation and producer tests: ACCEPT.
    Current evidence/model-bridge/verification.json binds to the current
    SaturatedLag source. Its positive case passes; changing feedback 2 to 3
    while retaining old vertex matrices fails at all four identity locations.
    Producer log records acceptance of the valid scalar and rejection of the
    corrupt residual and changed transition, plus 14 passing producer tests.
    These are rejection-boundary tests, not proofs the modified plant is unsafe.

13. Existing SOS checker comparison: ACCEPT.
    The translator emits 12 rational weighted squares per cubic residual,
    distributed over the nonnegative interval factors h and H-h: 48 total.
    It is not a global unconstrained SOS decomposition. The replay has four
    residual-nonnegativity, four transition-identity, and four decrease proofs.
    Input, generated-source, translator, emitted-source, manifest, and all three
    replay-log hashes match the current files. The Lean 4.33.1 / fb7ae41 replay
    times round to 20.593, 9.049, 9.260 s, median 9.260 s, as stated.
    The text correctly says existing-witness translation, cached imports,
    no discovery, different toolchain, and only vertex-level comparison.

15. Literature and novelty phrasing: ACCEPT.
    The primary sources inspected in this research support the current narrow
    descriptions. Hellwig et al. distinguish one-step invariance, intersample
    safety, and input admissibility; Arechiga et al. cover nondeterministic
    sampled loops; Doll and Shames cover Lean control foundations; existing
    SOS and MatrixSOS cover positivity checking; Palmeira et al. treat sampled
    magnitude/rate saturation. None are presented as executed competitor
    benchmarks except the explicitly pinned SOS replay. The manuscript avoids
    first-ever, new-theory, and general comparative-advantage claims.
    Primary reference URLs are listed below. No new broad literature search
    was needed for this delta review; this check reused inspected primary
    papers and the source-specific comparison evidence.

Remaining publication issues and suggested next decision
--------------------------------------------------------
- The core plant is intentionally small and exactly solvable, and the control
  tools are established. A reviewer may reasonably view it as an illustration
  rather than a research contribution. A workshop/case-study framing is honest;
  numerical precision, theorem counts, and AI effort do not answer novelty.
- The response ablation and genuine clipping give the application a concrete
  point. They support the chosen sufficient contract, not optimality. Preserve
  that distinction when writing an abstract for a venue.
- The physical transform is useful but does not substitute for a justified
  plant. A second model that exercises the same generic interfaces, or a
  documented axis contract with uncertainty/delay assumptions, would test
  applicability more strongly than additional elementary lemmas.
- Upstream SOS replay is a useful same-object representation check. It is not
  an independent full proof of the saturation, exponential, execution, or
  sensing layers. Preserve this boundary in any publicity.
- Before submission: human mathematical/application review, release license,
  stable artifact distribution, authorship details, and venue-specific length
  and formatting. None require altering the current mathematical statements.
- Optional claim-map polish: name TravelDisturbance.disturbance_energy_bound
  alongside response_energy_bound; name the physical continuity/ODE declarations
  in artifact documentation if the paper table is too narrow to include them.
