FOCUSED PAPER OUTLINE — 17 October 2026

Working title:
  Lean-Checked Travel Safety for a Sampled Actuator with Saturation
Optional subtitle:
  Exact certificates, bounded held errors, and continuous execution

Audience and scale: a 6-8-page formalization/benchmark case study plus a
reproducible source artifact. Adapt length and supplementary-material rules
only after choosing a venue. The existing generic library remains in the
repository; the paper need not explain every development branch.

THESIS

For one explicit saturated sampled actuator model, an untrusted exact producer
can supply witnesses that Lean connects all the way to a continuous execution
and a position bound at every real time. This is a complete application contract
and reusable formal composition, with measured conservatism. The underlying
control inequalities and certificate methods are established.

ABSTRACT — about 150-180 words

1. State the modeling challenge in one sentence: endpoint or approximate
   certificates alone do not state continuous-time safety of a clipped held loop.
2. State the concrete result: ellipsoid E_P<=12, |u|<=1, H=1/5, positive
   minimum dwell time, held error budget 1/100, |x(t)|<=2 for all t>=0.
3. State the exact active witness: z0=(0,0,1) requests -2 and applies -1.
4. State how it is checked: exact exponential semantics, four polynomial
   vertices, rational certificates, scaled perturbation, and clock assembly.
5. Add sensor/physical corollaries only after their final statements compile
   and are reviewed. Give one measured evaluation outcome when available.
6. Conclude with scope: a reproducible formal application, not hardware validation.
Avoid the broad plant catalog, theorem counts, unrelated conjecture, and repeated
phrases saying the paper's contribution is still being decided.

1. PROBLEM, MODEL, AND CONTRIBUTIONS — about 1 page

Open with the actuator axis and the requested position envelope. Show the
sampled clipped feedback, the first-order lag ODE, and the location of error.
One small diagram can distinguish sampled state, clamp, held drive, lag, and
continuous position. Define command bound versus post-clamp drive bound.

List three concrete contributions: complete exact execution/safety theorem;
reusable certificate/enclosure/error interfaces; evaluated reproducible artifact.
Use one paragraph to position these against existing formal control work.
State normalized units and full-state initial conditions here, not only in a
closing disclaimer. Explain that a later scaling corollary gives physical units.

2. MAIN SAFETY CONTRACT AND EXECUTION — about 1 page

Display P and S={z:E_P(z)<=12}. State the main theorem with all quantifiers:
every z0 in S, every h sequence in [hmin,1/5], every admitted held error
sequence, and every real t>=0. Show the active witness directly after it.

Define samples recursively and the real-time trajectory by its containing
half-open interval. Summarize the exact solution, initial identity, continuity,
and ODE on interval interiors. Explain positive dwell time and the boundary
convention. General ODE uniqueness is not needed for this defined execution
and is not claimed. Defer routine clock proof algebra to artifact documentation.

3. FROM THE EXACT PLANT TO RATIONAL CERTIFICATES — about 1.5 pages

Explain the held sector gain alpha, fixed from the initial sample. Show the
single shared exponential e=exp(-s), F0(s,e), and g(s,e). State the proved
enclosure 1-s<=e<=1-s+s^2 and multi-affine four-vertex interpolation.

Introduce only the certificate facts needed here:
  P-A(s)^T P A(s)-s*c*I = s*R(s),
  Bernstein representation of R on each rational partition,
  rational LDL factors with nonnegative diagonal,
  common P and positive energy bounds.
Give the generic vertex-energy implication. Make the untrusted producer and
checked model-to-transition identity visibly separate obligations.

This is the appropriate place for one compact proof-dependency diagram:
  exact ODE solution -> enclosure and clamp sector -> four vertex certificates
    -> nominal segment decrease -> perturbation budget -> segment invariant
    -> recursive samples and clock coverage -> continuous all-time travel.

4. HELD ERRORS, SENSOR COROLLARY, AND SCALING — about 1.25 pages

Prove the logical step that makes the result work at arbitrarily small offsets:
  E(gw)<=13*s^2*w^2,
  Young parameter theta=s*eta,
  E(next)<= (1-s*eta)E(start)+s*D,
  D<=eta*R.
Give exact final rational constants and the explicit s=0 treatment. Avoid
re-deriving the separate fixed-theta two-state ISS development.

If completed, add the actual pre-clamp sensor-error recurrence and its checked
conversion to a held command error through nonexpansiveness. State the combined
budget once. Ideal rounding gets one short corollary with its exact semantics;
do not call it floating-point or fixed-width verification.

If completed, provide the physical scaling relation, dimensions, resulting
controller gains, interval bound, travel distance, and initial ellipsoid. Use
one or two illustrative parameter sets labeled assumptions. They demonstrate
how to apply the theorem, not calibration or uncertainty robustness.

5. EVALUATION AND REPLAY — about 1.25 pages

Organize around questions, not repository history:
  Q1. Does the certificate enable the stated clipping/error/travel combination?
      Exact constants, witness, and one explanatory trajectory figure.
  Q2. Which relaxations limit the certified budget?
      Same-plant partition/sector/response/energy-bound study, including failures.
  Q3. What does replay cost and trust?
      Frozen source, exact pins, source hashes, fresh project build, axiom audit,
      producer/bridge rejection tests, and clear cached-dependency timing scope.

Suggested table columns:
  variant | valid region/sector | H | C | error budget | partition count |
  certificate size | generated-proof check time | evidence status.
Every row must identify whether only a witness was produced, Lean accepted it,
or the complete final model theorem was checked. Keep numerical diagnostics
visually separate from certified rows. One row must be the exact headline case.

Use a second compact table mapping main claims to theorem names. A global helper
declaration count belongs in artifact metadata, not the abstract or contribution.
If the existing SOS comparison is retained, explain that it is a different
double-integrator instance and only shared-witness interoperability. Prefer a
matched travel residual if that bounded comparison is completed.

6. RELATED WORK AND LIMITATIONS — about 0.75 page

Lead with the nearest formal safety pipeline, Hellwig et al. [1], then the older
nondeterministic sampled-control work [2]. Explain representation and contract
differences, without claiming superiority. Mention Lean control foundations [3]
and existing rational/SOS and interval matrix-certificate tools [4-5]. Cite
established sampled saturated actuator analysis [6] for the control ingredients.

Limits: normalized one-axis model; chosen P/controller; conservative sufficient
conditions; held error assumptions; explicit rather than arbitrary-ODE execution;
no hardware acceptance or machine arithmetic. Scaling is not parameter uncertainty.
If sensor-error proof completes, remove the obsolete blanket statement that
sensor error is absent, while retaining its exact budget/holding restrictions.

7. CLOSING — a short paragraph

State the checked application outcome and what the evaluation taught us.
Name one concrete next step, such as a calibrated model or same-object external
comparison. Do not end with a list of unrelated mathematical ambitions.

EDITORIAL ACCEPTANCE CHECK

A reader should be able to state the plant, quantified disturbances, sampling
assumptions, initial set, travel result, and evidence boundary after the first
two pages. Every later section should help justify, apply, or evaluate exactly
that statement. If a paragraph does none of those, move it to the supplement.
