Current fit against the call's benchmark criteria (our judgment):
- Clarity: strong. Full-state initial condition, held errors, rate range,
  sampled ODE semantics and physical scaling are explicit. Uniqueness closes
  the analytic-construction versus arbitrary-execution gap.
- Verification advantage: credible. The theorem covers all admitted timings,
  errors, fixed rates and real times; a trace simulation does not do this.
  Existing formal-control tools already cover comparable obligations, so no
  general advantage over them has been established.
- Relevance: weakest. First-order actuator lag and saturation are plausible
  axis phenomena, but no public measured axis, implementation or industrial
  acceptance test anchors the illustrative constants. A mathematical benchmark
  framing must be clear about this limitation.
- Reproducibility: strong local evidence, with pinned proof source, full check,
  regenerated rational certificates, failed model mutations and fresh-source
  replay. A clean external rebuild is still valuable before submission.
