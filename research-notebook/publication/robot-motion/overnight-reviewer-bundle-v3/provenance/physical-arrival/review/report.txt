What the proof establishes

The same actual state f appears in the plant equation and controller. Its only
initial error-cube premise is at time zero. IsTrajectory requires continuity
over [0,2.5] s and the actual differential equations throughout the interior.
No state or error cube is reset at 0.52 s or 0.62 s.

The full retained history is the history already checked in builder-01: a
20 mm increment at 0.22 s and a -20 mm increment at 0.62 s. The earlier prefix
is unchanged. The tracking theorem uses the actual reference derivatives and
the full jerk guard from time zero. PhysicalArrival then extracts coordinate
zero of the tracking cube, which is exactly physical position error.

The declared plant/controller conditions are essential. Rate lies in [80,120]
per second, disturbance magnitude is at most 0.01 m/s^2, and the scaled initial
cube radius is 0.0002 m. The budget is 41/1600 <= 0.04 in the declared units.
The reference at 0.5 s is 779/250000 m. The progress proof subtracts both possible
position errors, including the error in the initial position. It does not assume
that initial position is zero. The resulting lower bound exceeds the required
1 mm progress. This excludes an always-hold response from the task proof.

The theorem establishes the physical position tube on [0,2.5] s and the target
position band on [1.42,2.5] s, including the 1.70 s deadline. It is conditional
on an actual trajectory with the stated equations. It does not prove general
ODE existence, physical model identification, an online implementation, bounded
actuator effort, Franka behavior, or physical velocity/acceleration settling.

Non-vacuity and the rest boundary

Independent Lean witnesses construct an actual trajectory for rate 96. The
reference itself is a trajectory with zero disturbance and zero initial state.
More generally, a constant position offset e is a trajectory under disturbance
800*e. Setting e = 1/80000 m gives an admissible 12.5 micrometre offset under
disturbance 0.01. It meets the original initial cube and instantiates the arrival
theorem, but its terminal position is not exactly the target.

A second constructed trajectory adds t/1000000 m to reference position and
1/1000000 m/s to reference velocity. Its disturbance is (800*t+60)/1000000 and
stays within the declared bound over [0,2.5]. It also satisfies the original
initial cube and instantiates the arrival theorem. Its velocity at 1.70 s is
exactly 1 micrometre per second. Thus position arrival does not imply exact
physical rest. This witness does not violate the sampled 0.001 m/s tolerance.

Interpretation and next gate

The new guard repairs a genuine reference-limit failure and supports a conditional
physical position contract. It does not beat native fast on task completion or
speed in this comparison. Native fast has at least 13.598 mm actual progress
before revision, minimum-duration native has at least 6.439 mm, and both FIR
methods have at least 3.112 mm. Their revision states and return distances differ.
The lower FIR jerk-energy value is therefore not a matched-state or matched-path
advantage. The generous-case figure labels this limit clearly. Keep the tight
deadline failure table next to that figure when integrating it into a paper.

The next useful experiment should use one common prefix and carry one actual
state through revision availability. Compare the suffixes from that same state.
Include native fast and a native duration tied to the declared FIR arrival time.
Freeze the protocol first. Measure task success, jerk energy, actual error,
extra travel, waiting and rejection together. Do not reset physical state to
make the comparison easier. This tests the remaining motion-quality hypothesis
before adding broad superiority claims. A robust admission contract can be
useful without being a better general motion planner.
