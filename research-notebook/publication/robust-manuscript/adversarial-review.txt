1. Mathematical/model claims: no identified blocker, with precise boundaries

The execution statement is substantive. ExecutionUniqueness.IsExecution requires
continuity, the initial state, and derivatives on open holds with feedback taken
from f(sampleTime h n). It does not assume equality to the analytic construction
or the desired invariant. constructed_isExecution and execution_eq_state supply
existence and correspondence separately. held_ode_unique uses continuity at the
endpoints and interior derivatives, so switching times do not hide a derivative
assumption. The positive dwell lower bound supplies global clock coverage.

The manuscript's energy conclusion follows by composing execution_eq_state with
UncertainTravel.state_invariant; the position conclusion is exposed directly as
execution_travel_limit. The normalized source permits a combined error of 1/90,
so the paper's 1/100 statement is weaker. PhysicalExecutionUniqueness explicitly
normalizes arbitrary physical trajectories, including their own sample feedback
and time derivatives. Its final theorem uses the stated positive tau0, tau, U,
fixed ratio interval, nominal controller gains, and U/100 error budget.

Do not relax these boundaries in a revised abstract or submission summary:

* One fixed r is quantified across plants. Neither sample-varying nor continuously
  varying lag is established. Abstract line 19 says "while the actual lag varies";
  change this to "across the admitted fixed-lag plants" to remove a temporal
  ambiguity. The body is already explicit, so this is a wording repair rather
  than an identified false theorem.
* Errors are held scalars in the specified command channels, and their magnitudes
  share one budget. A continuous-time bounded external force/jerk is not covered
  merely by having the same numerical bound. The post-clamp error also means the
  total drive is not constrained by the clamp's unit limit.
* The full-state initial ellipsoid is essential. The result is not a safe-stop
  controller for every initial position inside the travel interval.
* Uniqueness fixes the same prescribed h, e, d sequences. This does not alone
  prove existence/uniqueness of a controller with an arbitrary state-dependent
  error mechanism. Universal sequence safety is still meaningful.
* The rate interval is not a symmetric plus/minus-ten-percent time-constant
  interval. The physical theorem and its displayed reciprocal interval are right.

The sector obstruction is correctly qualified at lines 180–190. It rejects this
P for an enlarged independent sector family; it does not show failure of the
actual clipped controller. The proposed obstruction vector is outside the safe
ellipsoid and alpha=1/4 cannot arise from the nominal clipping law inside it.
Calling it a "certificate abstraction ablation" would be more exact than using
it as evidence of plant difficulty or of an improved controller.

The response ablation is also appropriately limited. The coarse norm constant's
derivation is an arithmetic diagnostic; UncertaintyScope proves the displayed
budget comparisons, not the entire coarse-norm argument. Preserve that sentence
at line 204. Neither failing sufficient inequality proves unsafety, and neither
passing constant is optimal. The response bound and step-scaled Young argument
support a useful compositional proof, but are not themselves new control theory.

2. Contribution weaknesses that can cause a rejection despite correct proofs

A. Why this benchmark? The paper specifies a small synthetic third-order axis
well, but does little to explain why its exact combination of saturation, fixed
lag uncertainty, held errors and jitter is representative or difficult for
existing verification methods. The physical-unit example changes units; it is
not independent application evidence. State plainly that this is an academic
benchmark, then identify the verification obligations it is designed to expose.
Do not imply that numerical constants were motivated by measured robot limits.

B. Reuse is asserted more than demonstrated. The generic matrix invariant and
Bernstein interfaces are real reusable components. The twelve-vertex bridge,
exact exponential flow and execution uniqueness development are specialized.
A reviewer needs to know exactly what a second user supplies: P and polynomial
matrices, rational certificates, a model identity, an error response estimate,
and a clock/execution bridge. A list of module names and 830 theorem declarations
does not measure this effort. Identify the manual obligations and the automation
boundary, and show one concise invocation plus its expected checked output.

C. Evaluation is mostly correctness auditing. Mutation rejection and ablations
are useful, but they do not establish scalability, convenience, coverage or an
advantage over a competing verifier. The paper does not need an invented race
against another system. It does need a small reproducibility table and a concrete
description of what another investigator can attempt with this benchmark. The
existing records already support a 104.14-second fresh project check with cached
pinned dependencies and a separate 56.98-second uncertainty-artifact pipeline.
Label scopes exactly; the latter includes derivation, replay and negative tests,
and the former excludes producer tests/regeneration and dependency bootstrap.
These single runs are observations, not performance statistics or scaling data.

D. Prior work already covers the broad narrative. Hellwig et al. develops a
control-envelope certification pipeline with intersample safety and invariant
reasoning. Doll and Shames supplies general Lean stability/small-gain foundations.
Merely using Lean or proving all-time safety is therefore an insufficient novelty
claim. The current draft avoids claiming priority, which is appropriate. Replace
the short disclaimer-heavy positioning paragraph with a compact comparison of
problem assumptions, certificate objects, execution semantics and demonstrated
outputs. Do not infer a comparative advantage from different assistant names,
and do not describe the nominal SOS replay as verification of this entire case.

E. The local package has an observable documentation mismatch. The source README
still describes the prior nominal travel/sensor/four-SOS manuscript at lines
150–157. Its reading order omits UncertainLag, UncertainTravel, UncertainExecution,
ExecutionUniqueness and PhysicalExecutionUniqueness. A reader following it will
not arrive at this paper's primary result. Update the guide and release manifest
together, rather than claiming reproducibility from the source archive alone.

4. ARCH27 fit and final readiness judgment

The official call, checked 17 October 2026, accepts benchmark proposals, tool
presentations and experience reports; it allows small academic benchmarks and
does not require the benchmark to be solved. It asks for papers ideally 3–8 pages,
lists a 1 April 2027 deadline, and evaluates benchmark relevance, clarity and
verification advantages. Benchmark submissions require an accompanying data zip.
Source: https://cps-vo.org/group/ARCH/Call-for-Submissions

Against those criteria: clarity is already fairly strong; verification advantage
can credibly mean uniform, all-time certification over infinitely many admitted
sampling/error sequences, which finite simulation does not establish; relevance
is the weakest part and needs an explicit academic verification rationale. A
comparison proving superiority to all other formal tools is not required.

My recommendation would be major revision for a benchmark/experience report,
with a plausible path to a defensible submission using the work above. I would
not recommend submitting the current working draft unchanged, and I would not
recommend marketing it as new control theory. The official venue scope makes the
narrow framing credible; it does not establish acceptance or remove the need to
demonstrate usefulness. No numerical acceptance odds can be justified here.
