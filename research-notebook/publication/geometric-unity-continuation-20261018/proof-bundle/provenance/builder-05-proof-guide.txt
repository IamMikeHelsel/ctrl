Proof guide — actual complement action and split signature

Fixed data
J=Fin 7. Basis vectors are indexed by all 128 finite subsets T of J. The basis
is the accepted increasing-order exterior basis. Each negative generator is
Ni=creation(i)-contraction(i). K is the existing product N0*N1*...*N6.
The rightmost factor acts first. No generator or basis convention is changed.

1. Actual basis action
ComplementAction.prefix_length identifies the insertion/contraction sign with
the number of occupied coordinates below i. The accepted actual creation and
contraction formulas then give negative_basis and negative_mulVec.
product_mulVec transports these facts to an ordered generator product.

signedToggles_seven proves, by Lean kernel evaluation of a finite decidable
statement, that all 128 subsets satisfy
  K e_T = s(T) e_(T complement),
  s(T)=(-1)^(sum over i in T of (7-i)).
There is no native_decide or external calculation in this proof. The finite
identity is bookkeeping, not an assumption about the actual matrix. The bridge
from actual exterior operators to that bookkeeping is proved first.

Why the sign has this form: factor i contributes one sign for every occupied
index below it. A contraction contributes one more minus sign. Applying factors
in order 6,...,0 leaves the lower occupied indices unchanged at each step.
Thus an occupied index j contributes (6-j)+1=7-j signs.

2. Explicit real congruence
Let d(T)=+1 if coordinate 0 is absent and -1 if it is present. D is the diagonal
matrix with entries d(T). The complement action proves DK=-KD. The accepted
K^T=K and K^2=I remain the same actual-matrix theorems.

Define U=I+KD. Direct matrix algebra proves
  U^T U = U U^T = 2I,
  K U = U D,
  U^T K U = 2D,
  Uinverse=(1/2)U^T,
  Uinverse U = U Uinverse = I.

The columns are explicit. For each T not containing 0, let s=s(T):
  column T             = e_T + s e_(T complement),
  column (T complement)= e_(T complement) - s e_T.
The sign identity s(T complement)=s(T) is proved. On this ordered pair the
matrices are
  K = [[0,s],[s,0]], D = [[1,0],[0,-1]], U = [[1,-s],[s,1]].

The congruence remains indexed by the original finite subsets. Its diagonal
entry is +2 for the 64 subsets without 0, and -2 for the 64 subsets with 0.
The two cardinalities are proved. Thus it is diag(2I64,-2I64) after grouping
those two explicit sets of columns. No separate Fin 64 enumeration is needed
or asserted. Off-diagonal entries are proved zero.

3. Actual complex extension
ComplexSignature uses the accepted realToComplexMatrix map. Uc, Dc and the
inverse are entrywise extensions of U, D and Uinverse. The existing Kc is used.
It proves
  Uc^H Uc = 2I,
  Uc^H Kc Uc = 2Dc,
  Ucinverse=(1/2)Uc^H,
  Ucinverse Uc = Uc Ucinverse = I.
Here H denotes conjugate transpose. The complex congruence entries are explicitly
+2, -2 and zero on the same actual subset indices.

4. Non-vacuity and false claims
The vacuum column goes to the full subset. The singleton {0} needs a minus sign.
The real and complex forms have explicit positive and negative transformed
entries. Omitting D gives I+K, which kills the nonzero vector e_empty-K e_empty.
This prevents replacing the invertible change of basis with a singular projector.
Six false clients challenge signs, complementation, normalization, positive-only
signature and the omitted-D construction. Exact failure logs are preserved.

Trust and scope
All local source modules are rebuilt fresh. Pinned external dependency products,
compiler products and hardware remain trusted. The separate checker adds proof
replay evidence; it does not remove all software trust. The finite Python check
supplements the proof and is only a builder cross-check, not independent review.
This result formalizes standard split Clifford algebra. It is not GU physics.

Two different signatures
The (7,7) label describes the quadratic form on the 14 Clifford generators.
The (64,64) result here describes the compatible form on their 128-dimensional
spinor representation. These are different spaces and different forms. The
result does not change spacetime dimension or identify an observable spectrum.
