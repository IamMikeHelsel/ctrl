What the formalization revealed

1. Full compatibility is rigid; Spin invariance is not.
Requiring every real Clifford vector to be skew-adjoint fixes the form up to
scale. A nonzero-entry normalization then fixes it exactly. But actual
nondegenerate Hermitian matrices H_t=K(I+t rho(i omega)) are all Spin-invariant,
and H_1 is not any scalar multiple of K. This is a proved distinction between
two assumptions. It does not say the forms are inequivalent under changes of
spinor coordinates.

2. Invariant choices do not automatically preserve the Clifford relation.
For F_(a,b)(v)=a v+b omega v, the square is (a²-b²)Q(v). Preserving the original
quadratic form is equivalent to a²-b²=1. A real parameter family remains even
after that extra condition. The source does not explicitly require Phi_1 to
satisfy this added normalization; this is a test of a possible selection rule.

3. Formula freedom can be representation freedom.
Every normalized choice in that displayed family is implemented by conjugation
with an actual Clifford unit that centralizes Spin. The rational example
(5/3,4/3) is conjugation by 2+omega, with inverse (2-omega)/3. Thus different
formulas need not describe different algebraic models. This does not establish
equivalence under the complete theory's intended gauge group, or physical
equivalence. The negative branch has a separate adjoint-norm issue.

4. The unresolved operator question is now more specific.
There are explicit independent candidates for the low-degree invariant choices.
Their actual algebraic properties are checked; the complete invariant Hom-space
lower bound is still a hand proof. To determine whether these choices produce
different curvature operators, one must fix the brackets, projections, curvature
domain, normalization and Bianchi assumptions. The source's action exists, but
the variational data must also be fixed before deriving field equations.

These are useful insights from this project, not claims of new mathematical
classification or validation of Geometric Unity as a physical theory.
