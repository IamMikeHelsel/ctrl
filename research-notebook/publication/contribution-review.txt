CONTRIBUTION REVIEW — 17 October 2026

Decision: develop a focused formal-methods case study or benchmark paper around
the saturated actuator travel theorem. The existing checked result is coherent
and nonvacuous. The current broad manuscript obscures it and does not yet
establish the significance expected of a general new-method paper. Acceptance
cannot be predicted from a successful build or theorem count.

This review inspected the existing main.tex, the travel proof chain, the fresh
40-module/464-declaration replay, existing SOS comparison records, and primary
literature/official venue scopes. Sensor-error and physical-scaling extensions
are in progress; they are recommendations below, not claimed completed here.

1. ONE SENTENCE THAT THE PAPER SHOULD DEFEND

We provide a reproducible Lean certificate connecting an exact sampled
actuator-lag model with genuinely active command saturation to a continuous,
all-real-time travel bound under explicit timing and held-error budgets.

The known mathematical techniques are the means. The contribution to evaluate
is a complete, inspectable chain whose final assumptions and physical meanings
remain visible. Say what that chain contains rather than implying that using
Lean makes an otherwise routine controller novel.

Three concrete contribution statements are supportable:
  (a) An explicit held execution, continuity and interior-ODE proof, and a
      region-wide all-time travel theorem that includes an actively clipped
      initial state. The theorem has a positive disturbance budget and arbitrary
      sampling sequences in the stated interval.
  (b) Reusable exact certificate and composition interfaces: rational interval
      matrix positivity; common-energy convex enclosure; and elapsed-time-scaled
      quadratic perturbation. Their benefit must be shown by reuse and evaluation.
  (c) A reproducible artifact binding input data, generated witnesses, source,
      semantic bridges, and axiom audits to the final theorem, with a measured
      account of conservatism and clearly scoped application corollaries.

Do not claim: first formal control proof; first formal sampled-data safety;
first rational proof certificate; a new saturation criterion; an optimal safe
region; a general nonlinear-control verifier; physical acceptance; or 464 new
research theorems. Also avoid an unsupported claim that no existing framework
can express this exact benchmark. Our search establishes close overlap, not
the absence of all prior equivalent instances.

2. WHY A REVIEWER COULD REJECT THIS VERSION

R1. "This is a simple three-state LTI plant with clipping, checked by textbook
    inequalities. Why is the formalization scientifically interesting?"
Response needed: identify the reusable interfaces and the semantic obligations
that were easy to get wrong, then measure them. List concrete outcomes: exact
exponential remainder retained, same gain held across the segment, positive
error budget valid down to zero elapsed time, continuous clock-assembled
execution, and a clipped witness in the SAME invariant set. Do not inflate
these obligations into new control theory.

R2. "KeYmaera and existing Lean/SOS projects already do the hard parts."
Response needed: compare contracts, representations, and assumptions explicitly.
The Lean ecosystem integration and a compact certificate format may be useful;
a language port alone may not justify a full research paper. A small, well
evaluated case study is more credible than a broad framework claim here.

R3. "The example is hand picked; the constants are arbitrary and conservative."
Response needed: a same-plant study varying H, sector range, partitioning, and
error budget while preserving the full invariant-set constraints. Explain why
P was selected and which relaxation limits the result. Show failures as well
as successes. Merely adding more unrelated elementary plants will not fix this.

R4. "The physical relevance is undefined."
Response needed: checked dimensional scaling, an explicit engineering checklist
for when the model applies, and sensor-error corollaries with honest budgets.
No specific hardware is required for an academic case study, but an assumed
time constant, acceleration-command scale, and initial-set geometry must have
clear meanings. Scaling the same theorem is not an independent benchmark or
parameter-uncertainty proof. A held command-channel error is not every load force.

R5. "The evaluation compares different goals and toolchains."
Response needed: treat the existing double-integrator SOS experiment as
shared-witness interoperability, not evidence of superiority for this travel
problem. A search failure in two configurations says nothing about infeasibility.
Do not compare a single polynomial replay time with the whole project build.

R6. "The paper reads like a development diary."
Response needed: remove the separate matrix conjecture, old nominal P, two-state
rounding/inactive-clipping detour, 26-step recovery episode, and detailed baseline
catalog from the main story. They may remain in the repository or supplement.
Replace repeated statements that novelty remains under review with one accurate
contribution paragraph and one limitations section. The submission should make
a modest affirmative claim, not ask reviewers to discover the claim for us.

3. CLOSEST PRIMARY PRIOR ART

[P1] Hellwig, Schafer, Qian, Platzer, Althoff, From Zonotopes to Proof
Certificates: A Formal Pipeline for Safe Control Envelopes, iFM 2025,
proceedings 2026, pp. 248-267. Primary full paper inspected.
https://logic.kastel.kit.edu/ap/pub/zonotope-proofs.pdf
https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-10794-7_13
This is the closest methodological comparator. It connects numerical witnesses
to KeYmaera X proofs, and separates one-step invariance, within-step safety, and
control admissibility. Its displayed system uses a fixed sample period and a
control envelope; our target is a specified clipped controller under variable
sampling with a quadratic ellipsoid. Its Taylor-model/zonotope machinery is
broader than our explicit single-exponential enclosure. We have not executed
their artifact or established a performance/generalization advantage over it.

[P2] Arechiga, Loos, Platzer, Krogh, Using Theorem Provers to Guarantee
Closed-Loop System Properties, ACC 2012, DOI 10.1109/ACC.2012.6315388.
https://logic.kastel.kit.edu/ap/pub/TPgenloop.pdf
Primary full paper inspected. It already treats nondeterministic held updates,
sampling variation, and continuous closed-loop safety through theorem proving.
Jitter and a model-to-closed-loop guarantee are established concepts. Use this
to frame the problem, not as an unexecuted tool-performance comparison.

[P3] Doll and Shames, Foundations of Machine-Checked Control Theory in Lean,
arXiv:2607.19727v1. Primary paper inspected.
https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19727v1
Lyapunov and small-gain foundations are already formalized in Lean, with a
framework spanning continuous, discrete, and hybrid settings. Our exact held
instance and producer interface should be positioned as complementary. State
whether the artifact reuses their library; currently it does not depend on it.

[P4] leanprover/sos and lean-matrix-sos. Primary repositories inspected;
the local baseline SOS frozen witness was executed, MatrixSOS was not built.
https://github.com/leanprover/sos
https://github.com/selpoG/lean-matrix-sos
Rational certificate replay and interval matrix positivity are already present.
Our Bernstein test is sufficient and may reject a positive polynomial; MatrixSOS
has a stronger general interval characterization. The contribution is neither
new positivity mathematics nor verified numerical search.

[P5] Palmeira et al., Sampled-data control under magnitude and rate saturating
actuators, International Journal of Robust and Nonlinear Control 26 (2016),
3232-3252. Publisher abstract inspected; their numerical experiments not replayed.
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rnc.3503
Actuator dynamics, saturation sectors, quadratic/functional certificates, and
aperiodic sampling have established control-theoretic treatments. Our normalized
lag application is deliberately simpler. A control-theory venue would need a
substantial new design/performance result beyond this formal composition.

Optional nearby background, not a required new bibliography branch: CESAR
already synthesizes provably safe control envelopes for hybrid systems. Its
objective is controller-envelope synthesis rather than checking this fixed
clipped controller. Primary author paper and publisher metadata inspected.
https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~jlaurent/pdf/papers/tacas24-cesar.pdf
https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-57246-3_9

4. MINIMUM EVALUATION WORTH DOING NOW

E1. SAME-PLANT CONSERVATISM TABLE — highest priority; parent owns execution.
Keep the saturated lag model and explicit final safety specification fixed.
Record P, c, H, kappa, R, travel L, response C, Young eta, and error budget.
For every variant, check BOTH geometric requirements and the disturbance
budget; an improved vertex interval is not automatically an improved travel
theorem. Distinguish a producer's exact witness success from a full Lean replay
and from numerical diagnostics.

Use a small justified grid, not an opaque optimization sweep. Useful contrasts:
  * one interval versus finer Bernstein partitions;
  * broad kappa=1/4 sector versus a tighter sector still valid on the SAME region;
  * crude norm response bound versus the checked C=13 response bound;
  * trace-based energy bound versus a tighter rational PSD bound, if implemented.
One revealing ablation is already exact: the coarse response bound
B=9471/200 cannot certify W=1/100 at R=12, whereas C=13 can. It demonstrates
that a specific checked refinement enables the stated application budget.
Numeric sampling of the true exponential can indicate conservatism, but a
failure-free grid is not a proof of the true maximal H or safe region.

E2. SENSOR/SCALING COROLLARIES — agents are implementing these now.
For measured z+error before clipping, use clamp nonexpansiveness and the actual
gain to bound the resulting held command error. Prove the budget for the actual
new recurrence, rather than vaguely declaring all errors interchangeable.
Ideal nearest-grid rounding is a useful mathematical instance; finite-width
evaluation, overflow, or hardware ADC behavior remain separate. Verify an
actively clipped state remains admitted under the new theorem.
Physical scaling should give algebraic conditions and units for arbitrary
positive scales. Two illustrative scale choices help interpretation but are
not measured hardware or two independent dynamical benchmarks.

E3. FOCUSED REPLAY AND CLAIM MAP — feasible this turn.
Freeze and replay the final source including the added corollaries. Retain
the previous 464-declaration checkpoint. Give a table mapping each paper claim
to a public theorem and its assumptions. Report the main travel dependency
closure separately from the repository's exploratory theorem count, if easy.
Existing mutation checks are useful; one bridge-level mutation of a feedback
coefficient with the old generated transition would be a particularly clear
demonstration that the semantic identity is checked. Mutation rejection is
artifact testing, not an additional formal theorem.

E4. MATCHED COMPARISON — useful next, bounded scope this turn if cheap.
Translate one or all FOUR travel residuals, with identical P,c,H, into frozen
SOS witnesses using the installed comparison toolchain. Each cubic Bernstein
matrix residual converts into weighted rational squares of state polynomials.
Record source/certificate size and fresh-process replay time with the same
import/audit boundary. This is shared-witness interoperability, not independent
discovery or a complete second proof of travel safety. If it is not run, leave
the existing double-integrator comparison in the supplement and say why it does
not evaluate the new application. Do not force a rushed KeYmaera port.

E5. ONE EXPLANATORY FIGURE — already substantially present.
Show the admitted active-clipping trajectory with command, travel envelope,
and energy. Caption it as an illustration, since the theorem quantifies all
admitted sequences. A second figure should show the certified budget frontier
or ablation, not another attractive simulation of the same easy trajectory.

5. WHAT REALLY STRENGTHENS THE PAPER

Useful: quantified conservatism; genuine reuse in the sensor-error proof;
engineering scaling that can be checked by a reader; a stable artifact that
replays outside its author directory; a precise nearest-prior-work comparison;
and a short account of the model/proof boundaries exposed by formalization.

Weak substitutes: more helper lemmas, a larger theorem count, unrelated matrix
identities, wider claims of generality, screenshots of compilation, many random
simulations, or putting AI assistance itself at the center of the contribution.

For a workshop/case-study paper, a genuinely new general theorem is unnecessary
if the application, artifact, and lessons are useful. For a stronger full-paper
claim, require evidence that others can reuse the interface on a meaningful
new model or specification without recreating most of the proof by hand.

6. PLAUSIBLE PUBLICATION ROUTES — OFFICIAL SCOPES CHECKED

Best current fit to assess: ARCH benchmark/tool/experience paper. The official
call currently advertises ARCH 2027 online on 2 June 2027, a 1 April 2027
submission deadline, and papers ideally 3-8 pages. Academic small benchmarks
are allowed, with clarity, relevance, and verification value central criteria.
Our artifact could be a benchmark plus verified reference solution if the model
and scope are well explained. This is a fit judgment, not an acceptance forecast.
Recheck the live call before making a submission plan.
https://cps-vo.org/group/ARCH/Call-for-Submissions

Lean/ITP route: an ITP short paper or proof pearl is plausible if the reusable
formalization architecture is the main technical lesson. The official ITP 2026
call allowed short papers for ongoing work and requested formalization artifacts.
That edition is past; no future ITP deadline is asserted here. A regular paper
needs a stronger significance/reuse argument than the current single case.
https://itp-conference-2026.github.io/cfp.html

Lean Together is a good source of technical feedback, not equivalent to an
archival peer-reviewed paper. Its official 2026 page describes an open community
meeting and abstract-selected talks. That edition is past; no new submission
window is asserted. A talk can precede a later archival case study.
https://leanprover-community.github.io/lt2026/

TACAS-style case study is a higher bar. Its official 2026 scope asks for a
practically important problem, modeling discussion, detailed comparative results,
and conclusions useful beyond the tool community. The present normalized case
has not yet demonstrated that practical importance. Artifact evaluation supports
a paper; it does not replace novelty/significance. No current TACAS deadline is
asserted and no submission is recommended today.
https://etaps.org/2026/conferences/tacas/

7. READY/NOT-READY BOUNDARY

Ready now: a defensible checked mathematical application and a reproducible
reference artifact, with a clear active-saturation safety theorem.
Not ready now: a general novel control method, a practically validated actuator
solution, or a submission claiming broad comparative advantage.
Target for this turn: one focused draft, evaluated same-plant tradeoffs, checked
sensor/scaling corollaries if they complete, and refreshed immutable evidence.
Then obtain human domain/framing review before selecting the precise paper type.
