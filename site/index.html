Check Braking Limits & Allocate 4-Foot Wrenches Without an Online Optimizer

The engineering bottleneck: Emergency braking on a quadruped or wheeled-legged base can trigger foot slip or forward pitch-over if deceleration exceeds what friction and support geometry allow. Solving a 12-variable second-order cone program (SOCP) every 1 kHz tick works for general terrain, but introduces iterative solver latency and potential non-convergence near active contact limits.

What we proved & tested: Under the theorem's level-ground four-contact model, a_max = min(μ, W/h_G) * g is proved necessary and sufficient for existence of friction-cone-admissible, non-tipping contact forces, and a closed-form bilinear ZMP + proportional shear allocator realizes any safe acceleration with identically zero net Euler torque (netEulerTorque = 0). Integration obligations: supply conservative friction and stance geometry, an admissible backup command, measured delay and actuator limits, and validate contact behavior on your target.

Compare Rest-to-Rest Motion Profiles by Modeled Winding & Viscous Energy

The engineering bottleneck: Classical 5th-order minimum-jerk trajectories minimize integrated squared jerk, not electrical winding heat. On high-duty-cycle pick-and-place cells, peak acceleration and mid-stroke velocity drive motor I2R and viscous dissipation under identical move durations and zero-velocity/zero-acceleration endpoints.

What we proved & tested: By factoring boundary conditions into s^3(1-s)^3(2s-1) and proving rest-to-rest cross-power orthogonality (∫ a(t)j(t) dt = 0), the trajectory-family loss reduces to an exact rational quadratic in η. Setting η = −11 lowers the 1D inertial acceleration-squared integral by 11/60 = 18.33%. In our 6-DOF MuJoCo RNE rollout, this yields 4.1% less total modeled motor I2R loss (where static gravity holding torque dominates), 10.8% less modeled viscous loss, and 22.5% less dynamic modeled Joule loss (where lower peak velocity also reduces Coriolis/centripetal torque). Integration obligations: match drive losses, gravity, regeneration, and voltage/current limits on your hardware before selecting η.

Attenuate Impulsive Torque Disturbances Against a Tuned Computed-Torque Baseline

The engineering bottleneck: Contact-rich manipulation (deburring, insertion, payload handoff) subjects robot joints to sudden impulsive torque spikes on top of multi-frequency vibration. A fixed-gain linear Computed-Torque Controller (CTC) must trade off steady-state sensor-noise amplification against peak excursion during a transient impact.

What we proved & tested: Sontag's universal CLF stabilizer guarantees state-dependent Lie-derivative dissipation V̇ ≤ −√(a2 + b4), paired with strict-feedback backstepping and saddle-point H∞/ISS error bounds. In the reported 6-DOF MuJoCo torque-impulse experiment, the combined law reduced peak joint error by 39.4% (27.1 mrad → 16.4 mrad) and RMS error by 39.6% against a matched-pole high-gain Computed-Torque Controller (Kp = 416, Kd = 42, plus sliding damping). Integration obligations: identify plant dynamics and disturbance bounds, preserve sampling assumptions, tune a matched baseline, and test with actuator saturation and sensor noise.

Eliminate Optical-Axis Camera Retreat During Large Wrist Rotations

The engineering bottleneck: Classical image-based visual servoing (IBVS) linearizes feature motion in image coordinates. Under a large optical-axis camera roll (Chaumette, 1998), the coupled interaction matrix commands the camera to retreat backward by sec(Δφ/2), pulling an eye-in-hand manipulator toward workspace singularities.

What we proved & tested: Combining 4-point projective homography uniqueness and positive-depth cheirality filtering with decoupled log-depth feedback ḋ = −λ d ln(d/d*) makes the rotational Lie derivative of plane depth identically zero. In the reported MuJoCo rollout under a large camera roll, depth stays strictly monotone inside [1.015 m, 2.200 m], whereas the classical IBVS baseline retreats to 12.67 m (5.76×). Integration obligations: calibrate camera intrinsics, reject correspondence outliers, handle degenerate planar views, and enforce field-of-view and joint-workspace limits.

Step 2 · Zero-Heap C99 / PLC Kernel (codegen.py)

The research implementation expresses a convex-interpolation rule between a nominal command and a backup. It has a fixed three-component loop and no iterative solver or dynamic allocation. Its model requires an admissible backup and valid inputs; target timing, floating-point error, and PLC behavior need separate verification: 

void ctrl_lorentz_cbf_filter_3d(
    const double a[3], double b,
    const double u_nom[3], const double pi_b[3],
    double *lam_out, double u_safe[3]) {
  double num = b - ctrl_dot3(a, u_nom);
  double den = ctrl_dot3(a, pi_b) - ctrl_dot3(a, u_nom);
  double lam = (num <= 0.0) ? 0.0 : ((den > 0.0 && num < den) ? (num / den) : 1.0);
  *lam_out = lam;
  for (int i = 0; i < 3; ++i) u_safe[i] = (1.0 - lam) * u_nom[i] + lam * pi_b[i];
}
What connects the theorem and implementation: The separate research checkout associates Python functions with Lean declarations and tests selected formulas using exact rational inputs or float64, as appropriate. Its reported C99 differential checks found a maximum absolute discrepancy of 8.88 × 10−16 against the tested Python reference. Finite tests do not prove general program equivalence; an absolute error is not a universal ULP bound. The public theorem library, runtime implementation, compiler and physical plant are separate evidence layers.

3. What Is Formally Proved vs. What Is Empirically Tested

Formal verification in control theory and robotics has a well-known boundary between continuous real-number proofs and floating-point physics execution. These four layers carry distinct evidence and adoption obligations:

1. Lean theorem library Public ControlsLean/ (55 modules, 445 audited headline theorems) + erdos/ Lake workspace on main. Checked mathematical implications under their stated hypotheses. Run scripts/check.sh for the current build, escape-hatch and axiom audit. Does not by itself establish sensor accuracy, floating-point behavior, compiler correctness or hardware conformance.
2. Research Python references Separate research checkout: 48 registered functions = 42 domain functions + 4 benchmarks + 2 code-generation helpers. Not shipped on this public branch. Declaration-name/source audits and finite exact (Fraction) or numerical (float64) tests provide traceability and regression evidence. Observed outcomes for the configured multi-body model, integration settings, tasks and tuned baselines. Simulator outcomes do not validate unmodeled hardware, thermal behavior, all disturbances or a complete safety case.

2. Symmetric vs. Skew Lyapunov Conditioning (Byers–Nash n=6)

For the continuous Lyapunov operator ℒA(X) = A⊤X + XA, Byers and Nash asked whether the minimum singular value on skew matrices k(A) is always ≤ the minimum singular value on symmetric matrices s(A). Its control motivation concerns matrix-operator conditioning; no practical SDP solver improvement is established here. We formalized orthogonal decoupling on 𝕊n ⊕ 𝕂n, unconditional exclusion for rank ≤ 4, and a 15D Parseval decomposition of 𝕂6, plus exact rational LDL⊤ ≻ 0 certificates on a 10-pair non-Common-T grid. Bounding the remaining 3D commuting off-diagonal cross-term analytically is in work.
